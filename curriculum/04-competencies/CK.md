# CK — Culinary knowledge

Terminology, method theory, recipe literacy, and the classical and world repertoire. Entries follow the format of the
[competency framework §1.2](../04-competency-framework.md#12-the-entry-format); the evidence methods are defined in
§1.3. The mapping block under each entry is generated from the outline.

### CK-TERM — Kitchen terminology

**Elements:** cuts and shapes; cooking methods; equipment; sauces and their derivatives; garnishes and classical names;
menu and shop language. Each in English, Dutch (NL) and, where the kitchen uses it, French (FR).

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the core terms of S1 (cuts, methods, equipment, mise en place, *fond*, *blancheren*, *sauteren*) and what they
  mean in practice.
- L3: the terms of the classical techniques of S2: stocks, the sauce families, protein cookery and baking, including
  *nappe*, *monter au beurre*, *liaison*, *beurre manié*, *déglacer* and *à point*.
- L4: menu and repertoire language: classical garnish names, regional dish names, and terms in recipes from other
  traditions; knows where a term is used loosely or differently in NL, FR and EN.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L2–3: reads a recipe in any of the three languages and does what the terms ask.
- [S] L4: writes recipes, plans and menus in correct, consistent terminology.
- [J] L4: chooses the precise term over the vague one (*brunoise*, not "finely chopped"), and explains a term to a guest
  in plain words.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): uses a glossary.
- S2–S3 (3): reads and follows terms without one.
- S4–S5 (4): uses the terms actively and correctly in their own writing.

**Assessment:**
- quiz items in the module checks, including image identification (cuts, garnishes);
- the terminology in written plans, recipes and menus, reviewed against a term list;
- re-tested in the `MS-S1` knowledge quiz.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Matches 80 % of the S1 terms to their meaning and translation in a quiz, and follows them in a recipe without looking them up more than twice | quiz |
| 3 | Scores 80 % on the S2 terms; a framework written in their own words uses at least ten technical terms correctly | quiz, written |
| 4 | A menu and two recipes of their own use the terms correctly and consistently, with no vague term where a precise one exists; explains five terms to a non-cook in plain words | written, quiz |

**Not yet:** "sauté" used for any frying; mixing up cut names; inconsistent spellings or terms across one document;
classical names used without knowing what they promise to the guest.

### CK-METH — Cooking methods

**Elements:** dry heat (sauté, pan-fry, sear, roast, grill and broil, deep-fry); moist heat (boil, simmer, poach,
steam, blanch); combination (braise, stew, pot-roast, sous vide); live fire; the choice of method from an ingredient's
properties.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the three families, how each transfers heat, and what each does to surface and interior.
- L3: the variables of each method (temperature, medium, time, surface moisture, pan load) and their effect.
- L4: matching method to ingredient: tenderness, collagen, water content, size and shape; why two-stage methods work
  (sear then roast, blanch then sauté).
- L5: predicts the outcome of an unfamiliar method-ingredient pairing, and teaches the choice, including edge cases.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [J] L3: picks a suitable method for a familiar ingredient without a recipe.
- [J] L4: picks and justifies a method for a new ingredient or cut, including a fallback.
- [J] L5: optimises a method choice for a constraint (time, equipment, guests), and explains what would go wrong with
  the alternatives.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): classifies methods with help.
- S2 (3): chooses for familiar ingredients.
- S3–S4 (4): reasons the choice for new ingredients.
- S5 (5): teaches it.

**Assessment:** quiz (classification and scenarios); written method choices in framework and brief exercises;
comparison exercises (one ingredient, two methods); re-tested in the `MS-S1` quiz.
The capstone's brief analysis and defence show method choice too, but its rubric scores them under `JD-CHSE`.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Classifies 15 methods into the three families and names the heat transfer of each, at 80 % in a quiz | quiz |
| 3 | For six familiar ingredients, names a suitable method and its key variable in writing; scenario items at 80 % | quiz, written |
| 4 | For three unfamiliar cuts or vegetables, chooses and justifies a method from their properties, cooks one, and the result confirms the reasoning | written, photo |
| 5 | Over at least three briefs (including the capstone), method choices are reasoned from principles, with the rejected alternatives and their risks named; a short written guide to the method choice for one ingredient group would let another learner choose correctly | written, photo, quiz |

**Not yet:** choosing by habit ("I always fry it"); braising a tender cut or grilling a collagen-rich one without a
reason; describing methods only by recipe steps.

### CK-RCPT — Reading recipes critically

**Elements:** the intent of a recipe; quantities and ratios; missing or ambiguous steps; errors; turning a recipe into a
work plan.

**Knowledge:**
- L3: the parts of a recipe; how recipes leave things out (salt "to taste", pan size, doneness cues); common ratio
  checks (vinaigrette, roux, bread hydration, custard).
- L4: recipe genres (restaurant, home, historical, translated) and their typical gaps; units and conventions across
  countries.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: turns a recipe into a mise-en-place list and a timeline before cooking.
- [J] L3: spots missing information and fills it from principles.
- [J] L4: finds an error (a wrong ratio, an impossible time) before cooking, and corrects it with a reason.

**Independence:**
- S1–S2 (3): annotates a recipe with a checklist.
- S3–S5 (4): reads critically without one.

**Assessment:** written recipe annotations (an annotated recipe is a standard exercise artefact); quiz items with flawed
recipes; re-tested in the `MS-S2` plan.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Turns a full recipe into a mise-en-place list and a timeline, with the help of the checklist | written |
| 3 | Annotates an unfamiliar recipe: intent, missing cues, the pan size and the key ratio; the cook follows the annotation and succeeds | written, photo |
| 4 | Finds at least two of three planted errors in a recipe (ratio, time, quantity) and corrects them with a reason; adapts a recipe from another country's conventions | quiz, written |

**Not yet:** starting to cook before reading to the end; following an obviously wrong quantity; no timeline for a
recipe with waiting times.

### CK-CLAS — Classical repertoire

**Elements:** the mother sauces and their main derivatives; classical cuts and shapes; classical garnishes and dish
names; the structure of the classical menu; the brigade and its vocabulary as context.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the five mother sauces, their base and thickener, and three derivatives each.
- L3: how a derivative is built from its mother sauce: reduction, liaison, mounting with butter, garnish.
- L4: the repertoire as a system: why a derivative is built as it is; garnish names and what they promise; how the
  classical repertoire lives on in modern menus.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L2: makes a mother sauce and a derivative with guidance.
- [S] L4: makes classical preparations from their name alone (for example a *sauce Bercy*, *pommes duchesse*, a
  *jardinière* garnish).
- [J] L4: adapts a classical preparation for a modern menu, keeping its logic.

**Independence:**
- S2–S3 (2): with a reference sheet.
- S4–S5 (4): from memory and principle.

**Assessment:** quiz (repertoire, image identification); classical preparations in `CLS-4` from their name; re-tested in
the `MS-S4` knowledge re-test.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Names the five mother sauces with base and thickener, and three derivatives each, at 80 %; makes one derivative from a framework | quiz, photo |
| 3 | Explains how six derivatives are built from their mother sauce, and makes two of them from a framework, at the right consistency | quiz, written, photo |
| 4 | Cooks three classical preparations from their name alone, recognisably correct; classical quiz at 80 %; one written adaptation for a modern plate that keeps the logic | quiz, photo, written |

**Not yet:** knowing names without the structure behind them; derivatives with the wrong base; "classical" used as a
label for anything with butter.

### CK-CUIS — World culinary traditions

**Elements:** the traditions of `CUI-3`: French regional, Italian, Iberian, Levantine and North African, Indian,
Chinese, Japanese, Mexican. For each: its pantry, core techniques, flavour logic, meal structure, and the history that
shaped it.

**Knowledge:**
- L1: that traditions differ in pantry, technique and meal structure.
- L3: per tradition: the pantry and core techniques, its flavour logic, and three to five representative dishes and
  why they work.
- L4: compares traditions; distinguishes established practice from regional variation and personal preference; knows
  the limits of its own knowledge.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: cooks representative dishes of a tradition from frameworks, recognisably in style.
- [J] L4: plans a meal within a tradition without a recipe; substitutes respectfully when an ingredient is missing,
  naming what changes.

**Independence:**
- S2 (1): awareness.
- S3 (3): cooks from frameworks within each tradition.
- S4–S5 (4): plans and cooks within a tradition from principles.

**Assessment:** quiz per tradition (pantry, techniques, flavour logic); dishes judged against a style description; a
written comparison; re-tested in the `MS-S4` menu.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Describes the pantry, two core techniques and the meal structure of four traditions, at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | For each tradition in `CUI-3` taken, passes the tradition quiz at 80 % and cooks two dishes from frameworks that the style description and tasters recognise | quiz, photo, tasters |
| 4 | Plans and cooks a three-course meal within one tradition from principles, with a written rationale that names the pantry, techniques and flavour logic and marks any adaptation; a written comparison of two traditions | written, photo, tasters |

**Not yet:** "Asian" or "Mediterranean" used as one cuisine; one spice standing in for a whole tradition; substitutions
that change the dish's logic without saying so.

### CK-BEVR — Beverages and pairing

**Elements:** wine (grapes, styles, structure); beer and cider; non-alcoholic drinks (tea, coffee, juices, ferments,
alcohol-free pairings); principles of pairing; drinks as an ingredient in cooking.

**Knowledge:**
- L1: the main drink families and that structure (acid, sweetness, tannin, alcohol, carbonation) interacts with food.
- L2: structure in more detail; classic pairings and why they work; serving temperatures.
- L3: pairing principles applied: match or contrast weight and intensity, acid with acid, sweetness at least that of the
  dish, tannin with protein and fat, heat with alcohol.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L2: tastes drinks deliberately and describes their structure.
- [J] L3: chooses an alcoholic and a non-alcoholic pairing for each course of a menu and explains the choice.

**Independence:**
- S3 (1): awareness.
- S4–S5 (3): pairs independently.

**Assessment:** quiz (structure, principles); a tasting form; written pairings tested at the table with tasters;
re-tested in the `MS-S4` menu and the capstone.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Describes the structure of four drinks on a tasting form; pairing principles at 80 % in a quiz | quiz, form |
| 3 | For a three-course menu, chooses an alcoholic and a non-alcoholic drink per course with a written reason; at the table, most tasters judge each pairing to work, and the learner's notes explain any that didn't | written, tasters, form |

**Not yet:** pairing by colour alone ("white with fish"); a dry wine with a sweeter dessert; no non-alcoholic option.
