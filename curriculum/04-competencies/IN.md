# IN — Ingredient knowledge

Selection, quality, seasonality and storage, and how each ingredient group behaves. Entries follow the format of the
[competency framework §1.2](../04-competency-framework.md#12-the-entry-format). The mapping block under each entry is
generated from the outline.

### IN-SEL — Selection, quality and seasonal buying

**Elements:** freshness and ripeness signs per ingredient group; quality grades and labels; seasonality, with Dutch
produce as the baseline; where to buy (supermarket, market, specialist shops); value for money.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the freshness signs of vegetables, fruit, eggs, dairy, meat and fish; reading a label (dates, origin, grade).
- L3: seasonality of Dutch produce through the year; quality differences that matter for the method (waxy versus
  floury potatoes, dry-aged versus fresh beef).
- L4: judging quality against price and purpose; when a lesser grade is the right choice; sustainability labels for
  fish and meat, and their limits.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L2–3: picks good produce, meat and fish in the shop, with a checklist and then without.
- [J] L4: changes the plan when the best ingredient on the day is a different one, and explains the choice.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with a checklist.
- S2 (3): without one, for familiar items.
- S3–S5 (4): plans menus around the market.

**Assessment:**
- photos of purchases with a short quality note;
- quiz items on freshness signs (with images) and seasons;
- seasonal menus;
- re-tested in the `MS-S3` week plan.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Freshness and label items at 80 %; a shopping trip with a photo of each item and its freshness signs named | quiz, photo |
| 3 | Over two shopping trips without a checklist, every fresh item is photographed and judged correctly; a seasonal calendar quiz for Dutch produce at 80 % | photo, quiz, written |
| 4 | A week plan built from what was best in season and at the market; at least one change on the day, reasoned; the quality, price and purpose of the main items noted, and the receipts confirm the plan | written, photo, measure |

**Not yet:** buying by recipe regardless of season; tired produce not noticed; no idea of the price of what was bought.

### IN-STO — Storage

**Elements:** fridge zones and temperatures; freezing and thawing; dry storage; ripening; storing prepared food and
leftovers; labelling and rotation (first in, first out).

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the fridge at 4 °C and the freezer at −18 °C or below; where raw and ready-to-eat food go; what doesn't belong in
  the fridge (tomatoes, bread, most whole squash) [S22].
- L3: shelf lives of prepared food and leftovers; safe thawing; how storage changes quality (starch retrogradation,
  freezer burn, moisture loss).
- L4: planning storage for batch cooking and preserves; vacuum and wrapping; storage for quality as well as safety.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: stores a week's shopping and prepared food correctly, labelled and dated.
- [J] L4: decides what to prepare ahead and how to store it so that quality holds.

**Independence:**
- S1–S2 (3): stores correctly from a guide.
- S3–S5 (4): plans storage as part of the menu.

**Assessment:** quiz; photos of the fridge with a thermometer reading; labels; re-tested in the `MS-S1` quiz and
through the storage in milestone prep days.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Storage quiz at 80 %; the fridge reads 4 °C or below on a thermometer, photographed | quiz, measure, photo |
| 3 | A week of shopping and prepared food stored by zone, labelled and dated (photo); storage and shelf-life items at 80 % | photo, quiz, checklist |
| 4 | For a batch-cooking or prep-ahead plan, every item has a storage method, container, label and use-by date chosen for quality as well as safety; on the day of use, the quality notes show nothing lost to storage | written, photo, log |

**Not yet:** unlabelled containers; raw meat above ready-to-eat food; fridge temperature never checked; leftovers kept
"until they smell".

### IN-VEG — Vegetables and fruit

**Elements:** the vegetable groups (roots and tubers, alliums, brassicas, leaves, stems and shoots, fruiting
vegetables, legumes as vegetables, squash, mushrooms), fruit groups, their structure (cell walls, pectin, starch, water),
pigments and their reactions, and the best uses and methods for each.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the groups and their main members; which parts are eaten; basic preparation.
- L3: structure and what happens with heat, acid and alkali (pectin, chlorophyll, anthocyanins); matching group to
  method.
- L4: varieties and their differences (potato types, onion types, cabbage types), seasonality, and using the whole
  vegetable.
- L5: predicts the behaviour of an unfamiliar vegetable from its group and structure; teaches it.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: prepares and cooks every group by a suitable method.
- [J] L4: chooses the variety and method for a dish, and uses trimmings well.
- [J] L5: builds a vegetable-led course around an unfamiliar vegetable, with a reasoned treatment.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with recipes.
- S2 (3): with frameworks.
- S3–S4 (4): chooses variety and method alone.
- S5 (5): handles the unfamiliar.

**Assessment:** quiz (groups, structure, pigments, images); comparison exercises; vegetable-led dishes; re-tested at
`MS-S1`. The capstone's vegetable-led course re-tests the cooking under `TS-VEG`.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Groups, parts and basic preparation at 80 %; prepares four vegetables from different groups correctly for a recipe | quiz, photo |
| 3 | Explains in writing what heat, acid and alkali do to colour and texture, and shows it in a comparison (for example green beans with and without acid) | written, photo, quiz |
| 4 | For three dishes, chooses the variety and method with a reason; the trimmings of one dish become part of another; the results meet the texture targets | written, photo, form |
| 5 | Five vegetable-led dishes over at least 8 weeks, under three different conditions (one unfamiliar vegetable, one without a recipe, one for guests), each reasoned from structure; a short written guide to one group's behaviour | photo, written, tasters |

**Not yet:** every vegetable boiled the same way; grey green vegetables; trimmings thrown away by habit; an unfamiliar
vegetable avoided rather than reasoned about.

### IN-STA — Grains, legumes, pasta, rice and potatoes

**Elements:** rice types; other grains (wheat, barley, oats, maize, buckwheat, quinoa); dried and fresh pasta; noodles;
pulses; potato types.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the main types and their typical uses; basic ratios (rice to water, pasta to water and salt).
- L3: starch types (amylose, amylopectin) and what they mean for texture; soaking and cooking pulses; waxy versus
  floury potatoes.
- L4: choosing the variety for the dish; behaviour in storage and reheating (retrogradation); regional varieties.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: cooks each type to the intended texture without a recipe.
- [J] L4: chooses rice, pasta shape or potato type for a dish with a reason.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with a recipe.
- S2 (3): without one.
- S3–S5 (4): chooses for the dish.

**Assessment:** quiz; texture photos and cross-sections; comparisons (risotto rice versus long-grain); re-tested in the
`MS-S2` garnish.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Types and ratios at 80 %; cooks rice, pasta and potatoes from a recipe to the stated texture | quiz, photo |
| 3 | Cooks a pilaf, a pulse and a potato dish without a recipe to the intended texture (separate grains, creamy but intact pulses, potatoes cooked through without falling apart); explains the starch behaviour | photo, written, form |
| 4 | For three dishes, chooses the variety with a reason and gets the texture; one comparison shows the difference the variety makes | written, photo, form |

**Not yet:** gluey rice, chalky pulses, waterlogged potatoes; one rice or one potato for every purpose.

### IN-DAI — Eggs and dairy

**Elements:** eggs (structure, grades, freshness, functions); milk and cream (fat levels, behaviour with heat and acid);
butter (clarified, brown, cultured); cheeses (families, melting behaviour); cultured dairy.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: egg structure, freshness and safe handling; the fat levels of cream and why they matter.
- L3: egg proteins and their setting temperatures; why dairy curdles and how to prevent it; butter's components.
- L4: functions in cooking and baking (emulsifying, leavening, binding, enriching, tenderising); cheese families and
  how they melt.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: uses eggs and dairy in sauces, custards and baking without curdling or splitting.
- [J] L4: chooses the dairy product or egg function for the result wanted, and substitutes with a predicted effect.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with recipes.
- S2 (3): with frameworks.
- S3–S5 (4): chooses and substitutes.

**Assessment:** quiz; custards, sauces and bakes with cross-section photos; re-tested in the `MS-S2` baked item.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Freshness, structure and safe handling at 80 %; a recipe custard or sauce that does not curdle | quiz, photo |
| 3 | Explains setting temperatures and curdling in writing; a stirred custard taken to *nappe* without curdling, and a cream sauce reduced without splitting | written, photo, measure |
| 4 | In three dishes, chooses the dairy product or egg function with a reason; one substitution (for example cultured cream for cream in a warm sauce) with the effect predicted and confirmed | written, photo, form |

**Not yet:** scrambled custards; split cream sauces; cheese that turns stringy or oily without knowing why.

### IN-MEA — Meat and poultry

**Elements:** beef, pork, lamb, poultry, game. For each: anatomy and primal cuts; muscle structure and connective
tissue; grades and labels; matching cut to method.

**Knowledge:**
- L1: that cuts differ in tenderness and connective tissue.
- L3: primal cuts and their main subprimals for beef, pork, lamb and poultry; tender versus tough cuts and why; grades,
  dry ageing, welfare labels.
- L4: game and less common cuts; the economics of cuts; choosing the cut for the method and the budget.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: buys the right cut for a method and prepares it (trim, portion).
- [J] L4: substitutes a cut with a predicted effect on time and method.

**Independence:**
- S1 (1): awareness.
- S2–S3 (3): chooses with frameworks.
- S4–S5 (4): chooses and substitutes freely.

**Assessment:** quiz (anatomy diagrams, image identification of cuts); purchase photos with the chosen method;
re-tested at `MS-S2`.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Places 15 common cuts on the animal and names a method for each, at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | Buys and cooks three cuts from different animals by the right method; the results confirm the match | quiz, photo, measure |
| 4 | Substitutes two cuts in dishes (for example cheek for shoulder), with the change in time and method predicted in writing and confirmed by the result; a game or less common cut cooked well | written, photo, measure |

**Not yet:** stewing fillet; grilling shin; buying "stewing beef" without knowing which cut it is.

### IN-FSH — Fish and shellfish

**Elements:** round and flat fish, oily and lean; crustaceans; bivalves; cephalopods. Freshness, sustainability, and
matching species to method.

**Knowledge:**
- L1: the species groups and the freshness signs.
- L3: structure (short muscle fibres, little connective tissue) and why fish cooks fast; which species suit which
  method; sustainability labels and seasonal advice; handling live shellfish safely.
- L4: substitution between species by group; less common species; the risks of raw preparations.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: buys fresh fish and shellfish and prepares them for the method.
- [J] L4: substitutes a species with a reason, and turns down fish that isn't fresh.

**Independence:**
- S1 (1): awareness.
- S2–S3 (3): with frameworks.
- S4–S5 (4): chooses at the counter.

**Assessment:** quiz (images of species and freshness signs); purchase photos (eyes, gills, skin); dishes; re-tested at
`MS-S2`.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Species groups and freshness signs at 80 %, including from photos | quiz |
| 3 | Buys a whole round fish and a bivalve, judges their freshness with photos, and cooks each by a suitable method; live shellfish checked and handled safely | photo, quiz, checklist |
| 4 | Two substitutions between species of a group, reasoned and confirmed; a less common species cooked well; a sustainability choice explained | written, photo |

**Not yet:** fish bought without checking; mussels that don't close used anyway; one method for every fish.
