# TS — Technical skills

The physical techniques. Entries follow the format of the
[competency framework §1.2](../04-competency-framework.md#12-the-entry-format). The mapping block under each entry is
generated from the outline.

**Tolerances.** The cut sizes, core temperatures and times below are the program's working standards. They are measured
against a grid board, a ruler or a probe thermometer, and photographed. The same figures are used in the milestone
rubrics ([`04-assessment.md`](../04-assessment.md)):
- **Classical cuts:** julienne 2–3 mm thick and 5–6 cm long; *bâtonnet* 5–7 mm square and 5–6 cm long; brunoise
  2–3 mm; fine brunoise 1–2 mm; small dice 5–7 mm; medium dice 12 mm; large dice 20 mm; *paysanne* 10–12 mm across and
  2–3 mm thick (the last two sizes added in Phase 5 from `KNF-1`).
- **Accuracy:** "8 of 10" means that 8 of 10 randomly picked pieces fall within the range.

### TS-MEP — Mise en place

**Elements:** reading the job and listing tasks; setting up a station (boards, bowls, waste, tools); sequencing; prepping
to the right size and quantity; clean as you go; organising for service.

**Knowledge:** why order matters (longest first, clean before dirty, raw before cooked); what can be prepped ahead and
how it holds; the station layout for a domestic counter.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: sets up, preps and sequences a two-component meal from a recipe without forgetting anything.
- [S] L4: preps a multi-component dish from a framework, in a logical order, with the station clean throughout.
- [J] L5: organises a multi-course production over days (make-ahead, holding, labelling) and adapts the plan when
  something slips.

**Independence:**
- S1 (3): from a recipe.
- S2–S3 (4): from frameworks.
- S4–S5 (5): plans productions alone.

**Assessment:** timed set-ups with a log and photos of the station; checklists; re-tested at every milestone.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | With a checklist, prepares every item of a recipe before cooking starts; the station photo shows separate areas for product, waste and tools | photo, checklist |
| 3 | Without a checklist, a two-component meal fully prepped before the heat goes on, in a logical order, within the planned time; the station is clean at the end | log, photo, checklist |
| 4 | Three multi-component dishes from frameworks, prepped from a written task list in a reasoned order; no forgotten item; clean as you go shown in timed photos | written, log, photo |
| 5 | Five productions over at least 8 weeks (including a guest dinner and a timed service), each with a make-ahead plan, labels and a log; slips absorbed without a late course | log, written, photo |

**Not yet:** cooking while still chopping; a cluttered board; looking for tools during service; prep that doesn't match
the quantities needed.

### TS-KNF — Knife skills

**Elements:** grip and guiding hand; the classical cuts (julienne, brunoise, dice, *paysanne*, *chiffonade*, *tourné*);
chopping and mincing; peeling, segmenting, deboning cuts; honing and sharpening; knife care and safety.

**Knowledge:** the cut sizes and their uses; why uniformity matters (even cooking); edge geometry and the difference
between honing and sharpening; safe storage and handling.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: makes the classical cuts accurately at a steady pace with a safe grip; hones the knife.
- [S] L4: the full set of cuts at working speed, including on awkward produce (celeriac, squash); sharpens on a stone.
- [S] L5: fine and decorative cuts (fine brunoise, *tourné*) accurately and fast; can teach the grip and the cuts.

**Independence:**
- S1 (3): standard cuts.
- S2–S3 (4): working speed.
- S4–S5 (5): precision and speed.

**Assessment:** cut tests on a grid board (measure and photo); short videos of grip and motion for AI review;
sharpening (a paper test, photographed); re-tested at `MS-S1`, `MS-S2`, `MS-S4` and the capstone.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Claw grip and a stable board on video; small dice and julienne within range for 6 of 10 pieces, without a time limit | video, measure, photo |
| 3 | Brunoise, small dice and julienne within range for 8 of 10 pieces; an onion small-diced in under 3 minutes; safe grip throughout on video | measure, photo, video |
| 4 | The `MS-S1` list plus *paysanne* and *chiffonade* within range in 30 minutes; a knife sharpened on a stone that slices paper cleanly | measure, video, photo |
| 5 | Fine brunoise 1–2 mm (8 of 10), a *tourné* with seven even faces, and an onion small-diced in under 90 seconds; consistent over 5 sessions in 8 weeks; a short teaching note on the grip | measure, video, written |

**Not yet:** fingertips forward; ragged or mixed sizes; a dull knife "because it's safer"; sawing instead of slicing.

### TS-DRY — Dry-heat methods

**Elements:** sauté; pan-fry; sear; roast; grill and broil (oven grill); deep-fry. Six elements.

**Knowledge:** pan and oil choice per method; preheating; pan load; smoke points; fat temperature for frying and how it
recovers; resting.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: each method on familiar ingredients, with even browning and the right doneness.
- [J] L4: chooses and adapts the method for a new ingredient, quantity or pan.
- [M] L5: consistent results on varied ingredients and equipment, without a recipe, including for guests.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with recipes.
- S2 (3): with frameworks.
- S3–S4 (4): adapts.
- S5 (5): masters.

**Assessment:** photos of surface and cross-section; oil and core temperature readings; comparison exercises; re-tested
at `MS-S1`, through the protein rows of later milestones, and at level 5 in the capstone main course (`CAP-5.R6`).

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Sautés vegetables and pan-fries a protein from a recipe with even colour and no burning | photo |
| 3 | Four of the six methods without a recipe: even browning, the right interior, fat at the right temperature (deep-frying held at 170–180 °C, logged) | photo, measure |
| 4 | Five of the six methods, each also on a new ingredient or with a different pan or quantity, with the adaptation reasoned in writing; results to standard | photo, measure, written |
| 5 | All six methods to standard over at least 8 weeks under three conditions (an unfamiliar ingredient, no recipe, guests: the capstone main course counts); a written note on the variables and faults of dry heat | photo, measure, written |

**Not yet:** a crowded pan that steams; oil too cold (greasy) or smoking; no rest after cooking; pale roasts.

### TS-MOI — Moist-heat methods

**Elements:** boil; simmer; poach (shallow and deep); steam; blanch and refresh. Five elements.

**Knowledge:** the temperature of each (boil 100 °C, simmer 85–95 °C, poach 70–85 °C); salting the water; the ratio of
water to food; refreshing for colour and texture.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: each method to the intended texture, with the liquid at the right temperature.
- [J] L4: chooses and controls the method for delicate or new foods (fish, eggs, fruit).

**Independence:**
- S1 (3): basic methods.
- S2–S5 (4): adapts.

**Assessment:** photos; liquid temperature readings; texture forms; re-tested at `MS-S1`.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Boils, simmers and blanches vegetables from a recipe to the stated texture | photo, form |

| 4 | All five methods, including a delicate poach (fish or eggs at 70–85 °C) and a new food, with the method choice reasoned; results to standard on 3 occasions | measure, photo, written |

**Not yet:** poaching at a rolling boil; unsalted cooking water; grey refreshed vegetables; overcooked by habit.

### TS-CMB — Combination methods

**Elements:** braise; stew; pot-roast; sous vide (precise water bath). Four elements.

- [S] L3: braises and stews from frameworks, with the sauce reduced to the right body.

**Independence:**
- S2 (2): recipe.
- S3–S5 (4): plans and adapts.

**Assessment:** photos (texture, sauce body); time and temperature logs; re-tested in the `MS-S3` evening.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |

### TS-FIRE — Live fire and barbecue

**Elements:** building and controlling a charcoal or wood fire; direct grilling; indirect cooking; ember cooking;
low-and-slow barbecue. Five elements.

**Knowledge:** radiant and convective heat from coals; zones, vents and lid; fuel types; flare-ups and smoke compounds;
fire and carbon-monoxide safety; long-cook food safety.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L2: lights and controls a two-zone fire, and grills simple items.
- [S] L3: grills, roasts indirectly and cooks in embers to a target doneness; holds a lid temperature.
- [M] L4: runs a full live-fire meal and a low-and-slow cook for guests, consistently.

**Independence:**
- S3–S4 (3): from frameworks.
- S5 (4): plans and runs fire meals.

*No outdoor space:* the learner reaches level 2 at most, using a grill pan and the oven grill, and the record says so
(Phase 3, review answer 2).

**Assessment:** fire and meat temperature logs; cross-section photos; guest cards; re-tested in the `MS-S3` evening and
the S5 independent cooking.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Lights a chimney, builds a two-zone fire, and grills vegetables and a simple protein to doneness; without outdoor space, the same browning and doneness with a grill pan and the oven grill | photo, measure, log |
| 3 | Holds the lid at 120 °C and 180 °C (±15 °C) for 20 minutes each, logged; grills a protein to its target core (±3 °C) and roasts a bird indirectly to a safe core | log, measure, photo |
| 4 | A low-and-slow cook (pork shoulder or ribs) with a fire and meat log through the stall to a target texture, and a live-fire meal for at least four guests on time, each on 3 occasions in at least 2 conditions | log, photo, tasters |

**Not yet:** lighter fluid taste; flare-ups that char the food; no idea of the lid temperature; a long cook with no log.
