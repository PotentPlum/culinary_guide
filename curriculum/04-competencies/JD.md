# JD — Independent judgment

Reading cues, choosing methods, troubleshooting and self-assessment. Entries follow the format of the
[competency framework §1.2](../04-competency-framework.md#12-the-entry-format). The mapping block under each entry is
generated from the outline.

Judgment shows in **decisions**. Its evidence is the written reasoning (made before or during cooking, not
reconstructed afterwards) set against the result.

### JD-CUES — Reading cues

**Elements:** sight (colour, bubbles, shine, set); sound (sizzle, crackle, boil); smell (toast, caramel, burnt); touch
(springback, texture, dough feel); taste; confirmed by instruments (probe, thermometer, scale, timer).

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the cues of the basic techniques.
- L3–4: the cues of the classical techniques, and which cue is reliable for which result.
- L5: subtle and combined cues; teaching them.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [J] L3: judges readiness by cue, then confirms with an instrument.
- [J] L4: relies on cues in unfamiliar situations, with instruments as a check.
- [M] L5: reads cues reliably under pressure, and the instrument rarely disagrees.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with a cue list.
- S2 (3): alone.
- S3–S4 (4): unfamiliar situations.
- S5 (5): masters.

**Assessment:** "cue first, then reading" logs: the learner records the cue-based judgment before the reading.
Re-tested at `MS-S2`.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Names the cue for five basic techniques at 80 %; one cue-then-reading entry | quiz, log |
| 3 | Ten cue-then-reading entries over three sessions, with at least 8 within the tolerance of the technique (for example ±3 °C for a protein) | log, measure |
| 4 | Ten entries in unfamiliar situations (a new cut, a new oven, a new dough), with at least 8 within tolerance | log, measure, photo |
| 5 | Over at least 8 weeks, 20 entries under three conditions (guests, time pressure, no recipe) with at least 18 within tolerance; a short guide to the cues of one technique | log, measure, written |

**Not yet:** cooking by the clock alone; the probe used instead of looking; cues that are named but never checked.

### JD-CHSE — Choosing an approach from first principles

**Elements:** analysing an unfamiliar ingredient or dish (structure, composition, tradition); choosing method, ratio and
flavour logic; planning a fallback; working from a brief.

**Knowledge:**
- L2–3: the analysis questions (what is it, how is it built, what does it need).
- L4–5: applying the whole curriculum to the unknown; knowing what to look up and from which kind of source.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [J] L3: chooses an approach for an unfamiliar ingredient from principles and gets an acceptable result.
- [J] L4: plans an unfamiliar dish from a description, without a recipe.
- [M] L5: does so consistently, and explains the reasoning to others.

**Independence:**
- S1–S2 (1–2): guided.
- S3 (3): alone for ingredients.
- S4 (4): alone for dishes.
- S5 (5): masters.

**Assessment:** written analyses before cooking; results; re-tested in the capstone brief analysis and defence.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | With the analysis questions, chooses between two given approaches for an unfamiliar ingredient and explains the choice | written |
| 3 | Three unfamiliar ingredients analysed in writing and cooked by a chosen approach; each result acceptable | written, photo |
| 4 | Two dishes recreated from a description alone (no recipe), with a written plan and a fallback; the tasters recognise the dish | written, photo, tasters |
| 5 | Five briefs over at least 8 weeks, including the capstone, each with a written analysis that names alternatives and risks; every result to standard | written, photo, tasters |

**Not yet:** searching for a recipe first; avoiding unfamiliar ingredients; a plan without a reason.

### JD-TRBL — Troubleshooting and recovery

**Elements:** split or broken sauces; over-reduction; burnt bases; under- or over-cooking; dough problems; timing slips;
equipment failure. Diagnosing the cause, recovering the dish, and preventing it next time.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the common faults and their recoveries.
- L3–4: causes, from the science; which recoveries are possible and which aren't.
- L5: preventing faults by planning; teaching recovery.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [J] L3: recovers common faults during cooking.
- [J] L4: diagnoses an unfamiliar fault and chooses a recovery, or decides to start again.
- [M] L5: recovers under pressure without the guest noticing.

**Independence:**
- S1 (1): awareness.
- S2 (3): common faults.
- S3–S4 (4): unfamiliar faults.
- S5 (5): masters.

**Assessment:** fault logs (what went wrong, the cause, the recovery, the result), with photos before and after;
re-tested in the `MS-S3` flavour-correction test.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Common faults and recoveries at 80 %; one fault logged with its recovery | quiz, log |
| 3 | Three faults from their own cooking recovered, with photos before and after and the cause named | log, photo, written |
| 4 | Three unfamiliar faults diagnosed from the science and recovered (or a reasoned restart); the fault-diagnosis test passed | log, written, calibration |
| 5 | Over at least 8 weeks, five recoveries under three conditions (guests, time pressure, no recipe), with the guests unaware; a written guide to the faults of one technique | log, written, tasters |

**Not yet:** throwing away instead of recovering; recovering without knowing why it went wrong; the same fault three times.

### JD-REFL — Self-assessment and reflection

**Elements:** scoring one's own work against criteria; using evidence (photos, logs, tasters); finding causes; turning
results into next steps; the journal; stage reflections.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the reflection prompts and the rubric bands.
- L3–4: the difference between description and diagnosis; the biases of self-assessment.
- L5: calibrated self-assessment, and coaching others.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [J] L3: self-scores honestly, in line with the other evidence.
- [J] L4: diagnoses patterns across entries and plans practice.
- [M] L5: self-assessment that matches the outside evidence, consistently over time.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with prompts.
- S2 (3): alone.
- S3–S4 (4): finds patterns.
- S5 (5): calibrated.

**Assessment:** journal entries and stage reflections against the rubric (`written`); agreement between the learner's
bands and the AI and taster bands (assessment §3.4); re-tested at every milestone and the capstone.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | A reflection on the prompts that names one improvement and one weakness with evidence | written |
| 3 | Five journal entries where the learner's bands agree with the AI or tasters within one band in at least 4 | written, photo, tasters |
| 4 | A stage reflection that finds a pattern across at least ten entries and sets a practice plan; the next stage's entries show the plan worked or explain why not | written, log |
| 5 | Over at least 8 weeks, the learner's bands agree with the outside evidence within one band in 90 % of entries; the capstone defence is candid and specific | written, log, tasters |

**Not yet:** "it went well" without evidence; always scoring high, or always low; the same weakness noted every time with
no plan.
