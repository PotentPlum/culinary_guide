# SC — Food science

Heat, browning, proteins, starches, fats and emulsions, and microbiology: science that explains what happens in the pan.
Entries follow the format of the [competency framework §1.2](../04-competency-framework.md#12-the-entry-format). The
mapping block under each entry is generated from the outline.

Science competencies are shown mainly by quizzes and written explanations. A level counts when the learner can also
**use** the science: predicting or explaining a real result from their own cooking.

### SC-HEAT — Heat transfer and equipment

**Elements:** conduction, convection and radiation; specific heat and thermal mass; pan materials (steel, cast iron,
aluminium, copper, clad); hob types (gas, ceramic, induction); ovens (conventional, fan, grill); thermometers and
calibration.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the three modes of transfer and where each acts in common methods.
- L3: thermal mass and conductivity of pan materials; why a crowded pan steams; fan versus conventional ovens.
- L4: predicts how a change of equipment changes the result, and corrects for it (time, temperature, preheating, pan
  size).

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: calibrates a thermometer and the oven; preheats correctly.
- [J] L4: adapts a method to different equipment and gets the same result.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): explains with help.
- S2 (3): applies.
- S3–S5 (4): predicts and adapts.

**Assessment:** quiz; an oven-calibration log; comparisons (same steak in steel and cast iron); re-tested in the `MS-S1`
quiz.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | The modes of heat transfer and the main equipment items at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | Calibrates a probe thermometer in ice water (0 °C ± 1 °C) and logs the oven's real temperature at three settings; explains a crowded-pan result from their own cooking | measure, log, written |
| 4 | A dish cooked on two pieces of equipment (pan material, hob or oven type) with the changes predicted in writing; both results meet the same standard | written, photo, measure |

**Not yet:** trusting the oven dial; blaming the recipe when the pan was cold or crowded; one setting for every pan.

### SC-BRWN — Browning

**Elements:** the Maillard reaction; caramelisation; the variables (temperature, surface moisture, pH, sugars and
amino acids, time); fond and deglazing; burnt versus browned; acrylamide as a reason not to over-brown starches.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: that browning needs a dry, hot surface, and the difference between Maillard and caramelisation.
- L3: the variables and how to control each (drying, salting ahead, pan load, fat, alkalinity).
- L4: predicts and steers browning in a new situation (a wet vegetable, a sugary glaze, a crowded roasting tray).

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: browns meat, vegetables and onions evenly and deeply without burning the fond.
- [J] L4: changes a variable on purpose to get more or less browning.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): explains.
- S2 (3): controls in familiar dishes.
- S3–S5 (4): steers in new ones.

**Assessment:** quiz; comparison exercises (dry versus wet surface, baking soda in onions) with photos; re-tested in the
`MS-S2` quiz.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Maillard, caramelisation and the dry-hot-surface rule at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | A comparison of two variables photographed side by side and explained; a seared protein and caramelised onions with even, deep colour and no burnt fond | photo, written, quiz |
| 4 | Three new situations where browning was steered on purpose (more for one, less for another), with the variable named in advance and the photos confirming it | written, photo |

**Not yet:** grey meat from a wet surface; burnt garlic in a fond; "it didn't brown" without a cause.

### SC-PROT — Proteins

**Elements:** denaturation and coagulation; the setting temperatures of meat, fish and egg proteins; collagen to
gelatine; water-holding, brining and salting; resting and carryover; enzymes (tenderising, mushiness).

**Knowledge:**
- L2: that proteins set with heat and squeeze out water; why doneness is a temperature.
- L3: the temperature ranges of the main proteins; collagen conversion over time and temperature; how salt and brine
  change water-holding.
- L4: predicts the effect of time, temperature and salt on a new protein; explains carryover and resting with numbers.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: uses the science to hit a doneness and keep juiciness (salting ahead, resting).
- [J] L4: plans the time and temperature of a long cook from the collagen content.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): explains with help.
- S2 (3): applies.
- S3–S5 (4): plans from it.

**Assessment:** quiz; a salted-versus-unsalted comparison with weights (cooking loss); carryover logs; re-tested in the
`MS-S2` quiz.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Coagulation, doneness temperatures and collagen at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | A comparison of a dry-brined and an unsalted piece, weighed before and after (cooking loss in %), with the difference explained | measure, written, quiz |
| 4 | A carryover log for a roast (core temperature every 5 minutes after the oven) with the rise predicted beforehand to within 2 °C; a long-cook plan reasoned from the cut's collagen and confirmed by the result | log, measure, written |

**Not yet:** doneness by time alone; dry meat blamed on the cut; no rest after cooking.

### SC-STRC — Starches and gluten

**Elements:** gelatinisation and its temperature range; retrogradation (staling, setting); starch-thickened sauces and
their breakdown (acid, over-stirring, enzymes); gluten formation and relaxation; the effect of fat, sugar and water.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: starch thickens when heated in water; gluten forms when wheat flour meets water and work.
- L3: thickening power of different starches; why sauces thin or weep; dough development, rest and relaxation.
- L4: predicts texture changes from a formula change (more water, more fat, a different flour or starch).

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: thickens with the right starch and dose, and develops or limits gluten as needed.
- [J] L4: diagnoses a texture fault in a sauce or dough from the science, and fixes it.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): explains.
- S2 (3): applies.
- S3–S5 (4): diagnoses.

**Assessment:** quiz; comparisons (roux versus cornflour versus arrowroot; short versus long mixing); re-tested in the
`MS-S2` quiz.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Gelatinisation, retrogradation and gluten at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | A comparison of three starches thickening the same liquid (consistency photographed hot and cold) and a dough mixed short and long, each explained | photo, written, quiz |
| 4 | Two texture faults from their own cooking (a weeping sauce, a tough pastry) diagnosed in writing from the science, fixed, and the fix confirmed in a photo | written, photo |

**Not yet:** lumpy or gluey sauces; tough pastry from over-working; staling mistaken for drying out.

### SC-EMUL — Fats, emulsions, foams and gels

**Elements:** fat types and smoke points; emulsions (oil-in-water, water-in-oil), emulsifiers and how emulsions break;
foams (egg white, cream); gels (gelatine, pectin, starch, agar).

**Knowledge:**
- L1: that fat and water mix only with help.
- L3: how emulsifiers work; why emulsions break (temperature, ratio, speed) and how to repair them; how foams form and
  collapse.
- L4: gels and their setting ranges; predicts the stability of a new emulsion or foam from its components.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: makes and repairs a cold and a warm emulsion; whips stable foams.
- [J] L4: chooses an emulsifier or gelling agent for a purpose and sets the ratio.

**Independence:**
- S1 (1): awareness.
- S2–S3 (3): applies.
- S4–S5 (4): designs.

**Assessment:** quiz; deliberate break-and-repair exercises with photos; re-tested in the `MS-S2` quiz.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Emulsion types and the reasons they break at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | Breaks and repairs a mayonnaise and a warm butter emulsion on purpose, photographed at each step, with the cause and repair explained | photo, written, quiz |
| 4 | Sets a gel and a stable foam for a stated texture, with the ratio chosen in writing; the photos (and a timed stability check) confirm it | written, photo, measure |

**Not yet:** split sauces thrown away instead of repaired; meringue that weeps; gelatine boiled.

### SC-MICR — Microbiology and preservation hurdles

**Elements:** spoilage and pathogenic organisms; growth conditions (temperature, time, water activity, pH, oxygen);
toxins that survive cooking; fermentation as controlled growth; preservation hurdles (salt, sugar, acid, drying, cold,
heat).

**Knowledge:**
- L1: that bacteria grow in the danger zone and that some toxins survive cooking.
- L2: growth conditions and the main pathogens of the home kitchen.
- L3: hurdles and why a preserve is safe; why some foods need tested methods (low-acid canning, garlic in oil, curing);
  what fermentation does.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [J] L3: explains why a given preserve or ferment is safe, or isn't, and when to throw something away.

**Independence:**
- S1 (1): awareness.
- S2–S5 (3): explains and applies.

**Assessment:** quiz (scenarios); written safety reasoning for each preserve; re-tested in the `MS-S2` quiz.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Growth conditions and the main pathogens at 80 % | quiz |
| 3 | Scenario items at 80 %; for two of their own preserves or ferments, a written explanation of the hurdles that make them safe, citing the tested method | quiz, written |

**Not yet:** "it smells fine, so it's safe"; preserves made from untested recipes; not knowing why garlic in oil needs the
fridge.
