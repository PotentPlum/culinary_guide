# SA — Food safety

Hygiene, temperature control, physical safety, allergens and high-risk processes. Entries follow the format of the
[competency framework §1.2](../04-competency-framework.md#12-the-entry-format). The mapping block under each entry is
generated from the outline.

**Safety competencies are never compensated.** They have no allowance at the stage gate (framework §3.1), and every
milestone has a food-safety gate ([assessment §3](../04-assessment.md#3-scoring-and-pass-rules)). The figures used are
those of [assessment §9](../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment), from the Voedingscentrum [S21][S22]
and the FDA *Food Code* [S25].

### SA-HYG — Hygiene and cross-contamination

**Elements:** hand washing; clean work surfaces and tools; separating raw and ready-to-eat food (boards, storage,
utensils); dish cloths and sponges; personal hygiene; cleaning after raw protein.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the main routes of contamination and the rules that block them [S21].
- L3: why each rule works; high-risk moments (raw chicken, eggs, washing produce).
- L4–5: hygiene planning for productions: control points and the order of work.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: follows the hygiene routine without reminders.
- [J] L4: organises work so that contamination can't happen (raw last, separate stations).
- [M] L5: hygiene built into multi-day productions and guest dinners, with control points in the plan.

**Independence:**
- S1 (3): routine.
- S2 (4): organises.
- S3–S5 (5): built into planning.

**Assessment:** checklists; short videos for AI review; plans with control points; re-tested at `MS-S1`, `MS-S2` and in
the capstone plan.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Hygiene rules at 100 % in the quiz; the routine followed with a checklist for one meal | quiz, checklist |
| 3 | Two meals with raw protein where a video shows hand washing, separate boards and cleaning at the right moments, without reminders | video, checklist |
| 4 | A three-minute video of handling raw chicken with no cross-contamination at any point; a work order that puts raw protein last | video, checklist, written |
| 5 | Five productions over at least 8 weeks (including guests and the capstone) with hygiene control points in the plan and ticked in the log; no lapse in any video or photo | written, log, video |

**Not yet:** the same board for raw chicken and salad; washing raw chicken; one cloth for everything; hands not washed
after eggs or raw meat.

### SA-TEMP — Temperature control

**Elements:** cooking to safe core temperatures; cooling; chilling and freezing; reheating; hot and cold holding; the
thermometer.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the fridge at 4 °C, the freezer at −18 °C, leftovers out of the fridge no longer than 2 hours, steaming hot when
  reheated [S21][S22].
- L3: the core temperatures and the cooling, holding and reheating figures of [assessment §9](../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment).
- L4–5: designing cooling and holding for batches and guest dinners.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: probes and logs; cools and reheats correctly.
- [J] L4: plans cooling and holding for batches.
- [M] L5: every control point of a production logged and in range.

**Independence:**
- S1 (3): routine.
- S2–S3 (4): plans.
- S4–S5 (5): controls productions.

**Assessment:** probe readings and temperature logs; quiz; re-tested at every milestone and the capstone.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Temperature rules at 100 % in the quiz; the fridge thermometer reading photographed | quiz, measure |
| 3 | Probes poultry and minced meat to the safe core temperature and logs it; leftovers into the fridge within 2 hours, logged | measure, log |
| 4 | A stock or braise cooled from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours and to 5 °C within 6 hours, logged; reheated until steaming hot (74 °C or more) | log, measure |
| 5 | Five productions over at least 8 weeks with every cooling, holding and reheating control point logged and in range; a written cooling plan for a guest dinner | log, written, measure |

**Not yet:** a thermometer that is never used; a pot of stock left to cool overnight; reheating "until warm".

### SA-PHYS — Physical safety

**Elements:** knives (grip, carrying, washing, storage); burns and scalds (handles, steam, hot pans); hot oil (fill
level, water, fire); fire (pan fires, oven, grill, the fire blanket); lifting heavy pots; live fire and smoke (`FIR-3`).

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the rules and what to do in an emergency (a pan fire, a burn, a cut).
- L3–4: risks of specific techniques (deep-frying, flambéing, live fire, pressure cookers).

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: works safely with knives, heat and oil without reminders.
- [J] L4: sets up a station so that accidents can't happen.
- [M] L5: safe under pressure and with guests and children around.

**Independence:**
- S1 (3): routine.
- S2 (4): organises.
- S3–S5 (5): under pressure.

**Assessment:** videos; checklists; quiz; re-tested at `MS-S1` and `MS-S2`.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Safety rules and emergency actions at 100 % in the quiz | quiz |
| 3 | Videos of cutting and of moving hot pans show a safe grip, handles turned in, and a dry towel; a fire blanket within reach (photo) | video, checklist, photo |
| 4 | A deep-frying session with the oil level, temperature and a lid ready shown on video; a station layout reasoned for safety | video, measure, written |
| 5 | Five services over at least 8 weeks (guests, a timed service, live fire) with the safety checklist complete and nothing unsafe in any video | video, checklist, log |

**Not yet:** a knife left in the sink; a wet towel used as a pot holder; a pan handle sticking out; water near hot oil.

### SA-ALLG — Allergens

**Elements:** the 14 EU allergen groups [S23]; reading labels; hidden sources (stocks, sauces, spice mixes);
cross-contact in the kitchen; communicating with guests; adapting a menu safely.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: the 14 groups and where they hide.
- L3: cross-contact and how to prevent it; label rules.
- L4: planning a menu for a guest with an allergy, from shopping to plate.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: reads labels and keeps an allergen out of a dish.
- [J] L4: plans and cooks for a guest with an allergy, safely and without a lesser dish.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with a checklist.
- S2 (3): alone.
- S3–S5 (4): plans for guests.

**Assessment:** quiz (all 14 groups; label reading); allergen plans; re-tested at `MS-S1` and `MS-S3`.
The capstone brief always includes a guest with a requirement, and its food-safety gate checks the allergen plan.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Names the 14 groups and their common hidden sources, at 100 % | quiz |
| 3 | Label items at 100 %; one dish cooked free of a named allergen, with the labels of every ingredient photographed | quiz, photo, checklist |
| 4 | A menu for a guest with an allergen: an allergen plan from shopping to plate, labels photographed, separate tools; the guest's dish as good as the others (the tasters say so) | written, photo, checklist |

**Not yet:** "a little bit won't hurt"; stock cubes not checked; one spoon for every pot.

### SA-HRPR — High-risk processes

**Elements:** preserving (acid, sugar, fermentation); curing; sous vide and low-temperature cooking; long cooks and
barbecue; large-batch cooling; raw and lightly cooked preparations (eggs, fish); garlic or herbs in oil.

**Knowledge:**
- L1: that some processes need tested methods.
- L3: why each process is risky (botulism, *Listeria*, *Salmonella*), and the tested methods that control the risk
  ([S27], [assessment §9](../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)).
- L4: when to refuse a recipe; designing a production with these processes safely.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: follows a tested method exactly, with logs.
- [J] L4: judges a recipe's safety before using it, and plans high-risk steps with control points.

**Independence:**
- S2 (1): awareness.
- S3–S5 (4): judges and plans.

**Assessment:** quiz (scenarios; any failed item means a resit); logs; written safety notes per process; re-tested at
`MS-S3` and the capstone.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Names the high-risk processes and why they are risky, at 100 % | quiz |
| 3 | Two high-risk processes (for example a ferment and a sous vide cook) by tested methods, with complete logs and the source named | log, measure, written |
| 4 | High-risk scenarios at 100 %; one unsafe recipe identified and refused or corrected with the reason; a production plan with control points for every high-risk step, followed in the log | quiz, written, log |

**Not yet:** an untested canning recipe; sous vide below the tables; garlic in oil kept on the counter; a long cook with
no temperature log.
