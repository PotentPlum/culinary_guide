# FL — Flavour

Tasting, seasoning, balance, texture, composition and fixing faults. Entries follow the format of the
[competency framework §1.2](../04-competency-framework.md#12-the-entry-format). The mapping block under each entry is
generated from the outline.

Flavour cannot be checked from a photo. These competencies rest on **calibration tests**
([assessment §5](../04-assessment.md#5-calibration-tests)), **blind household tasters**, and consistency across many
entries (framework §2.4). A calibrated sensory form (`form`) counts only while a salt and an acid test passed in the
last 6 months stand behind it.

### FL-SENS — Sensory evaluation

**Elements:** the five tastes and chilli heat; aroma (orthonasal and retronasal); texture and mouthfeel; temperature;
describing with precise words; comparison methods (side by side, triangle, ranking).

**Knowledge:**
- L2: how taste, smell and texture combine into flavour; a tasting vocabulary; the influence of temperature.
- L3: thresholds and adaptation (why tasting the same pot repeatedly misleads); tasting order.
- L4: sensory test methods [S20] and how to set them up blind at home.
- L5: teaching another person to taste; limits of one's own palate.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L2–3: tastes deliberately and fills in the sensory form in precise words.
- [J] L4: detects small differences reliably and ranks samples.
- [M] L5: consistent, calibrated judgments over time; sets up tests for others.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with the form and vocabulary list.
- S2 (3): fills in the form without help.
- S3–S4 (4): reliable in blind tests.
- S5 (5): calibrated and able to teach.

**Assessment:** calibration tests (salt, acid, fault diagnosis); blind identification of aromas; sensory forms compared
with tasters; re-tested at `MS-S1` and `MS-S3`, and at level 5 in the capstone self-evaluation (`CAP-5.R7`).

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Passes the salt and acid ranking tests ([assessment §5](../04-assessment.md#5-calibration-tests)); a sensory form for one dish uses the vocabulary for taste, aroma and texture | calibration, form |
| 3 | Passes the salt and acid triangle tests; forms for three dishes that the tasters' cards broadly confirm | calibration, form, tasters |
| 4 | Passes the fault-diagnosis test; identifies 12 of 16 aromas blind; forms that match the tasters in at least 4 of 5 dishes | calibration, form, tasters |
| 5 | Calibration held over at least 8 weeks (all tests passed at least twice), and the capstone form agreeing with the guests' cards; sets up a blind triangle test for the household and interprets the result correctly; a short written guide to tasting | calibration, written, tasters |

**Not yet:** "it's nice" as a description; tasting only at the end; judging a cold sample for a hot dish; a palate that
drifts and is never re-calibrated.

### FL-SEAS — Seasoning and balance

**Elements:** salt (amount and timing); acid; sweetness; bitterness; umami; chilli heat; fat as a carrier; seasoning
at every stage (cooking water, marinade, during cooking, finishing).

**Knowledge:**
- L2: what each taste does to the others (salt suppresses bitterness, acid brightens, sweet softens acid); salt
  percentages as a guide (about 1 % for many savoury dishes).
- L3: seasoning stages; how reduction, temperature and fat change perceived seasoning.
- L4: balancing whole menus; the seasoning logic of different traditions.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L2–3: seasons during cooking and adjusts at the end.
- [J] L4: balances a dish with several tastes deliberately.
- [M] L5: every dish, every course, balanced for guests without a recipe, consistently.

**Independence:**
- S1 (2): with a guide.
- S2 (3): by routine.
- S3 (4): balances deliberately.
- S4–S5 (5): consistent at service.

**Assessment:** calibrated forms; blind tasters; salt weighed against the dish's weight; re-tested at `MS-S1`, `MS-S2`,
`MS-S4` and the capstone (at `MS-S3`, flavour is scored under `FL-ARCH` and `FL-CORR`).

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Passes the salt and acid tests; seasons a dish at the stages the recipe names; the tasters call it neither under- nor over-seasoned | calibration, tasters |
| 3 | Three dishes from frameworks seasoned at every stage (the salt weighed and noted); the calibrated form and the tasters agree on the balance | measure, form, tasters |
| 4 | Balances three dishes with at least three tastes in play, noting each adjustment and why; the tasters judge all three balanced | written, form, tasters |
| 5 | Five services over at least 8 weeks (guests, no recipe, a timed service): every course judged balanced by the blind tasters, matching the calibrated form; a note on seasoning faults | tasters, form, written, calibration |

**Not yet:** seasoning only at the end; salt as the only lever; dishes the learner likes but the tasters find salty;
acid forgotten.

### FL-TEXT — Texture and contrast

**Elements:** crisp, crunchy, tender, creamy, chewy, smooth; how cooking creates and destroys texture; contrast within a
dish and across a menu; temperature contrast.

**Knowledge:**
- L1–2: the texture vocabulary; the textures a technique aims for.
- L3: how to create and keep texture (frying, dehydrating, timing, holding); what kills it (steam, sauce, time).
- L4: designing contrast deliberately in a dish and a menu.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: hits the textures a dish needs and keeps them until service.
- [J] L4: adds a contrasting texture on purpose and protects it on the plate.

**Independence:**
- S1–S2 (1–2): guided.
- S3 (3): independent.
- S4–S5 (4): designs.

**Assessment:** cross-section and plate photos; forms; tasters; re-tested at `MS-S4` and the capstone.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Names the intended textures of a dish and hits them from a recipe (cross-section photo) | photo, form |
| 3 | Three dishes from frameworks with their textures hit and kept until the plate (crisp elements still crisp), photographed at service | photo, form, tasters |
| 4 | Three dishes and one menu designed with a contrast named in advance; the guests' cards and photos confirm it in every course | written, photo, tasters |

**Not yet:** soggy crisp elements under sauce; everything soft; texture never mentioned in a menu plan.

### FL-ARCH — Flavour composition

**Elements:** aromatic bases; layering (build, bloom, deglaze, finish); pairing principles (complement, contrast,
bridge ingredients); regional flavour profiles; balancing intensity.

**Knowledge:**
- L2: aromatic bases and why they come first.
- L3: layering through a dish; pairing principles; the flavour profiles of the traditions taught.
- L4: composing a new combination and predicting how it will taste.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [S] L3: builds layered flavour in a dish from a framework.
- [J] L4: composes a new combination and explains it.

**Independence:**
- S2 (2): with recipes.
- S3 (3): frameworks.
- S4–S5 (4): composes.

**Assessment:** written flavour plans; forms; tasters; re-tested in the `MS-S3` evening.

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Builds an aromatic base and layers it into a dish from a recipe; describes the layers on the form | photo, form |
| 3 | Three dishes from frameworks with a written flavour plan (base, layers, finish); the tasters find depth and balance | written, form, tasters |
| 4 | Two new combinations composed with a written prediction; cooked; the tasters' cards and the form show the prediction held, or explain why not | written, tasters, form |

**Not yet:** a flat, one-note dish; ingredients added all at once; combinations chosen because they are fashionable.

### FL-CORR — Diagnosing and correcting faults

**Elements:** flat (under-seasoned); too salty; too acidic; too sweet; bitter or burnt; greasy; one-dimensional; too
spicy. Corrections during cooking and at the end.

**Knowledge:**
- L1–2: the common faults and their usual fixes.
- L3: why each fix works, and which fixes cannot work (salt cannot be removed; dilute or bulk instead).
- L4–5: diagnosis from little evidence; preventing faults in the plan.

**Practical ability and judgment:**
- [J] L3: tastes a dish, names its fault and fixes it.
- [J] L4: diagnoses blind, including combined faults.
- [M] L5: catches faults early, fixes them under time pressure, and teaches it.

**Independence:**
- S1 (1): awareness.
- S2 (3): fixes common faults.
- S3–S4 (4): diagnoses blind.
- S5 (5): masters.

**Assessment:** the fault-diagnosis calibration test ([assessment §5.3](../04-assessment.md#53-fault-diagnosis-from-s3));
written diagnosis notes; tasters; re-tested in the `MS-S3` flavour-correction test and, at level 5, at the capstone
service (`CAP-5.R7`).

**Success criteria:**

| Level | A successful demonstration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Names the usual fix for six common faults at 80 %; fixes one under-seasoned dish | quiz, form |
| 3 | Three faults met in their own cooking named, fixed and written up with the reason; the tasters confirm the fix | written, form, tasters |
| 4 | Passes the fault-diagnosis test (four of five faults named, both corrections judged good) | calibration, written, tasters |
| 5 | Over at least 8 weeks, five faults caught and fixed during cooking under three conditions (guests, time pressure, no recipe), logged, the capstone service among them; a short written guide to fault correction | calibration, written, log |

**Not yet:** adding salt to every problem; serving a fault rather than fixing it; trying to fix over-salting with sugar.
