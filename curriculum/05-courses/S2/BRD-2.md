## Introduction

Bread is four ingredients (flour, water, salt and yeast) and a great deal of time. What turns them into a loaf with a
crackling crust and an open, chewy crumb is control of two living processes:
- **fermentation:** yeast producing gas and flavour;
- **gluten development:** the protein network that holds the gas.

Home bread fails mostly in reading those processes. The baker follows the clock instead of the dough, or bakes without
the steam and heat that make a crust.

`BRD-2` takes a lean dough through every stage:
- **yeast and fermentation;**
- **gluten,** developed by kneading or by stretch and fold;
- **mixing** to a target dough temperature;
- **bulk fermentation** read by rise, bubbles and feel;
- **shaping** with tension;
- **proofing** with the poke test, and retarding in the fridge;
- **baking** with steam in a home oven, scored and baked to a deep crust;
- **judging doneness and the crumb.**

It introduces an enriched dough, and ends with fitting bread into a working week and reviewing the bread log.

**The log** is the course's main tool. Every bake records:
- the formula in baker's percentages (`BAK-2`);
- the dough temperature;
- the times and the room temperature;
- the readings against the cues;
- photos of each stage and the crumb.

From it, the learner sees how temperature and time shape the bread, and plans bakes around a working day.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-BRD` reaches level 2: a lean loaf with a guiding recipe.
- Sourdough, enriched breads in depth and shaping without guidance come in `BRD-3`, which takes it to level 3.
- `JD-CUES` level 3 gets its dough entries.
- `SC-STRC` and `IN-BAK` are applied to dough.
- `KM-PLAN` gets a multi-stage schedule built around the fridge.

**Pacing.** About four weeks, mostly weekend baking with short weekday tasks once the overnight retard is learned. A
lean loaf takes about 5 hours from mixing to baking, mostly waiting.

#### BRD-2.M1.L1 — Yeast and fermentation

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 h · builds on `SCI-2.M6.L1` · moves IN-BAK→3, SC-MICR→3*

*Objectives:* [K] fresh, dry and instant yeast; temperature and time; flavour from fermentation
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Yeast** is a living fungus that eats sugars from the flour and produces carbon dioxide (the rise) and alcohol and
  acids (the flavour) (`SCI-2.M6.L1`).
- **The forms:**

  | Yeast (NL) | Use | Relative amount |
  |---|---|---|
  | Fresh yeast (*verse gist*) | Crumbled into the water | About 3 |
  | Active dried yeast | Dissolved in warm water first | About 1.5 |
  | Instant yeast (*gedroogde gist*, sachets) | Mixed straight into the flour | 1 |

  A formula with 1 % instant yeast needs about 3 % fresh.
- **Temperature and time:**
  - yeast works fastest at about 30–35 °C, slowly in the fridge, and dies at around 55–60 °C;
  - a dough at 24–26 °C ferments steadily;
  - the cooler and longer the fermentation, the more flavour, because acids and aromatic compounds build while the gas
    builds.

  That is why bakers use less yeast and more time, or an overnight rest in the fridge.
- **Salt** at about 2 % slows the yeast and tightens the gluten. A dough without salt rises fast and tastes flat.
- **Sugar** in small amounts feeds the yeast, but large amounts (enriched doughs) slow it, because sugar draws water
  away from the yeast.

**Sessions:**
1. Yeast at three temperatures (40 min, then 2 hours of watching): the same small dough in three marked containers, in
   the fridge, at room temperature, and somewhere warm; the rise photographed every 30 minutes.

**Cues:** the warm dough doubles fastest and the fridge dough barely moves, and the learner can explain why.

**Faults:**
- Water hot enough to kill the yeast.
- Salt poured onto the yeast in a small bowl.
- Following the clock in a cold kitchen.

#### BRD-2.M1.L2 — Developing gluten

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M3.L2` · moves SC-STRC→3*

*Objectives:* [S] kneading, *autolyse*, stretch and fold; [K] the windowpane test
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Gluten** forms when the proteins of wheat flour (glutenin and gliadin) take up water and are worked
  (`SCI-2.M3.L2`):
  - **glutenin** gives strength and elasticity;
  - **gliadin** gives extensibility.

  Together they hold the gas of fermentation.
- **Three ways to develop it:**
  - **kneading** by hand for about 10 minutes, or by mixer: fast and thorough;
  - ***autolyse*:** flour and water mixed and rested for 20–60 minutes before the salt and yeast. The gluten starts to
    form without work, and the dough becomes easier to handle;
  - **stretch and fold:** every 30 minutes during bulk fermentation, the dough is stretched up and folded over itself
    from four sides. Time does most of the work, and wetter doughs can be handled.
- **The windowpane test:** a small piece of dough stretched thin between the fingers. A well-developed dough stretches
  into a translucent sheet without tearing.

**Sessions:**
1. Knead and test (40 min): knead a small dough by hand; do the windowpane test after 3, 6 and 10 minutes; photograph
   each.

**Cues:**
- The dough turns from shaggy to smooth and elastic.
- A windowpane without tearing.

**Faults:**
- Adding flour while kneading until the dough is stiff.
- Stopping at a smooth surface without testing.
- Expecting gluten from rye or spelt to behave like wheat.

#### BRD-2.M1.X1 — Kneaded against stretch-and-fold

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-brd-2-m1-x1 -->
*comparison, recipe* — The same dough kneaded versus stretch-and-fold.
<!-- END generated:exercise-brd-2-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** SC-STRC→3, IN-BAK→3, SC-MICR→3

**Aim:** compare two ways of developing gluten in the same dough, and explain gluten and fermentation from the result.

**Conditions:**
- **Recipe support:** the lean dough recipe Phase 6 writes.
- **One batch, two halves:** one half kneaded for 10 minutes, the other developed by four sets of stretch and fold.
- **The same for both:** bulk fermentation at the same temperature, shaped alike, baked together.
- **The photos:** windowpane tests photographed for both halves.
- **The written part** explains:
  - gluten development by work and by time;
  - how temperature and time drove the fermentation, with the yeast-at-three-temperatures photos of `BRD-2.M1.L1`.

**Procedure:**
1. Mix.
2. Split the dough.
3. Develop one half by kneading and the other by folds.
4. Photograph the windowpanes.
5. Ferment, shape and bake both halves.
6. Cut them and photograph the crumbs side by side.
7. Write the explanation.

**Evidence:** photo, written, quiz:
- **`SC-STRC` level 3, the dough part:** a dough developed two ways, with the windowpanes and crumbs photographed and

  with the yeast and fermentation items at 80 %.

  how temperature and time control the yeast. The hurdles of the learner's own preserves follow in `PRS-3`.

#### BRD-2.M2.L1 — Mixing a lean dough

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 3 h · builds on `BAK-2.M1.L1` · moves TS-BRD→1*

*Objectives:* [S] weigh, mix, check dough temperature; [K] hydration
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The lean formula** in baker's percentages (`BAK-2.M1.L1`):
  - bread flour 100 %;
  - water 65–70 %;
  - salt 2 %;
  - instant yeast 0.5–1 %.

  The learner uses the bread formula scaled in `BAK-2.M1.X1`.
- **Hydration:**
  - **lower (60–65 %):** a firmer dough that is easier to shape, with a tighter crumb;
  - **higher (70–75 %):** a slacker, stickier dough, with a more open crumb.

  Wholemeal flour takes more water.
- **The target dough temperature:** about 24–26 °C after mixing. In a cold kitchen the water is warmer, in a warm one
  cooler. The learner probes the dough after mixing and logs it, so the bulk fermentation times become comparable.
- **Mixing:** flour and water first (optionally an *autolyse*), then the yeast and salt; mixed until no dry flour
  remains. Then gluten is developed by kneading or folds.

**Sessions:**
1. Mix and log (40 min, then 3 hours' fermentation): weigh the formula; choose the water temperature; mix; probe the
   dough; record the room temperature.

**Cues:**
- A dough at the target temperature.
- All weights in the log.
- A dough that feels as expected for its hydration.

**Faults:**
- Scooping flour.
- Ignoring the dough temperature.
- Adding flour to a sticky dough instead of using wet hands and a scraper.

#### BRD-2.M2.L2 — Bulk fermentation

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 3 h · builds on `BRD-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-BRD→2, JD-CUES→3*

*Objectives:* [S] folds; [J] read the rise, bubbles and feel instead of the clock
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Bulk fermentation** is the first rise, from mixing to shaping. Most of the flavour and strength develop here.
- **Folds:** two to four sets of stretch and fold in the first half, to build strength.
- **Reading readiness instead of the clock:**
  - **the rise:** about 50–75 % for a lean dough with folds, measured in a straight-sided, marked container;
  - **bubbles** on the surface and at the sides;
  - **the feel:** a dome, airy and jiggly when the container is shaken, and pulling away from the sides.

  At 24–26 °C this takes about 2–3 hours with 1 % yeast. Cooler doughs take longer.
- **The cue-then-reading entry** (`JD-CUES`): before checking the marks, the learner estimates the rise from the look
  and feel, then reads the container. Before probing, they estimate the dough temperature by touch.

**Sessions:**
1. Folds and a marked container (60 min over 3 hours): four sets of folds, photographed; rise estimated, then read,
   every 30 minutes.
2. Under and over (45 min over a day): one dough shaped at 25 % rise and one at about 100 %; both baked and compared
   with a dough at the right point.

**Cues:**
- A dough that has grown by half to three quarters.
- A domed top.
- Bubbles at the sides.
- Jiggles when shaken.

**Faults:**
- Shaping by the clock.
- A bowl with sloping sides, where the rise can't be read.
- A dough left to triple and collapse.

#### BRD-2.M2.L3 — Shaping

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 3 practical sessions · builds on `BRD-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-BRD→2*

*Objectives:* [S] pre-shape, rest, shape a round (*boule*) and an oval (*bâtard*); build tension
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Pre-shaping:** tip the dough onto a lightly floured surface, divide it with a scraper if needed, and round each
  piece loosely. Rest it for 20–30 minutes (the "bench rest"), so the gluten relaxes.
- **Shaping a round (*boule*):** fold the edges to the centre, flip it, and drag it in circles towards you on an
  unfloured patch of the bench. The surface tightens into a smooth skin.
- **Shaping an oval (*bâtard*):**
  1. Flatten it gently.
  2. Fold the top third down and the bottom third up.
  3. Roll it tightly and seal the seam.
- **Tension** is the aim: a taut surface holds the gas and helps the loaf rise up rather than out. Too little, and the
  loaf spreads; too much, and the skin tears.
- **Into the basket:** seam side up, in a floured basket or a bowl lined with a floured tea towel (rice flour sticks
  less).

**Sessions:**
1. Pre-shape and *boule* (45 min): pre-shape and shape two rounds.
2. *Bâtard* (45 min): pre-shape and shape two ovals.
3. Tension (40 min): shape one loaf loosely and one tightly; bake them; compare the spread.

**Cues:**
- A smooth, taut skin.
- A loaf that holds its shape on the bench.
- A sealed seam.

**Faults:**
- Too much flour on the bench, so the dough slides and won't tighten.
- Degassing the dough by pressing too hard.
- Torn skin.
- Skipping the bench rest.

#### BRD-2.M2.L4 — Proofing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l4 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BRD-2.M2.L3` · moves TS-BRD→2*

*Objectives:* [S] proof in a basket or bowl; the poke test; retarding in the fridge
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m2-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Proofing** is the final rise of the shaped loaf, at room temperature (about 1–2 hours) or in the fridge (8–16
  hours, retarding).
- **The poke test:** press a floured finger about 1 cm into the loaf.
  - **Springs back at once:** underproofed. Wait.
  - **Springs back slowly and partly:** ready. Bake.
  - **Doesn't spring back:** overproofed. Bake at once; it will spread.
- **Retarding** in the fridge, covered:
  - slows the yeast and develops flavour;
  - firms the dough for scoring;
  - lets the bake fit the day.

  The loaf is baked straight from the fridge.
- **Covered:** a shower cap or a bag over the basket, so the skin doesn't dry and crack.

**Sessions:**
1. Room-temperature proof (40 min over 2 hours): poke-test every 20 minutes; bake at "ready"; log the times and the
   predictions.
2. A retarded proof (40 min over two days): proof overnight in the fridge; poke-test in the morning; bake straight from
   the fridge.

**Cues:** a slow, partial spring-back, matched against the bake.

**Faults:**
- An uncovered dough that skins.
- Poke-testing too hard.
- Waiting for a dough that is already overproofed.

#### BRD-2.M2.X1 — A lean white loaf, three times

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-brd-2-m2-x1 -->
*guided, recipe* — A lean white loaf, three times over three weeks.
<!-- END generated:exercise-brd-2-m2-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-BRD→2, JD-CUES→3

**Aim:** carry a lean dough through every stage with a guiding recipe, three times over three weeks, reading the dough
instead of the clock.

**Conditions:**
- **Guided:** the recipe Phase 6 writes, in the baker's percentages of `BAK-2.M1.X1`.
- **The variable:** one change per bake, chosen and written in advance:
  - the hydration;
  - the shape (*boule* or *bâtard*);
  - room proof or retard.
- **The log,** for each bake:
  - the dough temperature;
  - the times;
  - the cue-then-reading entries: dough temperature by touch, then probe; rise by look, then the marks; doneness by
    sound and colour, then the core probe.
- **The bake:** in the steam method of `BRD-2.M3.L1` the learner has.

**Inputs:** 3 × 500 g of bread flour, salt and yeast. The loaves are the household's bread for the week.

**Procedure:**
1. Weigh, mix and probe.
2. Ferment with folds, logging the readings and estimates.
3. Pre-shape and shape.
4. Proof, with the poke test.
5. Score and bake.
6. Probe the core.
7. Cool on a rack for at least an hour.
8. Cut and photograph:
   - the dough at each stage;
   - the crust;
   - the crumb.

**Evidence:** photo, measure, log:
- **`TS-BRD` level 2:** a lean yeasted loaf from a formula, risen, baked through with the core at least 93 °C, and an
  even crumb in cross-section, three times.
- **`JD-CUES` level 3, the dough part:** at least ten cue-then-reading entries over the three bakes, at least eight
  within tolerance:
  - ±2 °C for the dough and core temperatures;
  - ±10 % for the rise.
- **AI review** scores the crumb photo:
  - **reference images:** *Competent* is an even crumb without dense patches, risen with an "ear" at the score;
    *Not yet* is a dense, gummy crumb or a large hole under the crust, a flat, spread loaf, or a pale crust;
  - **rubric:** risen (yes / no), even crumb without gummy patches (yes / no), crust deep golden (yes / no).

**Time:** 3 × about 1.25 h active over 5 hours.

### BRD-2.M3 — Baking in a home oven

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-brd-2-m3 -->
*5.5 h · 3 lessons · 5 practical sessions · reaches TS-BRD 2, KM-EQPT 3, JD-CUES 3*

*Scope:* steam, heat, scoring, doneness.

*Objectives:* [S] bake with steam for oven spring and crust; [J] judge doneness and crumb.

#### BRD-2.M3.L1 — Steam and heat at home

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `EQP-1.M2.L3` · moves TS-BRD→2, KM-EQPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] Dutch oven, baking stone, a tray of water; [K] oven spring and crust
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Oven spring:** in the first 10–15 minutes of baking, the gas expands and the yeast makes a last burst, until the
  crust sets. A loaf can grow by a third.
- **Why steam:** moisture on the surface keeps the crust soft and stretchy, so the loaf can spring fully. It also
  gelatinises the surface starch for a glossy, crackling crust. Without steam, the crust sets early and the loaf stays
  small, with a dull, thick crust.
- **Three ways at home:**
  - **a Dutch oven** (the casserole of `HEA-2`), preheated with the oven, lid on for the first 20 minutes: the loaf's own
    moisture is the steam. It is the simplest and the most reliable;
  - **a baking stone or steel,** preheated for 45 minutes, with a tray of boiling water on the shelf below for the first
    15 minutes;
  - **a tray of water** in a plain oven on a preheated sheet pan: the weakest of the three.
- **Heat:** a lean loaf bakes at about 230–250 °C, checked with the oven thermometer (`EQP-1`), then lower once the
  steam is gone.
- **Safety:**
  - a preheated Dutch oven and stone are about 250 °C, so dry oven gloves go on both hands;
  - the loaf is lowered in on baking paper, and the lid is lifted away from you;
  - boiling water is poured with the arm clear of the steam, and the pot has a metal or ovenproof knob;
  - water never touches a hot stone, glass or the oven door, which can crack.
- **The calibration log** (`KM-EQPT`): the oven temperature with the thermometer at the baking setting, and the
  preheat time the stone or pot needs.

**Sessions:**
1. Dutch oven (50 min over a bake): preheat; load safely; bake lid on, then lid off; photograph the spring.
2. Stone or tray (50 min over a bake): the same dough with the other method; compare the spring and the crust.

**Cues:**
- A loaf that grows and opens at the score.
- A glossy, deep crust.
- An oven temperature logged against the dial.

**Faults:**
- Loading a cold pot.
- Water poured onto glass.
- Removing the lid too early.
- Damp oven gloves.

#### BRD-2.M3.L2 — Scoring and baking to colour

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BRD-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-BRD→2*

*Objectives:* [S] score with a blade; bake to a deep crust
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Why score:** a cut controls where the loaf opens as it springs, instead of bursting at a weak point.
- **How:**
  - a razor blade or lame held at a shallow angle (about 30°), for an "ear" on an oval;
  - straight down for a cross on a round;
  - one quick, confident stroke about 0.5–1 cm deep;
  - a cold, retarded dough scores most cleanly.
- **Baking to colour:** the crust is baked to a deep golden brown, past where a beginner would stop. The colour is
  flavour (`SC-BRWN`), and a pale crust softens as the loaf cools.

**Sessions:**
1. Score (40 min): practise single slashes, a cross and a square on shaped dough; bake.
2. Colour (40 min): bake two loaves, one pulled at light golden and one at deep brown; compare the crust after an hour.

**Cues:**
- A clean, open score with an ear.
- A deep, even brown crust that crackles as it cools.

**Faults:**
- A hesitant, ragged score.
- Scoring too deep or too shallow.
- Pulling a pale loaf.

#### BRD-2.M3.L3 — Doneness and crumb

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m3-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `BRD-2.M3.L2` · moves JD-CUES→3*

*Objectives:* [J] colour, hollow sound, core temperature; read a crumb for faults
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The cues of a done loaf:**
  - a deep crust colour;
  - a hollow sound when the base is tapped;
  - a loaf that feels light for its size.

  The probe confirms it: the core of a lean loaf is at least 93 °C (the `TS-BRD` level 2 standard).
- **Cooling:** the crumb is still setting as steam escapes, so a loaf is cooled for at least an hour before cutting. A
  loaf cut hot is gummy.
- **Reading a crumb:**

  | Crumb | Likely cause |
  |---|---|
  | Dense, tight, a few big holes under the crust | Underfermented or underproofed |
  | Large irregular holes, a flat loaf that spread | Overproofed, or too little tension |
  | Gummy, wet | Underbaked, or cut while hot |
  | Even, with a mix of small and medium holes | As intended for a lean white loaf |

**Sessions:**
1. Predict and probe (30 min): for two loaves, predict doneness by colour and sound, then probe; cool, cut and read the
   crumb against the table.

**Cues:** predictions that match the probe; a crumb diagnosis that matches the log.

**Faults:**
- Probing through the score.
- Cutting a hot loaf.
- Blaming the flour without reading the log.

#### BRD-2.M4.L1 — A soft enriched dough

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-2-m4-l1 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 h · builds on `BRD-2.M2.L4` · moves TS-BRD→2*

*Objectives:* [S] milk bread or soft rolls; [K] fat and sugar slow fermentation and tenderise the crumb
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Enriched doughs** add fat (butter), sugar, milk and often egg. Examples are milk bread, soft rolls (*zachte
  bolletjes*), *krentenbollen* and brioche (in `BRD-3`).
- **What the enrichments do:**
  - **fat** coats the gluten and tenderises the crumb, giving a soft, fine texture and a soft crust. It is added after
    some gluten has formed, because it gets in the way;
  - **sugar** slows the yeast (it draws water away) and browns the crust faster;
  - **milk** softens the crumb and colours the crust;
  - **egg** adds richness, colour and structure.
- **The consequences:** a slower rise (more yeast or more time), a lower baking temperature (about 180–190 °C) so the
  crust doesn't burn, and a lower core at done (about 88–90 °C).
- ***Tangzhong*** (optional): a cooked flour-and-milk paste that holds more water and keeps the bread soft for days.
- **Shaping rolls:** equal pieces weighed, rounded under a cupped hand on an unfloured surface, set close together so
  they rise into each other; egg-washed for a shine.

**Sessions:**
1. Soft rolls (75 min active, about 4 hours in total): mix, add the butter after the gluten has started, ferment,
   divide by weight, shape, proof, egg-wash and bake; probe; photograph the crumb.

**Cues:**
- A smooth, soft, slightly tacky dough.
- Rolls of equal size.
- A shiny, golden crust.
- A soft, fine crumb.

**Faults:**
- Butter added at the start, so the dough won't develop.
- A dough fermented on the lean timing.
- A dark crust from a lean bread's oven heat.
- Rolls of uneven weight.

#### BRD-2.M5.L1 — A bread schedule and log

*Objectives:* [S] plan mixing, bulk and baking around work using the fridge; [J] review the log and set targets
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-2-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The fridge as a pause button:** a dough can be slowed at two points:
  - **after mixing** (a cold bulk fermentation);
  - **after shaping** (a retarded proof).

  So a loaf can be mixed in the evening, shaped the next evening, and baked the morning after. Or it can be mixed in
  the morning, shaped after work, retarded overnight and baked before work.
- **A backwards schedule** (`KM-PLAN`, `WRK-1.M2.L3`):
  1. Start from when the bread should come out of the oven.
  2. Work back through baking, preheating, proofing, shaping, bulk and mixing.
  3. Mark the control points: dough temperature, the rise, the poke test.

  The parallel tasks around a working day are marked too: dinner cooked during the bulk, for example.
- **The plan against the actual:** each stage gets a planned and an actual time, and a reasoned change where the dough
  wasn't ready.
- **The bread log review:** the learner reads the course's bakes for patterns, as in `BAK-2.M6.L2`:
  - fermentation times against dough temperature;
  - crumb faults against proofing;
  - their own banding against the AI review and the tasters.

  They set two or three targets for `BRD-3`.

**Sessions:**
1. The schedule (45 min): write a two-day schedule for a weekday bake, with control points and parallel tasks.
2. The review (45 min, after the bake): set plan against actual; read the log; mark the agreements; write the targets.

**Cues:**
- A schedule that fits around work.
- Every control point with a reading.
- A review that cites entries and photos.

**Faults:**
- A schedule that ignores the kitchen temperature.
- No control points.
- A review without evidence.
