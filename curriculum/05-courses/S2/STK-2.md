## Introduction

A good stock is the quiet foundation of sauces, braises, risottos and soups. Making it well teaches three things:
- **patience:** a stock is never boiled;
- **observation:** skimming, clarity, gel;
- **economy:** bones and trim that would be thrown away become the base of a week's cooking.

`STK-2` covers:
- **the aromatic bases** of several traditions, sweated or browned, with a *bouquet garni* or a *sachet*;
- **the four domestic stocks:** white chicken, brown, vegetable and a fast fish *fumet*;
- **storing and reducing** them safely: the cooling standard, portions in the freezer, *glace*;
- **soups:** clear soups, purées, cream soups and hearty soups;
- **a soup lab:** fixing faults, and a soup made from a brief.

**Seasoning** reaches level 3 here. A broth is seasoned to the learner's calibrated range and checked blind by the
tasters, and soups are tasted at every stage with the salt weighed.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-STK` reaches level 3. Consommé and clarification come in `CLS-4`.
- `SAU-2` builds its sauces on these stocks.
- The fault-fixing of the soup lab is the start of the flavour-correction work that `FLV-3` and `MS-S3` test.

**Pacing.** About four weeks. The stocks need weekend days: a white stock simmers for about 5 hours, a brown stock for
about 7. The soups fit weeknights once the freezer holds stock.

#### STK-2.M1.L1 — Aromatic bases

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `VEG-1.M4.L4` · moves FL-ARCH→2, CK-TERM→3*

*Objectives:* [K] *mirepoix*, *soffritto*, *sofrito*, the "holy trinity", ginger-garlic-scallion; [S] sweat versus brown them
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The aromatic bases** of several traditions:
  - ***mirepoix*** (France): onion, carrot, celery, roughly 2 : 1 : 1;
  - ***soffritto*** (Italy): the same, finely cut, slowly in olive oil;
  - ***sofrito*** (Spain): onion, garlic, tomato, peppers;
  - **the "holy trinity"** (Louisiana): onion, celery, green pepper;
  - **ginger, garlic and spring onion** (Chinese cooking).
- **Sweated or browned:** a sweated base is sweet and mild, for light soups and white stocks; a browned base is deep and
  savoury, for brown stocks and stews (`VEG-1.M4.L4`).
- **Cut size follows cooking time:** large for a long stock, a fine dice for a quick sauce.
- **Terms in three languages** go into the glossary (`CK-TERM`).

**Sessions:**
1. Four bases (60 min): a *mirepoix*, a *soffritto*, a *sofrito* and a ginger-garlic base, each tasted in a spoon of
   water or stock.

**Cues:** each base with its own character; the learner names it blind.

**Faults:**
- Browning a base meant to stay pale.
- Cutting a long-stock *mirepoix* small, so it turns to mush.
- Burning the garlic in a quick base.

#### STK-2.M1.L2 — Bouquet garni and sachet d'épices

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m1-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · moves IN-ARO→2*

*Objectives:* [S] tie a bouquet and a sachet; [K] when herbs and spices go in and come out
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A *bouquet garni*:** parsley stalks, thyme and bay, tied in a leek green or a celery stick, so it can be lifted out.
- **A *sachet d'épices*:** peppercorns, bay, thyme, parsley stalks and sometimes cloves, in muslin or a tea filter.
- **When aromatics go in and come out:**
  - whole spices and woody herbs early, because they release slowly and need time;
  - delicate herbs at the end, because they fade;
  - the bundle comes out when the flavour is right, before bitterness (bay, cloves) builds.
- **Fresh and dried:** dried herbs are stronger per gram and go in earlier; fresh soft herbs at the end. Toasting whole
  spices in a dry pan wakes them up.

**Sessions:**
1. Tie and time (45 min): tie a bouquet and a sachet; simmer two small pans of stock, one with the bundle for 20
   minutes and one for 90; taste both.

**Cues:** a bouquet that holds together and lifts out in one piece; the long-simmered pan noticeably more bitter or
woody.

**Faults:**
- Loose herbs that end up in the soup.
- A bundle forgotten for hours.
- Dried basil in place of fresh at the end.

#### STK-2.M2.L1 — Stock principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L1` · moves TS-STK→2, SC-PROT→3*

*Objectives:* [K] extraction, gelatin, clarity, ratios of bones, water and aromatics; why stock is not boiled
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Extraction:** flavour, minerals and gelatin move from bones, meat and vegetables into the water. Gelatin comes from
  collagen in bones, joints and skin (`SCI-2.M2.L1`). A good stock sets to a soft gel when chilled.
- **Ratios:** roughly 1 kg of bones for 1–1.5 litres of cold water, and aromatics at about 10–20 % of the bone weight.
  The bones are just covered, never drowned.
- **Clarity:** start cold, bring slowly to a bare simmer, skim the foam (coagulated proteins), never boil. Boiling
  churns fat and particles into the liquid and clouds it.
- **The bones decide the time:**
  - chicken: 3–5 hours;
  - beef or veal: longer;
  - fish: 20–30 minutes;
  - vegetables: about an hour.

  Beyond those times, bitterness and cloudiness build.
- **Salt** goes in later, at the dish, because stock is reduced.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): write the plan for a white and a brown stock: bones, ratio, timing, skimming, cooling.

**Cues:** a plan with a ratio, a time and a cooling method.

**Faults:**
- Starting with hot water.
- Boiling.
- Salting the stock.
- Too much water, which gives a thin stock that never gels.

#### STK-2.M2.L2 — White chicken stock

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 5 h · builds on `STK-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-STK→3, TS-MOI→4*

*Objectives:* [S] blanching (optional), skimming, gentle simmer, straining
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The steps:**
  - blanch the bones briefly if a very clear stock is wanted (optional), then rinse;
  - cover with cold water;
  - bring slowly to a bare simmer and skim;
  - add the *mirepoix* and the *bouquet garni* after the first skim;
  - simmer, uncovered, at about 85–95 °C, logged;
  - strain through a fine sieve without pressing.
- **Wings, backs and feet** give the most gelatin for the money.
- **Holding the simmer** is a moist-heat skill (`TS-MOI`): the surface only trembles.

**Sessions:**
1. The stock (2 h active over about 5 h): make the stock of `STK-2.M2.X1`.

**Cues:** a clear, golden liquid; a firm wobble after chilling.

**Faults:**
- A rolling boil.
- Stirring.
- Pressing the solids through the sieve, which clouds the stock.

#### STK-2.M2.L3 — Brown stock

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l3 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 7 h · builds on `STK-2.M2.L2`, `HEA-1.M2.L1` · moves TS-STK→3, SC-BRWN→3*

*Objectives:* [S] roast the bones and vegetables, *pincé* the tomato paste, deglaze; a domestic brown stock
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Colour and depth come from browning:**
  - roast the bones and the *mirepoix* to a deep, even brown, not burnt;
  - cook the tomato paste in the pan until it turns rust-coloured (*pincé*);
  - deglaze the roasting tin into the pot.
- **The domestic brown stock:** beef or veal bones, or chicken wings for a lighter one. It simmers for 6–8 hours, and
  can finish overnight on the lowest setting in a low oven with the lid ajar.
- **Burnt bones** give a bitter stock that cannot be fixed, so they are discarded.

**Sessions:**
1. The stock (2.5 h active over about 7 h): roast, *pincé*, deglaze, simmer, strain; photograph the roasted bones.

**Cues:** bones and vegetables a deep, even brown; a dark, clear stock that gels.

**Faults:**
- Pale bones.
- Burnt bits.
- Tomato paste added raw.
- The roasting-tin fond left behind.

#### STK-2.M2.L4 — Vegetable stock and fumet

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l4 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 1 h · builds on `STK-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-STK→3*

*Objectives:* [S] a clear vegetable stock; a fast fish *fumet*; [K] why fish stock is short
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Vegetable stock:**
  - sweated aromatics, leek greens, mushroom trim, a little tomato, herbs;
  - about 45–60 minutes, because longer turns it dull and sweet;
  - no brassicas, which turn sulphurous, and no starchy vegetables, which cloud it.
- **A fish *fumet*:**
  - white fish bones and heads from lean fish (not oily);
  - gills removed and the bones rinsed;
  - sweated with the aromatics, then wine and cold water;
  - simmered for only 20–30 minutes, because longer extracts bitter, gluey compounds.
- **Why fish stock is short:** fish bones are thin and their collagen gives up quickly.

**Sessions:**
1. Vegetable stock (60 min): from the trim bag, by ratio.
2. *Fumet* (60 min): from bones the fishmonger gives away.

**Cues:** a fresh, clear vegetable stock; a pale, clean-smelling *fumet*.

**Faults:**
- Cabbage in the vegetable stock.
- Mackerel bones in a *fumet*.
- A *fumet* simmered for an hour.

#### STK-2.M2.L5 — Storing and reducing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l5 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `SAF-1.M3.L3` · moves TS-STK→3, KM-WAST→3, SA-TEMP→4*

*Objectives:* [S] cool fast, portion and freeze; reduce to *glace*; [J] use trim and bones from the freezer
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m2-l5 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Cooling a stock** by the measurable standard
  ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)):
  - from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours, and to 5 °C or below within 6 hours in total;
  - by splitting it into shallow containers and an ice-water bath with stirring;
  - logged.
- **Storing:** 2 days in the fridge (§9), or frozen in labelled portions (500 ml tubs, ice-cube trays for small
  amounts).
- **Reducing to *glace***: a stock reduced to a syrup that sets firm. A concentrated base for sauces, frozen in cubes.
- **The trim and bone bags:** bones and vegetable trim saved in the freezer until there is enough for a stock (`KM-WAST`).

**Sessions:**
1. Cool and portion (45 min plus waiting): cool a batch of stock by the standard, logging it, portion and freeze it, and
   reduce one portion to *glace*.

**Cues:** a log within the limits; labelled portions; a *glace* that coats a spoon and sets in the fridge.

**Faults:**
- A full pot cooling on the stove.
- Unlabelled tubs.
- Reducing salted stock, which turns too salty.

#### STK-2.M3.L1 — Broth-based soups

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STK-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-STK→3*

*Objectives:* [S] garnish cut and timing; [K] *consommé* and clarification (the technique comes in `CLS-4`)
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A clear soup is only as good as its stock.** The garnish is cut small and evenly and cooked to the right point: in
  the broth if it cooks quickly, separately if it would cloud it (pasta, rice).
- **The Dutch *heldere soep***: a clear beef or chicken broth with fine vegetables, vermicelli and meatballs.
- **Consommé** is a stock clarified with a raft of egg white and minced meat, which traps every particle. It is named
  here and made in `CLS-4`.

**Sessions:**
1. A clear soup (75 min): a chicken broth with a fine *brunoise*, cooked separately vermicelli and herbs, seasoned at the
   end.

**Cues:** a clear broth; a garnish that is tender but holds its shape.

**Faults:**
- Pasta cooked in the broth, which clouds it.
- A garnish cut too large.
- A cloudy stock that no garnish can save.

#### STK-2.M3.L2 — Seasoning a broth

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m3-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L2` · moves FL-SEAS→3, FL-SENS→3*

*Objectives:* [S] season a broth to the calibration range; [J] adjust salt, acid and umami
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A broth shows seasoning nakedly.** It is salted to the learner's calibrated range from `TST-1` (around 8–12 g/l),
  weighed and noted, then adjusted by taste.
- **Acid and umami:** a few drops of acid lift it; umami (a Parmesan rind, dried mushroom, a dash of soy) deepens it.
  Each adjustment is noted.
- **Blind checking:** the tasters judge seasoning without knowing the target; their cards and the learner's calibrated
  form are compared.
- **This month's calibration** includes the salt and acid triangle tests ([assessment §5](../../04-assessment.md#5-calibration-tests)).

**Sessions:**
1. Season and check (60 min): season a broth by weight and taste, record every adjustment; the tasters judge it blind;
   run the triangle tests.

**Cues:** the learner's form and the tasters' cards agree; the triangle tests passed.

**Faults:**
- Salting by eye.
- Adding acid to a broth that needs salt.
- Over-correcting with umami until it tastes of soy.

#### STK-2.M4.L1 — Purée soups

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STK-2.M2.L4` · moves TS-VEG→4, FL-TEXT→2*

*Objectives:* [S] sweat, simmer, blend, pass; [J] adjust the body; [K] starch versus vegetable body
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The method:** sweat the aromatics, add the main vegetable and the stock, simmer until fully tender, blend, then
  pass through a sieve for a fine texture, and adjust the body.
- **Body:**
  - from starch: a potato, rice or a starchy vegetable thickens the soup;
  - from the vegetable itself: squash, peas and celeriac are thick enough alone.

  Thin soups are reduced or given more vegetable; thick ones are loosened with stock.
- **Blending hot liquids safely:** a jug blender filled no more than a third, the lid vented with a towel over it; a
  stick blender in the pot is safer.
- **Textures named before cooking:** velvety, coarse, with a crisp garnish (`FL-TEXT`).

**Sessions:**
1. A purée soup (75 min): a seasonal purée (squash, celeriac, pea or carrot), passed; photograph a spoonful falling.

**Cues:** a soup that coats a spoon and flows; no fibres; bright colour.

**Faults:**
- Undercooked vegetables that blend grainy.
- A blender lid blown off by steam.
- Too much potato, which goes gluey.

#### STK-2.M4.L2 — Cream and velouté soups

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STK-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-STK→3*

*Objectives:* [S] finish with cream or a liaison; [K] the classical cream soup
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The classical cream soup:** a *velouté* (stock thickened with a blond roux) flavoured with the main vegetable,
  blended, finished with cream, or with a *liaison* of yolks and cream.
- **The *liaison*:** yolks and cream tempered with hot soup, stirred in off the heat. It thickens and enriches, but
  curdles if boiled (`EGG-1.M4`).
- **Avoiding a split:** acid and boiling curdle cream. Acidic ingredients go in before the cream, and the soup is not
  boiled after it.

**Sessions:**
1. A cream soup (75 min): a leek, mushroom or cauliflower cream soup on a *velouté*, finished with a *liaison*.

**Cues:** a satin texture; no taste of raw flour; no curdling after the *liaison*.

**Faults:**
- A raw-tasting roux.
- Boiling after the *liaison*.
- Lemon added after the cream.

#### STK-2.M4.L3 — Hearty soups

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m4-l3 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 h · builds on `STK-2.M2.L4` · moves IN-VEG→3, TS-STK→3*

*Objectives:* [S] minestrone, chowder, a pulse soup; [K] layering ingredients by cooking time
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m4-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Hearty soups** (minestrone, chowder, a pea or pulse soup, the Dutch *erwtensoep*) are chunky meals in a bowl.
- **Layering by cooking time**, from the structure of each ingredient (`VEG-1.M1`):
  - dense roots and dried pulses first;
  - then softer vegetables;
  - then pasta or greens;
  - herbs last.
- **Body** comes from the pulses, potatoes or a little starch; the texture of each ingredient should still be
  recognisable.
- **Dried pulses** need soaking and long simmering (`LEG-2`). Acid and very hard water slow their softening.

**Sessions:**
1. A hearty soup (75 min plus about 2 hours simmering): a minestrone or a pea soup, the ingredients added in layers and
   each layer timed.

**Cues:** every ingredient cooked through and still distinct; a body that holds a spoon briefly.

**Faults:**
- Everything in at once, so the greens turn grey and the pulses stay hard.
- Pasta cooked to mush.
- Tomato added before the pulses soften.

#### STK-2.M5.L1 — Fixing soups

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-stk-2-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M4.L2`, `STK-2.M4.L1` · moves FL-CORR→3, JD-TRBL→2*

*Objectives:* [J] too thin, too thick, flat, too salty, split; [S] fix each
<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Diagnose, then fix:**
  - **too thin:** reduce, blend in more of the vegetable, or whisk in a little *beurre manié* (equal parts butter and
    flour) and simmer;
  - **too thick:** loosen with hot stock;
  - **flat:** salt first, then acid;
  - **too salty:** dilute with unsalted stock or add more vegetable, then re-blend;
  - **split** (a cream soup boiled with acid): blend hard, and add a little cold cream off the heat.
- **Record the fault, the cause and the fix** in the fault log (`JD-TRBL`), so the cause is not repeated.

**Sessions:**
1. The fix test (75 min): a household member prepares four faulty versions of a soup (thin, salty, flat, split) and keeps
   one correct; the learner diagnoses in writing, fixes each, and the household tastes the fixes blind against the
   correct one.

**Cues:** diagnoses written before any fix and right; fixes the tasters can't tell from the correct soup, or prefer.

**Faults:**
- Fixing too salty with acid.
- Thickening with raw flour.
- Fixing a split soup by boiling it.

<!-- END generated:lesson-stk-2-m5-l2 -->

**Sessions:**

**Cues:** a balanced soup without a recipe; the substitution's effect predicted and confirmed.

**Faults:**
- Too much liquid, which gives a thin soup.
- Swapping a starchy vegetable for a watery one without adjusting the body.
