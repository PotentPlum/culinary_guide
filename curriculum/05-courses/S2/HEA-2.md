## Introduction

`HEA-1` taught the basic methods one at a time. `HEA-2` puts them in a **matrix** — dry, moist and combination, by
medium and temperature — and adds the methods that need more control. Each module answers one question:

| Module | Question |
|---|---|
| Roasting at two temperatures, with resting planned | Where should a large piece of meat be cooked to its core, and how long should it rest? |
| Grilling with radiant heat and two heat zones | How is radiant heat used? |
| Deep-frying | How does one fry safely, at the right temperature? |
| Braising | How is a tough cut made tender? |

The course ends with a three-method lab on one protein.

**Frying** is the most dangerous thing a home cook does with heat. The module on it treats safety as the first skill:
- the station;
- the oil level and the thermometer;
- the lid within reach;
- never leaving hot oil.

It is assessed on video (`SA-PHYS` level 4).

**Choosing a method** is the judgment thread: from here, each framework and brief asks the learner to say why a method
suits the ingredient and the goal (`CK-METH`, `JD-CHSE`).

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-DRY` reaches level 3 here. The protein courses (`POU-2`, `MEA-2`, `FSH-2`) apply it.
- `TS-CMB` starts here and is deepened in `SLW-3`.
- Grilling over live fire comes in `FIR-3`.

**Pacing.** About four weeks. The roast, the braise and the fried dinners need a weekend slot. The method plan of
module 1 runs over two weeks of normal cooking.

*3 h · 2 lessons · 1 practical sessions · reaches CK-METH 3, JD-CHSE 2*

*Scope:* dry, moist and combination methods, by medium and temperature.

*Objectives:* [K] classify methods; [J] choose one for a cut and a goal.

*Check (outline):* quiz; the method plan with outcomes.
<!-- END generated:module-hea-2-m1 -->

#### HEA-2.M1.L1 — Classifying methods

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L5` · moves CK-METH→3*

*Objectives:* [K] dry, moist and combination methods by medium, temperature and outcome
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The matrix:**
  - **dry heat** (air, fat, metal or radiation): roast, bake, grill, sauté, pan-fry, deep-fry;
  - **moist heat** (water or steam): poach, simmer, boil, steam;
  - **combination** (brown first, then moist): braise, stew, pot-roast.
- **For each method:** the medium, the temperature range and the outcome (crust or none, even or gradient, tenderising
  or not).
- **Temperature at the surface against the core** explains why the same steak can be grey throughout or crusted and pink.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): place 20 dishes in the matrix and write the medium, the temperature and the outcome
of each.

**Cues:** every dish placed, with the reason in one line.

**Faults:**
- Calling a pot roast "roasting".
- Confusing a stew and a braise.
- Forgetting deep-frying is dry heat.

#### HEA-2.M1.L2 — Choosing a method

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M1.L1` · moves JD-CHSE→2, CK-METH→3*

*Objectives:* [J] choose from tenderness, size, fat and the result wanted; [K] when to combine methods
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The analysis questions** (the start of `JD-CHSE`):
  - How tender is it (a little connective tissue or a lot)?
  - How large and thick is it?
  - How much fat does it have?
  - What result do I want (crust, juiciness, tenderness, a sauce)?
- **Combining methods:**
  - sear, then roast, for a thick cut;
  - blanch, then grill, for a dense vegetable;
  - brown, then braise, for a tough cut.
- **A prediction is written before cooking**, so the result can confirm or correct the reasoning.

**Sessions:**
1. Analyse and cook (75 min): analyse an unfamiliar ingredient with the questions, choose between two methods, cook it,
   and compare the result with the prediction.

**Cues:** a choice reasoned from the ingredient, not from a recipe; a prediction the result confirms.

**Faults:**
- Choosing by habit.
- Ignoring size: a whole celeriac and a celeriac dice need different methods.
- No prediction written.

### HEA-2.M2 — Roasting and resting

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-hea-2-m2 -->
*5 h · 2 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches TS-DRY 3, KM-EQPT 3, SC-HEAT 3*

*Scope:* high- and low-temperature roasting; resting.

*Objectives:* [S] roast to a target; [K] manage carryover.

*Check (outline):* temperature log (objective) and cross-section photo.
<!-- END generated:module-hea-2-m2 -->

#### HEA-2.M2.L1 — Oven control for roasting

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EQP-1.M2.L3`, `HEA-1.M4.L2` · moves TS-DRY→3, KM-EQPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] high versus low temperature; convection; rack and pan choice
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **High temperature** (200–230 °C) gives a deep crust and a steep gradient: a band of overcooked meat under the crust.
  It suits small birds and quick roasts.
- **Low temperature** (around 120–150 °C) gives an even interior and little gradient. It suits large joints, finished
  with a hot blast or a sear for the crust (the "reverse sear").
- **Convection** (fan) browns faster and dries the surface.
- **Rack and pan:** a rack lets air reach the underside; a low-sided pan keeps the sides from steaming; the oven table
  of `EQP-1` sets the real temperature.
- **Checking the oven** before a long roast belongs in the calibration log (`KM-EQPT`).

**Sessions:**
1. Two ovens (75 min): two pieces of the same pork or chicken roasted, one hot and one low-then-hot, to the same core
   temperature; slice and photograph the gradients.

**Cues:** a grey band under the crust on the hot roast; an even colour edge to edge on the low roast.

**Faults:**
- Trusting the dial instead of the oven table.
- A deep pan that steams the roast.
- No rack.
- Opening the oven door often.

#### HEA-2.M2.L2 — Resting and carryover

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L3` · moves TS-DRY→3, SC-HEAT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] plan resting time; [K] juices, carryover and why resting works
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Carryover** (`HEA-1.M1.L3`) depends on size, oven temperature and shape: a large roast from a hot oven can rise
  5 °C or more; a small one from a low oven hardly at all.
- **Planning the rest:**
  - take the roast out below the target and let carryover finish it;
  - always confirm the safe core temperature
    ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)) after the rest.

  For poultry that means 74 °C, for a whole roast the time-temperature table.
- **Why resting works:** the gradient evens out, and the juices thicken slightly as the meat cools, so less runs out on
  slicing.

**Sessions:**
1. Predict the rise (45 min, within a roast): predict the carryover of a roast, then log the core every 5 minutes.
2. Rest or no rest (45 min): two steaks or chicken thighs, one sliced at once and one after resting; weigh the juice on
   the board.

**Cues:** the predicted rise within a few degrees; less juice on the board after resting.

**Faults:**
- Taking poultry out early and never checking it after the rest.
- Covering tightly, which steams the crust.
- Resting a thin piece for so long that it goes cold.

#### HEA-2.M2.X1 — A joint or a whole bird to a target core temperature

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-hea-2-m2-x1 -->
*framework, framework* — Roast a joint or whole bird to a target core temperature; log carryover.
<!-- END generated:exercise-hea-2-m2-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-DRY→3, KM-EQPT→3, SC-HEAT→3

**Aim:** a roast taken to its target by measurement, with carryover planned and logged, and the instruments checked
first.

**Conditions:** framework support: the roasting and resting framework; no recipe. The probe calibration and the oven
check are done before the roast, as part of the exercise.

**Inputs:** a whole chicken of about 1.5 kg, or a pork loin roast of about 1 kg; salt; vegetables for the tray.

**Procedure:**
1. Calibrate the probe in ice water and log it.
2. Check the oven at three settings with the oven thermometer and log them.
3. Dry-brine the meat the day before.
4. Roast to just below the target, predicting the carryover.
5. Log the core every 5 minutes while resting.
6. Confirm the safe core temperature.
7. Carve and photograph the cross-section.

**Evidence:** photo, measure, log:
- **`TS-DRY` level 3, the roasting part:** an even, browned roast with the right interior, without a recipe. The
  grilling and frying parts follow in `HEA-2.M3` and `HEA-2.M4`.
- **`KM-EQPT` level 3, the thermometer and oven part:** the calibration log entries.
- **`SC-HEAT` level 3:** the probe within 0 °C ± 1 °C, the oven at three settings, and the carryover explained.

**Time:** 2.5 h, mostly waiting.

### HEA-2.M3 — Grilling and broiling

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-hea-2-m3 -->
*4.5 h · 2 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches TS-DRY 3, SC-HEAT 3, TS-VEG 4, IN-VEG 3*

*Scope:* radiant heat: grill pan, oven grill, barbecue.

*Objectives:* [S] grill vegetables and fish with good marks and no sticking.

*Check (outline):* photos (AI review of colour and char).
<!-- END generated:module-hea-2-m3 -->

#### HEA-2.M3.L1 — Radiant heat

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L1` · moves TS-DRY→3, SC-HEAT→3*

*Objectives:* [K] grill pan, oven grill and barbecue; distance and heat zones; [S] set up two heat zones
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Radiant heat** falls off sharply with distance: closer is much hotter. That is the main control on an oven grill
  (*grill* / *gril*) and on a barbecue.
- **A grill pan** is conduction through the ridges, which give the marks, and radiation between them.
- **Two heat zones:** a hot zone for marks and crust, and a cooler zone to finish thick pieces without burning them.
  - On an oven grill: two rack heights.
  - On a barbecue: coals on one side (`FIR-3` goes deeper).
- **Fat and flare-ups:** fat dripping onto coals or the element flares; it is trimmed, and the food moved to the cool
  zone.

**Sessions:**
1. Map the grill (60 min): set up two zones on the oven grill or the barbecue, and grill bread or courgette slices at
   different distances to see how fast each zone browns.

**Cues:** a predictable difference between the zones; the learner knows how far from the element to grill a thick and a
thin piece.

**Faults:**
- Everything at one distance.
- The grill not preheated.
- Ignoring a flare-up.

#### HEA-2.M3.L2 — Grilling vegetables and fish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-VEG→4, IN-VEG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] oiling, preheating, marks, turning; [J] which vegetables and fish suit the grill
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The sequence:**
  - oil the food, not the grill;
  - preheat thoroughly;
  - lay the food down and leave it until it releases;
  - turn once for marks;
  - finish in the cooler zone.
- **Vegetables that suit the grill:**
  - firm and juicy ones: courgette, peppers, leeks, spring onions, aubergine in summer, fennel, chicory;
  - dense roots are blanched first, because the outside would char before the inside is done;
  - what heat does to colour and texture (`VEG-1.M3`) decides the choice.
- **Fish that suits the grill:** oily and firm (mackerel, herring, salmon), or whole fish with the skin on. Delicate
  white fillets need a grill basket or the pan.

**Sessions:**
1. Grilled vegetables (60 min): three vegetables, one blanched first; photograph the marks and the cross-sections.
2. Grilled fish (60 min): a whole mackerel or fillets with skin, grilled and probed to 63 °C.

**Cues:** clean dark marks without black soot; the fish lifting off cleanly; the vegetables tender inside.

**Faults:**
- Wet vegetables.
- Turning too early, which tears the food.
- A raw centre in a thick slice of celeriac.
- Delicate fish falling through the bars.

#### HEA-2.M3.X1 — A grilled vegetable and fish dinner

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-hea-2-m3-x1 -->
*brief, brief* — A grilled vegetable and fish dinner from a method outline.
<!-- END generated:exercise-hea-2-m3-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-DRY→3, SC-HEAT→3, TS-VEG→4, IN-VEG→3

**Aim:** a grilled dinner from a method outline: a fish and three vegetable garnishes, timed to finish together.

**Conditions:** brief support: the goal (a grilled fish with three seasonal vegetable garnishes, ready together), the

**Procedure:**
1. Plan the timing backwards, and the zone for each item.
2. Write why each vegetable gets its method.
3. Blanch the dense one and compare a slice with a raw-grilled slice.
4. Grill.
5. Probe the fish.
6. Plate and photograph.
7. Log the finish times.

**Evidence:** photo, measure, log, written:
- **`TS-DRY` level 3, the grilling part:** clean marks and the right interior, without a recipe.
- **`TS-VEG` level 4, the timing part:** three garnishes finished with the main, each method reasoned. The glaze and
  the purée follow in `STK-2.M4`.

- **`SC-HEAT` level 3, the explanation part:** the two-zone result explained from radiant heat.

- *Competent*: clean dark marks and an even colour;
- *Not yet*: soot-black patches, or pale with no marks.

#### HEA-2.M4.L1 — Oil, temperature and safety

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M4.L2`, `SCI-2.M4.L1` · moves SA-PHYS→4, KM-EQPT→3*

*Objectives:* [K] oils for frying, smoke point, reuse; [S] set up a safe frying station; never leave hot oil; fat-fire response
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Oils for frying:** refined, neutral oils with a high smoke point (rapeseed, sunflower, groundnut where no allergy).
  Reused oil darkens, foams and smokes earlier; it is strained, kept covered and discarded when dark or smelly.
- **The safe frying station:**
  - a deep, heavy pot on a back burner, filled no more than a third;
  - the frying thermometer clipped on;
  - a lid and a fire blanket within reach;
  - a spider or slotted spoon, and a tray with paper or a rack;
  - dry food, lowered away from the body;
  - nobody and no water nearby;
  - never leave hot oil;
  - turn the heat off before it smokes.
- **Fat fire response** (`SAF-1.M4.L2`): heat off, lid or blanket, never water, never move the pot.
- **The frying thermometer** is checked and logged in the calibration log.

**Sessions:**
1. Set up and film (45 min): set up the station, film it with the checklist read aloud, heat the oil to 170 °C with
   the thermometer, and fry a test piece of bread.

**Cues:** every checklist item visible on the video; the oil holding 170 °C; the bread browning in about a minute.

**Faults:**
- A pot more than half full.
- A front burner with the handle out.
- Wet chips into hot oil.
- No lid at hand.

#### HEA-2.M4.L2 — Batters and breadings

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m4-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M4.L1` · moves TS-DRY→3, SC-BRWN→3*

*Objectives:* [S] the standard breading (flour, egg, crumbs); a light batter; [K] why cold batter and hot oil give crispness
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The standard breading** (*paneren*): flour (dry), then egg (glue), then crumbs (crust). One hand for dry, one for
  wet. Rested briefly so it sets.
- **A light batter:** flour, a little starch, cold sparkling water or beer, barely mixed. Cold batter in hot oil sets
  instantly with little gluten, so it is crisp and light; warm, overmixed batter is heavy and greasy.
- **Temperature and colour:** at 170–180 °C the coating browns as the inside cooks; too cool and it soaks up oil; too
  hot and the outside burns before the inside is done.

**Sessions:**
1. Breaded and battered (75 min): fish or vegetable pieces breaded and battered, fried at 175 °C; one batch fried at
   150 °C for comparison.

**Cues:** an even golden crust that stays crisp; the low-temperature batch pale and greasy.

**Faults:**
- Clumped "club hands".
- Batter mixed smooth like a cake.
- Overloading the pot so the oil temperature crashes.

#### HEA-2.M4.L3 — Frying vegetables and potatoes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m4-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-2.M4.L1` · moves TS-VEG→4, TS-DRY→3*

*Objectives:* [S] double-fried chips, vegetable fritters; [J] drain, season, hold crisp
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m4-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Double-fried chips** (*friet*, the Dutch way):
  - floury potatoes cut evenly, rinsed and dried;
  - a first fry at a lower temperature until soft but pale;
  - a rest;
  - a second fry at about 180 °C until crisp and golden.

  The first fry cooks the inside; the second makes the crust.
- **Vegetable fritters:** grated or sliced vegetables (courgette, celeriac, onion) in a light batter; small and flat so
  they cook through.
- **Holding crisp:** drain on a rack, not stacked on paper; salt straight away; serve quickly or hold in a low oven on a
  rack.

**Sessions:**
1. Chips (75 min): double-fried chips with the oil temperature logged.
2. Fritters (60 min): vegetable fritters in a light batter.

**Cues:** chips crisp outside and fluffy inside; fritters golden and cooked through.

**Faults:**
- One fry at high heat (burnt outside, raw inside).
- Wet potatoes.
- Chips piled on paper until soggy.

#### HEA-2.M5.L1 — Braise versus stew

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-2-m5-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-2.M1.L1` · moves TS-CMB→2, CK-METH→3*

*Objectives:* [K] cut size, liquid level, lid, low oven; why tough cuts need time
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-2-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A braise:** large pieces, browned, then cooked covered with liquid partway up, gently, often in a low oven.
- **A stew** (*stoofpot*): smaller pieces, fully covered with liquid.
- **Why tough cuts need time:** collagen turns to gelatin only after long cooking at a gentle simmer
  (`SCI-2.M2.L1`). A boil tightens the muscle fibres and makes them stringy, so the liquid barely trembles, in a low
  oven or on the lowest heat.
- **The Dutch oven** (*braadpan*, tier 1) holds heat evenly and its lid traps steam.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): for six cuts and vegetables, decide braise, stew or neither, and write the liquid
level and the oven temperature.

**Cues:** the choice and the liquid level reasoned from cut size and toughness.

**Faults:**
- Boiling a stew.
- Too much liquid in a braise, which then boils.
- Lean, tender cuts braised for hours until dry.
