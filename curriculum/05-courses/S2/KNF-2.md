## Introduction

In S1 the learner learned to cut accurately and safely. In S2 the work grows. Soups and stocks for ten, a whole chicken,
a loin to portion: the knife has to be **fast, still accurate, and sharp**. `KNF-2` is short, but it runs through the
stage:
- **Speed with uniformity:** the timed drills continue weekly through S2, with targets that rise, and a fast set out of
  range still doesn't count.
- **Prep at volume:** aromatics and herbs for 8–10 portions, organised so nothing is cut twice and nothing waits too
  long.
- **The first protein cuts:** trimming silver skin and portioning by weight, at a raw-protein station kept separate from
  everything else.
- **The whetstone:** a sharp knife kept sharp, judged by tests and logged.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-KNF` reaches level 4 here and is re-tested at `MS-S2` (`MS-S2.R9`).
- Fabrication (`TS-FAB`) continues in the chicken, fish and meat courses.
- The hygiene of the raw-protein station is re-tested at every milestone.
- Fine and decorative cuts come in S4.

**Pacing.** The lessons take about two weeks. The drills (`KNF-2.M1.X1`) and the edge log (`KNF-2.M3.L2`) then run
weekly through S2. The soup prep for ten is a weekend batch cook that stocks the freezer.

#### KNF-2.M1.L1 — Speed with uniformity

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M5.L1` · moves TS-KNF→4*

*Objectives:* [S] timed drills against S2 targets; [J] find where time is lost
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The S2 targets:** the `MS-S1` cut list plus *paysanne* and *chiffonade*, all within range for 8 of 10 pieces, in
  30 minutes (the `TS-KNF` level 4 row). Tolerances as in the framework
  ([TS tolerances](../../04-competencies/TS.md)).
- **Where time is lost:**
  - handling a piece twice;
  - an untidy board;
  - stopping to sweep;
  - sticks that are not stacked;
  - the guiding hand moving in jumps instead of a steady retreat.

  Filming the drill shows them.
- **Economy of motion:**
  - the knife stays low;
  - finished cuts are moved off the board with the back of the blade, never the edge;
  - the waste bowl is within reach.

**Sessions:**
1. Baseline and film (60 min): the full list timed and measured; a 2-minute side video.
2. Fix one loss (60 min): correct the biggest time loss from the video and repeat the drill.

**Cues:** the time falling while the count within range stays at 8 of 10 or more.

**Faults:**
- Scraping the board with the edge.
- Speeding up the knife instead of removing wasted motion.
- Measuring only the best pieces.

#### KNF-2.M1.L2 — Prep at volume

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `WRK-1.M1.L3` · moves TS-MEP→4, TS-KNF→4*

*Objectives:* [S] aromatics and herbs for 8–10 portions; workflow and storage of cut produce
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Batch the motions:**
  - peel all the onions, then halve all, then slice all;
  - one task at a time across the whole batch is faster than finishing each item.
- **The prep list for volume:** quantities scaled, cut and container per item, the order (what keeps, what doesn't),
  and the time.
- **Storing cut produce:**
  - covered and cold;
  - cut onions and garlic in closed containers;
  - herbs cut last, or stored whole in a damp towel;
  - potatoes and celeriac under water if prepped ahead;
  - labelled with the time (`SAF-1`).

**Sessions:**
1. Aromatics for ten (75 min): onions, carrots, celery or celeriac and garlic for a large batch, from a written list,
   timed.
2. Herbs and finish (75 min): herbs and garnishes for ten, cut at the right moment; containers labelled.

**Cues:** one task at a time across the batch; containers labelled and in the fridge; the board clear between tasks.

**Faults:**
- Finishing one onion completely before starting the next.
- Herbs chopped an hour early.
- Unlabelled tubs.

#### KNF-2.M1.X1 — Weekly timed drills

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-knf-2-m1-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — Weekly timed drills through S2 (the knife thread).
<!-- END generated:exercise-knf-2-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-KNF→4

**Aim:** the S1 cut list plus *paysanne* and *chiffonade* at working speed, within range, before `MS-S2`.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the list and the targets. Weekly through S2, about 10 sessions. The variable changes
one at a time: the vegetable (by season), then the quantity.

**Inputs:** per week, about 1 kg of drill vegetables (onions, carrots, celeriac or leek, herbs), used in that week's

**Procedure:**
1. Each week, time the full list.
2. Measure 10 random pieces of each cut on the grid and photograph them.
3. Log the time, the counts and the next target.
4. Film one drill a month for the grip.

**Evidence:** measure, photo, video, log — the drill log. When the full list is within range for 8 of 10 pieces in 30
minutes, on two different days, this is the **cutting part** of the `TS-KNF` level 4 row; the stone-sharpened edge
follows in `KNF-2.M3.X1`.

AI review checks the grid photos against the tolerances and the monthly grip videos. Reference set for Phase 6:
- *Competent* and *Not yet* for *paysanne* and *chiffonade* (the S1 set covers the other cuts).

**Time:** about 40 min a week.

#### KNF-2.M2.L1 — Trimming and portioning

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAF-1.M2.L2` · moves TS-FAB→2, SA-HYG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] remove silver skin and excess fat; portion by weight; [S] keep the raw-protein station separate
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Silver skin** is a thin, tough membrane that doesn't soften with cooking. It is removed:
  - slide the tip under one end;
  - hold that tab;
  - run the knife along it with the blade angled slightly up.
- **Excess fat:** trimmed to the level the method needs. Some fat protects and flavours a roast; most is removed for a
  quick sauté.
- **Portioning by weight:**
  - decide the weight per portion;
  - cut, weigh and adjust the next cut;
  - aim for an even thickness so the portions cook alike.
- **The raw-protein station** (`SAF-1.M2`):
  - a separate board and knife;
  - raw protein last in the work order;
  - hands washed at every change;
  - the trim straight into a container for stock or the bin;
  - the station cleaned and disinfected at the end.

**Sessions:**
1. Trim (60 min): trim the silver skin and fat from a pork tenderloin or loin; keep the trim for stock.
2. Portion (60 min): portion the loin into equal steaks; weigh each; film the station.

**Cues:** clean, membrane-free meat with little flesh lost; portions within a few grams of the target; no contact
between the raw board and anything else.

**Faults:**
- Sawing off thick slabs with the silver skin.
- Portions by eye.
- The raw board used for the salad.
- The phone touched with raw hands.

#### KNF-2.M3.L1 — Sharpening on a whetstone

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `EQP-1.M3.L2` · moves KM-EQPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] angle, pressure, burr, grits, stropping
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Grits:**
  - a coarse stone (around 400) repairs a dull or chipped edge;
  - a medium stone (around 1000) sharpens;
  - a fine stone (3000–6000) refines and polishes.

  A combination stone of 1000/6000 covers most home needs.
- **Soaking or splashing:** water stones are soaked or kept wet, as the maker says.
- **The angle:** about 15–20° for most kitchen knives, the angle of the existing edge, held constant. Two coins under
  the spine help at first.
- **The burr:** grind one side until a fine burr can be felt along the whole edge on the other side, then the other
  side, then lighter strokes, alternating, to remove it.
- **Stropping** on leather, newspaper or the fine stone finishes the edge.
- **Flattening:** a dished stone gives a rounded edge, so the stone is flattened now and then.

**Sessions:**
1. Coarse to fine (75 min): sharpen an inexpensive knife first, then the chef's knife, through the grits, feeling for
   the burr.
2. Film the angle (60 min): sharpen again, filmed from the side, and compare the angle with the reference.

**Cues:** a burr along the whole length; a constant angle on the video; the paper test clean afterwards.

**Faults:**
- A rocking angle.
- Stopping before a burr has formed.
- Pressing hard on the fine stone.
- A dry stone.
- Grinding the tip round.

#### KNF-2.M3.L2 — Judging and logging the edge

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 wk · builds on `KNF-2.M3.L1` · moves KM-EQPT→3, JD-REFL→3*

*Objectives:* [S] paper and tomato tests; [J] decide when to hone or sharpen; keep a log
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Tests:**
  - **paper:** a sharp edge slices hanging paper cleanly, without catching;
  - **tomato:** a sharp edge bites the skin with no pressure;
  - **thumbnail (with care):** a sharp edge catches; a dull one slides.
- **Honing or sharpening?** Hone when the edge has rolled (honing restores it); sharpen when it no longer restores
  (`EQP-1.M3.L2`).
- **The edge log:**
  - the date;
  - the test result;
  - the action (honed or sharpened);
  - a self-score.

  Kept weekly for four weeks, with a photo of the paper test that AI review scores, so the learner's judgment is
  checked against an outside view.

**Sessions:**
1. Weekly check (four times, 15 min each, logged as one session): the tests, the decision and the log entry with the
   photo.

**Cues:** the learner's score and the AI band agree; the edge stays sharp through the four weeks.

**Faults:**
- Sharpening every week whether needed or not.
- Never sharpening because honing "still works".
- Scoring the edge without testing it.
