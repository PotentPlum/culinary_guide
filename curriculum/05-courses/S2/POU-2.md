## Introduction

Chicken is the protein most home cooks cook most often, and the one they most often cook badly:
- breasts dry and stringy;
- legs underdone at the bone;
- a roasting tin of pale skin.

It is also the protein with the strictest hygiene. Raw poultry often carries *Campylobacter* and *Salmonella*, and the
spread from board, hands and tap matters as much as the core temperature.

`POU-2` treats the bird as a whole:
- **buying it** by label and use;
- **handling it** cleanly;
- **breaking it down** into parts;
- **cooking it three ways:** roasted, sautéed or pan-roasted, and poached;
- **using every part:** carcass, fat and leftovers.

It ends with a lab that sets the learner's eye and fingers against the thermometer, and puts brining to a blind test.

**Doneness** is the thread. Every piece is probed. The safe figure for poultry is 75 °C (assessment §9), reached after
resting, and the learner learns to pull a breast early enough that carryover takes it there without overshooting. Legs
are cooked further, because their connective tissue needs it.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-PRO` reaches level 3 with the poultry part; `MEA-2` and `FSH-2` add meat and fish.
- `TS-FAB` level 2 gets its chicken part here; the fish follows in `FSH-2`.
- `SA-HYG` level 4 is completed with the raw-chicken video.
- The prediction log of `POU-2.M6` gives `JD-CUES` its ten entries. `MS-S2` re-tests doneness and safety.

**Pacing.** About four weeks. The roast and the whole-bird project are weekend cooking; sautés and breasts fit weeknights.
Each bird bought is used completely, so the course spends little on food the household would not eat.

#### POU-2.M1.L1 — Types, labels and buying

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves IN-MEA→2, KM-PURC→2*

*Objectives:* [K] chicken, duck, turkey, guinea fowl; welfare and quality labels; [J] choose by use and budget (Good/Best)
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The birds:**
  - **chicken:** from a fast-growing broiler of about six weeks to a slow-growing bird of two to three months, with
    firmer, more flavourful meat;
  - **duck:** dark, fatty meat, with a thick fat layer under the skin;
  - **turkey:** large and lean, mostly bought as parts in the Netherlands;
  - **guinea fowl (*parelhoen*):** between chicken and game.
- **The parts and their method:**

  | Part | Meat | Method |
  |---|---|---|
  | Breast | Lean, quick to overcook | Sauté, pan-roast, poach |
  | Thigh | Dark, more fat and connective tissue | Sauté, braise, roast |
  | Drumstick | Dark, sinewy | Roast, braise |
  | Wing | Skin and gelatin | Roast, fry, stock |
  | Carcass and neck | Bone, gelatin | Stock |

- **Labels in Dutch shops:**
  - ***Beter Leven*** stars (1 to 3) for animal welfare;
  - **organic (EU leaf, *biologisch*);**
  - **free range (*scharrel*, *vrije uitloop*);**
  - **"slow-growing"** breeds;
  - **the use-by date (*te gebruiken tot*)** on fresh poultry: a safety date, not a quality one.
- **Choosing by use and budget:**
  - **Good:** a whole standard chicken; it is cheaper per kilo than parts, and the carcass makes stock.
  - **Best:** a slow-growing or free-range bird of 1.5–2 kg, from a poulterer.

  For a quick weeknight sauté, thighs give more flavour per euro than breasts.

**Sessions:**
1. A shop visit (45 min): compare whole birds and parts on label, weight and price per kilo; buy the bird for
   `POU-2.M2`.

**Cues:** the learner can say what each label guarantees and what it doesn't, and works out the price per kilo of
usable meat.

**Faults:**
- Buying breasts for a dish that wants thighs.
- Reading "*scharrel*" as organic.
- Using a bird past its use-by date because it smells fine.

#### POU-2.M1.L2 — Handling raw poultry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m1-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M2.L2` · moves SA-HYG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] no rinsing; a separate board; clean-down; [K] *Campylobacter* and *Salmonella* risks
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The risks:** raw poultry often carries *Campylobacter* and *Salmonella*. Cooking to 75 °C (assessment §9) kills
  them in the meat. The danger at home is spreading them, by hands, boards, knives, taps, towels and splashes, to food
  that will not be cooked.
- **No rinsing.** Washing a chicken under the tap does not make it safer. It splashes bacteria over the sink and the
  worktop, up to tens of centimetres away. Pat it dry with kitchen paper and throw the paper away.
- **The station:**
  - a separate board for raw protein (`SAF-1`);
  - a tray or plate for the raw meat and one for the cooked;
  - the bin and the soap within reach;
  - ready-to-eat food finished and put away first, so the raw protein comes last in the work order (`KNF-2.M2`).
- **During the work:**
  - wash hands after touching raw poultry and before touching anything else, such as taps, spice jars or the phone;
  - use one hand for raw meat and the other for the salt, or pre-portion the salt.
- **Clean-down:** remove, wash, rinse, disinfect, dry (`SAF-1.M2.L3`). The board and knife go into a hot dishwasher
  cycle or are washed in hot water with detergent; the worktop is cleaned and disinfected.
- **Storage:** in the fridge at 4 °C, on the bottom shelf, in a closed container, so it can't drip onto other food.

**Sessions:**
1. A dry run (30 min): set up the station, handle a chicken (pat dry, season, into the tin), clean down; the household
   watches with the hygiene checklist and marks each contact point.

**Cues:**
- Hands washed after each touch of raw meat.
- No raw-contact item touches ready-to-eat food.
- A clean station at the end, with nothing left on the tap or handles.

**Faults:**
- Rinsing the bird.
- Touching the salt jar or the phone with raw hands.
- Using the same tray for raw and cooked chicken.
- Wiping the board with the dishcloth used for everything else.

#### POU-2.M2.L1 — Eight pieces

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-FAB→2*

*Objectives:* [S] legs, thighs, drumsticks, breasts, wings; follow the joints; keep the carcass
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Follow the joints, not the bone.** A knife goes through a joint without force. Where it meets bone, the cut is in
  the wrong place.
- **The sequence:**
  1. The legs: cut the skin between leg and body, pop the hip joint, and cut around the oyster so it stays on the
     thigh.
  2. Each leg into drumstick and thigh, at the joint marked by the line of fat.
  3. The wings, at the shoulder joint; the tips are kept for stock.
  4. The breasts: off the bone along the breastbone, or on the bone, cut through the ribs with the heel of the knife
     or shears.
- **The result:** eight pieces (two drumsticks, two thighs, two wings, two breasts), with the carcass, the wing tips and
  the trim frozen for stock (`STK-2`).
- **Yield:** weigh the bird, the pieces and the carcass. The pieces are usually a little over two thirds of the bird's
  weight; a lot of meat left on the carcass shows in the numbers.
- **The station** is set up as in `POU-2.M1.L2`, and the knife is the chef's or the boning knife (`KNF-2.M2.L1`).

**Sessions:**
1. A first breakdown with the video (60 min), paused at each step.
2. A second breakdown (45 min) without pausing; weigh the pieces and the carcass.

**Cues:**
- The knife finds the joints.
- The oyster stays on the thigh.
- The breastbone is scraped clean.
- The pieces are of even size, with the skin intact.

**Faults:**
- Sawing through bone.
- Leaving the oyster on the carcass.
- Torn skin on the breasts.
- A blunt knife, which makes the work slow and unsafe.

#### POU-2.M2.L2 — Spatchcock and boned thigh

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `POU-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-FAB→2*

*Objectives:* [S] spatchcock a chicken; bone a thigh
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Spatchcocking:**
  1. Cut out the backbone with shears or a heavy knife.
  2. Turn the bird over and press it flat.

  It roasts or grills evenly and fast: the legs are exposed to more heat and the breast less. The backbone goes into
  the stock bag.
- **Boning a thigh:** cut along the bone on the inside, scrape the meat free around it, and cut under the knuckle. A
  boned thigh cooks quickly and evenly, for a sauté, a stuffing or the grill.

**Sessions:**
1. Spatchcock a chicken and bone two thighs (50 min); roast the spatchcocked bird for dinner, and freeze or cook the
   thighs.

**Cues:**
- The bird lies flat.
- The thigh bone comes out clean, with the meat in one piece.

**Faults:**
- Cutting through the breast in place of along the backbone.
- Leaving cartilage in the thigh.
- Piercing the skin.

#### POU-2.M3.L1 — Roasting a whole chicken

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m3-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 1.5 h · builds on `HEA-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-PRO→3, IN-MEA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] dry, season, truss or not, roast, rest; [K] breast versus leg temperatures
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The problem of the whole bird:** the breast is done at 75 °C and dries out quickly above it. The legs are better
  well above that: around 80–85 °C, where their connective tissue softens. Everything in roasting a chicken is about
  closing that gap:
  - **dry skin:** pat dry, or dry-brine uncovered in the fridge overnight (`SCI-2.M2.L2`), for crisp skin and seasoned
    meat;
  - **trussing or not:** trussing gives a neat shape, but it shields the thighs and slows them down. An untrussed bird,
    or one with the legs loosely tied, cooks more evenly;
  - **starting position and heat:** a hot oven, about 200–220 °C; the legs towards the back of the oven, where it is
    hottest; a spatchcocked bird (`POU-2.M2.L2`) evens it out further.
- **Probing:**
  - the breast at its thickest part;
  - the thigh near the bone, without touching it.

  The breast comes out a few degrees below 75 °C. During the rest it must reach 75 °C or more (assessment §9),
  confirmed by the probe, and the legs must be well above that.
- **Resting:** 15–20 minutes, loosely covered (`HEA-2.M2.L2`). The juices of the tin go into the gravy.
- **Carving:** legs off first, then the breasts off the bone in one piece and sliced across.

**Sessions:**
1. A first roast (90 min plus resting), with the probe log: breast and thigh every 10 minutes in the last half hour,
   and after resting.
2. A second roast (90 min), spatchcocked or dry-brined, compared with the first.

**Cues:**
- Skin browned and crisp.
- The juices from the thigh run clear.
- The leg moves loosely in its joint.
- The probe reading after the rest at or above the figure.

**Faults:**
- Pulling the bird on the breast reading alone, with the thighs underdone.
- Roasting until the breast reads 80 °C or more.
- A wet bird going into the oven, so the skin turns pale and soft.
- Carving without resting.

#### POU-2.M3.L2 — Gravy from the pan

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m3-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAU-2.M2.L1`, `SAU-2.M3.L3` · moves TS-SAU→3*

*Objectives:* [S] deglaze the roasting pan, thicken, strain, season
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The tin is the fond:** the browned juices and the fat of the roast.
- **The sequence**, applying `SAU-2.M2.L1` and `SAU-2.M3.L3`:
  1. Pour the juices into a jug and let the fat rise; spoon it off and keep it.
  2. Deglaze the tin on the hob with wine or water, scraping.
  3. Add stock and the defatted juices.
  4. Reduce.
  5. Thicken lightly: a little of the fat cooked with flour as a roux, a slurry or *beurre manié*.
  6. Strain, and season at the end.
- **The consistency:** a pouring gravy is thinner than a *nappe* sauce; it coats the spoon lightly and runs.

**Sessions:**
1. A gravy from the first roast (30 min): defat, deglaze, thicken, strain; photograph it on a spoon.

**Cues:**
- A glossy, brown gravy without a layer of fat.
- It tastes of the roast, not of flour.

**Faults:**
- A greasy gravy because it was not defatted.
- Lumps from dry flour.
- A burnt tin deglazed, so the gravy tastes bitter.
- Salting before reducing.

#### POU-2.M3.L3 — A roast dinner timeline

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m3-l3 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `WRK-1.M2.L3` · moves KM-PLAN→3*

*Objectives:* [S] a backwards timeline for a roast with two sides and gravy
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The backwards timeline** (`WRK-1.M2.L3`) starts from the time the meal is served and works back through:
  - resting (20 min), roasting and preheating;
  - the sides, which have their own times;
  - the gravy, made during the rest.
- **The oven is the bottleneck.** The roast and a tray of vegetables share it, or the vegetables cook on the hob or
  roast during the rest at a higher heat.
- **Parallel tasks** are the point of level 3: the gravy, a hob side and the carving happen while something else is
  cooking.
- **The plan against the actual:** each step gets a planned and an actual time in the log, and every component should
  reach the table within 5 minutes of the plan.

#### POU-2.M4.L1 — Pan-roasting a breast

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-PRO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] even thickness, sear, finish in the oven, rest; [J] pull at the right temperature
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Even thickness first:** a breast is thick at one end and thin at the other. Flatten the thick end gently between
  two sheets of baking paper, or cut it horizontally into two thinner pieces.
- **Pan-roasting:**
  1. Season, and dry-brine if there's time.
  2. Sear skin side down in an ovenproof pan until golden.
  3. Turn the breast.
  4. Finish in an oven at about 200 °C.

  The sear gives colour and the oven cooks it evenly.
- **Pulling at the right temperature:** carryover in a breast is small but real, a few degrees.
  - **When to pull:** a few degrees below 75 °C.
  - **The rest:** 5 minutes.
  - **The check:** probe again after the rest. The reading must be 75 °C or more (assessment §9).

  The learner writes the pull temperature they choose and the reading after the rest, and adjusts next time.
- **Skin-on or skinless:** skin protects the meat and crisps. A skinless breast needs a gentler finish.

**Sessions:**
1. Two breasts (45 min): one flattened and one not, pan-roasted together; both probed and cut; compare the
   cross-sections.
2. A pull-temperature trial (45 min): two breasts pulled at two chosen temperatures below 75 °C; rest, probe again and
   taste.

**Cues:**
- An even, golden skin.
- The meat is moist and opaque to the centre, with juices that run clear.
- The probe after the rest at or above the figure.

**Faults:**
- A thick end raw while the thin end dries.
- Pulling too early, so the rest doesn't reach the safe figure.
- Cutting straight away, so the juices run out.
- Pale skin from a cold pan.

#### POU-2.M5.L1 — Poached poultry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L4` · moves TS-MOI→4*

*Objectives:* [S] poach a whole chicken or legs; keep the broth; [J] check doneness
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A delicate poach:** poultry in liquid held at about 70–85 °C, below a simmer, with the surface barely trembling
  (`HEA-1.M5.L4`). A gentle poach keeps the breast tender; a boil tightens the meat and clouds the broth.
- **The liquid:** water or light stock with aromatics (`STK-2.M1`). The bird or the legs go in cold or into hot liquid:
  - **starting cold** gives a clearer broth and even cooking of a whole bird;
  - **starting hot** is faster for parts.
- **Checking doneness:**
  - probe the thickest part of the thigh and of the breast: 75 °C or more (assessment §9);
  - the juices run clear;
  - the leg moves loosely.

  For a whole bird the thigh decides.
- **Keep the broth:** it is a light stock for soups and risottos. It is cooled within the limits of assessment §9 and
  frozen (`STK-2.M2.L5`).
- **Why poach:** for *suprême* sauce (`SAU-2.M3.X2`), salads, soups and sandwiches. The meat stays moist for cold
  dishes.

**Sessions:**
1. A poached chicken or legs (90 min): the liquid temperature logged every 15 minutes and held at 70–85 °C; the core
   probed; the broth strained and cooled.

**Cues:**
- A surface that trembles but doesn't bubble.
- Moist, tender meat.
- A clear broth.

**Faults:**
- Boiling the liquid.
- Taking the bird out on the breast reading alone.
- Leaving the broth to cool overnight on the stove.

**Concepts:**

- **Dry-brining** (`SCI-2.M2.L2`): salt at about 1–1.5 % of the weight, rested uncovered in the fridge for about a day.

- **Reading the result:** one trial is a hint, not a proof (`SCI-2.M1.Q6`). The conclusion says how sure it is.

**Sessions:**
1. The comparison (60 min, plus a day of brining): two pairs of chicken legs or breasts, one pair dry-brined, all
   roasted together to the same pull temperature; weighed, probed, and tasted blind.

**Cues:**
- A template filled in before cooking.
- Pieces matched in weight within about 10 %.
- Plates coded by someone else.

**Faults:**
- Changing two things at once (for example brined and a hotter oven).
- Unmatched pieces.
- Tasters who know which is which.
- A conclusion stronger than one trial supports.

#### POU-2.M6.L2 — Doneness by cue and thermometer

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pou-2-m6-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `POU-2.M4.L1` · moves JD-CUES→3, SA-TEMP→4*

*Objectives:* [J] predict doneness by touch, juices and look, then check with the thermometer
<!-- END generated:lesson-pou-2-m6-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The cues for poultry:**
  - **touch:** a raw breast is soft, and a done one springs back firmly;
  - **juices:** pink and cloudy when raw, clear when done;
  - **look:** the flesh opaque at the thickest point;
  - **legs:** they move loosely, and the meat pulls away from the bone at the drumstick.
- **The cue-then-reading entry:**
  1. Predict the core temperature from the cues.
  2. Write the prediction down.
  3. Probe, and log the difference.

  Ten entries, with at least eight within ±3 °C, show the learner's cues are calibrated (`JD-CUES` level 3).
- **The thermometer still decides.** A cue can say "close", but the safe figure is confirmed by the probe: 75 °C for
  poultry (assessment §9).
- **Cooling and reheating leftovers** from the lab, with the figures of §9:
  - cooled from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours, and to 5 °C or below within 6 hours in total, in shallow containers;
  - reheated to 74 °C for the next day's meal.

**Sessions:**
1. Predict and probe (60 min): during a sauté or pan-roast of several pieces, predict and log at least four readings;
   cool the leftover pieces with the cooling logged.

**Cues:** predictions that close in on the probe reading as the entries accumulate.

**Faults:**
- Probing before writing the prediction.
- Pressing the meat with a fork rather than a finger.
- Trusting clear juices alone in a thick piece.
