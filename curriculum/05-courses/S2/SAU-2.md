## Introduction

A sauce turns cooked food into a dish. It brings moisture, richness, acidity or contrast, and it carries the flavour
of the pan, the stock or the vegetable into every bite. Sauces have a reputation for being difficult, but most of that
difficulty comes down to a few physical rules:
- how far to reduce;
- how starch thickens;
- how fat stays suspended in water.

This course teaches those rules through the five sauce elements of `TS-SAU`:
- **reductions and pan sauces:** deglazing the fond of a sear, reducing, and mounting with cold butter;
- **starch-thickened sauces:** roux, *béchamel*, *velouté*, slurries and *beurre manié*;
- **cold emulsions:** mayonnaise and its derivatives, by hand and by machine;
- **warm emulsions:** *sabayon*, *hollandaise* and *beurre blanc*;
- **vinaigrettes:** ratio, emulsified or broken, matched to the leaves.

It ends with a sauce clinic in which the learner diagnoses and repairs faults they did not cause and did not see coming.

**Consistency** is the thread through the course. Every sauce is judged on a spoon: it coats the back as a thin, even
layer (*nappe*), and a line drawn through it with a finger stays clean. The learner photographs that line for each
element, so the AI review and the learner judge the same thing.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-SAU` reaches level 3 (each element from a framework).
- Choosing the sauce for a dish and repairing under pressure (level 4) come in `S3`, and service standard in `CLS-4`.
- The emulsion science of `SCI-2.M4` is applied here.
- The classical system of `CK-CLAS` is introduced as a map, not a syllabus. `CLS-4` returns to it in depth.
- `POU-2`, `MEA-2` and `FSH-2` finish their proteins with these sauces, and `MS-S2` asks for a pan or emulsion sauce
  on its plate.

**Pacing.** About five weeks. Most sessions fit a weeknight, because the sauces sit on meals the household eats anyway:
- a pan sauce on a weeknight sear;
- a *béchamel* in a gratin;
- mayonnaise with boiled potatoes;
- *hollandaise* on a weekend brunch.

The freezer of stock from `STK-2` is the course's main ingredient.

#### SAU-2.M1.L1 — What a sauce does

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `STK-2.M2.L1` · moves CK-CLAS→2, CK-TERM→3*

*Objectives:* [K] flavour, moisture, contrast, richness; families (reductions, starch, emulsions, purées, vinaigrettes); the five mother sauces as a map [S11]
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **What a sauce adds to a dish:**
  - flavour, often concentrated;
  - moisture, for lean or dry food;
  - contrast, such as acid against fat or a cold sauce on warm fish;
  - richness, a little fat that carries aroma.
- **The families, by what holds them together:**
  - **reductions:** water evaporated until the gelatin and dissolved solids give body;
  - **starch-thickened:** gelatinised starch from a roux, a slurry or *beurre manié* (`SCI-2.M3.L1`);
  - **emulsions:** fat suspended in water, or water in fat, held by an emulsifier (`SCI-2.M4.L2`);
  - **purées:** vegetable or fruit fibre and starch;
  - **vinaigrettes:** a temporary emulsion of oil and acid.
- **The five mother sauces** (*sauces mères*) as a map of the classical system:

  | Mother sauce | Liquid | Thickener | Derivatives |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | *Béchamel* | Milk | White roux | *Mornay*, *soubise*, cream sauce |
  | *Velouté* | White stock | Blond roux | *Suprême*, *allemande*, Bercy |
  | *Espagnole* | Brown stock | Brown roux and tomato | *Demi-glace*, *bordelaise*, Robert |
  | Tomato | Tomato and stock | Reduction (classically a roux) | Provençale, Creole, Portuguese |
  | *Hollandaise* | Butter | Egg yolk emulsion | *Béarnaise*, *mousseline*, *maltaise* |

  The table is the point of the lesson: a derivative is its mother sauce plus a flavouring or a finish. *Espagnole*
  and *demi-glace* are named here and cooked in `CLS-4`.
- **Terms** in French, Dutch and English go into the glossary, and the learner uses them from now on:
  - *nappe*, *monter au beurre*, *roux*;
  - *liaison*, *beurre manié*, *réduction*;
  - *jus*, *sabayon*, *émulsion*.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h). The learner builds the family table in their own words and names the family of five
sauces from their own cooking.

**Cues:** the learner can say, for any sauce on a plate, what holds it together and which family it belongs to.

**Faults:**
- Treating the mother sauces as recipes to learn rather than as a map.
- Confusing a reduction with a starch-thickened sauce (a *jus* with flour in it).
- Calling every creamy sauce an emulsion.

#### SAU-2.M2.L1 — Deglazing and pan sauces

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L3`, `STK-2.M2.L5` · moves TS-SAU→2*

*Objectives:* [S] deglaze the fond, add stock, reduce, adjust; [J] judge consistency by eye and spoon
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The fond:** the browned juices stuck to the pan after a sear (`HEA-1.M3.L3`). Brown is flavour; black is bitter and
  is wiped out, not deglazed.
- **The pan sauce sequence:**
  1. Pour off most of the fat.
  2. Sweat a shallot.
  3. Deglaze with wine, vinegar, cider or water, scraping the fond loose.
  4. Reduce almost dry.
  5. Add stock (`STK-2.M2.L5`).
  6. Reduce to the consistency wanted.
  7. Add the resting juices of the meat.
  8. Season, and finish with butter, cream or herbs.
- **Judging the consistency** by eye and spoon:
  - bubbles turn from small and fast to larger and slower as it thickens;
  - the sauce coats the spoon;
  - the line through it stays clean.

  Salt goes in at the end, because reduction concentrates it.
- **Gelatin does the work.** A stock that gelled in the fridge gives a pan sauce body without flour. A thin, unset
  stock never reaches *nappe* by reduction alone without becoming too salty or too strong.
- **Proportions:** about 100–150 ml of stock per two portions, reduced by roughly half.

**Sessions:**
1. A pan sauce after a weeknight sear (30 min): chicken thigh or pork chop; deglaze with water and stock; reduce to
   *nappe*.
2. The same sauce with wine or cider (30 min): compare the body, colour and acidity of the two.

**Cues:**
- Large, slow bubbles as the sauce approaches *nappe*.
- A clean line through the sauce on the spoon.
- A glossy surface.

**Faults:**
- Deglazing a burnt fond.
- Adding salt before reducing.
- Reducing a thin stock until it is too strong to eat.
- Leaving the alcohol raw by adding stock too soon.

#### SAU-2.M2.L2 — Reductions and *monter au beurre*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M2.L1`, `SCI-2.M4.L2` · moves TS-SAU→3, SC-EMUL→3*

*Objectives:* [S] reduce to *nappe*; mount with cold butter; [K] why it emulsifies and why it breaks
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Reducing to *nappe*:** a stock reduced by about two thirds coats a spoon. Reduced further, it becomes a *glace* that
  sets firm when cold (`STK-2.M2.L5`).
- **Mounting with butter (*monter au beurre*)** gives a reduction body and gloss:
  1. Take the reduction off the boil.
  2. Swirl or whisk in cold butter, a few cubes at a time.

  The butter melts into tiny droplets of fat that the reduction's gelatin and the milk solids of the butter hold in
  suspension. This is a warm butter emulsion (`SCI-2.M4.L2`).
- **Why it breaks:** above about a simmer, the emulsion separates into a greasy layer on a thin liquid:
  - the fat droplets merge;
  - the milk proteins tighten.

  Too much butter for the liquid does the same.
- **The repair:**
  1. Take the pan off the heat.
  2. Add a spoon of cold water or stock.
  3. Whisk hard, or whisk the broken sauce slowly into a fresh spoon of reduction.
- **Serve soon:** a mounted sauce is made at the last moment and held warm, never boiled again.

**Sessions:**
1. Mount and hold (40 min): reduce stock to *nappe*; mount half with cold butter; compare the two halves on a spoon
   and on the plate.
2. Break and repair (40 min): mount a sauce, then boil it until it breaks; photograph it; repair it; photograph the
   result.

**Cues:**
- The sauce turns glossy and slightly paler as the butter goes in.
- The broken sauce shows oil beads on the surface and runs off the spoon.
- The repaired sauce coats the spoon again.

**Faults:**
- Melted butter in place of cold.
- Mounting over full heat.
- Too much butter for too little reduction.
- Reheating a mounted sauce to a boil.

#### SAU-2.M3.L1 — Roux

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-SAU→2, RD-RATO→2*

*Objectives:* [S] white, blond and brown roux; [K] ratios of roux to liquid for thin, medium and thick
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A roux** is equal weights of fat (usually butter) and flour, cooked together. The fat coats the starch granules so
  they disperse in the liquid without clumping, and they gelatinise as the sauce comes to the boil (`SCI-2.M3.L1`).
- **Three colours**, by cooking time over moderate heat:

  | Roux | Cooking | Use | Thickening power |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | White | 2–3 min, no colour, smells of biscuit rather than raw flour | *Béchamel* | Highest |
  | Blond | 5–8 min, pale straw | *Velouté* | Slightly lower |
  | Brown | 15–30 min, hazelnut to brown | Brown sauces, gumbo | Clearly lower: browning breaks down some starch |

- **Ratios per litre of liquid** (white roux, equal weights of butter and flour):

  | Thickness | Roux per litre | Use |
  |---|---|---|
  | Thin | About 60 g (30 g butter, 30 g flour) | Soups, a pouring sauce |
  | Medium | About 100 g (50 g, 50 g) | A coating sauce at *nappe* |
  | Thick | About 160 g (80 g, 80 g) | A binding sauce: croquettes, a soufflé base |

  A brown roux needs more for the same thickness.
- **Avoiding lumps:** add the liquid to the roux gradually, whisking. One of the two is hot and the other cool or warm:
  - a hot roux with cool liquid;
  - or a cooled roux with hot liquid.

  Both hot causes lumps. The sauce reaches full thickness only at the boil. It then simmers for several minutes, which
  cooks out the raw-flour taste.

**Sessions:**
1. Three roux (40 min): white, blond and brown from the same weights; photograph them side by side; thicken 250 ml of
   stock with each at the medium ratio and compare.

**Cues:**
- The raw-flour smell turns to biscuit, then to hazelnut.
- The sauce thickens sharply as it reaches the boil.

**Faults:**
- A burnt roux with black specks; it is thrown away, not used.
- Adding all the liquid at once.
- Judging the thickness before the boil.
- Not cooking the sauce long enough, so it tastes of raw flour.

#### SAU-2.M3.L2 — *Béchamel* and derivatives

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-SAU→3, CK-CLAS→2*

*Objectives:* [S] a smooth *béchamel*; *Mornay* and others; [J] adjust thickness
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A *béchamel*:** milk, often infused with onion, bay and a clove, thickened with a white roux at the ratio for its
  use, simmered and seasoned with salt, white pepper and nutmeg.
- **Derivatives**, made from a framework:
  - ***Mornay*:** grated cheese off the heat, for gratins;
  - ***soubise*:** sweated, puréed onion;
  - **cream sauce:** finished with cream;
  - **parsley sauce:** chopped parsley.
- **Adjusting the thickness:**
  - too thick: whisk in hot milk;
  - too thin: simmer it down, or whisk in a little *beurre manié* (`SAU-2.M3.L3`).
- **Keeping it smooth:**
  - stir to the corners of the pan, where it catches;
  - strain through a fine sieve if lumps form;
  - cover the surface with buttered paper or film against a skin.
- **Cheese** goes in off the boil, because boiling makes it stringy and greasy.

**Sessions:**
1. A *béchamel* at the medium ratio (40 min), infused, simmered, adjusted to *nappe* and photographed on a spoon.
2. A *Mornay* for a weeknight gratin (45 min) with seasonal vegetables: cauliflower, leek, chicory or celeriac.

**Cues:**
- A smooth, glossy sauce that coats the spoon.
- No taste of raw flour.
- The cheese melted in without strings.

**Faults:**
- Lumps from adding the milk too fast.
- A sauce that catches on the bottom.
- A skin on the surface.
- A gratin that turns watery because the vegetables were not drained.

#### SAU-2.M3.L3 — *Velouté*, slurries and *beurre manié*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m3-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M3.L1`, `STK-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-SAU→3*

*Objectives:* [S] a stock-based *velouté*; quick thickening with a slurry or *beurre manié*
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A *velouté*:** white stock thickened with a blond roux, simmered and skimmed until silky. Its quality is the
  quality of the stock (`STK-2.M2.L2`).
- **A *suprême*:** a chicken *velouté* finished with cream. It is the natural sauce for poached chicken.
- **Quick thickeners** for a sauce that is already made:

  | Thickener | How | Result |
  |---|---|---|
  | Slurry | Cornflour or potato starch mixed with twice its volume of cold liquid, whisked into a simmering sauce | Thickens at the boil; glossy and clear (cornflour slightly cloudy); thins again if boiled long or stirred hard |
  | *Beurre manié* | Equal weights of soft butter and flour kneaded together, whisked in small pieces into the simmering sauce | Adds body and gloss; needs a few minutes of simmering to cook the flour |
  | *Liaison* | Egg yolk and cream, tempered with hot sauce and stirred in off the boil | Richness and a light body; curdles if boiled |

- **How much:** start small, about a teaspoon of starch for 250 ml, because a slurry or *beurre manié* thickens fully
  only at the boil.

**Sessions:**
1. A chicken *velouté* (60 min) from frozen white stock, at the medium ratio, skimmed and finished with cream as a
   *suprême*.
2. Three rescues (30 min): thicken three cups of the same thin stock with a slurry, *beurre manié* and a *liaison*;
   compare the gloss, body and taste.

**Cues:**
- The *velouté* tastes of the stock, not of flour.
- The slurry sauce turns clear and glossy at the boil.
- The *liaison* thickens without curdling.

**Faults:**
- Starch added dry, so it forms lumps.
- A *liaison* boiled after it goes in.
- A slurry sauce boiled hard for a long time, so it thins again.
- A *velouté* from a weak stock.

#### SAU-2.M4.L1 — Mayonnaise

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SCI-2.M4.L2` · moves TS-SAU→3, SC-EMUL→3*

*Objectives:* [S] by hand and by blender; [K] oil ratio, temperature, rate; [S] rescue a broken mayonnaise
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Mayonnaise is oil in water:** tiny droplets of oil suspended in the water of the yolk and the acid, held apart by
  the yolk's lecithin and proteins (`SCI-2.M4.L2`).
- **The three variables:**
  - **ratio:** one yolk holds about 150–200 ml of oil comfortably in a home batch. More is possible, but the sauce
    becomes stiff and needs a little water.
  - **temperature:** yolk and oil at room temperature. Cold oil and cold yolks emulsify poorly.
  - **rate:** the oil goes in drop by drop until the emulsion forms, then in a thin stream. Too much oil too early
    floods the emulsifier.
- **By hand:** a bowl on a damp cloth, a whisk, and the oil from a jug with a spout.
- **By blender:** a whole pasteurised egg (from a carton, about 50 g) in a narrow beaker with a stick blender, the oil
  on top; the blender drawn up slowly. It is faster and more forgiving, and gives a lighter, paler sauce.
- **Rescuing a broken mayonnaise:** start again with a fresh yolk, or with a teaspoon of mustard or water in a clean
  bowl, then whisk the broken sauce in slowly, as if it were the oil.
- **Seasoning:** salt, acid (vinegar or lemon) and mustard. A thick mayonnaise is thinned with a few drops of water.

**Sessions:**
1. By hand (40 min): one yolk, about 175 ml of oil; photograph the stages: first drops, thickening, finished.
2. By blender and rescue (40 min): a blender mayonnaise; then a hand mayonnaise broken on purpose by adding the oil too
   fast, rescued, and photographed at each step.

**Cues:**
- The sauce turns from translucent to opaque and thick in the first minutes.
- It holds a ribbon on the whisk.
- A broken sauce turns thin and oily, with a curdled look.

**Faults:**
- Cold ingredients straight from the fridge.
- Pouring the oil too fast at the start.
- Strong extra-virgin olive oil as the only oil, which turns bitter when whisked hard.
- Giving up on a broken sauce instead of rescuing it.

#### SAU-2.M4.L2 — Derivatives and safety

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAU-2.M4.L1`, `SAF-1.M1.L1` · moves TS-SAU→3*

*Objectives:* [S] *aïoli*, *rémoulade*, tartare; [K] raw-yolk safety and storage
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Derivatives**, made from a framework:
  - ***aïoli*:** garlic pounded to a paste, with olive oil;
  - ***rémoulade*:** mustard, gherkins, capers and herbs;
  - **tartare sauce:** gherkins, capers, shallot and herbs, sometimes chopped boiled egg;
  - **herb mayonnaise:** chopped soft herbs, or a green purée.
- **Raw-yolk safety:**
  - **Raw eggs can carry *Salmonella*.** A raw-yolk sauce is made with pasteurised egg yolk, for every guest
    (assessment §9). This matters most for people in risk groups: pregnant people, young children, older people and
    anyone with weakened immunity.
  - **Eggs:** fresh, clean and uncracked, from the fridge; shells kept away from the finished sauce.
  - **Storage:** a home-made mayonnaise goes into the fridge at 4 °C, covered, and is used within 24 hours, also when it
    is made with pasteurised yolk (the raw-egg row, assessment §9 [S44]). It is out of the fridge for no longer than
    2 hours in total.

    temperature.
- **Labelling:** the date on the jar (`SAF-1`).

**Sessions:**
1. Three derivatives (45 min) from one batch of mayonnaise: an *aïoli*, a *rémoulade* and a herb mayonnaise, served with
   boiled potatoes and seasonal vegetables. The leftover is labelled and chilled.

**Cues:**
- Each derivative is recognisable by its flavour.
- The jar is labelled and in the fridge before the meal ends.

**Faults:**
- Watery additions, such as wet herbs or undrained gherkins, that thin the sauce.
- A raw-yolk sauce made with unpasteurised yolk.
- A bowl of mayonnaise left on the table all evening.
- Garlic oil stored at room temperature.

#### SAU-2.M5.L1 — *Sabayon* and *hollandaise*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m5-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M4.L1`, `EGG-1.M4.L1` · moves TS-SAU→3, TS-EGG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] cook yolks over a bain-marie to the ribbon stage; add butter; [J] save it from overheating
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A *sabayon*:** egg yolks whisked with a little liquid (water, a reduction or wine) over a bain-marie until they
  thicken to a pale foam.
  - **The ribbon stage:** a trail from the whisk holds on the surface for a few seconds.
  - **The heat:** with water and acid in the mix, the yolks thicken at a higher temperature than a plain yolk
    (`EGG-1.M2.L1`), and they scramble if the bowl gets too hot.
  - **The bowl:** it sits over barely simmering water without touching it, and it comes off the heat whenever the edges
    start to set.
- ***Hollandaise*:**
  - a *sabayon* of yolks and a reduction of vinegar, shallot and peppercorns, or of water and lemon;
  - warm clarified or melted butter whisked in slowly, roughly 50–75 g per yolk;
  - seasoned with salt, lemon and cayenne.

  It is a warm butter emulsion, thickened by the cooked yolk.
- **Saving it from overheating:**
  - lift the bowl off the heat;
  - whisk in a spoon of cold water;
  - if it has split, whisk it slowly into a fresh yolk with a spoon of water over gentle heat.
- **Holding it:** *hollandaise* is made for the meal. Two options:
  - in a bowl over warm water off the heat, or in a pre-warmed vacuum flask;
  - held hot at 57 °C or above (assessment §9), below the point where the yolk scrambles.

  It is not cooled and reheated, and what is left after the meal is thrown away.
- **Safety:** the yolks in a *sabayon* or *hollandaise* are only lightly cooked when served, so they are pasteurised
  egg yolk, for every guest (assessment §9, the raw-egg row).
- **Adapting:** the method scales from 1 to 6 yolks; a larger batch takes longer, and it is timed against the rest of
  the plate.

**Sessions:**
1. *Sabayon* to ribbon (40 min): two yolks with a reduction; photograph the ribbon; take the temperature at the ribbon
   with the probe and note it.
2. *Hollandaise* and rescue (50 min): a two-yolk *hollandaise*; overheat a spoonful in a small pan until it splits, and
   rescue it; hold the rest for 30 minutes and log its temperature every 10 minutes.

**Cues:**
- Volume about tripled.
- A ribbon that holds.
- A *hollandaise* that coats the spoon and shines.
- No grains of cooked yolk.

**Faults:**
- Water boiling under the bowl.
- Butter added too fast or too hot.
- Graininess from scrambled yolk.
- A sauce held on the stove until it splits.
- A sauce kept for the next day.

#### SAU-2.M5.L2 — *Beurre blanc*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m5-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-SAU→3*

*Objectives:* [S] reduce, mount with butter, hold warm; [K] why temperature control matters
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m5-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***Beurre blanc*:** a reduction of shallot, white wine and white-wine vinegar, reduced almost dry, then mounted with
  a large quantity of cold butter, whisked in piece by piece over low heat. It has no yolk. The milk solids of the
  butter and the reduction hold the emulsion (`SAU-2.M2.L2`).
- **Why temperature control matters:**
  - too hot, and the butterfat melts out of the emulsion and the sauce turns oily;
  - too cool, and the butter stops melting into it and the sauce thickens and sets.

  The working range is warm to the touch, never near a simmer.
- **A spoon of cream** in the reduction (*beurre nantais*) makes it more forgiving.

  - ***beurre rouge*:** with red wine;
  - **with herbs**, or a reduction of cider.

   minutes in a flask and check it.

**Cues:**
- A pale, creamy sauce that coats the spoon.
- The butter melts into a sauce rather than into oil.
- No separation in the flask after 20 minutes.

**Faults:**
- A reduction that is not reduced enough, so the sauce is thin and sour.
- Butter added all at once.
- Too much heat.
- Holding it next to the burner.

#### SAU-2.M6.L1 — Vinaigrettes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m6-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SCI-2.M4.L2`, `TST-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-SAU→3, RD-RATO→2*

*Objectives:* [S] the 3:1 ratio and its variants; emulsified versus broken; [J] adjust acid and sweetness to the leaves
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m6-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The ratio:** about 3 parts oil to 1 part acid by volume, then adjusted by taste.
  - **Sharper:** 2 : 1, for bitter or robust leaves such as chicory and endive, or for a potato salad that absorbs it.
  - **Softer:** 4 : 1, for delicate leaves such as butterhead lettuce or lamb's lettuce.
- **The order:**
  1. Dissolve the salt in the acid first, because salt does not dissolve in oil.
  2. Add the mustard.
  3. Add the oil.
- **Emulsified or broken:**
  - **emulsified:** with mustard, shaken or whisked hard, or blended; it clings to leaves and holds for a while;
  - **broken:** oil and acid only, or drizzled separately; it is lighter and suits delicate leaves and grilled
    vegetables.
- **Adjusting:**
  - **acid and sweetness to the leaves:** bitter leaves take a touch of honey or apple syrup; sweet leaves take more
    acid;
  - **dressing late:** just before serving, because salt and acid wilt leaves;
  - **tasting on a leaf,** not from a spoon (`TST-1.M3.L1`).
- **Acids:** wine vinegar, cider vinegar, lemon. **Oils:** rapeseed and sunflower (neutral), olive, walnut.

**Sessions:**
1. Three ratios (30 min): 2 : 1, 3 : 1 and 4 : 1 with the same oil and vinegar, each tasted on the same leaf.
2. Broken and emulsified (30 min): the same dressing shaken with and without mustard, timed until it separates, then
   tasted on a delicate and a bitter leaf.

**Cues:**
- An emulsified dressing that coats a leaf.
- A balance where neither the oil nor the acid stands out on the leaf.
- Leaves still crisp at the table.

**Faults:**
- Salt in the oil.
- Too much acid for delicate leaves.
- A salad dressed an hour before the meal.
- Tasting from the spoon.

#### SAU-2.M7.L1 — Sauce clinic

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sau-2-m7-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M5.L2` · moves JD-TRBL→3, FL-CORR→3*

*Objectives:* [J] broken, lumpy, too thin, too thick, greasy, flat, too salty: cause and fix for each family
<!-- END generated:lesson-sau-2-m7-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Diagnosis first:** look, taste and name the cause before any fix. The same symptom has different causes in
  different families. A thin sauce, for example, can be:
  - under-reduced;
  - under-thickened;
  - a broken emulsion;
  - a slurry boiled too long.
- **The clinic table**, cause and fix per family:

  | Fault | Reduction or pan sauce | Starch-thickened | Emulsion |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Broken or greasy | Over-boiled after mounting: cold liquid, whisk | Fat floating from too much roux fat: skim, whisk | Rescue with fresh yolk or water |
  | Lumpy | — | Strain, or blend and simmer | Grainy yolk: strain, rescue |
  | Too thin | Reduce, or mount with butter | Simmer down, *beurre manié*, slurry | More oil (cold) or a further ribbon (warm) |
  | Too thick | Add stock | Add hot liquid | Add water a few drops at a time |
  | Flat | Salt, acid, a reduction of wine | Salt, nutmeg, cheese | Salt, acid, mustard |
  | Too salty | Dilute with unsalted stock, add cream or butter | Add more unsalted sauce base | Make more unsalted sauce and combine |

- **Flavour faults** are fixed with the tools of `FL-CORR` (`TST-1`, `STK-2.M5.L1`):
  - acid lifts a flat sauce;
  - fat or sweetness softens a harsh one;
  - salt is corrected by dilution, never by masking it.
- **Write it up:** the fault, the cause, the fix and why it worked. These are the entries of the learner's fault log.

**Sessions:**
1. Structural faults (60 min): three sauces from the learner's own cooking, each with one structural fault the household
   introduces unseen from a fault card. The learner diagnoses, names the cause, repairs and photographs before and
   after.
2. Flavour faults (60 min): three sauces again, each with one flavour fault (flat, too acidic, too salty or too sweet).
   The learner tastes, corrects, writes up the reason, and the household tastes before and after.

**Cues:**
- The cause is named before anything is added.
- One change at a time, tasted after each.
- A repaired sauce at *nappe* and balanced.

**Faults:**
- Adding flour to a broken emulsion.
- Masking salt with sugar.
- Adding several corrections at once.
- Throwing away a sauce that could be saved.
