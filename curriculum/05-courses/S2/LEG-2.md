## Introduction

Dried beans, lentils, whole grains, milk and cheese are the cheapest and most versatile food in the kitchen. They are
also where home cooking most often falls back on tins and packets. This course gives the learner control over them:
- **legumes:** soaked or not, salted or not, cooked to creamy but whole, across several cuisines;
- **whole grains and polenta:** cooked in a batch and used through the week, safely;
- **dairy:** how milk, cream, butter and cheese behave with heat and acid; browned butter; a smooth cheese sauce; a
  fresh cheese made at home;
- **the pantry:** sensible substitutions, an allergen-free dish, and a meal from what is in the cupboard.

The thread is **prediction**. Before each comparison and substitution, the learner writes what they expect to happen to
time, texture and flavour, and then checks it. This is the start of `RD-ADPT`, the ability to adapt a recipe, which
grows through S3 and S4.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-STA` level 3 gets its pulses and grains, after the pilaf, pasta and mash of S1.
- `IN-STA` and `IN-DAI` reach level 3.
- `KM-WAST` level 3 gets its planned-leftover meals.
- The pantry meal is the first step of `CR-IMPV`, the improvisation that ends in the mystery basket of `S4`.
- The legumes across cuisines prepare `CUI-3`.

**Pacing.** About three weeks. Beans need overnight soaking and an hour or two on the hob; the fresh cheese needs a day
to drain and set. Everything else fits a weeknight. The pot of beans and the grain batch cover several meals.

#### LEG-2.M1.L1 — Legume types and soaking

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 12 h · builds on `STA-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [K] beans, chickpeas, lentils, split peas; soaking, salting, baking soda; digestibility
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The types:**
  - **beans:** white beans, borlotti, kidney beans, black beans, and Dutch brown beans (*bruine bonen*) and
    *kapucijners*;
  - **chickpeas;**
  - **lentils:** green and brown (*Puy*-style), which hold their shape, and red and yellow, which collapse;
  - **split peas:** green or yellow, which collapse into a purée (*snert*).
- **Soaking:** whole beans and chickpeas soaked for 8–12 hours in plenty of water cook faster and more evenly.
  Lentils and split peas need no soaking. Soaked beans are drained and cooked in fresh water.
- **Salt in the soak** (about 1 % of the water's weight): the myth says it toughens beans; in fact it softens the skins
  and gives creamier, more intact beans. The comparison of `LEG-2.M1.X2` tests it.
- **Baking soda:** a pinch in the soak or the cooking water softens skins in hard water. Too much makes the beans mushy
  and soapy.
- **Acid** (tomato, vinegar, wine) slows softening, so it goes in once the beans are tender.
- **Digestibility:** beans contain sugars (oligosaccharides) that the gut ferments. Soaking, cooking well and eating
  legumes regularly help.
- **Safety:** dried kidney beans and some other beans contain a lectin that cooking destroys:
  - the beans are brought to a full, rolling boil and kept there for several minutes before simmering;
  - they are never cooked from dry in a slow cooker;
  - tinned beans are already cooked.

  The figure is a gap for the S2 review (§8 of the overview).

**Sessions:**
1. Soak (20 min, then overnight): set up the four soaks for `LEG-2.M1.X2`, weighed and labelled.

**Cues:** for each legume, the learner can say whether it needs soaking, how it will cook, and what salt, soda and acid
do.

**Faults:**
- Soaking lentils.
- Adding tomatoes at the start.
- A spoonful of baking soda.
- Kidney beans in a slow cooker.

#### LEG-2.M1.L2 — Cooking beans and chickpeas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `LEG-2.M1.L1` · moves TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] simmer or pressure-cook to creamy and whole; cook with aromatics; store in their liquid
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Simmer gently:**
  1. Cover the beans by a few centimetres of water.
  2. Bring them to a boil and skim.
  3. Lower to a bare simmer, partly covered.

  Hard boiling bursts the skins. Most soaked beans take 45–90 minutes, chickpeas up to 2 hours, depending on age and
  size.
- **Aromatics:** a halved onion, garlic, bay, a carrot, a strip of kombu or a rind of Parmesan, all lifted out at the
  end.
- **Salt:** in the soak, and in the cooking water once the beans start to soften.
- **The target:** creamy all the way through, with no chalky centre, and the skin intact. Test five beans, because they
  cook unevenly.
- **Pressure-cooking** (optional) cuts the time to about a third. The pot is not required on this course.
- **Storing:**
  - beans kept in their cooking liquid, which keeps them moist and makes a ready-made sauce base;
  - cooled and chilled within 2 hours;
  - kept for at most 2 days in the fridge, or frozen in portions with their liquid (assessment §9).

**Sessions:**
1. A pot of beans (60 min active, about 2 hours in total): soaked white beans or chickpeas with aromatics; tested every
   15 minutes from the 45-minute mark; cooled and stored.
2. Use them (40 min): a dish from the pot, such as beans on toast with greens, or a white-bean and kale soup.

**Cues:**
- Five beans in a row creamy to the centre.
- Skins intact.
- A slightly thickened, flavoursome liquid.

**Faults:**
- A rolling boil.
- Judging on one bean.
- Draining away the liquid.
- Salting only at the very end, so the beans taste flat inside.

#### LEG-2.M1.L3 — Lentils and split peas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l3 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `LEG-2.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→1, TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] lentils that hold their shape versus ones that collapse; a dal and a pea soup; [K] legumes across cuisines
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Lentils that hold their shape** (green, brown, black *beluga*) are simmered in plenty of water for 20–30 minutes,
  for salads and sides. They are drained when just tender and dressed while warm.
- **Lentils that collapse** (red, yellow) cook in 15–20 minutes into a purée: the base of a **dal**. A dal is finished
  with a ***tadka***: whole spices, garlic and onion fried in hot fat and poured over at the end (`IN-ARO`).
- **Split peas:** a Dutch *snert* simmers green split peas with leek, celeriac, potato and smoked sausage until thick.
  The spoon stands up in a good one.
- **Legumes across cuisines** (`CK-CUIS` level 1):

  | Tradition | Legume dishes |
  |---|---|
  | Indian subcontinent | *Dal*, *chana masala* |
  | Middle East | Hummus, *ful medames*, falafel |
  | Mediterranean | *Pasta e fagioli*, cassoulet, chickpea and spinach stews |
  | Latin America | Black beans and rice |
  | The Netherlands | *Snert*, *bruine bonensoep*, *kapucijners* with bacon |

  Each pairs a legume with a grain or bread, an aromatic base and a fat.

**Sessions:**
1. Lentils two ways (45 min): green lentils as a warm salad, and red lentils as a dal with a *tadka*.
2. *Snert* or a Dutch brown-bean soup (60 min, more on the hob) for a winter dinner.

**Cues:**
- Green lentils separate and tender.
- A dal smooth and spooned, not runny.
- A *tadka* that sizzles and smells toasted, not burnt.

**Faults:**
- Green lentils cooked to mush.
- A *tadka* added to cold dal.
- Garlic burnt in the *tadka*.
- An under-salted dal.

#### LEG-2.M1.X2 — Beans soaked and unsoaked, salted and unsalted

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-leg-2-m1-x2 -->
*comparison, recipe* — Beans soaked and unsoaked, salted and unsalted.
<!-- END generated:exercise-leg-2-m1-x2 -->

**Evidences:** IN-STA→3

**Aim:** test two bean myths at once, by cooking the same beans four ways and comparing time and texture.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the comparison recipe Phase 6 writes. One bean variety from one bag, in four pots:
- unsoaked, unsalted;
- unsoaked, salted;
- soaked, unsalted;
- soaked in salted water.

They are cooked side by side at the same simmer. The learner writes predictions first.

**Inputs:** 400 g of dried white beans or borlotti (100 g per pot), salt, four small pans. The beans then become soups
and salads.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the predictions.
2. Set up the soaks.
3. Cook the four pots, testing five beans every 15 minutes and logging the time until each is creamy.
4. Photograph a spoon from each pot side by side, and count the burst skins in 20 beans.
5. Taste on the form.
6. Write the conclusion against the predictions.

**Evidence:** photo, written, form — the four results with times, burst-skin counts and tasting forms, and the effect
of soaking and salt explained. This is part of `IN-STA` level 3: the explanation of pulse behaviour.

**Time:** 1 h active, plus soaking and simmering.

#### LEG-2.M2.L1 — Polenta

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M4.L2` · moves TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] soft polenta and set polenta; grilled or fried slices
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Polenta:** coarse maize meal cooked in water, stock or milk. A common ratio is 1 part of meal to about 4–5 parts
  of liquid by volume for a soft polenta, and less liquid for a firmer one.
- **The method:**
  1. Whisk the meal into simmering salted liquid in a thin stream.
  2. Lower the heat.
  3. Stir often until the grains are tender: about 30–45 minutes for coarse polenta, a few minutes for instant.
- **Soft polenta** is finished with butter and cheese and served at once, because it sets as it cools.
- **Set polenta:**
  1. Spread it 1.5–2 cm thick in an oiled tin.
  2. Cool it completely.
  3. Cut it into slices.
  4. Grill or fry the slices until crisp outside and soft inside.
- **The safety of starches** (`STA-1.M4`): cooked grains and polenta are cooled and chilled within 2 hours (assessment
  §9).

**Sessions:**
1. Soft polenta (45 min) with butter and cheese, served with a mushroom or bean ragù; half spread in a tin to set.
2. Set polenta (40 min): the set half sliced and grilled or fried; served with greens.

**Cues:**
- A soft polenta that slowly flows on the plate.
- Smooth and without lumps.
- Set slices that hold and crisp without sticking.

**Faults:**
- Meal dumped in, so it forms lumps.
- Undercooked, gritty polenta.
- Slices fried before they set.
- A pan of soft polenta left to stiffen while waiting.

#### LEG-2.M2.L2 — Batch-cooking grains

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STA-1.M4.L3` · moves KM-WAST→3, IN-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cook, cool and store grains for the week; [J] plan uses across meals
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Whole grains** grown in or near the Netherlands: spelt (*spelt*), pearl barley (*gerst*), oat groats, buckwheat
  and wheat berries. They are cooked like pasta in plenty of salted water until tender with a slight chew, then
  drained. That takes 20–60 minutes, depending on the grain.
- **A batch for the week, safely** (assessment §9):
  1. Spread the cooked grains on a tray to cool quickly, and chill them within 2 hours.
  2. Keep two days' worth in the fridge (at most 2 days, covered).
  3. Freeze the rest flat in portions.
  4. Thaw in the fridge, and reheat until steaming hot, once.

  This follows the stricter Dutch rule (§2 of the overview).
- **Planning the uses:**
  - a warm grain salad with roast vegetables;
  - a grain bowl with beans and a vinaigrette (`SAU-2.M6`);
  - a soup thickened with grains;
  - a *risotto*-style *orzotto*;
  - fried grains with egg.

  Each meal uses a different flavour direction, so the batch doesn't feel repeated.
- **The waste log:** what is left at the end of the week, compared with earlier weeks (`KM-WAST`).

**Sessions:**
1. Cook and store a batch (40 min active): a grain batch for the week, cooled on a tray, portioned, labelled, and the
   fridge and freezer photographed; the three meals planned.

**Cues:**
- Grains tender with a slight chew, not split or mushy.
- In the fridge within 2 hours.
- Every container labelled and dated.

**Faults:**
- A deep pot of grains cooling on the stove.
- A week's batch kept in the fridge for a week.
- Three meals of the same flavour.

### LEG-2.M3 — Dairy

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-leg-2-m3 -->

#### LEG-2.M3.L1 — Milk, cream and butter

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M4.L1` · moves IN-DAI→3*

*Objectives:* [K] fat content, curdling with acid and heat, reducing cream, browning butter; [S] *beurre noisette*
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Milk** is water with fat droplets, lactose and proteins: casein, which curdles with acid and rennet, and whey
  proteins, which set with heat.
- **Fat content** decides how dairy behaves under heat and acid (`SCI-2.M4.L1`):

  | Product (NL) | Fat | Boil and reduce? | With acid |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Milk (*volle melk*, *halfvolle melk*) | About 3.5 % or 1.5 % | Scorches, skins, curdles easily | Curdles |
  | Cooking cream (*kookroom*) | About 15–20 % | Usually, thanks to its stabilisers | Mostly holds |
  | Whipping cream (*slagroom*) | About 35 % | Yes: reduces to a sauce | Holds |
  | *Crème fraîche* | About 30–40 % | Yes | Holds |
  | Sour cream (*zure room*) | About 20 % | Splits when boiled | Use off the heat |

- **Curdling:** heat and acid together make casein clump, and the more fat, the more it is protected. So a lemon sauce
  is made with cream, not milk, or the acid goes in off the heat.
- **Reducing cream:** whipping cream simmered until it coats a spoon becomes a sauce on its own. It must not boil hard
  with salt and acid too early.
- **Butter:** about 80 % fat, the rest water and milk solids.
  - ***Beurre noisette*:** butter heated until the water boils off and the milk solids turn golden brown and smell of
    hazelnut. It is stopped by pouring it into a cold bowl, or with lemon.
  - **Clarified butter:** the milk solids removed, so it can be heated higher without burning (`SAU-2.M5`).

**Sessions:**
1. Three dairy tests and a *beurre noisette* (50 min):
   - milk and cream, each simmered with lemon, compared;
   - cream reduced to coat a spoon;
   - *beurre noisette* stopped at golden, and spooned over a vegetable or fish.

**Cues:**
- The milk curdles and the cream holds.
- The reduced cream coats the spoon.
- The *beurre noisette* is golden with brown flecks and smells of nuts, not burnt.

**Faults:**
- Lemon into a milk sauce on the boil.
- Sour cream boiled.
- *Beurre noisette* left in the hot pan until black.
- Cream reduced with salty stock until it splits.

#### LEG-2.M3.L2 — Cooking with cheese

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAU-2.M3.L2` · moves IN-DAI→3*

*Objectives:* [K] cheese families; melting and stringiness; [S] a smooth cheese sauce
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Cheese families:**
  - **fresh:** ricotta, *paneer*, *hüttenkäse* (cottage), *kwark*;
  - **soft-ripened:** brie, camembert;
  - **semi-hard:** young Gouda (*jong*, *jong belegen*), Edam;
  - **hard and aged:** *belegen*, *oud*, Parmesan;
  - **blue;**
  - **stretched curd:** mozzarella.
- **Melting:**
  - **young, moist cheeses** melt smoothly, such as young Gouda, Emmental and Gruyère;
  - **aged, dry cheeses** melt less and can turn oily and grainy, so they are grated finely, added off the heat, or
    used for flavour;
  - **fresh acid-set cheeses** (*paneer*, *halloumi*) don't melt at all, and they fry and grill.
- **Stringiness:** stretched-curd and some young cheeses form strings when hot; stirring hard and boiling make it
  worse.
- **A smooth cheese sauce:**
  - start from a *béchamel* (`SAU-2.M3.L2`), or add starch to the cheese;
  - add grated cheese off the boil, in handfuls, stirring gently;
  - a little acid (wine) or a melting salt (sodium citrate) keeps it smooth.
- **Dutch cheese ages:** *jong* (about 4 weeks), *jong belegen*, *belegen*, *extra belegen*, *oud* (about a year).
  The older the cheese, the more flavour and the less melt.

**Sessions:**
1. A melt test and a sauce (50 min): five cheeses melted on toast under the grill, photographed side by side; then a
   smooth cheese sauce for a vegetable gratin, a mac-and-cheese style pasta or a cauliflower cheese.

**Cues:**
- Young cheeses melt into a smooth layer.
- Old ones brown and turn oily.
- The sauce is glossy and smooth, with no grains or oil.

**Faults:**
- Boiling a cheese sauce.
- Adding all the cheese at once, in one lump.
- Expecting *paneer* to melt.
- Grating old cheese coarsely for a sauce.

#### LEG-2.M3.L3 — A fresh cheese

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l3 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `LEG-2.M3.L1` · moves IN-DAI→3*

*Objectives:* [S] make ricotta-style cheese or *paneer* from milk and acid
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Acid-set cheese:** whole milk (pasteurised, not UHT, which sets poorly) heated to near the boil, around 85–90 °C on
  the probe, then acidified with lemon juice or vinegar. The casein clumps into curds and leaves a yellow-green whey.
- ***Paneer*:**
  1. Stir in all the acid at once.
  2. Strain the curds through muslin.
  3. Rinse them briefly.
  4. Press them under a weight for 1–2 hours into a firm block that slices and fries.
- **Ricotta-style:**
  1. Heat milk, with a little cream, more gently.
  2. Add the acid, and let the curds rest without stirring.
  3. Ladle them into muslin and drain for 15–60 minutes, depending on how firm it should be.
- **Yield:** about 150–200 g of cheese from a litre of whole milk. The whey makes a bread dough or a soup.
- **Storage:** a fresh cheese is perishable: covered in the fridge at 4 °C, eaten within 2 days (assessment §9).

**Sessions:**
1. *Paneer* or ricotta (45 min, plus pressing or draining and a night in the fridge): from 2 litres of milk; the
   heating temperature logged; the curds, whey and yield weighed and photographed.

**Cues:**
- Clear separation into curds and a yellow-green whey.
- A block that holds when sliced (*paneer*), or soft, moist curds (ricotta).

**Faults:**
- UHT milk.
- Too little acid, so the whey stays milky.
- Stirring ricotta hard, so the curds are tiny and grainy.
- Unweighted *paneer* that crumbles.

#### LEG-2.M4.L1 — Substitutions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m4-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `LEG-2.M1.L2` · moves RD-ADPT→2, SA-ALLG→3*

*Objectives:* [J] swap legumes, grains and dairy and predict the effect on time, texture and flavour; replace milk, egg or gluten for an allergic eater and check every label
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Substituting by function:** before swapping an ingredient, ask what it does in the dish (body, starch, fat, acid,
  binding), then choose a replacement that does the same.
- **Legumes, grains and dairy:**

  | Swap | Effect to predict |
  |---|---|
  | Chickpeas for white beans | Firmer, nuttier, longer cooking from dry |
  | Green for red lentils | Holds its shape, so the dal doesn't thicken |
  | Pearl barley for rice in a risotto | Chewier, longer cooking, cloudier |
  | Spelt for wheat berries | Similar, a little faster |
  | *Crème fraîche* for cream | Tangier; holds when boiled |
  | *Kwark* or yoghurt for sour cream | Tangier; splits on the boil, so add off the heat |

- **The prediction:** each swap is written with its effect on time, texture and flavour, then checked.
- **Cooking for an allergic eater** (`SA-ALLG` level 3):
  - **replacing milk:** oil or a plant-based butter, oat or soy drink;
  - **replacing egg:** a flax "egg", aquafaba;
  - **replacing gluten:** gluten-free grains such as buckwheat, rice, polenta, and certified gluten-free oats.
- **Reading every label:** the 14 EU allergen groups are printed in bold in the ingredient list (`SAF-1.M4.L1`).
  Hidden sources include:
  - milk in stock cubes;
  - gluten in soy sauce;
  - mustard and celery in spice blends.

  "May contain" warnings count for a severe allergy.
- **Cross-contact:** clean tools, boards and pans; the allergen-free dish cooked first and covered.

**Sessions:**
1. A swap with a prediction (45 min): one dish from earlier in the course cooked with one substitution; the prediction
   written first, the result noted against it.

**Cues:**
- A prediction written before every swap.
- Labels checked and photographed for the allergen dish.

**Faults:**
- Swapping by name rather than function.
- Missing a hidden allergen in a stock cube.
- Cooking the allergen-free dish after the others, with the same spoon.
