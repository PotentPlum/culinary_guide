## Introduction

Fish is quick to cook and easy to ruin. Its muscle has little connective tissue and its proteins set at low
temperatures, so the difference between moist and dry is a minute or two. It also spoils faster than meat, and the
choice at the counter is the start of every good fish dish: is it fresh, is it sustainable, and is it in season?

`FSH-2` follows the fish from the counter to the plate:
- **knowing and buying fish:** round and flat, lean and oily; freshness signs; MSC and ASC labels and the North Sea
  seasons;
- **handling:** scaling, gutting and filleting a round fish; pin-boning, skinning and portioning; storing it cold;
- **cooking it five ways:** pan-fried skin-on, poached, steamed, roasted or *en papillote*, and grilled, with doneness
  judged by translucency, flake and the probe;
- **shellfish:** live mussels, checked and steamed, and prawns;
- **a fish plate** composed from a brief.

**Doneness** is again the thread. Fish reaches 63 °C for 15 seconds at the core (assessment §9). The learner learns to
pull it just before, as the flesh turns from translucent to opaque and begins to flake, because fish overshoots fast.

**Sourcing.** Exercises use MSC-labelled fish or a seasonal North Sea fish:
- **round fish:** whiting (*wijting*), coley (*koolvis*), mackerel (*makreel*), herring;
- **flat fish:** plaice (*schol*), dab (*schar*);
- **farmed fish:** ASC-labelled.

Mussels come from Zeeland in their season, roughly from July to April. Each exercise gives a choice, so the course works
in any month.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-PRO` level 3 is completed with fish and shellfish, after `POU-2` and `MEA-2`.
- `TS-FAB` level 2 gets its round fish.
- `TS-MOI` level 4 gets its delicate fish poach.
- `IN-FSH` reaches level 3.
- Flat fish filleting without guidance comes in `S3` (`TS-FAB` level 3).

**Pacing.** About four weeks. Almost everything fits a weeknight. Fish is bought on the day it is cooked, or the day
before, so the sessions follow the fishmonger's days.

#### FSH-2.M1.L1 — Fish types and structure

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M2.L1` · moves IN-FSH→2*

*Objectives:* [K] round versus flat, lean versus oily; flakes and connective tissue; why fish cooks fast
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Round and flat:**

  | Type | Body | Fillets | Examples |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Round | Eyes on both sides, swims upright | Two fillets, one from each side | Cod, whiting, coley, mackerel, herring, salmon |
  | Flat | Both eyes on the top side, lies on the seabed | Four fillets, two from each side | Plaice, dab, sole, turbot |

- **Lean and oily:**
  - **lean (white) fish** store fat in the liver: cod, whiting, coley, plaice;
  - **oily fish** store it in the flesh: mackerel, herring, salmon, sardines.

  Oily fish stay moist on high heat and suit the grill; lean fish dry out quickly and suit gentle or quick methods.
- **The structure:**
  - **flakes:** fish muscle is short blocks of fibres (*myotomes*), separated by thin sheets of connective tissue that
    turn to gelatin at low temperatures. That is why cooked fish flakes;
  - **little collagen:** there is little connective tissue and no long cooking needed;
  - **why it cooks fast:** the proteins set at lower temperatures than in meat, so fish cooks in minutes and overcooks
    in a minute more (`SCI-2.M2.L1`).
- **Terms:** fillet, *suprême*, steak (*moot*), *darne*, pin bones, bloodline.

**Sessions:**
1. Look and feel (30 min): at the fishmonger or with photos, name and place ten fish as round or flat, lean or oily.

**Cues:** the learner names the type of any fish on the counter and says how it should be cooked.

**Faults:**
- Grilling a thin lean fillet until it dries.
- Expecting four fillets from a round fish.
- Cooking fish "until it's done" by the clock.

#### FSH-2.M1.L2 — Freshness and sustainability

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FSH-2.M1.L1` · moves IN-FSH→3, IN-SEL→3, KM-PURC→2*

*Objectives:* [J] eyes, gills, smell, flesh; sustainability labels and guides (e.g. MSC/ASC); seasonality; Good/Best
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Freshness signs** in a whole fish:
  - **eyes:** clear and bulging, not cloudy or sunken;
  - **gills:** bright red, not brown;
  - **skin:** shiny, with a natural slime and firmly attached scales;
  - **flesh:** firm, springing back when pressed;
  - **smell:** of the sea, not of fish or ammonia.
- **Freshness in a fillet:**
  - translucent, moist flesh without gaps between the flakes;
  - no browning at the edges;
  - no liquid in the pack.
- **Sustainability:**
  - the **MSC** label (wild-caught, from an assessed fishery);
  - the **ASC** label (farmed, to standards);
  - the Dutch ***VISwijzer*** guide;
  - choosing species that are plentiful.
- **Seasons in the Netherlands:**
  - *Hollandse Nieuwe* herring from June;
  - Zeeland mussels from about July to April;
  - plaice at its best in summer;
  - mackerel in summer;
  - grey shrimp most of the year.

  A fish plate in season pairs these with the vegetables of the month (`VEG-1`).
- **Good and Best:**
  - **Good:** a frozen MSC fillet (frozen at sea, often fresher than "fresh") or a cheap seasonal round fish, such as
    whiting, coley or mackerel;
  - **Best:** a whole line-caught fish from a fishmonger.

**Sessions:**
1. A fishmonger visit (45 min) for `FSH-2.M1.X1`, with the freshness checklist.

**Cues:** for a whole fish and a fillet, the learner can name three freshness signs and a label, and decide to buy or
not.

**Faults:**
- Buying by colour alone.
- Ignoring the label.
- Buying "fresh" fish that has been thawed, without asking.
- Buying fish for three days ahead.

#### FSH-2.M2.L1 — Preparing fillets

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-FAB→2, SA-HYG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] scale, gut (whole fish), pin-bone, skin a fillet
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Scaling:** from tail to head with the back of a knife or a scaler, inside a large bag or under water in the sink,
  so the scales don't fly. Mackerel and herring need no scaling.
- **Gutting a whole fish:**
  1. Cut from the vent to the head.
  2. Pull out the innards.
  3. Scrape the bloodline along the spine.
  4. Rinse the cavity briefly under cold running water, and dry it.

  Unlike poultry, the cavity of a fish is rinsed, because blood and innards spoil it. Then the sink is cleaned.
- **Filleting a round fish:**
  1. Cut behind the head down to the bone.
  2. Cut along the back, keeping the knife flat against the bones.
  3. Lift the fillet in long strokes.
  4. Turn the fish and repeat.

  The frame goes into a *fumet* (`STK-2.M2.L4`).
- **Pin-boning:** run a finger along the fillet to find the pin bones; pull each with tweezers in the direction it
  lies.
- **Skinning:**
  1. Lay the fillet skin side down and hold the tail end.
  2. Angle the knife slightly towards the skin.
  3. Move the skin rather than the knife.
- **Hygiene:**
  - a separate board, the raw-protein station of `POU-2.M1.L2`, and raw fish last in the work order;
  - the frame and trim straight into a bag in the fridge or freezer;
  - the board, knife and sink cleaned and disinfected.

**Sessions:**
1. A whole round fish (60 min) with the course video: scale (if needed), gut, fillet both sides; photograph the fillets
   and the frame.
2. Pin-bone and skin (45 min): two shop fillets, pin-boned and skinned; photograph them.

**Cues:**
- Fillets in one piece, with little flesh left on the frame.
- No pin bones felt under a fingertip.
- Skin removed in one strip with no flesh on it.

**Faults:**
- Sawing.
- A knife angled up into the flesh.
- Pin bones pulled against the grain, tearing the flesh.
- Scales over the kitchen.

#### FSH-2.M2.L2 — Portioning and storing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m2-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M3.L1` · moves IN-STO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] portion by weight; store on ice in the fridge; [K] freezing fish
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Portioning by weight:**
  - about 120–150 g of fillet per main course;
  - even thickness within a portion, so it cooks evenly;
  - the thin tail end folded under or kept for a fish cake.
- **Storing fresh fish:**
  - the coldest part of the fridge (set to 4 °C, assessment §9);
  - on a tray, covered;
  - ideally on ice in a container that drains, so it stays cold and doesn't sit in water;
  - cooked on the day it is bought, or the day after.
- **Freezing fish** (`IN-STO`):
  - wrap it tightly and airtight, to prevent freezer burn;
  - label and date it;
  - store it at −18 °C (§9);
  - thaw it in the fridge.

  Oily fish keeps less well in the freezer than lean fish, because the fat goes rancid. Fish frozen at sea and kept
  frozen is often of better quality than "fresh" fish several days old.
- **The fridge by zone:** raw fish and meat on the bottom shelf, ready-to-eat food above, everything covered,
  labelled and dated (`SAF-1`).

**Sessions:**
1. Portion and store (40 min): portion the fillets of `FSH-2.M2.L1` by weight; store two for the week on ice in the
   fridge; freeze two, labelled.

**Cues:**
- Portions within about 10 g of the target.
- Fish on ice on the bottom shelf.
- Every pack labelled and dated.

**Faults:**
- Fish sitting in its own liquid.
- Fish on the top shelf.
- Unlabelled freezer bags.
- Thawing on the counter.

#### FSH-2.M3.L1 — Pan-frying skin-on

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L2` · moves TS-PRO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] dry skin, a hot pan, press, cook mostly skin-side; crisp skin
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Dry skin:** pat the skin dry, or leave the fillet uncovered in the fridge for an hour. Scrape the skin with the back
  of a knife to remove moisture and scales. Salt just before cooking.
- **The pan:**
  - hot, with a thin film of oil;
  - non-stick or a well-seasoned steel pan (`EQP-1`);
  - not crowded.
- **Press:** lay the fillet skin side down, away from you. Press it flat with a fish spatula for the first 20–30
  seconds, because the skin contracts and lifts the fillet.
- **Mostly skin-side:** cook about 80 % of the time on the skin, until the flesh is opaque most of the way up. Turn it
  for the last moments, or baste with butter, off the heat.
- **Doneness:** 63 °C at the core (assessment §9). Pull a little before, because the residual heat finishes it.

**Sessions:**
1. Two fillets (40 min): one pressed and one not; compare the skins.
2. A weeknight fish (40 min): skin-on fillet, crisp, probed, with a quick sauce or *beurre noisette*.

**Cues:**
- The fillet releases from the pan on its own when the skin is crisp.
- A rising line of opaque flesh up the side.
- The probe at or just above 63 °C after a minute's rest.

**Faults:**
- Wet skin that sticks and tears.
- A curled fillet that wasn't pressed.
- Turning too early.
- Cooking it equally on both sides, so it dries.

#### FSH-2.M3.L2 — Poaching and steaming

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L4` · moves TS-MOI→4, TS-PRO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] poach in court bouillon or oil; steam over aromatics
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Poaching in *court bouillon*:** water with wine or vinegar, onion, carrot, herbs and peppercorns, simmered for 20
  minutes and then cooled to a poaching heat.
  - **The liquid temperature:** about 70–85 °C, the surface barely trembling (`POU-2.M5.L1`).
  - **The time:** a fillet of 2 cm is done in about 6–8 minutes.
  - **A whole fish** starts in cold liquid.
- **Poaching in oil:** fish submerged in olive or rapeseed oil at a low temperature, around 60–70 °C on the probe, for
  a silky texture. The fish still reaches 63 °C at the core (§9).
- **Steaming** over aromatics (ginger and spring onion, or fennel and herbs):
  - the fish on a plate in a steamer over simmering water, covered;
  - gentle and moist;
  - the plate collects the juices for the sauce.
- **Why moist heat for lean fish:** it can't overshoot the water temperature, so the window between done and dry is
  wider.

**Sessions:**
1. Poach (45 min): a white fish in *court bouillon*, the liquid logged at 70–85 °C; probed; served with a sauce from
   `SAU-2` (*beurre blanc* or a herb *velouté*).
2. Steam (40 min): a fillet steamed over ginger and spring onion, or fennel; finished with hot oil and soy sauce, or
   olive oil and lemon.

**Cues:**
- The poaching liquid barely trembles.
- Fish opaque and just flaking.
- Moist, intact portions.

**Faults:**
- Boiling the fish.
- An unseasoned *court bouillon*.
- Overcooking in the steamer while the lid is closed.
- Oil-poaching without a thermometer.

#### FSH-2.M3.L3 — Roasting and *en papillote*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l3 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-PRO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] roast portions and whole fish; cook in parchment
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Roasting portions:** thick fillets in a hot oven (about 200–220 °C) on an oiled tray, often after a quick sear.
- **Roasting a whole fish:**
  - scored on both sides, so the heat penetrates;
  - stuffed with herbs;
  - probed at the thickest part, behind the head.
- ***En papillote*:**
  - fish and finely cut vegetables in a sealed parchment parcel with a little liquid and fat;
  - baked in a hot oven, where they steam in their own juices;
  - opened at the table.
- **Matching the vegetables:** they are cut thin enough to cook in the time the fish needs:
  - julienned leek and carrot;
  - thin fennel;
  - spinach;
  - courgette ribbons in summer.
- **Checking a parcel:** time it, then probe through the paper into one parcel; the steam on opening shows the timing
  was right.

**Sessions:**
1. A whole fish and a parcel (60 min): a whole mackerel or whiting roasted; a portion *en papillote*; both probed and
   compared.

**Cues:**
- The skin crisp on the roast fish.
- A puffed parcel.
- Vegetables tender and the fish just flaking.

**Faults:**
- Vegetables cut too thick for the fish's time.
- Parcels that leak.
- A whole fish probed near the tail.

#### FSH-2.M3.L4 — Fish doneness

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l4 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FSH-2.M3.L1` · moves JD-CUES→3*

*Objectives:* [J] translucency, flake, temperature; carryover in fish
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The cues:**
  - **translucency:** raw fish is translucent and turns opaque as it cooks;
  - **flake:** at done, a gentle press with a fingertip or a fork tip separates the flakes, which still look moist;
  - **bone:** the flesh lifts from the bone of a whole fish;
  - **the probe:** a thin probe into the thickest part.
- **The figure:** 63 °C for 15 seconds at the core (assessment §9). Lightly cooked or raw fish (*sushi*, ceviche) is not
  taught at this stage.
- **Carryover in fish** is small, a degree or two in a fillet. Pull it just before 63 °C, rest it for a minute, and
  probe again.
- **The cue-then-reading entry** (`POU-2.M6.L2`): predict, write, probe. The fish entries extend the learner's log.

**Sessions:**
1. Predict and probe (40 min): three portions of different thickness cooked by the same method; predict the core from
   translucency and flake, then probe; log each entry.

**Cues:** predictions within ±3 °C of the reading.

**Faults:**
- Judging by the clock.
- Probing the thin end.
- Cutting the fish open to look, so it dries.
- Waiting for "flakes easily", which means overcooked.

#### FSH-2.M3.L5 — Oily fish on the grill

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l5 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-DRY→3*

*Objectives:* [S] grill mackerel or salmon; [K] why oily fish suits high heat
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m3-l5 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Why oily fish suits high heat:**
  - the fat in the flesh keeps it moist through a fierce sear;
  - the skin crisps and chars;
  - a sharp sauce (mustard, vinegar, horseradish) cuts the richness.
- **The grill:** the oven grill, a grill pan or a barbecue (`HEA-2.M3`).
  - **Skin:** whole mackerel or herring scored and grilled skin-on; salmon fillets skin side to the heat first.
  - **Sticking:** a clean, very hot, oiled grill, and a fish that isn't moved until it releases.
- **Doneness:** 63 °C (§9), probed at the thickest part.

**Sessions:**
1. Grilled mackerel (45 min): whole mackerel scored, grilled skin-on, probed, with a mustard or horseradish sauce and
   a sharp salad of the season.

**Cues:**
- Blistered, charred skin without tearing.
- Moist flesh that lifts from the bone.

**Faults:**
- A cold grill.
- Moving the fish too early.
- A lean fillet grilled hard until dry.
- No acid with oily fish.

#### FSH-2.M4.L1 — Mussels

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-FSH→3, SA-HYG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] check, debeard, discard dead ones; steam with aromatics; [K] the safety rules for live shellfish
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Live shellfish:** mussels are bought alive and must be alive when they are cooked. A dead mussel spoils quickly.
- **Storing them:**
  - in the fridge at 4 °C (§9), in a bowl covered with a damp cloth;
  - never in water, which drowns them;
  - never in a closed plastic bag, which suffocates them;
  - cooked as soon as possible, ideally on the day they are bought.
- **Checking them** just before cooking:
  - discard any with cracked or broken shells;
  - tap an open mussel on the worktop: if it doesn't close within a few seconds, it is dead and goes in the bin;
  - after cooking, discard any that stay firmly shut.
- **Cleaning them:**
  - rinse in cold water;
  - pull off the beard (*byssus*) towards the hinge;
  - scrape off barnacles if needed.
- **Steaming (*moules marinières*):**
  1. Sweat shallot and garlic.
  2. Add white wine.
  3. Add the mussels, and cover.
  4. Steam over high heat, shaking the pot, for about 4–6 minutes, until they open.

  The juices make the sauce, finished with butter or cream and parsley.
- **Allergens:** molluscs and crustaceans are both EU allergen groups; the learner asks guests before serving them
  (`SA-ALLG`).

**Sessions:**
1. Mussels (60 min): check, clean and steam 1–2 kg of mussels; count the discarded ones before and after cooking.

**Cues:**
- Shells tightly closed, or closing when tapped, before cooking.
- Open and plump after a few minutes' steaming.
- A broth that tastes of the sea, not of sand.

**Faults:**
- Mussels stored in water or a sealed bag.
- Cooking dead or cracked mussels.
- Overcooking until they shrink.
- Not asking about shellfish allergies.

#### FSH-2.M4.L2 — Shrimp and prawns

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fsh-2-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STK-2.M2.L4` · moves TS-PRO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] peel, devein, cook quickly; use the shells for stock
<!-- END generated:lesson-fsh-2-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **What is sold:**
  - **North Sea grey shrimp (*Hollandse garnalen*)** are cooked on board and sold cooked, peeled or unpeeled. They need
    only peeling and no further cooking;
  - **raw king prawns** are usually imported and farmed; ASC-labelled ones are the better choice.
- **Preparing raw prawns:**
  - peel them, leaving the tail on or not;
  - devein: cut along the back and lift out the dark intestine;
  - keep the shells and heads for a quick shellfish stock (`STK-2.M2.L4`).
- **Cooking fast:** prawns are done in 1–3 minutes, depending on size. Cues:
  - they turn from grey and translucent to pink and opaque;
  - they curl into a loose "C", where a tight "O" means overcooked;
  - large ones reach 63 °C at the thickest part (§9), probed with a thin probe.
- **Methods:** a hot pan with garlic and chilli; the grill; poached in a sauce at the end.

**Sessions:**
1. Peel, devein and pan-fry (40 min): king prawns with garlic; probe two of the largest; shells into the freezer for
   stock.
2. Grey shrimp (40 min): peel cooked grey shrimp by hand; use them in a classic dish (*garnalenkroketten* filling,
   shrimp on toast, or a stuffed tomato in summer).

**Cues:**
- A "C" shape, pink and opaque, still juicy.
- The intestine removed in one piece.

**Faults:**
- Overcooked, rubbery prawns.
- Cooking grey shrimp a second time.
- Throwing the shells away.
