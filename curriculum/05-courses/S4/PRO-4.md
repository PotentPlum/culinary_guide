## Introduction

S2 taught the everyday proteins and S3 the long cooks, the grill and home butchery. `PRO-4` adds the proteins a home
cook meets less often, and asks for precision with all of them:
- **duck and game birds:** a duck breast with rendered skin and a pink centre, and pigeon, pheasant and partridge cooked
  without drying out;
- **game:** farmed venison cooked precisely, and wild game (wild boar, wild venison, hare) braised;
- **offal:** calf's liver, kidneys and sweetbreads, where the window between right and ruined is narrow;
- **whole fish:** roasted or salt-baked, filleted at the table, with doneness judged per species;
- **shellfish:** crab and lobster (dispatched by the fishmonger, or bought cooked), scallops and squid;
- **a doneness map:** every protein and method the learner knows, with their own prediction record.

Several of these foods have their own safety rules, and the course follows them exactly (assessment §9):
- **Duck and game birds are poultry.** Their "pink" is reached with the low-temperature poultry table (for example
  60 °C held for 35 minutes at the core), not with a pan-only rare breast.
- **Farmed game is meat; wild game is not.** Farmed venison is cooked like intact meat (63 °C for 15 seconds); hunted
  game (wild boar, wild deer, hare) to 75 °C, which suits braising. Wild boar is bought only from a supplier of game
  examined for *Trichinella*.
- **Offal is not whole muscle.** Liver and kidneys are cooked as non-intact meat (for example 66 °C held for 1 minute),
  which still leaves calf's liver rosy. Liver is not served in pregnancy (the risk-group row of §9: too much
  vitamin A).
- **Fish with a translucent centre** below 60 °C is fish that was frozen first by the fish row of §9, and is not served
  to risk groups.
- **Live crabs and lobsters are not dispatched at home.** The checked welfare sources [S40][S41] say manual dispatch
  may fail in untrained hands and that humane slaughter cannot be done at home. They are bought cooked, or dispatched by
  a trained fishmonger and cooked without delay.

Meat and fish are Dutch or North Sea: duck from a Dutch farm, game birds and wild game from a poulterer in the autumn
and winter season, farmed venison from a Dutch deer farm or a butcher, calf's liver and kidneys from a butcher, North Sea
fish (sea bass, brill, gurnard, plaice), Oosterschelde lobster in its spring and summer season, North Sea brown crab,
MSC-labelled scallops and squid.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-PRO` reaches level 5: five services over at least eight weeks, one in each of `PRO-4.M2` to `PRO-4.M6`, each plate
  probed and cut through. `MS-S4` re-tests it at its service (`MS-S4.M1.L4`).
- `IN-MEA` and `IN-FSH` reach level 4: substitutions predicted and confirmed, less common cuts and species cooked well.
- `SA-TEMP` reaches level 5 in the logged productions of the course (`PRO-4.M5.X1`); `MNU-4` and `MS-S4` re-test it.
- `JD-REFL` level 4 begins with the doneness map (`PRO-4.M6.X1`); `PLT-4`, `MNU-4` and `MS-S4.M1.L6` complete it.

**Pacing.** About five weeks at 6.5 hours a week, with the protein dishes as household and guest meals. The five
`TS-PRO` services stretch over at least eight weeks, so `PRO-4.M6` closes during the next course. Game birds and wild
game are in season in autumn and winter, lobster in spring and summer: the order of the modules follows the season.

#### PRO-4.M1.L1 — Duck breast

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m1-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BUT-3.M1.L2` · moves TS-PRO→4, IN-MEA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] score, render from a cold pan, cook pink, rest
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The breast:** a thick fat layer under the skin over lean, dark meat. The aim is skin rendered thin and crisp and a
  rosy, juicy centre.
- **Scoring:** a crosshatch through the skin and fat, not into the meat, so fat can escape and the skin doesn't curl.
- **Rendering from a cold pan:** skin-side down in a cold, dry pan over medium-low heat for 10–15 minutes, pouring off
  the fat as it runs (kept for potatoes, `BUT-3.M1.L2`), until the skin is thin, brown and crisp.
- **Pink, safely:** duck is poultry (assessment §9). The program reaches a rosy centre with the low-temperature poultry
  table:
  - with a circulator (`SLW-3`): the breast sealed and held in the bath until the core has been at 60 °C for 35 minutes
    (or 63.3 °C for 11.5 minutes, a little less pink), then skin-side down in the pan to crisp;
  - without one: the stove-top water bath of `SLW-3.M5.L1`, the core probed and the time counted from 60 °C;
  - a pan-only breast is cooked to 75 °C at the core.
  A pan-only rare breast (around 55 °C) has no row in §9 and is not taught.
- **Resting:** 5 minutes skin-up, so the skin stays crisp; sliced across the grain.
- **Seasonal sauces:** a duck *jus* with Dutch cherries in summer, apples or pears in autumn, or a *bigarade*-style
  orange sauce from pantry citrus in winter.

**Sessions:**
1. The water-bath breast (90 min): one breast held by the table, then crisped; probe log; cross-section photographed.
2. Pan-only to 75 °C (45 min): a second breast rendered and roasted to 75 °C for comparison; both tasted side by side.

**Cues:**
- Skin thin, brown and crisp, with no white fat left under it.
- An even rosy colour from edge to edge in the bath-cooked breast.
- The probe log showing the core time at temperature.

**Faults:**
- A hot pan at the start, so the skin burns before the fat renders.
- Scoring into the meat, so juices run out.
- Counting the bath time from when the bag goes in, not from when the core reaches the temperature.
- Slicing at once, so the juices flood the board.

#### PRO-4.M1.L2 — Game birds

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m1-l2 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `PRO-4.M1.L1` · moves IN-MEA→4*

*Objectives:* [K] pheasant, pigeon, partridge; hanging and season; [S] roast and rest without drying
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The birds:**
  - pheasant: mild, lean, the hen more tender than the cock;
  - partridge: small (one per person), delicate;
  - pigeon (wood pigeon or farmed squab): dark, rich breast, best rosy; the legs tough;
  - all lean, so they dry out fast.
- **[K] Hanging and season:** game birds are hung (aged) by the supplier for flavour and tenderness; the longer the hang,
  the stronger the taste. Wild birds come in the autumn and winter shooting season; the poulterer's offer shows what is
  in season. Shot may be left in the meat: the learner feels for it and warns guests.
- **Safety:** game birds are poultry (assessment §9): the breasts by the low-temperature poultry table for a rosy
  centre, or 75 °C; the legs braised.
- **Roasting without drying:**
  - barding (a thin sheet of pork fat or bacon over the breast) or basting with butter;
  - a hot oven for a short time for the whole bird, then rested, with the breasts finished to the table if needed;
  - or taken apart: breasts cooked separately, legs braised or confit.
- **Garnishes of the season:** cabbage, celeriac, chestnut-free bread sauce, juniper, apples and pears.

**Sessions:**
1. A game bird two ways (2 h): a pheasant or two pigeons taken apart; the breasts by the poultry table and crisped; the
   legs braised; a sauce from the carcass.

**Cues:**
- Moist, rosy breast meat at the logged core time.
- Legs tender off the bone.
- No shot found by guests.

**Faults:**
- A whole bird roasted until the legs are done, so the breast is dry.
- Pink breast meat without the table's time at temperature.
- Strong flavour from a long hang that the guests didn't expect.

#### PRO-4.M2.L1 — Venison and wild boar

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m2-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `MEA-2.M3.L3` · moves TS-PRO→5*

*Objectives:* [K] lean meat and doneness; marinade myths; [S] sear and rest a venison loin
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Lean meat and doneness:** venison has almost no marbling, so it goes from juicy to dry within a few degrees. It
  is cooked fast and hot, or slow and wet; never in between.
- **Farmed or wild** (assessment §9):
  - farmed deer (commercially raised): intact cuts cooked like other intact meat, 63 °C for 15 seconds at the core,
    which leaves the loin rosy-pink; the steak row is for beef only;
  - wild game (hunted deer, wild boar, hare): 75 °C, so the tender cuts are better used in braises and the loin is for
    farmed deer;
  - wild boar only from a supplier of inspected game, whose carcasses are examined for *Trichinella* as EU law requires.
- **[K] Marinade myths:** a marinade flavours the surface; it does not tenderise lean meat deeply, and acid left long
  makes the outside mushy. The "gamey" taste is tamed by trimming silver skin and fat, not by soaking.
- **Searing and resting a loin:** trimmed of all silver skin, dried, seasoned; seared hot on all sides, then finished in
  a low oven to just below the target, rested to 63 °C (carryover predicted); sliced across the grain.

**Sessions:**
1. A farmed venison loin (75 min): seared and finished to 63 °C; carryover logged; cross-section photographed.
2. A second lean cut (60 min): a venison steak or a fillet of farmed deer, cooked by cue first, then probed.

**Cues:**
- A deep crust and an even rosy centre with a thin grey band.
- The probe within 2 °C of the target after the rest.
- No silver skin on the plate.

**Faults:**
- Silver skin left on, so the loin curls and is chewy.
- A loin taken to 63 °C in the oven, then rising to 68 °C on the board.
- Wild venison served rosy.

#### PRO-4.M2.L2 — Game stews and sauces

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 3 h · builds on `SLW-3.M2.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a game braise; [J] balance gamey flavours with fruit, juniper, acid
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The game braise** (`SLW-3.M2.L1`): shoulder, neck or leg of wild boar, wild venison or hare, browned in batches,
  braised with red wine and stock at a bare simmer until tender (2.5–3.5 hours, judged by a fork), well above the 75 °C
  of wild game.
- **[J] Balancing gamey flavours:**
  - fruit for sweetness against the depth (Dutch apples, pears, plums, red currants or cranberries in season);
  - juniper, bay, thyme and black pepper for resinous, warm notes;
  - acid (red-wine vinegar, the wine itself) to cut the richness;
  - a little bitter chocolate or a spoon of *stroop* (apple syrup) to round the sauce, tasted in small steps.
- **Sauces:** the braising liquid reduced; a *poivrade* (`CLS-4.M3.L2`) for game loins.
- **Cooling and reheating:** a braise for the next day cooled by the rule of assessment §9 and reheated to 74 °C.

**Sessions:**
1. A wild-boar or venison braise (60 min active, 3 h elapsed): balanced with fruit, juniper and acid; tasted at three
   points and adjusted; cooled and logged for the next day.

**Cues:**
- Meat tender enough to cut with a spoon.
- A sauce that is deep but bright, with no single note too loud.
- The cooling and reheating log in range.

**Faults:**
- Boiling the braise, so the meat is stringy.
- Too much fruit, so the sauce tastes of jam.
- Juniper not crushed, or too much of it.

#### PRO-4.M3.L1 — Preparing offal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MEA-2.M1.L1` · moves IN-MEA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] trim and soak liver, kidneys, sweetbreads; [K] freshness and sourcing
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Freshness and sourcing:** offal spoils faster than muscle meat. It is bought fresh from a butcher close to
  cooking (or frozen), smells clean and sweet, looks glossy and firm, and is kept at 4 °C and cooked soon. Calf's liver is
  milder and more tender than pig's or ox liver; veal kidneys are milder than pork.
- **Liver:** the outer membrane peeled off, any tubes cut out, sliced about 1 cm thick; optionally soaked in milk for an
  hour for a milder taste.
- **Kidneys:** the fat and membrane removed, halved, the white core cut out; pork kidneys soaked briefly in salted water.
- **Sweetbreads** (thymus): soaked in cold water to draw out blood, blanched briefly, the membranes peeled, pressed under
  a weight in the fridge so they hold their shape.
- **Hygiene:** raw offal on its own board, as raw meat (`SAF-1`).

**Sessions:**
1. Preparing three kinds (75 min): a calf's liver sliced, veal kidneys cleaned, sweetbreads soaked, blanched, peeled and
   pressed for the next day.

**Cues:**
- Liver slices of even thickness, with no membrane.
- Kidney halves with the core cut out cleanly.
- Sweetbreads firm and evenly shaped after pressing.

**Faults:**
- Membrane left on liver, so the slices curl.

#### PRO-4.M3.L2 — Cooking offal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m3-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `PRO-4.M3.L1` · moves TS-PRO→5*

*Objectives:* [S] calf's liver pink, kidneys with a sauce, crisp sweetbreads; [J] the narrow doneness window
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The rule** (assessment §9): offal is not whole muscle, so it is cooked as non-intact meat: 68 °C for 17 seconds, or
  66 °C for 1 minute, 63 °C for 3 minutes, 75 °C instantaneous. Chicken livers are poultry (75 °C). In pregnancy no
  liver at all, however it is cooked (the risk-group row of §9 [S46]).
- **Calf's liver pink:** floured lightly, seared in hot butter about 1–2 minutes a side, then rested off the heat in a
  warm pan until the core has held 66 °C for 1 minute: rosy, tender and moist. *Lever met uien*, the Dutch classic, is
  the same liver with slowly cooked onions.
- **Kidneys with a sauce:** seared hot and fast in batches, rested to the same target, then into a mustard and cream
  sauce off the heat (*rognons à la moutarde*).
- **Crisp sweetbreads:** the pressed sweetbreads sliced or in pieces, floured, cooked in butter until golden and crisp
  (well above the targets), with a caper and brown butter sauce or a *jus*.
- **[J] The narrow window:** liver and kidneys turn from tender to grainy and rubbery within a few degrees and a minute;
  the probe and the rest decide, and the cook plans the plate so the offal is cooked last.

**Sessions:**
1. Calf's liver (45 min): liver with slowly cooked onions; probe log; cross-section photographed.
2. Kidneys and sweetbreads (75 min): kidneys in mustard sauce; crisp sweetbreads; each probed.

**Cues:**
- Liver rosy and soft, the probe log at the target.
- Kidneys tender, not bouncy.
- Sweetbreads crisp outside, creamy inside.

**Faults:**
- Liver fried until grey and grainy.
- Kidneys crowded in the pan, so they boil in their juice.
- Mustard sauce boiled after the kidneys go in.

#### PRO-4.M4.L1 — Whole fish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m4-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BUT-3.M2.L1` · moves IN-FSH→4, TS-FAB→3*

*Objectives:* [S] roast or salt-bake; fillet at the table
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Why whole:** the bone and skin keep the flesh moist and add flavour; a whole fish at the table is a centrepiece.
- **Roasting:** the fish scaled, gutted, the gills removed, slashed on the thick part, seasoned inside and out, the
  cavity filled with herbs and lemon or fennel; roasted hot until the flesh by the backbone reaches 63 °C (assessment
  §9) and lifts from the bone.

  and a little water; baked; the crust cracked at the table. The flesh steams in its own juice and is not salty.
- **Filleting at the table:**
  1. The skin peeled back.
  2. The top fillet lifted off the bones in pieces with a fish knife and spoon, along the centre line.
  3. The backbone lifted out whole from the tail, taking the head.
  4. The bottom fillet served, the pin bones checked.

  or in season.

**Sessions:**

2. A salt-baked fish (75 min): baked in salt; crust cracked and filleted at the table for guests or the household.

**Cues:**
- Flesh that lifts cleanly from the backbone at 63 °C.
- Whole fillet pieces, with the backbone lifted out in one.
- No bones on the plates.

**Faults:**
- Gills left in, giving a bitter taste.
- A thin tail burnt while the thick part is raw (no slashes).
- Pieces broken into flakes while serving.

#### PRO-4.M4.L2 — Doneness across species

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pro-4-m4-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `FSH-2.M3.L4` · moves TS-PRO→5, JD-CUES→4*

*Objectives:* [J] lean versus oily, firm versus delicate; translucent centres on purpose
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Lean against oily:** lean white fish (cod, whiting, plaice) firm and dry quickly once they flake; oily fish (mackerel,
  herring, salmon) stay moist longer.
- **Firm against delicate:** firm fish (monkfish, turbot, brill) take longer and hold together; delicate fish (sole,
  plaice, whiting) need gentle heat.
- **[J] Translucent centres on purpose:** some fish is best just short of opaque at the centre (salmon at about 50 °C,
  for example). Assessment §9 allows this only for fish frozen first by the fish row (−20 °C in all parts for 24 hours,
  or bought so treated, or 7 days in a home freezer shown to hold −20 °C), and never for risk groups; otherwise fish is
  cooked to 63 °C. The learner decides per dish and writes the reason.
- **Cues and the probe:** flesh changing from glassy to opaque, flaking under light pressure, the probe at the thickest
  part; the cue written before the probe, as in the prediction log.

**Sessions:**
1. Three species compared (60 min): a lean, an oily and a firm fish cooked by the same method; cue first, then probe;
   texture noted.
2. A translucent centre (60 min): a frozen-first salmon or trout portion cooked to a chosen translucent target; the
   freezing record kept.

**Cues:**
- Each species at its own best point, recorded.
- The freezing record for the translucent dish.

**Faults:**
- Treating all fish the same, so lean fish dries out.
- A translucent centre from fresh, never-frozen fish.
- Probing the thin end.

#### PRO-4.M5.L1 — Crab and lobster

*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FSH-2.M4.L1` · moves IN-FSH→4, SA-TEMP→5*

*Objectives:* [K] humane dispatch (by a trained fishmonger) and why it is not done at home; [S] cooking times, extracting meat; [K] storage and safety of live and cooked shellfish
<!-- END generated:lesson-pro-4-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Humane dispatch, and why it is not done at home** (assessment §9) [S40][S41]:
  - crabs and lobsters can feel pain, and boiling alive can take minutes to kill them;
  - the methods judged most reasonable (double spiking for crabs, splitting a lobster along its whole length, ideally
    after electrical stunning) destroy all the nerve centres quickly, but may fail in untrained hands;
  - boiling alive, drowning in fresh water, chilling in a freezer or on ice, and pulling off the tail or claws alive are
    inhumane;
  - so the program buys crab and lobster cooked, or has a trained fishmonger dispatch them, and cooks them without delay.
- **Cooking:** a dispatched lobster split and grilled, roasted or cut and poached, until the meat is opaque and reaches
  63 °C (the fish row, assessment §9); a cooked brown crab (*Noordzeekrab*) bought from the fishmonger.
- **Extracting the meat:**
  - lobster: claws cracked and the meat pulled out whole, the tail meat in one piece, the coral and the green tomalley
    (liver) noted;
  - crab: legs and claws cracked, the body opened, the gills ("dead man's fingers") discarded, the white meat picked
    from the body and the brown meat scooped from the shell.
- **[K] Storage and safety of live and cooked shellfish:** cooked shellfish kept at 4 °C and eaten within 2 days, or
  frozen (the leftovers row of §9); a dispatched lobster cooked without delay; mussels and clams that stay open when
  tapped are discarded, as in `FSH-2.M4`.
- **The shells:** roasted for a shellfish stock or a shellfish butter (`PRO-4.M5.X1`).

**Sessions:**
1. Crab or lobster (2.5 h): a lobster dispatched by the fishmonger and cooked the same hour, or a cooked brown crab;
   the meat extracted and weighed; the shells into a stock or butter, cooled and logged.

**Cues:**
- Lobster meat opaque and springy, probed at 63 °C.
- Claw meat out in whole pieces; no shell in the picked crab.
- The shell stock cooled within the limits.

**Faults:**
- Overcooked lobster, tough and stringy.
- Gills left in the crab meat.
- A dispatched lobster kept in the fridge until tomorrow.
