# MNU-4 — Menu design and multi-course execution

**Purpose (outline):** menu progression and balance; seasonal and themed menus; quantities and procurement; backwards timelines; holding and plating for several; beverage basics and food–drink pairing, alcoholic and non-alcoholic (required module, after CIA HOSP-355 [S1]); hosting and service at home.

## Introduction

`MNU-4` comes last in S4 because it brings the stage together: the plating of `PLT-4`, the recipe development of
`RDV-4` and the kitchen management of `KMG-3`, applied to a dinner for guests. The learner leaves able to:
- **design menus:** progression from light to rich, variety of technique, colour, texture and temperature, around a
  season or a theme, and knowing what to cut;
- **make them feasible:** portions and a consolidated order for six to eight, a make-ahead plan, and where to buy what;
- **run the kitchen:** a timeline from a week before (T−7) to service, several courses on four burners and one oven,
  holding, reheating and plating for six to eight;
- **pair drinks:** the basics of wine, beer, cider and fortified wines, the non-alcoholic drinks, the principles of
  pairing, and cooking with alcohol; this module is **required**, following the CIA's *Wine and Beverage Studies* [S1];
- **host:** the table, the order and pace of service, guests' allergens and needs, and a post-mortem with guest cards;
- **deliver:** a dinner project for four to six guests, which rehearses `MS-S4`.

**Drinks and guests** (assessment §9): every menu with drinks has a non-alcoholic pairing for every course; no alcohol
for anyone under 18, none in pregnancy, and the advice not to drink while breastfeeding; a dish cooked with alcohol is
not alcohol-free (85 % of the alcohol remains when it is stirred into a boiling liquid and taken off the heat, 75 % when
flamed, still 5 % after 2.5 hours of simmering). The program teaches tasting and pairing, not drinking: tastings are
small sips, spitting is normal, and the alcohol-free route through the module is complete on its own.

Produce is Dutch and in season; the drinks include Dutch wines (Limburg, Gelderland, Zeeland), Dutch beers and ciders,
and Dutch alcohol-free drinks and juices.

**In the spiral.**
- `CR-MENU` reaches level 4: menus designed in `MNU-4.M1` and confirmed by the guests at the dinner project
  (`MNU-4.M6.X1`).
- `KM-PLAN` and `KM-PURC` reach level 4, and `KM-EQPT` level 4 is completed with the equipment plan for a guest dinner
  (`MNU-4.M3.X1`), after the calibration of `MOD-4.M2.X1`.
- `TS-MEP` reaches level 5 over five productions (`MNU-4.M3.X1`); `SA-TEMP` level 5, shown in `PRO-4.M5.X1`, is re-tested
  with guests.
- `CK-BEVR` reaches level 3 (`MNU-4.M4.X2`).
- `SA-ALLG` reaches level 4 with a guest's allergen plan (`MNU-4.M5.X1`).
- `JD-REFL` level 4: the post-mortem of `MNU-4.M5.X1` reviews the stage's record; `MS-S4.M1.L6` completes the row.
- `MS-S4` re-tests menu design, drinks, planning, prep days, service and the post-mortem (`MS-S4.M1.L1`–`L6`).

**Pacing.** About five weeks at 6.5 hours a week. The modules build towards the dinner project, so the order list
(`MNU-4.M2`), the timeline (`MNU-4.M3`), the pairings (`MNU-4.M4`) and the guest cards (`MNU-4.M5`) are all made for
the menu cooked in `MNU-4.M6`.

#### MNU-4.M1.L1 — Progression and balance

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M1.L1` · moves CR-MENU→3*

*Objectives:* [K] light to rich, variety of technique, colour, texture and temperature; avoiding repetition
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Light to rich:** a menu rises from light, fresh and acidic towards the richest course (usually the main), then
  steps down to a dessert that refreshes or comforts without overwhelming.
- **[K] Variety:**
  - **technique:** not two braises or two fried courses; a raw or cured element, a roast or sear, a slow cook;
  - **colour:** each course with its own colours, the menu not all brown or all white;
  - **texture:** crisp, creamy, tender and chewy spread across courses (`FL-TEXT`);
  - **temperature:** a cold starter, a hot main, a frozen or warm dessert.
- **[K] Avoiding repetition:** no main ingredient twice (cream in three courses, tomato in two), no flavour that tires
  (garlic in every course), no two courses with the same structure (a mousse and a terrine).
- **Portions across the menu** (`PLT-4.M1.L2`): the sum matters; a four-course menu has smaller courses than a
  two-course one.

**Sessions:**
1. Menus marked (45 min): three published menus marked for progression, variety and repetition against the checklist;
   one rewritten.

**Cues:** the learner can mark the progression, the variety and any repetition on a given menu.

**Faults:**
- A rich starter that blunts the main.
- Cream, butter or cheese in every course.

#### MNU-4.M1.L2 — Seasonal and themed menus

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `MNU-4.M1.L1` · moves CR-MENU→4*

*Objectives:* [S] design menus around a season or a theme; [J] what to cut
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A seasonal menu:** built from what is at its best (the Dutch seasonal calendar, `VEG-1`): spring (asparagus, new
  potatoes, rhubarb, lamb), summer (tomatoes, beans, courgettes, strawberries), autumn (pumpkin, mushrooms, game, apples
  and pears), winter (roots, cabbages, chicory, kale, mussels).
- **A themed menu:** a tradition (`CUI-4`), a region, a memory, a colour or a technique; the theme carried by every
  course without forcing it.
- **Drinks and a dietary need:** each menu with a drink idea per course (alcoholic and non-alcoholic) and one guest's
  need (an allergen, vegetarian, no alcohol) planned in from the start rather than added later.
- **The rationale:** a short written reason for each course and for the order.
- **[J] What to cut:** a course or component that repeats, overloads the stove, or doesn't serve the theme is cut; the
  menu is often better with one element fewer.

**Sessions:**
1. A seasonal menu (45 min): written with its rationale, drinks and a dietary need; checked against the criteria.
2. A themed menu (45 min): the same; one element cut and the reason written.

**Cues:**
- Menus that pass the progression and variety checklist.
- A cut that makes the menu clearer.

**Faults:**
- A "seasonal" menu with imported summer produce in winter.
- A theme that turns into a gimmick.
- The dietary need solved by leaving that guest's plate half empty.

#### MNU-4.M2.L1 — Quantities and procurement

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M2.L1` · moves KM-PURC→4*

*Objectives:* [S] portions for 6–8 across courses; a consolidated order list; [J] where to buy what
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Portions for 6–8 across courses:** the portion per course (`PLT-4.M1.L2`) times the guests, plus a margin (about
  10 % on the main protein, none on the dessert); yields from the learner's own records (`BUT-3`, `KMG-3`) to turn
  portions into purchase weights.
- **A consolidated order list:** every recipe's ingredients summed across courses, grouped by shop and by day of
  purchase, with what is already in the pantry crossed off.
- **[J] Where to buy what:** the butcher or fishmonger ordered a few days ahead for the main item; produce from a market
  or farm shop on the day before; pantry goods early; drinks early. Good and Best chosen where the choice changes the
  result.
- **The budget:** the expected cost per guest from the list; the receipts compared afterwards.

**Sessions:**
1. The order list (60 min): quantities and the consolidated list for the chosen menu, with shops, days and the budget.

**Cues:** a list a second person could shop from, with the reason for each quantity.

**Faults:**
- Multiplying a recipe for four by two without checking yields.
- One trip for everything, so the fish is bought too early.

#### MNU-4.M2.L2 — Make-ahead analysis

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M1.L2` · moves KM-PLAN→4*

*Objectives:* [J] which components keep and which must be last-minute; [S] a make-ahead plan
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] What keeps and what doesn't:**
  - keeps or improves: stocks, braises, sauces bases, doughs, pickles, ice cream, terrines, many desserts;
  - must be last-minute: searing, frying, dressing leaves, emulsions, plating, anything crisp;
  - in between: blanched vegetables (refreshed and finished at service), par-cooked starches.
- **The make-ahead plan:** each component with the day it is made, how it is stored (labelled, dated, cooled by the
  cooling rule, in the fridge at most 2 days or frozen, assessment §9) and how it is finished.
- **Control points:** the moments where something is checked (the stock cooled in time, the braise reheated to 74 °C,
  the dough risen); each with its target and what to do if it fails.

**Sessions:**
1. The plan (60 min): the make-ahead plan for the chosen menu, with storage, control points and fallbacks.

**Cues:** every component placed on a day, with storage and a control point where it needs one.

**Faults:**
- Everything left to the day.
- Make-ahead food kept longer than §9 allows.

#### MNU-4.M3.L1 — Backwards timelines

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MNU-4.M2.L2` · moves KM-PLAN→4, TS-MEP→5*

*Objectives:* [S] a timeline from T−7 days to service, with buffers
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Working backwards from service:** the time the guests sit down; each course's serving time; each component's
  finishing, cooking and prep times counted back from there.
- **From T−7 days:**
  - T−7 to T−5: menu final, orders placed, pantry and drinks bought, equipment checked;
  - T−3 to T−2: stocks, doughs, desserts that freeze, pickles;
  - T−1: braises, sauces bases, desserts, vegetables prepped, table planned, fish and produce collected;
  - the day: mise en place by the early afternoon, the oven plan, plating stations, a rest before guests arrive;
  - service: a minute-by-minute plan for the last hour.
- **Buffers:** 15–20 minutes of slack before each course; a "first thing to drop" if time runs out.
- **Labels and the log** (`KMG-3`): every make-ahead item labelled and dated; the log records each control point.

**Sessions:**
1. The timeline (60 min): the full timeline from T−7 for the dinner project's menu, checked against the checklist.

**Cues:** a timeline with buffers and a named first thing to drop.

**Faults:**
- No time to rest before guests arrive.
- Two courses' last-minute steps at the same moment.

#### MNU-4.M3.L2 — Scheduling the stove and oven

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EQP-1.M2.L3` · moves KM-EQPT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] fit several courses into four burners and one oven; [J] reorder to avoid clashes
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The equipment plan:** a grid of time against equipment (four burners, one oven, the circulator, the grill, the
  fridge and freezer space) with each component placed; clashes shown at a glance.
- **[J] Reordering to avoid clashes:**
  - one oven temperature at a time: dishes grouped by temperature, or one cooked ahead and reheated;
  - burners freed by moving components to the oven, the circulator or a vacuum flask;
  - resting time used for the oven's next job;
  - a component changed (roasted vegetables to glazed on the hob) when it can't fit.
- **Workarounds:** a missing tool replaced with a reason (`MOD-4.M2.X1`); a borrowed burner (an induction plate) as a
  plan B.

**Sessions:**
1. The equipment plan (60 min): the grid for the dinner project; two clashes found and resolved; one rehearsal of the
   last hour with water in the pans.

**Cues:** a grid without clashes and a rehearsal that runs to time.

**Faults:**
- Three dishes needing the oven at three temperatures at once.
- Forgetting the fridge space for make-ahead dishes.

#### MNU-4.M3.L3 — Holding, reheating and plating for several

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L1`, `PLT-4.M3.L1` · moves SA-TEMP→5, TS-PLT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] hold safely, reheat without loss, plate 6–8 consistently
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Holding safely** (assessment §9): hot food at 57 °C or above (a low oven, a covered pan on the lowest heat, a water
  bath), cold food at 5 °C or below; held food probed.
- **Reheating without loss:** braises and sauces brought to 74 °C in every part, within 2 hours, gently and stirred;
  vegetables refreshed and finished in butter; meat rested and sliced at the last moment, not reheated in slices.
- **Plating 6–8 consistently** (`PLT-4`): plates warmed, the plates lined up, each component on all plates in turn, a
  second pair of hands if possible; the first plate checked against the sketch, the rest matched to it.
- **The cooling plan for leftovers:** what is cooled, how, and by when, written before the dinner.

**Sessions:**
1. A rehearsal for eight (60 min): one course held, reheated and plated for eight (household and neighbours or
   frozen portions), timed and probed; the plates photographed together.

**Cues:**
- Eight matching plates in under ten minutes.

#### MNU-4.M4.L1 — Wine basics

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FLV-3.M6.L2` · moves CK-BEVR→2*

*Objectives:* [K] main grapes and styles; acidity, tannin, body, sweetness; serving temperature; [S] structured tasting
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Main grapes and styles:**
  - white: Sauvignon Blanc (crisp, herbaceous), Riesling (high acid, dry to sweet), Chardonnay (unoaked and fresh, or
    oaked and rich), Pinot Gris and Grüner Veltliner; the Dutch whites from Limburg and Gelderland (Johanniter, Solaris,
    Auxerrois) fit here;
  - red: Pinot Noir (light, red fruit), Gamay, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot (fuller, tannic), Syrah (spicy), Tempranillo
    and Sangiovese;
  - rosé, sparkling (traditional method and others) and sweet wines.
- **[K] Structure:** acidity (mouth-watering), tannin (drying, from skins and oak), body (light to full, mostly alcohol
  and extract), sweetness (dry to sweet); these, not colour, decide a pairing.
- **[K] Serving temperature:** sparkling and sweet whites cold (about 6–8 °C), most whites cool (8–12 °C), light reds
  slightly cool (12–14 °C), fuller reds at a cool room temperature (16–18 °C).
- **[S] Structured tasting:** look (colour, clarity), smell (fruit, flowers, spice, oak), taste (acidity, tannin, body,
  sweetness, length), conclusion; small sips; spitting is normal; water and bread between wines. The same method works
  for alcohol-free wines.

**Sessions:**
1. A structured tasting (60 min): three wines of different structure (or their alcohol-free equivalents), tasted with the
   form; serving temperatures measured.

**Cues:** tasting notes that describe structure, not only flavour.

**Faults:**
- Judging by colour alone.
- Serving red wine warm from a heated room.
- Drinking rather than tasting.

#### MNU-4.M4.L2 — Beer, cider, fortified wines and cooking with alcohol

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MNU-4.M4.L1` · moves CK-BEVR→2*

*Objectives:* [K] styles and uses; [S] cook with wine, beer and spirits (reduction, deglazing, flambé safety)
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Beer:** lager and pilsner (crisp, bitter), wheat beer (soft, spicy), pale ale and IPA (hoppy, bitter), brown
  ales, *bokbier* in autumn, stouts and porters (roasted), Belgian and Dutch abbey beers (*dubbel*, *tripel*), sour beers.
  Bitterness, carbonation and malt sweetness are what pair.
- **[K] Cider:** dry to sweet, still or sparkling; Dutch and Normandy ciders; apple and pear.
- **[K] Fortified wines:** sherry (*fino* dry and salty, *oloroso* nutty, *Pedro Ximénez* sweet), port (ruby, tawny),
  madeira; small glasses, with specific foods (*fino* with salty snacks, tawny with cheese).
- **[S] Cooking with alcohol:**
  - wine reduced before stock or cream goes in, so it doesn't taste raw (`CLS-4.M3`);
  - deglazing with wine, beer or cider;
  - beer in braises (*stoofvlees*) and batters;
  - spirits added at the end or flamed;

- **[S] Flambé safety:** the extractor hood off, nothing flammable above the pan, the spirit measured into a jug (never

**Sessions:**

**Cues:**
- The flambé done with the hood off and the lid in hand.
- A pan sauce without a raw-wine taste.

**Faults:**
- Pouring spirit from the bottle into a flaming pan.
- The hood running during a flambé.
- Calling a wine-braised dish alcohol-free.

#### MNU-4.M4.L4 — Pairing principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l4 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `MNU-4.M4.L3` · moves CK-BEVR→3, FL-SEAS→5*

*Objectives:* [K] match and contrast; acid, tannin, sweetness, salt and heat; [S] pair one menu with both alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Match and contrast:** a drink matches the dish's weight and intensity (light with light), and either echoes a
  flavour (an earthy Pinot Noir with mushrooms) or contrasts it (a crisp acid wine with a rich, fatty dish).
- **[K] The interactions:**
  - **acid:** the drink needs at least the dish's acidity, or it tastes flat;
  - **tannin:** softened by fat and protein (red meat); harsh with oily fish and made metallic by it;
  - **sweetness:** the drink at least as sweet as the dessert, or it tastes sour;
  - **salt:** softens tannin and bitterness, loves acid and sweet (salty cheese with sweet wine);
  - **heat (chilli):** made hotter by alcohol and tannin; cooled by low alcohol, a little sweetness and fizz.
- **[S] Pairing a menu with both:** each course given an alcoholic and a non-alcoholic drink with the same reason
  (for example an acidic white and a rhubarb shrub with a rich fish).
- **Seasoning for the drink** (`FL-SEAS`): the dish's acid and salt adjusted slightly when a pairing is planned (a
  touch more acid in the dish for a high-acid wine); judged blind by the tasters.

**Sessions:**
1. Principles tested (60 min): one dish with four drinks (two alcoholic, two not) showing acid, tannin, sweetness and
   heat interactions; notes on each.
2. A menu paired (60 min): one menu paired course by course with both kinds of drink; the seasoning adjusted for the
   pairing and judged blind.

**Cues:** pairings explained by structure; the blind tasters judge the adjusted seasoning balanced.

**Faults:**
- A tannic red with oily fish.
- A dry wine with a sweet dessert.
- A strong alcoholic drink with a hot chilli dish.
