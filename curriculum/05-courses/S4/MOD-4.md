## Introduction

S3 brought the sous vide circulator into the kitchen (`SLW-3.M5`) and the gelatin mousse into pastry (`PST-3.M4`).
`MOD-4` widens this into the toolkit of modern cooking [S19], with one question in front: does the technique make
the dish better, or only different?
- **judgment:** where modernist cooking came from, and how to tell technique in service of flavour from novelty;
- **precision cooking II:** eggs and vegetables at exact temperatures, texture tables, a calibrated circulator;
- **gels:** gelatin and agar by percentage, set gels, fluid gels and gel sheets;
- **foams and stabilised emulsions:** lecithin airs, whipped foams, an optional siphon, xanthan in sauces and
  dressings;
- **dehydration and crisps:** dried vegetables, tuiles, crisps and powders.

Every element is used in a dish with a written reason. `TS-MOD` stays at level 2 (with guidance, from formulas); S5
takes it further. Hydrocolloids are dosed by weight on a 0.01 g scale.

**Safety in this course** (assessment §9):
- eggs cooked below 71 °C (the soft textures of `MOD-4.M2`) are raw or lightly cooked when served: not for risk groups;
- the siphon is used only as its maker's instructions say: the maker's chargers (N₂O for cream), never above the fill
  line, never inhaled; chargers bought for food use, a few at a time (the Dutch ban on nitrous oxide does not cover food
  use [S50]; assessment §9);
- dehydration is for vegetables, fruit and starches only; no meat is dried in this course.

Vegetables and fruit are Dutch and in season; the hydrocolloids (agar, xanthan, soy lecithin, gellan optional) come in a
small starter set.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-MOD` reaches level 2 across `MOD-4.M2` to `MOD-4.M5`: the gel (`MOD-4.M3.X1`), the foam and the xanthan sauce
  (`MOD-4.M4.X1`), each weighed to 0.1 g, and the elements used in dishes with a reason.
- `SC-EMUL` reaches level 4: a gel (`MOD-4.M3.X1`) and a foam (`MOD-4.M4.X1`) for a stated texture.
- `JD-CHSE` level 4 begins with a dish recreated from a description (`MOD-4.M1.X1`); `RDV-4.M2.X1` adds the second.
- `FL-TEXT` level 4 gets its third dish here (`MOD-4.M4.X1`), after `PST-4.M2.X1` and `PST-4.M5.X1`.
- `KM-EQPT` level 4: the circulator calibrated and a missing tool worked around (`MOD-4.M2.X1`); the equipment plan for
  a guest dinner follows in `MNU-4.M3.X1`.
- `MS-S4` asks for at least one modern element where it adds value (`MS-S4.M1.L4`).

**Pacing.** About three weeks at 6.5 hours a week, or alongside another S4 course. Gels and foams are quick; drying
takes a day in a low oven.

#### MOD-4.M1.L1 — Modernist cooking in context

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mod-4-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `SLW-3.M5.L3` · moves JD-CHSE→4*

*Objectives:* [K] history and principles; [J] technique in service of flavour and texture, not novelty
<!-- END generated:lesson-mod-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] History:** food science entered restaurant kitchens from the 1980s (Hervé This and Nicholas Kurti's "molecular
  gastronomy"); in the 1990s and 2000s chefs such as Ferran Adrià (elBulli) and Heston Blumenthal used foams, gels,
  spherification and low-temperature cooking on the plate; *Modernist Cuisine* [S19] gathered the techniques for
  cooks in 2011. Many are now ordinary: sous vide, a xanthan-thickened dressing, a fluid gel.
- **[K] Principles:** control of temperature and texture through physics and chemistry; ingredients used as tools
  (hydrocolloids, emulsifiers); measuring instead of guessing; testing.
- **Criticism:** the same foam on every plate, technique for show, food that surprises but doesn't satisfy; a
  backlash towards produce and fire.
- **[J] Technique in service of flavour and texture:** a modern technique earns its place when it:
  - gives a texture that couldn't be had otherwise (a fluid gel that holds on a plate, a light air of a strong flavour);
  - improves control or consistency (an egg at exactly 64 °C every time);
  - concentrates or clarifies a flavour;
  - and the dish is better for it, as the tasters can say.
  The questions: what does it add, would the classic way do as well, and would a guest miss it?

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** the learner can name, for any modern element, what it adds and what the classic alternative would be.

**Faults:**
- Judging a technique by how new it is.
- Adding an element because it is possible.

#### MOD-4.M2.L1 — Time, temperature and texture

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mod-4-m2-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 4 h · builds on `SLW-3.M5.L2` · moves TS-MOD→2, KM-EQPT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] eggs and vegetables at precise temperatures; [S] calibrate the circulator; [K] texture tables
<!-- END generated:lesson-mod-4-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Texture tables:** for eggs and vegetables, the texture depends on the temperature reached and the time held:
  - eggs in the shell: the white and yolk proteins set at different temperatures, so a 45–60 minute bath gives a
    distinct texture at each degree between about 60 and 70 °C (a barely set, custardy white and runny yolk at the low
    end; a fudgy yolk around 64–65 °C; firm at the high end);
  - vegetables: the cell-wall pectin softens only above about 80–85 °C, so root vegetables are cooked at 83–85 °C for
    an even, dense tenderness that keeps flavour in the bag.
  Tables in [S19] and similar sources give the starting points; the learner's own table follows from the tests.
- **Safety:** eggs held below 71 °C are lightly cooked when served: not for risk groups (assessment §9); pasteurised
  eggs exist for them.
- **[S] Calibrating the circulator:** the bath set to a temperature, checked against a reference thermometer (the
  calibrated probe, checked in ice water at 0 °C); the offset noted and corrected; the water level and circulation
  checked; descaled as its manual says.
- **Workarounds:** without a circulator, a pan on the hob with a probe and frequent adjustment (`SLW-3.M5.L1`); without
  a vacuum sealer, a zip-top bag by water displacement.

**Sessions:**
1. Calibration and eggs (90 min): the circulator calibrated; eggs held at five temperatures; the textures photographed.
2. Vegetables (75 min): carrots or celeriac at 84 °C against boiled; tasted and compared.

**Cues:**
- A calibration log with the offset.
- A row of five eggs with a visible, graded texture.
- Vegetables tender and bright, dense rather than soggy.

**Faults:**
- Trusting the display without a check.
- Eggs taken from too crowded a bath, which drops in temperature.
- Vegetables at 70 °C, which stay crunchy for hours.

#### MOD-4.M3.L1 — Gelatin and agar

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mod-4-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `PST-3.M4.L3` · moves SC-EMUL→4*

*Objectives:* [K] strength, setting and melting temperature, acid tolerance; [S] set gels by percentage
<!-- END generated:lesson-mod-4-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Two gelling agents compared:**

  | | Gelatin | Agar |
  |---|---|---|
  | Source | animal collagen (pork or beef) | red seaweed (vegetarian) |
  | Dissolves | in warm liquid (about 50 °C), never boiled | only when boiled (about 1–2 minutes) |
  | Sets | slowly, in the fridge (below about 15 °C) | quickly, at about 35–40 °C |
  | Melts | at about 30–35 °C, in the mouth | only above about 80–85 °C, so it can be served warm |
  | Texture | elastic, melting | firm, brittle, clean |
  | Acid | weakened by strong acid and by raw kiwi or pineapple enzymes | tolerates acid better |

- **[K] Strength by percentage:** the dose as a percentage of the liquid's weight; for agar about 0.5–1 % for a
  sliceable gel, less for a soft one; for gelatin, by sheets of known bloom strength (`CLS-4.M4.L2`), or about 1.5–2 %
  for powder in a soft set.
- **[S] Set gels by percentage:** the dose weighed on the 0.01 g scale, dispersed in cold liquid, heated as the agent
  needs, poured into a tray or mould, set, cut into cubes or shapes.

**Sessions:**
1. Gelatin against agar (60 min): the same apple or beetroot juice set with each; served warm and cold; textures
   compared.
2. Strength by percentage (60 min): agar at three doses; cut and photographed side by side.

**Cues:**
- Doses logged to 0.01 g.
- The agar gel holds its shape warm; the gelatin melts.

**Faults:**
- Agar not boiled, so it doesn't set.
- Gelatin boiled, so it weakens.
- Doses by teaspoon.

#### MOD-4.M3.L2 — Fluid gels and sheets

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mod-4-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `MOD-4.M3.L1` · moves TS-MOD→2*

*Objectives:* [S] fluid gels by shearing; gel sheets
<!-- END generated:lesson-mod-4-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The fluid gel:** a firm agar gel (about 0.8–1 %) blended to a smooth, glossy purée: it pours and spreads when stirred,
  but holds its place on the plate, carrying a strong flavour (an apple, a beetroot, a herb or a stock). Passed through a
  sieve; air bubbles removed by resting or tapping.
- **Gel sheets:** a thin layer (2–3 mm) of a stronger gel poured onto a flat tray, set, cut and draped over a dish (for
  example a stock or vegetable sheet over a vegetable dish or a piece of fish).
- **Flavour first:** the base is seasoned before gelling; gels mute flavour slightly, so the base is a little stronger.

**Sessions:**
1. A fluid gel (60 min): an agar fluid gel from a seasonal juice or stock, blended and sieved; used as a sauce.
2. A gel sheet (60 min): a sheet cut and draped; the thickness measured.

**Cues:**
- A fluid gel that flows when stirred and stands on a plate with a glossy surface.
- A sheet that lifts without tearing.

**Faults:**
- A grainy fluid gel, from too little blending or too firm a gel.
- A sheet poured unevenly, so it tears at the thin side.

### MOD-4.M4 — Foams and stabilised emulsions

#### MOD-4.M4.L1 — Foams and airs

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mod-4-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SCI-2.M4.L2` · moves SC-EMUL→4, TS-MOD→2*

*Objectives:* [S] lecithin airs; whipped foams; a siphon (optional); [K] foam stability
<!-- END generated:lesson-mod-4-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Foam stability:** a foam is gas held in a liquid by a film; it lasts when the film is strengthened (proteins,
  fat crystals, lecithin, gelatin) and when the liquid is thick enough (sugar, xanthan) to slow drainage. Without
  them, bubbles merge and collapse within minutes.
- **The lecithin air:** a thin, well-flavoured liquid (a stock, a juice, a whey) with soy lecithin at about 0.3–1 %,
  whisked at the surface with a stick blender held at an angle until a light, bubbly air forms; spooned off the top
  just before serving; lasts a few minutes.
- **Whipped foams:** a base stabilised with gelatin or with cream, whipped in a mixer; firmer and longer-lasting.
- **The siphon (optional):** a warm or cold base (a purée, a cream-based mixture, or a liquid with gelatin) in the
  siphon, charged with N₂O, shaken, dispensed. Used only as its maker's instructions say: the maker's chargers, never
  above the fill line, the head screwed on straight, the pressure released before opening, never inhaled; the chargers
  bought for food use, a few at a time (assessment §9 [S50]).

**Sessions:**
1. Airs (60 min): a lecithin air from a stock or juice; its life timed; photographed at 0, 2 and 5 minutes.
2. Whipped or siphon foam (60 min): a stabilised foam whipped (or made in a siphon); stability timed against the air.

**Cues:**
- An air of fine, even bubbles with a clear flavour.
- A stabilised foam that holds its shape for the time stated.

**Faults:**
- A thick base that won't form an air.
- Too much lecithin, giving a soapy taste.
- A siphon filled above the line or opened under pressure.

#### MOD-4.M4.L2 — Stabilised emulsions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mod-4-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MOD-4.M4.L1` · moves SC-EMUL→4, FL-TEXT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] xanthan in dressings and sauces; [J] dose by texture
<!-- END generated:lesson-mod-4-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Xanthan gum:** a microbial polysaccharide that thickens cold or hot liquids at tiny doses (about 0.1–0.5 % of the
  liquid's weight) and keeps emulsions from separating; it gives a clinging, pourable body, slightly slick at higher
  doses.
- **Dispersing it:** sprinkled into the liquid while blending (or mixed with sugar or oil first) to avoid lumps; it
  thickens at once.
- **Uses:** a vinaigrette that doesn't split on the plate, a vegetable *jus* with body without butter or flour, a purée
  that doesn't weep.
- **[J] Dose by texture:** start low (0.1 %), add in steps of 0.05 %, judging by the coat on a spoon and the line it
  leaves on a plate; stop before slickness.

**Sessions:**
1. Two stabilised sauces (60 min): a vinaigrette and a vegetable *jus*, each dosed in steps and plated; the doses and
   textures logged.

**Cues:**
- A vinaigrette that stays emulsified on the plate for 15 minutes.
- A *jus* that coats a spoon and holds a clean line, without slickness.

**Faults:**
- Xanthan dumped in, so it forms lumps.
- An overdose that tastes slimy.

#### MOD-4.M5.L1 — Crisps, tuiles and powders

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mod-4-m5-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 1 d · builds on `PRS-3.M5.L1` · moves TS-MOD→2, FL-TEXT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] dehydrate in a low oven; make tuiles and vegetable crisps; fat powders (optional)
<!-- END generated:lesson-mod-4-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Dehydrating in a low oven** (`PRS-3.M5.L1`): thin, even slices of vegetables or fruit (beetroot, parsnip, carrot,
  celeriac, apple, pear) dried at about 60–90 °C with the door slightly open, or in a dehydrator, until brittle; a
  mandoline with its guard for even slices (`SAF-3.M2.L2`). Vegetables, fruit and starches only; no meat in this course.
- **Tuiles:** a thin batter (butter, sugar, egg white and flour, or a savoury version with cheese or seeds) spread thin
  through a stencil, baked until golden and shaped while warm.
- **Vegetable crisps:** thin slices fried or baked; or a purée spread thin and dried into a sheet, then broken into
  shards.
- **Powders (optional):** a dried vegetable ground fine; a fat powder from oil and tapioca maltodextrin, which turns a
  flavoured oil into a powder that melts in the mouth.
- **Keeping crisp:** in an airtight box with a little dry rice or a desiccant sachet; added at the last moment.

**Sessions:**
1. Dried crisps (60 min active, a day of drying): two vegetables sliced and dried; their weights before and after
   logged.
2. Tuiles and a shard (60 min): a tuile and a purée shard; one powder (optional).

**Cues:**
- Crisps that snap, with even colour.
- Tuiles thin and even, shaped without cracks.
- A clear flavour in each crisp element.

**Faults:**
- Uneven slices, some burnt and some leathery.
- A tuile spread too thick, so it stays soft.
- Crisps left out, so they soften in an hour.
