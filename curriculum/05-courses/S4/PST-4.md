## Introduction

S3 completed the six pastry elements of `TS-BAK` level 4 (choux, laminated dough, tarts, creams, mousses and chocolate)
in `PST-3`. `PST-4` adds the components of the restaurant dessert and teaches how to put them together:
- **meringues:** French, Swiss and Italian, and what each is for;
- **ice cream and sorbet:** the science of freezing point, fat and air, a custard-base ice cream with or without a
  machine, and sorbets by ratio;
- **sugar work:** the sugar stages, dry and wet caramel, caramel sauce, praline and brittle;
- **a layered dessert:** sponge, mousse, insert, crunch and glaze, planned over two days;
- **the plated dessert:** contrast of temperature, texture and taste, plated four times alike.

Eggs follow the S4 rows of assessment §9:
- an egg-thickened base cooked for later (custard, ice-cream base, Swiss meringue) reaches 71 °C in every part, checked
  with a probe;
- where the egg is raw or lightly cooked when served (an uncooked ice-cream base, a mousse lightened with uncooked
  meringue, an Italian meringue that stays below 71 °C), pasteurised eggs or egg products are used, for every guest.

Fruit is Dutch and in season (rhubarb, strawberries, red currants, cherries, plums, apples and pears); chocolate,
vanilla and citrus are pantry goods. Nuts are replaced by seeds by default, as in S3: the praline is made with pumpkin
or sunflower seeds, and a learner who uses nuts applies the allergen rule (`SA-ALLG`).

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-BAK` level 4 was completed in `PST-3`; the meringue, ice cream, caramel and layered dessert are further elements
  at that level.
- `IN-BAK` level 4: two substitutions in formulas, one in meringue (`PST-4.M1.X1`) and one in ice cream
  (`PST-4.M2.X1`).
- `RD-RATO` level 4: the ice cream and sorbet derived as percentages (`PST-4.M2.X1`); `RDV-4.M1` continues with
  savoury ratios.
- `FL-TEXT` level 4: the ice cream (`PST-4.M2.X1`) and the plated dessert with its menu (`PST-4.M5.X1`); the third
  dish is a modern texture of `MOD-4`.
- `CR-COMP` and `TS-PLT` reach level 4 with the plated dessert; `PLT-4` and `MNU-4` complete the courses, and `MS-S4`
  re-tests the plating (`MS-S4.M1.L4`).

**Pacing.** About five weeks at 6.5 hours a week. The layered dessert spans two days and needs freezer space; the ice
cream needs a day for the base to chill. Desserts are served at household and guest meals.

#### PST-4.M1.L1 — Three meringues

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BAK-2.M4.L2` · moves TS-BAK→4, IN-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] French, Swiss and Italian; [K] how sugar and heat stabilise egg foam
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] How sugar and heat stabilise egg foam:** whisking unfolds the egg-white proteins, which line the air bubbles.
  Sugar dissolves into the water between the bubbles and makes it thicker, so the foam drains and collapses more
  slowly; heat sets the proteins, which makes the foam permanent. Fat (a trace of yolk, a greasy bowl) stops the foam
  forming.
- **The three meringues:**
  - *French*: whites whisked to soft peaks, then sugar added gradually until stiff and glossy; the least stable;
    baked dry (meringues, pavlova) or folded into batters. Raw when used uncooked;
  - *Swiss*: whites and sugar warmed together over a water bath, whisking, until the sugar dissolves and the mixture
    reaches 71 °C (assessment §9), then whisked to stiff and cool; dense, glossy and stable;
  - *Italian*: sugar syrup cooked to 118–121 °C and poured in a thin stream onto whites whisked to soft peaks, then
    whisked until cool; the most stable, used for mousses, buttercream and toppings. The hot syrup alone does not bring
    every part to 71 °C, so for uses where it stays uncooked the program uses pasteurised whites (§9).
- **The ratio:** about twice the sugar to the egg white by weight for a firm meringue (for example 100 g white to 200 g
  sugar), less for soft toppings.
- **Peaks:** soft (the tip folds over), firm (the tip bends slightly), stiff (the tip stands).

**Sessions:**
1. French and Swiss (60 min): the same weights made both ways; the Swiss probed to 71 °C; stability compared after 30
   minutes.
2. Italian (60 min): the syrup cooked to stage on the thermometer and poured; the meringue used for a topping.

**Cues:**
- A glossy meringue that holds a stiff peak and feels smooth between the fingers (no sugar grains).
- The Swiss mixture at 71 °C on the probe before whisking off the heat.

**Faults:**
- A trace of yolk or a greasy bowl, so the whites won't whip.
- Sugar added too early or all at once, so the volume is low.
- Overwhipped whites that look grainy and dry.
- Syrup poured onto the whisk, so it spins into threads.

#### PST-4.M1.L2 — Meringue applications

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `PST-4.M1.L1` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] pavlova, Italian meringue for mousse or buttercream, toasted topping
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Pavlova:** French meringue with a little cornflour and vinegar, shaped as a nest, baked at a low temperature
  (about 120 °C) until crisp outside and marshmallowy inside, then cooled in the switched-off oven to avoid cracks;
  topped at the last moment with cream and seasonal fruit.
- **Italian meringue at work:**
  - in a mousse: folded into a fruit purée or a cream for lightness and body;
  - in buttercream: soft butter beaten into the cooled meringue (Italian meringue buttercream).
- **A toasted topping:** Swiss or Italian meringue piped onto a tart or a dessert and coloured with a blowtorch (used
  as the maker's instructions say: on a stable surface, away from the body and anything that burns, the gas closed
  after use; assessment §9).
- **Piping** (`PST-3`): a bag with a plain or star nozzle, even pressure, the bag twisted shut.

**Sessions:**
1. A pavlova (60 min active, 2 h elapsed): baked and cooled; topped with seasonal fruit.
2. Toppings and fillings (60 min): an Italian meringue piped and torched on a tart; an Italian meringue buttercream.

**Cues:**
- A pavlova crisp outside, soft inside, with few cracks.
- Even, golden peaks from the blowtorch.
- A smooth buttercream that holds its shape.

**Faults:**
- A pavlova baked too hot, so it browns and weeps sugar.
- Cream and fruit added hours early, so it softens.
- The blowtorch held too close, so the peaks burn.

#### PST-4.M2.L1 — Ice cream science

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `PST-3.M4.L1` · moves IN-BAK→4, FL-TEXT→4*

*Objectives:* [K] sugar and freezing point, fat, solids, air, ice crystals
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Sugar and freezing point:** dissolved sugar lowers the freezing point, so part of the water stays liquid at
  freezer temperature and the ice cream stays scoopable. Too little sugar gives a hard, icy block; too much a soft,
  syrupy one. Glucose syrup, dextrose and invert sugar lower it more than sucrose for the same sweetness.
- **[K] Fat:** milk fat (about 10–16 % of the mix in a custard ice cream) gives creaminess and slows melting; egg yolk
  adds fat and emulsifiers that keep the texture smooth.
- **[K] Solids:** milk solids, sugar and fat together (about 36–42 % of the mix) bind water, so fewer, smaller ice
  crystals form.
- **[K] Air:** churning beats in air (the overrun) while freezing; home machines reach about 20–30 %, which keeps the ice
  cream dense and rich.
- **[K] Ice crystals:** fast freezing and constant stirring give small crystals (smooth); slow freezing and
  temperature swings give large ones (icy). A pre-chilled base and a cold machine bowl help.
- **The balance sheet:** a formula written in percentages of the total mix, so it can be checked and changed.

**Sessions:**
1. One base, three changes (60 min, then frozen overnight): a simple base divided in three: as written, with 3 %
   less sugar, and with part of the sugar replaced by dextrose; scooped and compared the next day.

**Cues:** the learner can explain each result from the balance sheet.

**Faults:**
- Changing two variables at once.
- Comparing samples at different freezer temperatures.

#### PST-4.M2.L2 — Custard-base ice cream

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 1 d · builds on `PST-4.M2.L1` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] with a machine, and still-frozen without one
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The base** (`PST-3.M4.L1`): milk and cream heated with an infusion (vanilla, a herb such as lemon verbena, toasted
  seeds), yolks and sugar whisked, tempered, and cooked stirring until the custard coats a spoon (*nappe*), with every
  part above 71 °C on the probe (assessment §9) and below about 84 °C, where it curdles.
- **Cooling and ageing:** strained, cooled fast in an ice bath (assessment §9 cooling rule), and aged in the fridge for at
  least 4 hours or overnight, so the fat crystallises and the proteins hydrate: smoother ice cream.
- **With a machine:** the bowl frozen (or the compressor run) before churning; the base churned until it looks like
  soft-serve; hardened in the freezer for a few hours.
- **Without a machine (still-frozen):** the base frozen in a shallow tray, stirred hard with a fork or whisk every 30
  minutes for 2–3 hours, or frozen solid in cubes and blended; for smoother results, whipped cream folded into a richer
  base (a *parfait*) so the air comes from the cream.
- **Storage:** at −18 °C or below (assessment §9), covered against the surface; best within a week or two for texture.
  Melted ice cream is not refrozen.

**Sessions:**
1. The base (45 min, aged overnight): a vanilla or seasonal infusion; probe log; cooling log.
2. Churned and still-frozen (60 min): half churned in a machine (if there is one), half still-frozen; texture compared
   at the same temperature.

**Cues:**
- A custard that coats a spoon, with the probe log above 71 °C.
- A smooth, dense scoop that melts cleanly.

**Faults:**
- Custard boiled, so it curdles.
- A warm base churned, so it never freezes properly.
- Ice cream left soft on the counter and refrozen.

#### PST-4.M2.L3 — Sorbets and granitas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m2-l3 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 6 h · builds on `PST-4.M2.L1` · moves RD-RATO→4*

*Objectives:* [S] sugar ratio by taste and by measurement; [J] adjust sweetness for serving temperature
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The sorbet:** fruit purée or juice, sugar syrup and water; no dairy. The sugar decides the texture: about 25–30 % of
  the mix for most fruit sorbets, counting the fruit's own sugar.
- **Measuring sugar:**
  - by measurement: a refractometer reads the sugar as °Brix (aim around 25–30 °Brix), or the formula's percentages are
    calculated from the fruit's sugar content;
  - by the egg test: a clean, washed egg in the mix shows a circle about the size of a coin above the surface when the
    sugar is right; quick, but rough.
- **[J] Sweetness and serving temperature:** cold dulls sweetness, so a base tasted at room temperature must taste
  slightly too sweet; acid (lemon or a tart fruit) keeps it fresh.
- **The granita:** a looser mix with less sugar, frozen in a tray and scraped with a fork every 30 minutes into flakes.
- **Seasonal Dutch fruit:** rhubarb, strawberries, red currants, cherries, plums, apples (with cider or *appelstroop*),
  pears.

**Sessions:**
1. A sorbet by measurement (60 min, then frozen): a fruit sorbet balanced by calculation or refractometer; churned or
   still-frozen.
2. A sorbet by taste and a granita (60 min, then frozen): a second fruit balanced by taste and the egg test; a granita.

**Cues:**
- A sorbet that scoops smoothly from the freezer.
- Sweetness that seems right when cold, with a fresh finish.
- A granita of separate, glittering flakes.

**Faults:**
- Too little sugar, so it freezes rock hard.
- Tasting the base cold from the fridge only.
- A granita left to freeze in a block.

#### PST-4.M3.L1 — Sugar stages and caramel

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M2.L2`, `SAF-3.M2.L2` · moves SC-BRWN→4*

*Objectives:* [K] stages by temperature; dry versus wet caramel; [S] safety with hot sugar
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Stages by temperature:** as a syrup boils, water leaves and the temperature rises with the sugar concentration:
  thread (about 110 °C), soft ball (112–116 °C, fudge), firm ball (118–120 °C, Italian meringue), hard ball
  (121–130 °C), soft crack (132–143 °C, toffee), hard crack (146–154 °C, brittle, spun sugar), then caramel (from about
  160 °C, light to dark). A sugar thermometer reads them; the cold-water test confirms them.
- **[K] Dry versus wet caramel:**
  - dry: sugar melted alone in a heavy pan, stirred only once it starts to melt; fast, but easy to burn;
  - wet: sugar with water, boiled without stirring (the sides brushed with water) until it colours; slower and more
    even, but it can crystallise if stirred or seeded with stray crystals.
- **Browning steered on purpose** (`SC-BRWN`): the colour chosen for the use (light for a glaze, deep amber for flavour);
  stopped at once by taking the pan off the heat and adding the liquid or cooling the base.
- **[S] Safety with hot sugar** (`SAF-3.M2.L2`): caramel at 170 °C sticks to skin and burns deeply. Long sleeves, a dry
  heavy pan, the handle turned in, no children nearby, a bowl of cold water ready for testing, and cream added
  off the heat and slowly, because it boils up. A burn is cooled at once under lukewarm running water, for as long as
  the first-aid guidance says (`SAF-1`).

**Sessions:**
1. The stages (60 min): a syrup taken through the stages, each confirmed by the cold-water test and photographed.
2. Dry and wet caramel (45 min): one of each, stopped at the same colour; times and temperatures logged.

**Cues:**
- The cold-water test matching the thermometer.
- An even amber caramel with no burnt smell.

**Faults:**
- Stirring a wet caramel, so it crystallises.
- Looking away at 170 °C.
- Cold cream poured in fast, so the caramel spits.

#### PST-4.M3.L2 — Caramel sauces, praline and brittle

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `PST-4.M3.L1` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a salted caramel sauce, praline paste, a brittle
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Salted caramel sauce:** a dry or wet caramel taken to deep amber, warm cream added off the heat in stages, then
  butter and salt (about 1 % of the weight); the thickness set by the amount of cream.
- **Praline paste:** a caramel poured over toasted seeds (pumpkin or sunflower; hazelnuts or almonds only with the
  allergen rule), cooled, broken and blended until the oil is released into a smooth paste.
- **Brittle:** sugar taken to hard crack with a little butter, seeds stirred in, a pinch of bicarbonate for an airy
  texture, spread thin on a mat and broken when cold.
- **Storage:** brittle and praline in an airtight jar (humidity softens sugar work); caramel sauce in the fridge.

**Sessions:**
1. Caramel sauce and brittle (60 min): both made; the brittle's colour and snap photographed.
2. Praline paste (60 min): a seed praline blended to a paste; used in a cream or as a spread.

**Cues:**
- A sauce that coats a spoon and pours when warm.
- A brittle that snaps cleanly.
- A praline paste that is smooth and glossy.

**Faults:**
- Caramel too pale, so the sauce is only sweet.
- Blending the praline too briefly, so it stays sandy.
- Brittle left open overnight, so it turns sticky.

#### PST-4.M4.L1 — Components

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m4-l1 -->
*3.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `PST-3.M4.L3` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [K] sponge, mousse, insert, crunch, glaze; [S] make the components
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The components of an *entremets*:**
  - a sponge base (a *génoise* or a *biscuit*), thin and even, sometimes soaked with a syrup;
  - a mousse (`PST-3.M4.L3`) set with gelatin, the body of the dessert;
  - an insert: a contrasting centre (a fruit *compote* set with gelatin or pectin, a *crémeux*, a caramel), frozen
    first so it holds its shape;
  - a crunch: a thin crisp layer (the seed praline of `PST-4.M3` with puffed rice or crushed wafers);
  - a glaze: a shiny mirror glaze (sugar, glucose, condensed milk, chocolate and gelatin) or a fruit glaze, poured over
    the frozen dessert.
- **Planning the flavours:** one main flavour, one contrast, one texture accent; each component tasted alone and
  together.
- **Making ahead:** the insert and sponge on day 1; the components kept covered in the fridge (cooked creams and
  mousses within 2 days, assessment §9) or frozen.

**Sessions:**
1. The components (3.5 h over a day): a sponge sheet, a frozen insert, a crunch layer and a mousse base prepared; each
   tasted and photographed.

**Cues:**
- An even, springy sponge.
- An insert frozen solid and the size of the ring's inner mould.
- A crunch that stays crisp under the mousse.

**Faults:**
- A thick sponge that dominates.
- An insert that melts during assembly.
- A crunch without fat to seal it, so it goes soggy.

#### PST-4.M4.L2 — Assembling, setting and glazing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-4-m4-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `PST-4.M4.L1` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] assemble in a ring, freeze, glaze; [J] plan over two days
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-4-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Assembling in a ring (upside down or right way up):** acetate inside the ring; mousse piped in, the frozen insert
  pressed in, more mousse, the crunch and the sponge last (upside down), or the reverse; smoothed flat.
- **Freezing:** overnight, so the dessert is solid enough to glaze and unmould cleanly.
- **Glazing:** the glaze at the right pouring temperature (about 30–35 °C for a mirror glaze; the thermometer decides),
  poured over the frozen dessert on a rack in one movement; the excess allowed to drip; transferred to a board.
- **Thawing:** in the fridge for several hours before serving (kept at 5 °C or below; eaten within 2 days of thawing).
- **[J] Planning over two days:** a written plan backwards from service: components, assembly, freezing, glazing,
  thawing, with the fridge and freezer space checked.

**Sessions:**
1. Assembly, glazing and slicing (over two days): day 1, the dessert assembled from `PST-4.M4.L1`'s components and
   frozen overnight (2 h); day 2, glazed, thawed and sliced with a hot knife, the cross-section photographed (1.5 h).

**Cues:**
- Straight sides and clean layers in the slice.
- An even, glossy glaze with no bubbles.
- The plan followed without a rush.

**Faults:**
- A glaze too hot, so it runs off, or too cold, so it sets in lumps.
- Not frozen solid, so the glaze melts the surface.
- No acetate, so the sides tear.
