## Introduction

Until S4 the program asked for food that is cooked well and served hot. `PLT-4` adds intent to how it looks:
- **composition:** colour, height, negative space, a focal point, odd numbers; the plate's size, shape and colour;
  portions that fit the course;
- **saucing and garnish:** pools, lines and dots, sauce at the table, and garnish that is edible and adds flavour;
- **styles:** classical, family-style and modern, and which suits which occasion;
- **photography for evidence:** a photo corner that makes every later photo reliable for AI review and for the
  learner's own record.

Plating serves the food: a plate is judged hot and good to eat first, then composed. The dishes are the learner's own
repertoire, cooked with Dutch seasonal produce, so the course costs little beyond household meals.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-PLT` reaches level 4: three courses designed with sketches and plated for at least four guests. The first is the
  plated dessert of `PST-4.M5.X1`; the second and third are here (`PLT-4.M2.X1`, `PLT-4.M3.X1`), and the photos are
  re-taken for AI review in `PLT-4.M4.X1`. `MNU-4.M3` plates for six to eight, and `MS-S4` re-tests plating at its
  service (`MS-S4.M1.L4`).
- `CR-COMP` reaches level 4: three dishes composed with a written idea (`PST-4.M5.X1`, and two versions in
  `PLT-4.M3.X1`).
- `JD-REFL` level 4: the pattern and plan of `PRO-4.M6.X1` are checked here, and the photo record is reviewed for its own
  pattern (`PLT-4.M4.X1`); `MNU-4` and `MS-S4.M1.L6` complete the row.

**Pacing.** About two weeks at 6.5 hours a week, or alongside another S4 course: each lesson is plated on household
meals, with two guest dinners.

#### PLT-4.M1.L1 — Composition

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-plt-4-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · moves TS-PLT→3*

*Objectives:* [K] colour, height, negative space, focal point, odd numbers; [S] plate one dish three ways
<!-- END generated:lesson-plt-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The principles:**
  - **colour:** contrast between the components (green against brown, white against dark); the colour of the food comes
    from the food, not from decoration;
  - **height:** a little height draws the eye and shows care; leaning components, not towers that fall;
  - **negative space:** empty plate around the food, so it reads clearly; about a third of the plate left free on a
    composed plate;
  - **focal point:** one place the eye goes first, usually the main item, often slightly off-centre;
  - **odd numbers:** three or five pieces look natural; even numbers look arranged;
  - **flow:** the components placed so the guest can eat them together, in the order intended.
- **The sketch:** a top-down drawing of the plate before plating: where each item goes, how large, the empty space.
- **Hot food first:** warm plates (about 60 °C, from a low oven or hot water), plating fast, the plate leaving within a
  minute; a beautiful cold plate of hot food is a failure.

**Sessions:**
1. One dish three ways (60 min): a household main course plated three ways from three sketches (centred, off-centre,
   linear); photographed from above and at 45°; compared.

**Cues:**
- A clear focal point in each version.
- The food hot when the photo is taken.

**Faults:**
- Food spread to the rim, with no space.
- A stack that topples on the way to the table.
- Inedible decoration.

#### PLT-4.M1.L2 — Plates and portions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-plt-4-m1-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `PLT-4.M1.L1` · moves TS-PLT→3*

*Objectives:* [J] plate size, shape and colour; portion size for the course
<!-- END generated:lesson-plt-4-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The plate:** white and plain shows food best; dark plates suit pale food; a wide rim frames a small portion; a bowl
  holds sauce and keeps food warm; size matched to the portion (a starter on a large plate looks lost, a main on a
  small plate looks crowded).
- **[J] Portion size for the course:** a starter of about 80–120 g of the main item, a main course of 150–200 g of
  protein with vegetables and starch, a dessert small and intense; the portion judged against the whole menu, not only
  the dish.
- **Shape:** round plates for most food; rectangular or oval for long items (a whole fish, a terrine slice).

**Sessions:**
1. Plate choices (60 min): the same starter on three plates of different size and colour; photographed; the best
   choice written with the reason.

**Cues:** the learner's choice of plate and portion has a reason that a guest would see.

**Faults:**
- A large portion on every course, so the menu is too heavy.
- A coloured pattern plate that fights the food.

#### PLT-4.M2.L1 — Saucing and garnishing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-plt-4-m2-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `PLT-4.M1.L1` · moves TS-PLT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] pools, lines, dots, sauce at the table; [J] every garnish is edible and adds flavour
<!-- END generated:lesson-plt-4-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Sauce placement:**
  - **pool:** under or beside the main item, spooned and tilted to spread; for sauces that the item sits in;
  - **line or swoosh:** a spoonful dragged with the back of the spoon; for thick sauces and purées;
  - **dots:** from a squeeze bottle, for intense sauces in small amounts (a reduction, a fluid gel);
  - **at the table:** poured from a jug in front of the guest, for sauces that would spoil a crisp skin or set
    (*beurre blanc*, a consommé over a garnish).
- **Clean edges:** a damp cloth or paper to wipe the rim; sauce never under a crisp skin.
- **[J] Every garnish is edible and adds flavour:** a herb leaf that belongs to the dish, a crisp that adds texture, a
  pickle that adds acid; no inedible decoration, no garnish that only adds colour.
- **The amount:** enough sauce to go with every bite, no more; about 40–60 ml for a main course.

**Sessions:**
1. Saucing drill (60 min): one sauce placed ten ways on ten plates; photographed together.
2. A course for guests (60 min): the best saucing and a purposeful garnish used on a course for four.

**Cues:**
- Clean, deliberate lines and pools.
- Every garnish eaten and mentioned on the cards.

**Faults:**
- A sauce that covers the crisp skin.
- Dots of different sizes.
- Smears on the rim.

#### PLT-4.M3.L1 — Classical, family-style and modern

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-plt-4-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `PLT-4.M2.L1` · moves TS-PLT→4, CR-COMP→4*

*Objectives:* [K] the three styles; [S] platters and shared dishes
<!-- END generated:lesson-plt-4-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The three styles:**
  - **classical:** the main item at the front, the garnish arranged around it, the sauce beside or under; the plate
    ordered and symmetrical (`CLS-4.M5`);
  - **family-style:** platters and bowls on the table, guests serving themselves; generous, warm, relaxed; the food
    arranged to look abundant and easy to serve;
  - **modern:** a composed plate built in the kitchen, often asymmetric, with height and negative space and several
    textures; one idea per plate (`PST-4.M5`, `MOD-4`).
- **[S] Platters and shared dishes:**
  - the platter warmed;
  - the main item carved or portioned before it goes out, so guests can serve themselves easily;
  - the sauce in a jug, the garnish in its own bowl or scattered over;
  - serving spoons for every dish, and allergen-free dishes kept apart with their own spoons (`NUT-3`).
- **The idea behind the plate** (`CR-COMP`): one sentence that says what the dish is about, which every style keeps.

**Sessions:**
1. A family-style platter (60 min): a main course for the household on a platter with shared bowls; photographed.

**Cues:**
- A platter that looks generous and is easy to serve from.
- Each style recognisable in the photos.

**Faults:**
- A whole joint sent out uncut.
- A platter piled so high that it spills.
- One spoon shared between dishes.

#### PLT-4.M3.L2 — One dish, three styles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-plt-4-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `PLT-4.M3.L1` · moves CR-COMP→4*

*Objectives:* [S] plate a dish in each style; [J] which suits which occasion
<!-- END generated:lesson-plt-4-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[S] One dish in each style:** the same components plated classically, family-style and modern.
- **[J] Which suits which occasion:**
  - a family dinner or a crowd: family-style (warmer, quicker, less washing-up, guests choose portions);
  - a formal dinner or a celebration: plated (classical or modern), each guest served the same;
  - a dish whose texture or temperature must be exact (a soufflé, a seared scallop): plated in the kitchen;
  - the cook's time: plating for eight takes help or practice (`MNU-4.M3`).
- **The idea stays the same:** whatever the style, the dish's idea and flavours are the same; only the presentation
  changes.

**Sessions:**
1. One dish, three styles (60 min): a dish plated in each style; photographed; the occasion for each written.

**Cues:** each version suits a named occasion, and the same idea is clear in all three.

**Faults:**
- Plating every course for a casual meal, so the food waits.
- A formal dinner served from pans on the table.

#### PLT-4.M4.L1 — Evidence photos

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-plt-4-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `ORI-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-PLT→4, JD-REFL→4*

*Objectives:* [S] daylight, angle, background, scale reference; cross-sections; a consistent set-up for AI review
<!-- END generated:lesson-plt-4-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Why it matters:** AI review and the learner's record compare photos over months; a change of light, angle or
  background can look like a change in the food. A consistent set-up makes the evidence reliable (assessment §6).
- **The photo corner:**
  - **daylight** from one side (a window), no direct sun, no kitchen lamps mixed in; a white card opposite to soften
    shadows;
  - **background:** a plain, matt surface (a grey or white board);
  - **angles:** from above (90°) for composition, at 45° for height, level for layers and cross-sections;
  - **scale:** a ruler or the grid board in the frame for cuts, crumbs and portions;
  - **the camera:** a phone on a stand at a fixed distance, the lens wiped, no zoom, no filters.
- **Cross-sections:** cut with a sharp, clean knife (wiped between cuts), photographed level and at once.
- **The evidence checklist:** light, angle, background, scale, focus, the date and the exercise ID in the file name.
- **Reviewing the record:** the photos since S1 looked through for a pattern (what makes some hard to judge), and a plan
  to fix it.

**Sessions:**
1. Setting up the corner (60 min): the corner built; three test shots against the checklist; the set-up photographed
   for repeating.
2. Re-shoots (60 min): three dishes re-shot in the corner, including a cross-section.

**Cues:**
- Photos that pass every point of the checklist.
- The same light and angle on different days.

**Faults:**
- Mixed light, giving yellow or blue food.
- A busy background.
- Cross-sections cut with a blunt knife.
