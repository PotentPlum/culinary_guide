## Introduction

Through S3 the learner cooked from recipes, frameworks and briefs. `RDV-4` turns them into someone who makes recipes:
- **ratios:** the proportions behind doughs, batters, custards, stocks, vinaigrettes, brines and cures [S14], so a recipe
  is no longer needed for the basics, and any recipe can be read as a ratio;
- **deconstruction:** taking a restaurant or cookbook dish apart into components, techniques and flavour logic, and
  recreating it from a menu description or a memory;
- **substitution and adaptation:** replacing what an ingredient *does*, and adapting a recipe to another oven, pan,
  quantity, season or diet;
- **testing:** one variable at a time, controls, blind panels and versions, until the dish is better or the test says
  stop [S17];
- **writing:** a recipe someone else can cook to the same result, and a first original dish.

The test kitchen is the household kitchen, and the tasters are the household and guests, as in every course. Produce
follows the Dutch seasons; adapting a recipe "to the season" means to what is grown here now.

**In the spiral.**
- `RD-RATO` reaches level 4 with savoury formulas (`RDV-4.M1.X1`), after the ice cream and sorbet of `PST-4.M2.X1`.
- `KM-MATH` level 4 is shown again with a costed week from the ratio sheets.
- `JD-CHSE` level 4 is completed with the second dish recreated from a description (`RDV-4.M2.X1`), after
  `MOD-4.M1.X1`.
- `RD-ADPT`, `RD-TEST` and `RD-WRIT` reach level 4, and `CR-IMPV` and `CR-ORIG` level 3.
- `MS-S4` re-tests adaptation for a guest's dietary need (`MS-S4.M1.L1`) and the written plan (`MS-S4.M1.L2`); S5
  develops original dishes further.

**Pacing.** About four weeks at 6.5 hours a week. The test series of `RDV-4.M4` and the original dish of `RDV-4.M5` need
several rounds a few days apart, so they overlap with the next course.

#### RDV-4.M1.L1 — The core ratios

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BAK-2.M1.L1` · moves RD-RATO→4*

*Objectives:* [K] doughs, batters, custards, stocks, vinaigrettes, brines, cures [S14]; [S] cook three from ratios alone
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The core ratios** [S14], by weight:
  - bread dough: 5 parts flour to 3 parts water (60 % hydration), with salt at 2 % of the flour and yeast to taste
    (`BRD-2`);
  - pasta: 3 parts flour to 2 parts egg (`CUI-3.M3`);
  - pie dough (*pâte brisée*): 3 flour, 2 fat, 1 water;
  - pound cake: 1 flour, 1 butter, 1 egg, 1 sugar;
  - crêpe batter: 1 flour, 2 liquid, 1 egg;
  - custard: 2 parts milk or cream to 1 part egg (a set custard); a pouring custard with yolks only;
  - stock: 3 parts water to 2 parts bones;
  - vinaigrette: 3 parts oil to 1 part acid;
  - roux: equal weights of flour and fat; a *velouté* or *béchamel* about 60–100 g roux per litre, by the thickness
    wanted;
  - brine: 5–6 % salt by weight of the water for meat and poultry (`SLW-3`, `GRD-3`);
  - dry cure: salt at about 2.5–3 % of the meat's weight by the equilibrium method, with nitrite only where a tested
    recipe needs it (assessment §9).
- **Why ratios:** they scale to any quantity, show at once what a recipe is doing, and let the cook vary flavour without
  breaking structure.
- **Ratios are starting points:** flours, eggs and fats differ; the cook adjusts by feel and records the change.

**Sessions:**
1. Three from ratios (60 min): a bread dough, a crêpe batter and a vinaigrette made from the ratios alone, scaled to the
   household's needs.
2. Two more (60 min): a custard and a brine or cure, from the ratios alone; results photographed.

**Cues:**
- Each preparation within standard without a recipe.
- The scaled weights written before cooking.

**Faults:**
- Measuring by volume, which breaks the ratio.
- Forgetting the fruit's or vegetable's own water in a batter or dough.

#### RDV-4.M1.L2 — From recipe to ratio

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `RDV-4.M1.L1` · moves RD-RATO→4, KM-MATH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] turn five recipes into ratios; spot outliers
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Converting a recipe:** every ingredient into grams (cups and spoons converted with a table), then divided by the
  base ingredient (flour for doughs, liquid for custards, meat for brines), and written as parts or percentages.
- **Spotting outliers:** a recipe whose ratio is far from the core (a "pound cake" with half the butter, a vinaigrette at
  1:1) is either a different preparation or a mistake; the cook decides which.
- **Baker's percentages** (`BRD-2`): every ingredient as a percentage of the flour; the same idea for other bases.
- **Scaling with a reason:** salt, spices, leavening and cooking times don't always scale in a straight line
  (`KM-MATH`).

**Sessions:**
1. Five recipes into ratios (60 min): five recipes from books or the learner's file converted; outliers marked and
   explained.

**Cues:** each recipe written as a ratio, compared with the core, and its outliers explained.

**Faults:**
- Converting volumes without a table.
- Calling every difference a mistake.

#### RDV-4.M2.L1 — Analysing a dish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FLV-3.M5.L1` · moves JD-CHSE→4, FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [J] components, techniques and flavour logic of a restaurant or cookbook dish
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The analysis grid:** for a dish eaten out or read in a book:
  - components (the main item, sauce, garnish, texture elements);
  - techniques for each (how it was cooked, how it was cut, what was made ahead);
  - flavour logic (`FLV-3`): the base, the contrast, the acid, the fat, the aromatics, the season;
  - texture and temperature contrasts;
  - what the cook wanted the guest to notice.
- **[J] Reading from taste:** what the tongue and nose tell (salt, acid, sweetness, bitterness, umami, the aromatics),
  what the texture tells about the technique, and what the colour tells about browning.
- **Uncertainty:** the analysis names what is certain, what is likely and what is a guess, and how a test would decide.

**Sessions:**
1. Two dishes analysed (60 min): one from a restaurant meal (notes and photos taken at the table), one from a cookbook
   photo and title; each written in the grid.

**Cues:** each component, technique and flavour role named, with the certainty marked.

**Faults:**
- Listing ingredients without their role.
- Treating a guess as a fact.

#### RDV-4.M2.L2 — Recreating from a description

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `RDV-4.M2.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→3*

*Objectives:* [S] recreate a dish from a menu description or a tasting
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **From analysis to plan:** each component turned into a method from the learner's repertoire (frameworks, ratios);
  the uncertain parts tested small first.
- **Fallbacks:** for the riskiest step a simpler alternative ready (a pan sauce if the emulsion splits).
- **Working from what's at hand:** a recreation often meets a missing ingredient; the substitute is chosen by function
  (`RDV-4.M3`), and a meal can be improvised from the fridge and pantry the same way.
- **Side-by-side notes:** the recreation compared with the memory or description: what matched, what didn't, why.

**Sessions:**
1. A recreation (60 min): one of the analysed dishes cooked from the plan.
2. An improvised meal (45 min): a meal from what is in the fridge and pantry, photographed first, cooked within 45
   minutes without a recipe.

**Cues:**
- A dish the tasters recognise from the description.
- An improvised meal on the table within 45 minutes.

**Faults:**
- Cooking the most uncertain component for the first time at the dinner.
- Improvising by adding everything.

#### RDV-4.M3.L1 — Function-first substitution

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `NUT-3.M2.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→4*

*Objectives:* [J] identify what an ingredient does (structure, moisture, fat, flavour) and replace that function
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] What an ingredient does:** before replacing it, name its functions:
  - **structure:** gluten (flour), egg proteins, starch, gelatin;
  - **moisture:** water, milk, fruit and vegetable purées;
  - **fat:** tenderness, richness, carrying flavour, browning;
  - **flavour:** taste, aroma, acidity, sweetness;
  - **other:** leavening, emulsifying (egg yolk, mustard), colour, binding.
- **Replacing the function:** a substitute must do the same job; if it does only part, the rest is covered by another
  ingredient (an egg in a cake replaced by a flax "egg" for binding plus a little extra leavening for lift).
- **Allergens and diets** (`NUT-3`): gluten-free flour blends need a binder (xanthan, psyllium); dairy-free butter
  substitutes differ in water content; nuts replaced by seeds; the 14 allergen groups checked on every label.
- **Predicting the change:** written before cooking; checked against the result.

**Sessions:**
1. Three substitutions by function (60 min): for example egg in a batter, butter in a crumble, cream in a sauce; each
   predicted, cooked against the original, compared.

**Cues:** the prediction names the function lost and how it is replaced; the result is close to the original.

**Faults:**
- Swapping like for like by name (any flour for any flour).
- Forgetting that a substitute carries an allergen of its own.

#### RDV-4.M3.L2 — Adapting to equipment, scale and season

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SLW-3.M4.L2` · moves RD-ADPT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] adapt a recipe for a different oven, pan, quantity or season; [J] predict what changes
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Another oven:** oven thermometer checks (`EQP-1`); fan ovens about 20 °C lower than conventional; hot spots turned
  around.
- **Another pan:** a wider pan reduces faster and browns more; a smaller pan crowds; cast iron holds heat, stainless
  responds faster.
- **Another quantity:** weights scaled, but salt and spices adjusted by taste, cooking times by thickness rather than
  weight, and batches split when the pan or oven can't take them (`KM-MATH`).
- **Another season:** a summer dish moved to winter keeps its idea with the vegetables of the season (courgette for
  celeriac, fresh herbs for dried or for parsley root), and its cooking adjusts (roasting rather than raw).
- **[J] Predicting what changes:** written before cooking (time, texture, browning, seasoning), checked after.

**Sessions:**
1. Twice the quantity (60 min): a household dish doubled, with the predicted changes written and checked.
2. Another season (60 min): a dish from the other half of the year adapted to the current season; tasted against the
   memory or notes of the original.

**Cues:** predictions that hold, or a clear reason why not.

**Faults:**
- Doubling the salt and the time.
- Keeping a summer dish's raw method for winter roots.

### RDV-4.M4 — Test plans and iteration

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-rdv-4-m4 -->
*5.5 h · 2 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches RD-TEST 4, RD-WRIT 3*

*Scope:* test design; iteration and version control.

*Objectives:* [S] plan tests with controls and blind panels; [J] decide between versions.

*Check (outline):* the test log (objective structure); panel results.
<!-- END generated:module-rdv-4-m4 -->

#### RDV-4.M4.L1 — Designing tests

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M1.L1` · moves RD-TEST→4*

*Objectives:* [S] one variable at a time, controls, blind panels, scoring forms
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **One variable at a time:** each round changes one thing (the salt, the time, the fat), so the result can be traced to
  it.
- **Controls:** the unchanged version cooked alongside, so day-to-day differences (the oven, the ingredients) don't hide
  the effect.
- **Blind panels** (`TST-1`, [S20]): samples coded with numbers, served in a random order, tasters scoring on a form; a
  triangle test to check whether a difference can be tasted at all.
- **Scoring forms:** the attributes that matter for the dish (texture, seasoning, aroma), each on a fixed scale, with
  room for comments.
- **Small batches:** tests at a small scale save food; the final version is cooked at full size.

**Sessions:**
1. A test design (60 min): a test plan for one variable with a control and a blind panel; run once on a small batch
   (for example a vinaigrette at two acid levels).

**Cues:** a plan with one variable, a control, coded samples and a form; results that can be read.

**Faults:**
- Changing the recipe and the oven at the same time.
- Tasters who know which sample is which.

#### RDV-4.M4.L2 — Iteration

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m4-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 wk · builds on `RDV-4.M4.L1` · moves RD-TEST→4, RD-WRIT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] document versions; [J] decide what to change next and when to stop
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Version control:** each version written in full with a number and date, what changed, why, and the result; the
  log kept with photos.
- **[J] Deciding what to change next:** the panel's comments sorted by how often they appear and how much they matter;
  the largest problem tested first.
- **[J] When to stop:** when the panel can't tell the new version from the last (a triangle test fails), when the
  remaining comments contradict each other, or when the dish meets its aim.
- **Writing so it repeats:** weights, cues and timings in the final recipe, cooked again a week later to check it
  gives the same result.

**Sessions:**
1. Round two (60 min): the second version from round one's results; panel and log.
2. Round three and the write-up (60 min): the third version; the decision written; the recipe written in full.

**Cues:** a log that shows why each change was made and what it did.

**Faults:**
- Testing until the cook likes it, ignoring the panel.
- Losing the earlier versions.

#### RDV-4.M4.X1 — Improve one dish through three versions

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-rdv-4-m4-x1 -->
*project, brief* — Improve one dish through at least three documented versions.
<!-- END generated:exercise-rdv-4-m4-x1 -->

**Evidences:** RD-TEST→4, RD-WRIT→3

**Aim:** improve a dish through a documented test series, and write recipes that repeat.

**Conditions:** brief support: the test-plan template. One dish of the learner's repertoire improved through at least

two other recipes of the learner's written with weights, cues and timings, and each repeated by the learner a week later
to the same result, as the `RD-WRIT` level 3 row asks.

**Inputs:** the dish's ingredients for small test batches; the panel (household or friends).

**Procedure:**
1. Write the aim and the test plan.
2. Run three or more rounds; code the samples; collect the forms; log each version.

#### RDV-4.M5.L1 — Writing for others

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KMG-3.M5.L1` · moves RD-WRIT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] precise quantities, ordered steps, cues, yields, timings; [J] test with another cook
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The recipe's parts:**
  - title and yield (portions and weight);
  - ingredients in the order of use, by weight, with the state (diced, softened) and the allergens marked;
  - equipment that matters (pan size, a thermometer);
  - steps in order, one action each, with the cue that ends the step (golden, coats a spoon, 63 °C at the core) and
    the time as a range;
  - make-ahead and storage notes within assessment §9.
- **Writing for another cook:** no unexplained terms (`CK-TERM`); cues before times; the safety figure where there is
  one.
- **[J] Testing with another cook:** a household member cooks from the text alone while the writer watches silently,
  noting every hesitation; the recipe is rewritten where they hesitated.

**Sessions:**
1. A recipe rewritten (60 min): one of the learner's recipes written for another cook.
2. The silent test (60 min): a household member cooks it; the hesitations noted; the recipe revised.

**Cues:** another cook's result matches the writer's, and their hesitations led to clear changes.

**Faults:**
- "Season to taste" without a starting amount.
- Times without cues.
- Steps that combine three actions.

#### RDV-4.M5.L2 — An original dish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-4-m5-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 wk · builds on `RDV-4.M4.L2` · moves CR-ORIG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] idea, test plan, iterations, final recipe; [J] what makes it yours
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-4-m5-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The idea:** one sentence (`CR-COMP`): a season, a memory, a technique, an ingredient pairing; Dutch produce in
  season as the starting point.
- **The test plan:** what is uncertain about the idea, and how two or more rounds will settle it (`RDV-4.M4`).
- **Iterations:** the dish cooked, judged by tasters, changed one thing at a time, logged.
- **The final recipe:** written for others (`RDV-4.M5.L1`).
- **[J] What makes it yours:** not novelty for its own sake, but a choice of ingredients, technique or balance that

**Sessions:**
1. Idea and first round (60 min): the idea and test plan written; the first version cooked and judged.
2. Second round (60 min): the second version from the results; judged; the recipe written.

**Cues:** a logged development, a final recipe and a note on what makes it the learner's own.

**Faults:**
- An idea with too many parts.
- Changing everything between rounds.
