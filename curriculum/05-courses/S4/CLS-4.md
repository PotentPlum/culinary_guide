## Introduction

S2 taught the mother sauces and S3 the regional French kitchen (`CUI-3.M2`). `CLS-4` teaches the classical system that
sits behind most restaurant cooking in Europe: Escoffier's mother sauces and their derivatives, the classical cuts and
vegetable garnishes, consommé, the named preparations, and how *nouvelle cuisine* and today's cooking grew out of it
[S11][S12]. The learner leaves able to:
- **read and write the language:** decode a classical menu item into its components, and name their own dishes
  correctly;
- **cut to the classical standard:** *tourné*, *parisienne* and a fine *brunoise*, measured;
- **make the sauces:** a home-scaled demi-glace, and derivatives of brown sauce, *velouté*, the butter emulsions and
  tomato sauce, finished to a refined standard;
- **clarify:** a consommé clear enough to read print through;
- **judge the classics:** what to keep and what to lighten.

The repertoire is cooked with Dutch produce: beef, veal and chicken bones from a butcher, North Sea sole or plaice, Dutch
eggs and butter, and seasonal vegetables for the garnishes (carrots, turnips, celeriac, pearl onions, peas and beans in
summer). Citrus for *maltaise* is a pantry good, as in S2 and S3.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-SAU` and `FL-SEAS` reach level 5 across the sauce services of `CLS-4.M3`; `MS-S4` re-tests them at its service
  (`MS-S4.M1.L4`, `MS-S4.M1.L5`).
- `TS-STK` level 4 begins in S3 with stocks adapted to the bones available and is **completed here**, by the consommé of
  `CLS-4.M4.X1`.
- `TS-KNF` reaches level 5 in the measured drill of `CLS-4.M2.X1`, re-tested at `MS-S4.M1.L7`.
- `TS-VEG` reaches level 5 in five vegetable-led courses (`CLS-4.M2.X2`).
- `CK-CLAS` and `CK-TERM` reach level 4: the names, the quiz, three preparations cooked from their names alone
  (`CLS-4.M5.X1`) and a modern adaptation (`CLS-4.M6.X1`).

**Pacing.** About six weeks at 6.5 hours a week. The knife drill and the vegetable courses run over eight weeks, so they
start in the first week and continue alongside the next S4 course. The brown stock for the demi-glace and the stock for
the consommé are made together and frozen in portions.

#### CLS-4.M1.L1 — Escoffier's system

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M1.L1` · moves CK-CLAS→3, CK-TERM→4*

*Objectives:* [K] the brigade, codified recipes, mother sauces and derivatives; why the system mattered [S11]
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The brigade** (*brigade de cuisine*): the kitchen organised into stations under a chef, the *saucier*, *poissonnier*,
  *rôtisseur*, *entremetier* (vegetables and eggs), *garde-manger* (cold kitchen) and *pâtissier*. It is why classical
  recipes are built from components made by different people and assembled at service.
- **Codified recipes:** Escoffier's *Le Guide culinaire* (1903) [S11] wrote down the repertoire so that a dish with a
  name was the same dish in every kitchen. A name is a promise of components.
- **Mother sauces and derivatives:** five bases (*espagnole*, *velouté*, *béchamel*, *sauce tomate* and hollandaise as
  the emulsion family), each giving many sauces by adding a reduction, a garnish or a finish. Learning the logic is
  learning hundreds of sauces at once.
- **Why the system mattered:** it made large kitchens reliable, let cooks move between them, and gave a shared
  vocabulary that menus still use.
- **What it costs at home:** a classical sauce rests on a good stock made days ahead; the home version keeps the logic
  and scales the quantities down (`CLS-4.M3.L1`).

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** the learner can name the five mother sauces, the base of each and two derivatives of each.

**Faults:**
- Treating the names as decoration rather than a list of components.
- Thinking a derivative needs its own long recipe, rather than a mother sauce plus an addition.

#### CLS-4.M1.L2 — Classical terminology

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CLS-4.M1.L1` · moves CK-TERM→4, CK-CLAS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] garnish names (*à la …*), preparations and menu French
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Garnish names** (*à la …*, *… à la façon de …*): a name stands for a fixed garnish. Examples: *à la bordelaise*
  (red wine and shallot, often bone marrow), *chasseur* (mushrooms, shallot, white wine, tomato), *Du Barry*
  (cauliflower), *Crécy* (carrots), *Parmentier* (potatoes), *florentine* (spinach), *bonne femme* (mushrooms, onions,
  potatoes or a white-wine sauce, by the dish), *meunière* (floured, cooked in butter, finished with brown butter, lemon
  and parsley).
- **Preparations:** *poché*, *braisé*, *rôti*, *sauté*, *poêlé* (cooked covered in butter), *en papillote*, *au
  gratin*, *glacé*.
- **Menu French:** the order (main item, method, garnish), the article and agreement (*suprême de volaille*, *filet de
  sole*), and when a modern menu uses the French and when plain words work better.
- **Decoding:** a menu item broken into its protein, cut, method, sauce and garnish, and from that into the techniques
  needed.

**Sessions:**
1. Decoding practice (45 min): five classical menu items decoded into components and techniques; checked against the
   reference glossary; the mistakes noted.

**Cues:** each component and technique named; the decoding agrees with the glossary.

**Faults:**
- Guessing a garnish from the sound of the word.
- Mixing French and English in one menu line.
- Using a classical name for a dish that does not have its components.

#### CLS-4.M2.L1 — Precision cuts

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m2-l1 -->
*3 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 4 wk · builds on `KNF-2.M1.L1` · moves TS-KNF→5*

*Objectives:* [S] *tourné*, *parisienne*, *brunoise fine* to tolerance at speed
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The *tourné*:** a barrel shape about 5 cm long with seven even faces and flat ends, cut with a turning knife (or a
  paring knife) in single curved strokes from end to end. The piece is first cut into even blocks, so the faces come
  out equal. It is a lesson in control more than a garnish to make every day.
- **The *parisienne*:** balls scooped with a melon baller, the scoop pressed in, turned a full circle and lifted out;
  from potatoes, carrots, celeriac or turnips.
- **Fine *brunoise*:** 1–2 mm cubes, from a fine *julienne* cut across; the knife-work standard of
  [`TS-KNF` level 5](../../04-competencies/TS.md).
- **Speed with tolerance:** the onion small dice (`KNF-2`) under 90 seconds without losing size; speed comes from the
  grip and the rhythm, not from hurrying.
- **Using the trim:** the trim of *tourné* and *parisienne* goes to purées, soups and stocks.
- **The teaching note:** writing down how the grip and guiding hand work, for someone else, makes the learner's own
  technique explicit.

**Sessions:**
1. *Tourné* (60 min): carrots and potatoes cut into blocks, then *tourné*; the best five measured and photographed on
   the grid; the trim to a soup.
2. *Parisienne* and fine *brunoise* (60 min): celeriac *parisienne*; carrot and shallot fine *brunoise*, measured on the
   grid.
3. The timed set (45 min): an onion small-diced against the clock, a fine *brunoise* and a *tourné*, filmed, at least
   four weeks after session 1.

**Cues:**
- Seven faces that meet at clean edges, and flat ends that let the piece stand.
- Fine *brunoise* cubes that look like grains, with no long pieces.
- Steady rhythm on the timed onion.

**Faults:**
- Facets cut with sawing strokes, leaving steps.
- Blocks of uneven size, so the barrels differ.
- A *brunoise* cut from a *julienne* of uneven thickness.

#### CLS-4.M2.L2 — Classical vegetable garnishes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-3.M3.L1` · moves TS-VEG→5*

*Objectives:* [S] glazed vegetables (*glacés à blanc / à brun*), *jardinière*, *printanière*; [J] timing several garnishes together
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Glazing** (*glacer*), from `VEG-3.M3.L1`:
  - *à blanc*: the vegetables cut evenly, barely covered with water, a little butter, sugar and salt, cooked uncovered
    until tender as the liquid reduces to a shiny syrup that coats them, without colour (carrots, turnips, pearl
    onions);
  - *à brun*: the same, taken on until the syrup caramelises lightly and the vegetables are rolled in it (pearl onions,
    chestnut-coloured turnips).
- **The classical garnishes:** *jardinière* (batons of carrot and turnip, peas, green beans), *printanière* (spring
  vegetables, turned or cut small), *bouquetière* (vegetables arranged in small bunches), *Vichy* (carrot rounds
  glazed).
- **[J] Timing several garnishes together:** each vegetable cooked on its own (they cook at different speeds), held
  just under done, and finished together in butter at service; a written order of work backwards from service.
- **Seasonal choices:** spring carrots and peas, summer beans, autumn turnips and celeriac, winter pearl onions and root
  vegetables.

**Sessions:**
1. Glazing (60 min): carrots *à blanc* and pearl onions *à brun*; the glaze judged on a spoon.
2. A garnish for service (75 min): a *jardinière* of three vegetables cooked separately and finished together for a
   meal, timed from a written plan.

**Cues:**
- A glaze that clings, shiny, with no liquid left in the pan.
- Each vegetable tender with a slight bite, and the colours bright.
- Everything ready at the same moment.

**Faults:**
- Too much water, so the vegetables are soft before the glaze forms.
- Mixed vegetables cooked in one pan, some raw and some mushy.
- *À brun* taken too far, so the sugar tastes bitter.

#### CLS-4.M3.L1 — *Espagnole* and a domestic demi-glace

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m3-l1 -->
*3.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 d · builds on `STK-2.M2.L3` · moves TS-SAU→4, TS-STK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a brown sauce and a demi-glace scaled for home; [K] the modern shortcut (reduced brown stock)
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The brown stock first** (`STK-2.M2.L3`): veal or beef bones roasted dark, with *mirepoix* and tomato purée, simmered
  long; adapted to the bones available (veal knuckle gives the most gelatin; beef shin and oxtail work; a chicken
  carcass makes a lighter brown stock). Cooled by the rule of assessment §9 and frozen in portions.
- **The *espagnole*:** brown stock thickened with a brown roux, with a browned *mirepoix* and tomato, simmered and
  skimmed for about two hours, then strained.
- **The classical demi-glace:** *espagnole* and brown stock in equal parts, reduced by half, skimmed, strained through a
  fine sieve: glossy, coating a spoon, deeply savoury.
- **Scaled for home:** 1 litre of each makes about 1 litre of demi-glace; frozen in ice-cube portions, it lasts for many
  sauces.
- **[K] The modern shortcut:** a brown stock reduced without flour until it coats a spoon (*jus* or *fond lié* thickened
  lightly with starch); clearer and lighter than the classical sauce, and what most kitchens use today. Both are
  made in this lesson to taste the difference.
- **Safety:** the stock and the sauce cooled within the time limits of assessment §9 (57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours,
  to 5 °C within 6 hours), in shallow containers or an ice bath, and frozen or kept at most 2 days in the fridge.

**Sessions:**
1. A home demi-glace (over two days): day 1, brown stock from the available bones; day 2, the *espagnole* and the
   demi-glace; a reduced-stock *jus* alongside; both cooled and logged; tasted side by side.

**Cues:**
- A deep brown, clear stock.
- A demi-glace that coats the back of a spoon and holds a clean line.
- The cooling log within the limits.

**Faults:**
- Pale, under-roasted bones, giving a weak colour.
- A stock boiled hard, so it is cloudy and greasy.
- The roux burnt, so the sauce is bitter.
- A big pot left to cool on the stove overnight.

#### CLS-4.M3.L2 — Brown sauce derivatives

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m3-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CLS-4.M3.L1` · moves TS-SAU→5, CK-CLAS→4, CK-TERM→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *bordelaise*, *chasseur*, *poivrade*, *Robert*; [K] the derivative logic and how the names are built
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The derivative logic:** a reduction (wine, vinegar, shallot, herbs) + the mother sauce + a finish (butter,
  herbs, a garnish). The name tells which:
  - *bordelaise*: red wine and shallot reduced, demi-glace, thyme; poached bone-marrow slices (optional);
  - *chasseur*: mushrooms and shallot sautéed, white wine, demi-glace, tomato, tarragon and parsley;
  - *poivrade*: *mirepoix* and vinegar reduced, demi-glace, crushed peppercorns at the end (for game, `PRO-4.M2`);
  - *Robert*: onion cooked soft, white wine reduced, demi-glace, mustard off the heat (for pork).
- **How names are built:** the place or person (*bordelaise*, *Robert*), the cook's role (*chasseur*, the hunter), or
  an ingredient (*poivrade*, pepper).
- **Finishing:** *monter au beurre* (cold butter swirled in off the heat for gloss); the final reduction judged by
  *nappe*.
- **Portion:** 40–60 ml of a reduced sauce per plate; a derivative made for two from 150 ml of demi-glace.

**Sessions:**
1. *Bordelaise* and *Robert* (60 min): both made from the frozen demi-glace, for a steak and for pork chops; *nappe*
   photographed.
2. *Chasseur* and *poivrade* (60 min): for chicken and for a game cut or a beef cut.

**Cues:**
- Each sauce tastes of its addition, not only of the demi-glace.
- A glossy *nappe*, without a skin.
- Seasoning adjusted at the end.

**Faults:**
- The wine not reduced, so the sauce tastes raw and thin.
- Mustard boiled, so it turns bitter.
- Butter added over the heat, so the sauce turns greasy.

*Objectives:* [S] *suprême*, *allemande*, *bercy*; [J] finish with cream, butter or liaison
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The *velouté*** (`SAU-2.M3.L3`): a white chicken, veal or fish stock thickened with a blond roux, simmered and

- **The derivatives:**

  - *bercy*: fish *velouté* with shallot and white wine reduced, finished with butter and parsley.
- **[J] The finish:**

#### CLS-4.M4.L1 — Clarifying with a raft

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m4-l1 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 h · builds on `STK-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-STK→4, SC-PROT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] the *clarification* raft (lean mince, egg white, *mirepoix*), gentle simmer, straining; [K] protein trapping
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The clarification mix:** lean minced beef (or chicken for a chicken consommé), egg whites, a finely chopped
  *mirepoix*, tomato and herbs, mixed cold into cold, fat-free stock.
- **[K] Protein trapping:** as the stock heats slowly, the egg-white and meat proteins unfold and set into a mesh that
  rises and forms a raft; the fine particles that cloud the stock are caught in it. The mince also adds flavour, and
  the *mirepoix* aroma.
- **Method:**
  1. The stock fully degreased, cold.
  2. The mix stirred in, brought up slowly while stirring gently on the bottom until the raft begins to form (around
     50–60 °C), then left alone.
  3. A hole made in the raft so the liquid can bubble through it gently; simmered at a bare tremble for about an hour.
  4. Ladled out through the hole and strained through a cloth-lined sieve; fat lifted with paper.
- **Why it fails:** boiling breaks the raft; stirring after it forms clouds the stock again; fat in the stock dulls the
  shine.
- **Safety:** the mince is raw meat (its own board and hands washed, `SAF-1`); the consommé served hot, or cooled by the
  rule of assessment §9 and kept as leftovers.

**Sessions:**
1. A beef consommé (3 h with simmering): from frozen brown stock; clarity photographed through a glass over print.

**Cues:**
- A raft that holds together and rises.
- Liquid through the hole that is clear and amber.
- Print legible through a glass of the consommé.

**Faults:**
- Warm or greasy stock at the start.
- A rolling boil that breaks the raft.
- Stirring after the raft has formed.

#### CLS-4.M4.L2 — Gelatin clarity and aspic introduced

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `CLS-4.M4.L1` · moves SC-PROT→4*

*Objectives:* [K] gelatin strength and clarity; [S] a small aspic or jelly
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Gelatin strength and clarity:** the gelatin from bones (collagen dissolved in long cooking) or added sheets sets a
  liquid as it cools. More gelatin makes a firmer set; a clear liquid gives a clear jelly. A consommé that sets
  softly in the fridge already has natural gelatin.
- **Sheets:** soaked in cold water, squeezed and dissolved in warm (not boiling) liquid; a soft set from about 1.5–2
  sheets per 250 ml, a firm one for unmoulding with more; the exact strength depends on the brand (bloom).
- **Aspic** (*aspic*, *gelée*): clarified stock set with gelatin, used to coat or hold cold preparations; today mostly a
  small jelly or a glaze.
- **Testing the set:** a spoonful set on a cold plate in the fridge for 10 minutes.
- **Safety:** an aspic is a cooked, chilled food: set in the fridge, kept at 5 °C or below, eaten within 2 days
  (assessment §9).

**Sessions:**
1. A small aspic (60 min active, set overnight): consommé set with gelatin around seasonal vegetables in small moulds;
   the set tested first; unmoulded and photographed.

**Cues:**
- A set that holds its shape but melts in the mouth.
- The vegetables visible through the jelly.

**Faults:**
- Gelatin boiled, so it weakens.
- Too much gelatin, so it is rubbery.
- A cloudy stock, giving a cloudy jelly.

#### CLS-4.M6.L1 — Lightening the classics

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cls-4-m6-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CLS-4.M5.L2` · moves CK-CLAS→4, FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [K] *nouvelle cuisine* and after; [J] what to keep and what to change in a classic dish
<!-- END generated:lesson-cls-4-m6-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] *Nouvelle cuisine* and after** [S12]: from the 1960s and 1970s, cooks such as Bocuse, the Troisgros brothers
  and Guérard turned away from flour-thickened sauces and long cooking towards reductions, shorter cooking, fresh
  produce, the menu written daily and the plate composed in the kitchen. Later came the regional, seasonal and
  technical kitchens of today, which still use the classical logic.
- **What changed:**
  - sauces: roux-thickened *espagnole* gave way to reduced stocks, *jus*, butter sauces and vegetable sauces;
  - cooking: shorter for fish and vegetables; precise for meat;
  - plates: one plate composed by the cook instead of platters served at the table;
  - produce: the season and the place named on the menu.
- **[J] What to keep and what to change:** keep the logic (stock, reduction, balance of sauce and garnish, the dish's
  identity); change the weight (less flour and cream, more acidity and vegetables), the portion and the plate. A modern
  version is still recognisably the classic.
- **A Dutch modern classic:** a sole with brown butter, sea vegetables (*zeekraal*) and a few seasonal potatoes is
  *meunière* with the season named.

**Sessions:**
1. A lightened classic (90 min): one classical dish of the course cooked twice, classically and in a lighter version
   (for example *suprême* with a reduced *jus* and vegetable purée instead of a cream sauce); tasted side by side.

**Cues:** the lighter version is still recognisable as the classic; tasters can say what was kept.

**Faults:**
- Changing so much that the dish loses its identity.
- Lightening by removing flavour (less sauce) rather than weight.
