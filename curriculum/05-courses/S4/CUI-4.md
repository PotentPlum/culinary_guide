## Introduction

`CUI-3` studied eight traditions through one lens, a few dishes each. `CUI-4` goes deep into **three traditions the
learner chooses**, from `CUI-3` or new ones. In each, the learner moves from cooking dishes to cooking *from
principles*: the extended pantry and its regions, two signature techniques practised to a high standard, and a regional
meal designed and cooked without recipes.

Every deep dive follows the same 14-hour template, one module per tradition:
1. **Pantry and regions in depth** (`L1`): the extended pantry, regional variation, seasonality, history and where to
   buy it in the Netherlands.
2. **Signature technique I** (`L2`) and **II** (`L3`): the techniques that define the tradition, each practised twice.
3. **A regional menu** (`L4`): a three-dish meal of one region, designed and cooked without recipes, with a written
   rationale through the `CUI-3` lens.

The three modules differ in what they add:
- `CUI-4.M1` uses the catalogue below as its guide;
- `CUI-4.M2` adds a written comparison of the first two traditions;
- `CUI-4.M3` is planned **without the catalogue**: the learner writes their own catalogue entry from sources before
  cooking, which also makes a tradition outside the catalogue possible.

**Respect and adaptation,** as in `CUI-3`: each tradition is regional and alive; adaptations to a Dutch kitchen are
allowed and named in every exercise, with their effect:
- Dutch seasonal vegetables stand in for produce that is out of season or flown in;
- pantry goods (spices, rice, noodles, pulses, dried chillies, *masa harina*, olive oil, citrus) come from specialist
  shops: Turkish, Moroccan and Surinamese shops, Asian supermarkets and *tokos*, Italian and Spanish delicatessens, and
  online;
- meat and fish are Dutch or North Sea, by the rules of the earlier courses;
- nuts are replaced by seeds by default, as in S3, with the allergen rule where a learner uses them.

**Safety** follows assessment §9 in every tradition: forcemeats and minced fillings to the minced row (68 °C for
17 seconds or a row of its table), poultry to 75 °C or the poultry table, fish to 63 °C or, for raw fish in *sushi*,
only fish frozen first by the fish row and never for risk groups; cooked rice and stocks cooled by the cooling rule;
frying by `HEA-2.M4`; allergens named for guests.

**In the spiral.**
- `CK-CUIS` reaches level 4: three regional meals cooked from principles with written rationales, and a written
  comparison of two traditions (`CUI-4.M2.X2`).
- `FL-ARCH` level 4: in each deep dive, the second practice of each technique is a new combination with a written
  prediction.
- `IN-ARO` level 4: a spice blend composed for each tradition and judged balanced by the tasters.
- `JD-CHSE` level 4: in each regional menu, two dishes recreated from a description alone, with a plan and a fallback.
- `MS-S4` may take its theme from a `CUI-4` tradition (`MS-S4.M1.L1`).

**Pacing.** About seven weeks at 6.5 hours a week, roughly two weeks per tradition, with the regional meal at the end
of each, often with guests. The three traditions can be spread through S4 between other courses, so each is cooked in a
suitable season.

### Deep-dive catalogue

For each `CUI-3` tradition: the regions to choose from, the extended pantry, the two signature techniques, a regional
menu frame, the special equipment (Good and Best) and the usual Dutch substitutions. The techniques go beyond what
`CUI-3` taught.

**French regional** (from `CUI-3.M2`)
- *Regions:* Burgundy, Alsace, Normandy and Brittany, Provence, the South-West.
- *Extended pantry:* regional fats (butter, cream, duck fat, olive oil), *crème fraîche*, Dijon mustard, *fines herbes*,
  cider and *calvados* for cooking, regional cheeses, *herbes de Provence*, wine for cooking.
- *Technique I:* The regional braise. *Daube*, *bœuf bourguignon*, *coq au vin*: marinating, browning, braising in wine,
  the garnish cooked separately, the sauce finished (`SLW-3`, `CLS-4.M3`).
- *Technique II:* *Pâtés* and *terrines*. A forcemeat by ratio (meat, fat, salt about 1.5–1.8 %, a panade or egg),
  seasoned and tested by poaching a spoonful, baked in a water bath to the minced row of §9 at the core (68 °C for
  17 seconds; 75 °C with poultry), pressed, cooled by the cooling rule, kept at most 2 days or frozen.
- *Menu frame:* a regional starter (a *terrine*, a vegetable *velouté*), a braise or roast with its garnish, a regional
  dessert (a *tarte Tatin* in Normandy, a *kugelhopf* in Alsace).
- *Equipment:* terrine mould (Good: a loaf tin; Best: an enamelled cast-iron *terrine* with a press).
- *Substitutions:* Dutch apples and cider for Normandy; Dutch white asparagus in spring for Alsace; Dutch beef and
  chicken for the braises.

**Italian** (from `CUI-3.M3`)
- *Regions:* Piedmont and Lombardy, Emilia-Romagna, Tuscany, Campania, Sicily.
- *Extended pantry:* *Carnaroli* and *Arborio* rice, *polenta*, *Parmigiano Reggiano* and *Grana*, *pecorino*, cured
  pork (*guanciale*, *pancetta*), anchovies, capers, olive oils, *tipo 00* flour, dried porcini.
- *Technique I:* Risotto and polenta. Toasting the rice, adding hot stock in stages, stirring, the *mantecatura* with
  butter and cheese off the heat, the wave (*all'onda*); soft and grilled polenta.
- *Technique II:* Pizza and focaccia. High-hydration doughs (`BRD-3`), a long cold ferment, shaping by hand, baking on a
  preheated steel at the oven's highest setting.
- *Menu frame:* an *antipasto*, a *primo* (risotto or pasta), a *secondo* with a *contorno*, a *dolce*, from one region.
- *Equipment:* baking steel (see `BRD-2`); a wide heavy pan for risotto.
- *Substitutions:* Dutch pumpkin, chicory, kale and mushrooms in autumn; Dutch summer tomatoes, otherwise
  tinned.

**Iberian** (from `CUI-3.M4`)
- *Regions:* Asturias and Galicia, Castile, Catalonia, Andalusia; Portugal's Minho and Lisbon.
- *Extended pantry:* dried beans and chickpeas, *chorizo*, *morcilla*, *pimentón* (sweet, hot, smoked), saffron, sherry
  vinegar, salt cod (*bacalao*, *bacalhau*), olive oils, *piquillo* peppers.
- *Technique I:* Slow pulse stews. *Fabada*, *cocido*, *caldo verde*: soaking and cooking the pulses (the bean row of
  §9 for common beans), layering the meats, the broth served as a separate course.
- *Technique II:* Salt cod and *escabeche*. Desalting salt cod in the fridge for 24–48 hours with the water changed
  several times, then gentle cooking (*bacalhau à Brás*, *brandada*); *escabeche* of cooked fish or poultry in a warm
  vinegar and oil marinade, kept cold and eaten within 2 days.
- *Menu frame:* a *tapa* or soup, a stew or fish main, a dessert (*crema catalana*, *pastéis de nata* introduced).
- *Equipment:* a clay *cazuela* (Good: a heavy casserole; Best: a glazed *cazuela* used with a heat diffuser).
- *Substitutions:* Dutch cabbage and kale for *caldo verde* greens; Dutch potatoes throughout; North Sea fish for the
  *escabeche*.

**Levantine and North African** (from `CUI-3.M5`)
- *Regions:* Lebanon and Syria, Palestine and Jordan, Turkey's south-east, Morocco, Tunisia.
- *Extended pantry:* *bulgur*, *freekeh*, couscous, chickpeas and lentils, *tahini*, *sumac*, *za'atar*, Aleppo and
  Urfa pepper, *ras el hanout*, *harissa*, preserved lemons, *pomegranate molasses*, orange-blossom and rose water.
- *Technique I:* Couscous by hand. Semolina rolled with water into grains, steamed two or three times over a stew in a
  couscoussier, fluffed with butter or oil between steamings.
- *Technique II:* Flatbreads. Pita (a hot steel puffs the pocket), *msemen* (laminated with butter or oil),
  *manakish* with *za'atar*.
- *Menu frame:* a *mezze* spread or a *harira*, a couscous or tagine, a dessert or fruit with mint tea.
- *Equipment:* couscoussier (Good: a steamer basket over a pot, the seam sealed with a cloth; Best: a couscoussier);
  tagine (Good: a heavy casserole; Best: a glazed tagine with a diffuser).
- *Substitutions:* Dutch pumpkin, carrots, turnips and courgettes in the couscous; Dutch summer peppers and aubergine;
  pomegranate molasses as a pantry good.

**Indian subcontinent** (from `CUI-3.M6`)
- *Regions:* Punjab, Gujarat, Bengal, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, Goa.
- *Extended pantry:* whole and ground spices, *ghee*, mustard oil, pulses (*urad*, *chana*, *toor*, *moong*), basmati and
  short-grain rice, *atta*, *besan*, *jaggery*, tamarind, curry leaves, *asafoetida*, coconut.
- *Technique I:* The *tandoor* adapted for home. Yoghurt and spice marinades; chicken (to 75 °C) and paneer cooked under
  a very hot grill, on a steel or in a lidded grill; naan baked on a steel or a hot cast-iron pan.
- *Technique II:* Fermented batters. *Idli* and *dosa* from soaked, ground rice and *urad* dal, fermented overnight in a
  warm place, steamed or spread thin on a hot pan, with *sambar* and a coconut chutney.
- *Menu frame:* a regional meal (a Punjabi meal with *tandoori* dishes and *dal makhani*, or a South Indian meal around
  *dosa*), with rice or bread, a vegetable, a pickle or *raita*.
- *Equipment:* *tawa* (Good: a cast-iron skillet; Best: a flat iron *tawa*); a blender or wet grinder for batters.
- *Substitutions:* Dutch cauliflower, spinach, potatoes, peas and pumpkin; Dutch yoghurt; fresh coconut as a pantry
  good.

**Chinese** (from `CUI-3.M7`)
- *Regions:* Sichuan, Guangdong (Cantonese), Shandong and the north, Jiangsu and Shanghai, Hunan.
- *Extended pantry:* light and dark soy sauce, *Shaoxing* wine, black vinegar, *doubanjiang*, fermented black beans,
  Sichuan pepper, dried chillies, oyster sauce, rock sugar, star anise, wheat and rice noodles.
- *Technique I:* Hand-made noodles. Knife-cut and hand-pulled (*biangbiang*) noodles from a rested wheat dough, the
  dough stretched and torn; dressed with chilli oil and vinegar.
- *Technique II:* Red-braising and Sichuan seasoning. *Hong shao* pork belly with soy, sugar and *Shaoxing*; a master
  stock, which is brought to the boil, cooled by the cooling rule and frozen between uses; *mala* seasoning with
  Sichuan pepper and chilli.
- *Menu frame:* a family-style meal of one region: a cold dish, a braise or stir-fry, a vegetable, rice or noodles, a
  soup.
- *Equipment:* wok (see `CUI-3`); a long rolling pin for noodles.
- *Substitutions:* pointed cabbage, chard, spinach and Chinese cabbage grown in the Netherlands; Dutch pork.

**Japanese** (from `CUI-3.M8`)
- *Regions:* Kanto (Tokyo), Kansai (Osaka and Kyoto), Hokkaido, Kyushu.
- *Extended pantry:* *kombu*, *katsuobushi*, miso varieties, *mirin*, *sake* for cooking, rice vinegar, sushi rice,
  *nori*, *wasabi*, *shichimi*, *panko*, soba and udon.
- *Technique I:* Sushi rice and *sushi*. Washing, soaking and cooking the rice, seasoning with vinegar, sugar and salt,
  cooling while fanning; *maki* and *nigiri* shaped by hand. Fish served raw only if frozen first by the fish row of §9
  (or bought so treated) and never for risk groups; otherwise cooked fish (seared mackerel to 63 °C, cooked prawns),
  omelette (*tamagoyaki*) or vegetables. Sushi rice is made close to serving and is not out of the fridge longer
  than 2 hours (the leftovers row of §9).
- *Technique II:* *Tempura* and *agemono*. An ice-cold, barely mixed batter; oil at the right temperature checked with
  the thermometer; frying safety by `HEA-2.M4`; seasonal vegetables and fish.
- *Menu frame:* a seasonal set meal (*ichijū sansai*: soup and three dishes with rice) or a *sushi* and *tempura* meal.
- *Equipment:* sushi tub and mat (Good: a wide bowl and a mat or cling film; Best: a wooden *hangiri* and a *makisu*);
  frying pot (see `HEA-2`).
- *Substitutions:* Dutch radish, carrots, cucumbers, pumpkin, parsnips, mushrooms and leeks; North Sea
  mackerel and herring.

**Mexican** (from `CUI-3.M9`)
- *Regions:* Oaxaca, Puebla, the Yucatán, Jalisco, Mexico City.
- *Extended pantry:* dried chillies (*ancho*, *guajillo*, *pasilla*, *mulato*, *chipotle*), *masa harina*, Mexican
  oregano, *achiote*, Mexican chocolate, *piloncillo*, dried beans, pumpkin seeds (*pepitas*), sesame.
- *Technique I:* *Mole*. Dried chillies toasted and soaked, seeds and spices toasted, everything fried in stages and
  blended, simmered for an hour or more into a dense sauce; nuts replaced by seeds.
- *Technique II:* *Tamales*. *Masa* beaten with fat and stock until light, spread on soaked corn husks, filled, folded and
  steamed until the dough comes away from the husk; fillings with meat follow the minced or poultry rows.
- *Menu frame:* a regional meal: a soup or *antojito*, a *mole* or *tamales* with rice and beans, a dessert.
- *Equipment:* *comal* (Good: a cast-iron skillet; Best: a flat iron or clay *comal*); a steamer for *tamales* (see
  `HEA-1`); a tortilla press (see `CUI-3`).
- *Substitutions:* Dutch greenhouse tomatoes and peppers in summer, tinned tomatoes otherwise; Dutch pumpkin, sweet corn
  in season and courgettes.

**A tradition outside the catalogue** (for `CUI-4.M3` only): the learner writes the same entry from at least two
sources, checked against `CUI-3.M1.L2`'s reading method, before cooking.

#### CUI-4.M1.L1 — Pantry and regions in depth

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→4, IN-ARO→4*

*Objectives:* [K] the extended pantry, regional variation, seasonality, history; sourcing
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The extended pantry:** beyond the core pantry of `CUI-3`, the ingredients that make regional cooking possible
  (the catalogue entry); for each, what it does in the food, how it is stored and how long it keeps.
- **[K] Regional variation:** the tradition as a set of regions with their own climate, produce, fats and dishes; one
  region chosen for the menu of `CUI-4.M1.L4`.
- **[K] Seasonality:** the tradition's own calendar (what is cooked when) and how it maps onto the Dutch seasons; the
  deep dive placed in a season that suits the chosen region.
- **[K] History:** the trade, migration, religion and climate behind the region's food; one dish traced to its origins.
- **[K] Sourcing:** where each pantry item is bought in the Netherlands, at what price and quality (Good and Best), and
  what is substituted when it is not found.
- **The spice blend:** the tradition's characteristic blend or seasoning (*herbes de Provence*, *ras el hanout*, a
  *garam masala*, *shichimi*, a *mole* spice base), composed by the learner for a stated dish.

**Sessions:**
1. Pantry and blend (60 min): the extended pantry bought and tasted raw and cooked; the learner's own spice blend
   composed for a stated dish and tasted in it.

**Cues:**
- A written pantry map with sources and substitutions.
- A blend whose reason is written and whose taste is balanced in the dish.

**Faults:**
- Treating the tradition as one national cuisine.
- Buying the whole pantry list before knowing what the menu needs.
- A blend copied from a jar label.

#### CUI-4.M1.L2 — Signature technique I

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-4.M1.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] the tradition's defining technique (e.g. *mole*, hand-pulled noodles, *tagine*, *tandoor* adapted for home)
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The defining technique** of the tradition, from the catalogue (for example a regional braise, risotto, a slow pulse
  stew, couscous by hand, the *tandoor* adapted for home, hand-made noodles, *sushi*, *mole*).
- **The method and its cues:** the steps, the critical points (the moment the rice is right, the stage of the chillies,
  the feel of the dough), and the faults to expect, studied from at least two sources.
- **The high standard:** what the dish should look, smell and taste like when it is right, described in writing before
  cooking and checked against it after.
- **Practising twice:** the first time following the tradition closely; the second time as a new combination (a Dutch
  seasonal ingredient, a new filling or garnish) with a written prediction of its effect.
- **Safety:** the §9 row that applies to the technique, named in the plan (the catalogue entry gives it).

**Sessions:**
1. The technique as the tradition makes it (90 min): cooked from the method; checked against the written description of
   the standard.
2. A new combination (90 min): the same technique with a seasonal Dutch ingredient or a new element, the effect
   predicted in writing; tasters' cards and the form.

**Cues:**
- The result matches the written description of the standard.
- The prediction for the new combination is checked honestly.

**Faults:**
- Skipping the critical point because the clock says so.
- Changing the technique itself in the new combination, instead of an ingredient.

#### CUI-4.M1.L3 — Signature technique II

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l3 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-4.M1.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a second technique or a preparation family (e.g. dumplings, *sushi* rice, *mezze* breads)
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The second technique or a preparation family,** from the catalogue (for example *terrines*, pizza and focaccia,
  salt cod and *escabeche*, flatbreads, fermented batters, red-braising, *tempura*, *tamales*).
- **A family, not a dish:** the technique learned so that several dishes follow from it (several fillings, several
  shapes, several sauces).
- **Practising twice,** as in `CUI-4.M1.L2`: once close to the tradition, once as a new combination with a written
  prediction.
- **Safety:** the §9 row for the technique (for example forcemeats to the minced row, frying by `HEA-2.M4`, raw fish
  only frozen first).

**Sessions:**
1. The technique as the tradition makes it (90 min): cooked, photographed, checked against the standard.
2. A new combination (90 min): a second version with a predicted change; tasters' cards and the form.

**Cues:**
- Two dishes of the family to standard.
- The safety row met and logged where there is one.

**Faults:**
- Treating the family as a single recipe.
- A forcemeat or filling not probed.

#### CUI-4.M1.L4 — A regional menu

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l4 -->
*4.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `CUI-4.M1.L3` · moves JD-CHSE→4, CK-CUIS→4*

*Objectives:* [J] design and cook a three-dish regional meal without recipes
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] Designing a regional meal without recipes:**
  - three dishes from one region, chosen for balance (the `CUI-3` lens: pantry, techniques, flavour profile, meal
    structure);
  - at least two of them recreated from a description alone (a regional cookbook's description, a menu, a memory), with
    a written plan and a fallback for the riskiest step;
  - the seasonal Dutch substitutions named, with their effect;
  - the drinks the region serves with it (alcohol-free options for every guest, assessment §9).
- **The rationale:** a written page through the lens: why these dishes, how they fit the region, what was adapted.
- **The day plan:** what is made the day before (the elapsed day of the outline), what on the day, and the order of
  service.

**Sessions:**
1. The regional meal (2–3 h on the day, prepared the day before): cooked from the plan for the household or guests;
   photographed; tasters' cards with the description to hand.

**Cues:**
- The tasters recognise each recreated dish from its description.
- The rationale names the pantry, techniques and flavour logic and every adaptation.

**Faults:**
- Three dishes that compete (three rich braises).
- Recreating from a recipe found later instead of from the description.
- No fallback for the riskiest step.

#### CUI-4.M1.X1 — The two signature techniques, each practised twice

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-cui-4-m1-x1 -->
*framework, framework* — The two signature techniques, each practised twice.
<!-- END generated:exercise-cui-4-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** FL-ARCH→4, IN-ARO→4

**Aim:** cook the tradition's two signature techniques to a high standard, and extend each with a predicted new
combination.

**Conditions:** framework support: the catalogue entry and the technique framework. The two signature techniques of the
first tradition, each practised twice. The extensions:
- the second practice of each technique is a new combination with a written prediction, cooked and judged by tasters
  with the form, as the two combinations of the `FL-ARCH` level 4 row;
- the learner's spice blend of `CUI-4.M1.L1` composed for a stated dish with a written reason and judged balanced by the
  tasters, as the blend part of the `IN-ARO` level 4 row.

**Inputs:** the extended pantry; Dutch seasonal produce; meat or fish by the rules of the earlier courses.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the standard for each technique and the §9 row that applies.
2. Cook each technique once as the tradition makes it; photograph; compare with the standard.
3. Write the two new combinations with predictions; cook them; collect cards and the form.
4. Compose the blend for a stated dish; write the reason; collect the tasters' verdict.

**Evidence:** photo, written, tasters, form:
- **`FL-ARCH` level 4:** two new combinations composed with written predictions, cooked; the cards and the form show
  whether the predictions held.
- **`IN-ARO` level 4, the blend part:** a blend composed for a stated dish with a written reason, judged balanced by the
  tasters. The blind identification and the correction test were completed in S3 (`FLV-3.M1.X1`, `FLV-3.M2.X2` and the
  `MS-S3` test).

**Time:** 6 h over two weeks.

#### CUI-4.M1.X2 — The regional menu

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-cui-4-m1-x2 -->
*brief, brief* — The regional menu, with a written rationale through the `CUI-3` lens.
<!-- END generated:exercise-cui-4-m1-x2 -->

**Evidences:** CK-CUIS→4, JD-CHSE→4

**Aim:** plan and cook a regional meal from principles, without recipes.

**Conditions:** brief support: the brief and the catalogue entry. A three-dish meal of one region, with a written
rationale through the `CUI-3` lens. The extension: at least two of the dishes recreated from a description alone, each
with a written plan and a fallback, and the tasters asked whether they recognise them, as the `JD-CHSE` level 4 row
asks.

**Inputs:** the pantry; seasonal Dutch produce; the descriptions of the dishes.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the menu, the rationale and the plan with fallbacks.
2. Prepare the day before; cook and serve on the day.
3. Photograph each dish; collect tasters' cards with the descriptions to hand.
4. Note the adaptations and their effect.

**Evidence:** written, photo, tasters:
- **`CK-CUIS` level 4, meal one of three:** a three-course meal within one tradition cooked from principles, the
  rationale naming the pantry, techniques and flavour logic and marking the adaptations. The written comparison of two
  traditions is in `CUI-4.M2.X2`.
- **`JD-CHSE` level 4:** two dishes recreated from a description alone with a written plan and a fallback; the tasters
  recognise them.

**Time:** 4.5 h over two days.
