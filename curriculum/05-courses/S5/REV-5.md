## Introduction

By `MS-S4` the learner has a record of four stages: competency levels, evidence with dates, milestone bands and the
journal. That record is uneven. Some competencies were shown once, under one condition, a long time ago. Others passed
at *Developing* on a milestone row, and a few rest on evidence the learner and the reviews disagreed about. `REV-5` turns
the record into practice: the learner finds the two or three weakest competencies, practises them in three
deliberate blocks, and decides on the evidence whether each gap is closed before the capstone.

The course is personal. The outline names no competencies besides `JD-REFL`, because the gaps come from the learner's
own record (Phase 1 §10.3: evidence ages, and weak spots are revisited). So this file gives:
- **one template** for the gap analysis and the practice blocks (`REV-5.M1.L1`–`L4`);
- **a short catalogue** of worked gap types (below), following the pattern of `CUI-4`: the template does the work, and
  the catalogue shows it applied.

The new evidence for each gap goes into the record against that competency's own success row
([`04-competencies/`](../../04-competencies/)). In this file the exercise is tagged only `JD-REFL`, the competency the
outline moves.

**In the spiral.**
- `JD-REFL` reaches level 5 over S5: at least 8 weeks in which the learner's bands agree with the outside evidence
  within one band in 90 % of entries, and a candid, specific capstone defence. The three blocks of `REV-5` run over
  about nine weeks and log a band and an outside band at every session, so they hold most of that window.
  `RDV-5.M3.X1` adds the reflection on a personal style, and the capstone defence (`CAP-5.M3.L3`, scored by
  `CAP-5.R12`) completes the row.
- `REV-5` comes first in S5, so that its gap analysis steers `IND-5` and `RDV-5`. A gap in a technique the capstone
  must show (a stock- or emulsion-based sauce, a protein to an exact doneness, a bread or pastry element, a preserve, a
  vegetable-led course) is always a candidate.
- **Revision material:** the knowledge checks of S1–S4 (about 1,360 items) are the item bank for revision. The learner
  retakes the checks of the modules that taught each gap competency. The safety items are under review by a second
  reader before Phase 6 (overview §10.3); until that review, a safety item that disagrees with assessment §9 follows §9.

**Pacing.** Three blocks of three sessions, each block spread over about three weeks, so the course takes about ten
weeks of calendar time at about 1.5 hours a week, alongside `IND-5` and `RDV-5`.

### Gap catalogue

Each worked gap shows what the record shows, which lesson is revisited, how the practice varies one thing at a time
(Phase 1 §9.2), and what closes it. The first match in this order decides priority: a safety gap, then a capstone
requirement, then the oldest evidence, then the rest.

- **A safety competency** (`SA-TEMP`, for example).
  - *Record:* a cooling log with a missing reading, or a milestone gate passed only after a resit.
  - *Lesson revisited:* the cooling and storage lessons of `SAF-1` and `SAF-3` (`SAF-3.M1`).
  - *Practice:* three batches cooled and logged (a stock, a braise, cooked rice), varying the quantity.
  - *Closed when:* every reading is within assessment §9 and the `SA-TEMP` row is met, with no reading missing.
  - *Rule:* a safety gap is always block 1.
- **A technique for the capstone** (`TS-SAU`, for example).
  - *Record:* the emulsion of `MS-S2` and `MS-S4` at *Developing*, with a sauce that split once.
  - *Lesson revisited:* `SAU-2.M5.L1` and `CLS-4.M3`.
  - *Practice:* the warm emulsion made three times, varying the fat (clarified butter, whole butter, brown butter), then
    once held for service.
  - *Closed when:* the `TS-SAU` row at the learner's target level is met, with the photo judged *Competent* by the AI
    review.
- **A judgement** (`JD-CUES`, for example).
  - *Record:* cue-then-reading entries outside tolerance on roasts more than on pan work.
  - *Lesson revisited:* `PRO-4.M6.L1`.
  - *Practice:* ten roast predictions, varying the cut and the oven.
  - *Closed when:* at least 8 of 10 are within tolerance.
- **A technique with old evidence** (`TS-KNF`, for example).
  - *Record:* last shown in S2, more than six months ago, so it is up for review (Phase 1 §10.3).
  - *Lesson revisited:* `KNF-2`.
  - *Practice:* timed cuts against the grid, varying the vegetable.
  - *Closed when:* the row's tolerances are met again, with the new date in the record.
- **A self-assessment that disagrees** (any competency).
  - *Record:* two or more entries where the learner's band and the outside band differ by two bands (assessment §3.4).
  - *Lesson revisited:* the lesson of the competency, and `ORI-1` on scoring.
  - *Practice:* each session scored by the learner before seeing the outside band.
  - *Closed when:* the disagreements fall to one band or less.

#### REV-5.M1.L1 — Gap analysis

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `MS-S4` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [J] review the competency record and evidence age; pick the two or three weakest competencies; plan practice
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The record:** every competency with its level, the date and conditions of each piece of evidence, the milestone
  bands, and the journal entries in which the learner's band and the outside band (AI review, tasters, readings) were
  both written (`ORI-1`, assessment §3.4).
- **What counts as a gap:**
  - a competency below its stage target;
  - a milestone row passed at *Developing*;
  - evidence older than about six months (Phase 1 §10.3: it comes up for review, but it is not taken away);
  - a level reached under fewer conditions than its row asks for;
  - two or more entries with a two-band disagreement (assessment §3.4).
- **[J] Choosing two or three:** the catalogue's order (safety, capstone requirements, oldest evidence, the rest); then
  the competencies where practice costs least and helps most across dishes. More than three spreads the practice too
  thin.
- **The plan, per gap:** the competency and its success row at the target level; the lesson to revisit; the one
  variable that changes from session to session (Phase 1 §9.2); the condition of the test session; and what "closed"
  means, written before practice starts.
- **The agreement log:** from the first session of block 1, every entry of S5 has the learner's band, then the outside
  band. The share within one band is counted every two weeks; this is the `JD-REFL` level 5 measure.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:**
- Every chosen gap points to a line in the record, not to a feeling.
- The closing rule is written before practice starts.

**Faults:**
- Choosing the competencies the learner enjoys, not the weak ones.
- A gap described as "sauces" rather than as a competency and a level.
- No closing rule, so every gap stays open or is closed by hope.

#### REV-5.M1.L2 — Targeted practice, block 1

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l2 -->
*4.5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `REV-5.M1.L1` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [S] deliberate practice on the first gap, with the matching lesson revisited
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Deliberate practice:** a narrow target, repeated with feedback, one variable at a time; harder than ordinary cooking,
  and shorter.
- **The lesson revisited:** the lesson that taught the competency is reread before the first session, with its cues and
  faults, and its knowledge check is retaken from the item bank.
- **Feedback at every session:** a reading, a photo or a taster's card against the success row, and the learner's band
  written before the outside band is seen.
- **Safety:** the practice uses the assessment §9 figures that the lesson used; a safety gap goes first.

**Sessions:**
1. Baseline (90 min): the competency shown as the record left it, under the plan's starting condition; scored against
   the success row; the lesson's knowledge check retaken.
2. Focused practice (90 min): the weak element repeated with one variable changed; readings or photos at each attempt.
3. Test (90 min): the competency shown under the condition the row asks for; outside band logged; the gap judged against
   its closing rule.

**Cues:**
- The baseline and the test are scored the same way, so the change shows.
- The outside band and the learner's band move closer over the block.

**Faults:**
- Practising the whole dish instead of the weak element.
- Changing several things at once, so the cause of an improvement is unknown.
- Scoring after seeing the outside band.

#### REV-5.M1.L3 — Targeted practice, block 2

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l3 -->
*4.5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `REV-5.M1.L2` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [S] deliberate practice on the second gap
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The second gap:** the same pattern as block 1, on the second competency of the plan.
- **Interleaving:** the first gap comes back once in this block, inside an ordinary meal, to see whether it held.
- **The plan adjusted:** if block 1 showed the gap was larger than planned, the plan is changed in writing (another
  block, a lower target for now), not quietly.

**Sessions:**
1. Baseline (90 min): the second competency as the record left it, scored; its knowledge check retaken.
2. Focused practice (90 min): the weak element with one variable changed; the first gap shown once in the same meal.
3. Test (90 min): under the row's condition; outside band logged; judged against the closing rule.

**Cues:** both gaps scored on the same day show whether the first held.

**Faults:**
- Dropping the first gap entirely once block 2 starts.
- Moving the closing rule to fit the result.

#### REV-5.M1.L4 — Targeted practice, block 3

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l4 -->
*4.5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `REV-5.M1.L3` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [S] a third block on the weakest remaining gap, under a condition not yet used (time, guests or no recipe); [J] decide whether the gap is closed
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The weakest remaining gap:** chosen from the results of blocks 1 and 2, or the third competency of the plan.
- **A condition not yet used** (Phase 1 §10.3): time pressure, guests, or no recipe. The new condition shows whether the
  competency holds outside practice, which levels 4 and 5 ask for.
- **[J] Closed or not:**
  - *closed:* the success row met with new evidence, and the outside band agrees within one band;
  - *open:* the row not met, so the gap goes into the capstone plan as a known risk, with a fallback dish and a test cook
    (`CAP-5.M1.L5`).
- **The written decision:** for each gap, the evidence before and after, the decision, and why.

**Sessions:**
1. Practice under the new condition (90 min): for example a timed run, or the dish without a recipe.
2. Practice for others (90 min): the competency inside a meal for at least two people other than the learner, scored by
   their cards.
3. Test and decision (90 min): the final demonstration; the decision for every gap written, and the agreement log
   counted.

**Cues:**
- Every gap has a decision with evidence on both sides.
- An open gap has a fallback in the capstone plan.

**Faults:**
- Calling a gap closed from one good result under easy conditions.
- Hiding an open gap instead of planning for it.

#### REV-5.M1.X1 — The gap plan and three practice blocks

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-rev-5-m1-x1 -->
*brief, brief* — The gap-practice plan and the three blocks, with new evidence for each gap.
<!-- END generated:exercise-rev-5-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** JD-REFL→5

**Aim:** analyse the record honestly, close the gaps that matter before the capstone, and show that the learner's own
scoring agrees with the outside evidence.

**Conditions:** brief support: the brief and the record. The gap-practice plan and three blocks, each spread over about
three weeks. The variable changes within each block (Phase 1 §9.2), and block 3 adds a condition not yet used. The
extension: the agreement log runs over every S5 entry from block 1 on, at least 8 weeks, as the `JD-REFL` level 5 row
asks.

**Inputs:** the record and journal; the success rows of the chosen competencies; the item bank of the S1–S4 knowledge
checks; ingredients as each gap needs, on household meals where possible.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the gap analysis: the candidates from the record, the two or three chosen, and why.
2. Write the plan per gap: the success row and level, the lesson revisited, the variable, the test condition, the
   closing rule.
3. Run the three blocks; at each session, write the learner's band, then the outside band.
4. Write the decision for each gap, with evidence before and after; put any open gap into the capstone plan with a
   fallback.
5. Count the agreement log every two weeks, and at the end.

**Evidence:** written, log, tasters:
- **The gap evidence:** for each gap, the new evidence against its own success row, entered in the record with the date
  and condition.
- **`JD-REFL` level 5, in part:** the gap analysis and the closing decisions; the agreement log over at least 8 weeks
  with at least 90 % of entries within one band of the outside evidence. `RDV-5.M3.X1` adds the style reflection, and
  the capstone defence (`CAP-5.M3.L3`, scored by `CAP-5.R12`) completes the row.

**Time:** 13.5 h active, over about nine weeks.

## Assessment criteria

<!-- BEGIN generated:assessment -->
| Module | Knowledge check | Safety items | Evidence rows (competency → level) |
|---|---|---|---|

**Re-tested at the next milestone:** `CAP-5.M2.L7` (JD-REFL→5), `CAP-5.M3.L2` (JD-REFL→5), `CAP-5.M3.L3` (JD-REFL→5)
<!-- END generated:assessment -->

**Completing the course.** The evidence check is met: a gap analysis from the record, a plan with closing rules, three
blocks with new evidence for each gap, a written decision for each, and the agreement log. Every retaken knowledge
check of a gap competency is passed. A gap that stays open does not fail the course, provided it is planned for in the
capstone; a safety gap that stays open does, because the capstone's food-safety row is a gate (`CAP-5.R3`).

**What it feeds.** The decisions go into the capstone plan (`CAP-5.M1`); the agreement log carries on through `IND-5`
and `RDV-5`; and the defence (`CAP-5.M3.L3`) returns to what this course found.
