## Introduction

`IND-5` is the stage question in practice: can the learner cook anything well without a recipe? The teaching is done.
What remains is consistent, independent performance under conditions that change: an unfamiliar ingredient, a basket
someone else chose, what is left in the fridge, a dish known only from a description or a taste, too little time, the
wrong equipment, a guest's diet, and faults introduced mid-cook. Almost every exercise is a brief (Phase 1 §6.6): a goal,
constraints and criteria, and nothing else.

The course is a series of briefs:
- **first principles** (`IND-5.M1`): a decision framework from structure to method to flavour, used on three unfamiliar
  ingredients;
- **mystery baskets** (`IND-5.M2`): a method, then three rounds of increasing difficulty;
- **pantry and leftovers** (`IND-5.M3`): a weekly habit for four weeks;
- **recreating dishes** (`IND-5.M4`): from a written description, a tasted dish, and a classical text without
  quantities;
- **constraints** (`IND-5.M5`): time, equipment and diet;
- **a troubleshooting clinic** (`IND-5.M6`): rescues under pressure and doneness by the senses alone.

**Rules for every brief.**
- **Safety first:** assessment §9 applies to every dish. A brief, a basket or a historical text never overrides it:
  poultry to 74 °C, minced meat to its row, leftovers within 2 days and reheated once, raw egg pasteurised for every
  guest. Doneness judged by the senses is checked with the probe before a §9 food is served.
- **Ingredients:** Dutch seasonal vegetables (velt.nu harvest windows) and MSC or seasonal North Sea fish. A basket or
  an "unfamiliar ingredient" is bought, not foraged.
- **The pantry:** salt, oils, vinegars, flour, sugar, dried spices and herbs, stocks from the freezer, dried pulses and
  grains are always available unless the brief removes them.
- **The record:** every session logs the plan, timestamps, photos, the learner's band, then the outside band (the
  agreement log of `REV-5`).

**In the spiral.** Most competencies here reach level 5, which asks for at least five demonstrations under three
conditions over at least 8 weeks, with at least one without a recipe and one under time pressure or for guests, plus a
short written explanation (Phase 1 §10.3). S5 runs for at least 8 weeks, so the evidence spreads across `IND-5`,
`RDV-5` and the capstone. Each evidence row below says which part this course gives and where the rest is.
- **Completed here:** `CR-IMPV` 4 (round 3 of the baskets).
- **Built here, completed by the capstone:**
  - judgement: `JD-CHSE` 5, `CK-METH` 5, `JD-TRBL` 5, `FL-CORR` 5 and `JD-CUES` 5;
  - adapting and planning: `RD-ADPT` 5, `KM-PLAN` 5 and `TS-DRY` 5;
  - with `RDV-5` as well: `FL-SENS` 5 (whose triangle test is in `RDV-5.M3.X1`) and `IN-VEG` 5 (whose written guide
    is in `RDV-5.M2.X1`).
- **Further evidence:** `CK-CLAS` 4, which `CLS-4` reached; the classical text of `IND-5.M4.X2` adds one more
  preparation from its name alone.
- **`TS-FIRE` 4, in part:** level 4 asks for three low-and-slow cooks and three live-fire meals in at least two
  conditions. `FIR-3` gave the first of each. `IND-5.M5.L2` gives the second live-fire meal. The outline has no hours for
  the rest, so the remaining cooks are logged as household cooks during S5 (overview §11). A learner without outdoor
  space stays at level 2, and the record says so (`FIR-3`).

**Pacing.** About 43.5 hours over at least 8 weeks, at about 5 hours a week, alongside `RDV-5` and the blocks of
`REV-5`. The pantry meals run once a week for four weeks.

#### IND-5.M1.L1 — A decision framework

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M1.L2`, `RDV-4.M2.L1` · moves JD-CHSE→5, CK-METH→5*

*Objectives:* [J] structure (water, fat, protein, connective tissue, starch) → method → flavour → texture; [K] the transfer questions from Phase 1 §2.3
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] From structure to method:**
  - **water:** high-water vegetables (courgette, cucumber, spinach) collapse and release liquid, so they suit fast, hot
    methods or salting first; dense, starchy roots (celeriac, parsnip, potato) need time to soften and take browning well
    (`VEG-3`, `HEA-2.M1.L2`);
  - **fat:** fatty fish and cuts tolerate high heat and longer cooking; lean ones dry out and want gentle or fast methods;
  - **protein:** coagulates and tightens with heat, so the target doneness sets the method (`SCI-2`);
  - **connective tissue:** much collagen needs long, moist cooking until it becomes gelatine; little allows a fast method
    (`PRO-4`, `SLW-3`);
  - **starch:** needs water and heat to gelatinise, and retrogrades as it cools (`STA-1`).
- **Then flavour, then texture:** what the ingredient brings (sweet, bitter, umami), what it needs (acid, fat, salt),
  and the texture contrast it lacks (`FLV-3`).
- **[K] The transfer questions** (Phase 1 §2.3): what is it and what is it for; how is it done; why does it work; what
  happens physically and chemically; which variables change it; which mistakes are common; how to recognise them; how to
  correct them; where else does it transfer.
- **The written analysis:** structure, the chosen method and two rejected ones with their risks, the flavour plan, the
  texture plan, and the cue and reading that will say it is done.

**Sessions:**
1. Framework on a known ingredient (60 min): a familiar vegetable (a leek or a celeriac) analysed in writing with the
   framework and cooked by the chosen method; the analysis checked against what happened.

**Cues:**
- The analysis predicts the result before cooking.
- Every rejected method has a named risk, not only "I don't like it".

**Faults:**
- Starting from a dish already known, not from the ingredient's structure.
- Forgetting the safety figure for a protein in the analysis.

#### IND-5.M1.L2 — Unfamiliar ingredients

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `IND-5.M1.L1` · moves JD-CHSE→5, IN-VEG→5, CK-METH→5*

*Objectives:* [S] cook three ingredients you've never cooked (e.g. kohlrabi, salsify, a new fish); [J] justify each choice of method
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Choosing the three:** ingredients the record shows were never cooked, from the season and bought: for example
  kohlrabi, salsify, Jerusalem artichoke, sea kale, purslane or a North Sea fish not yet used (dab, gurnard, witch).
- **First contact:** looking, smelling and tasting raw (vegetables only), cutting a sample, reading a reliable source
  ([S16]) for structure and known risks.
- **[J] Twice, with different reasoning:** each ingredient cooked by two methods chosen for different reasons (for
  example salsify roasted for browning and simmered then glazed for tenderness), and the two compared on the same plate.
- **Safety:** a new fish follows the fish row (63 °C for 15 seconds) unless it was frozen first for serving translucent
  (assessment §9); a vegetable new to the learner is checked for any known need to cook it (as with dried beans, §9).

**Sessions:**
1. Ingredients one and two (90 min): each analysed, cooked two ways, tasted side by side, photographed.
2. Ingredient three (60 min): analysed and cooked two ways, served in a meal; household cards.

**Cues:**
- Both versions of each ingredient are good, and they differ for the reason written.
- The learner can say which method suits which use.

**Faults:**
- Two methods that are really the same (roasted and baked).
- A new fish cooked to "looks done" without a reading.

#### IND-5.M1.X1 — Three unfamiliar ingredients, each cooked twice

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-ind-5-m1-x1 -->
*brief, brief* — Three unfamiliar ingredients, each cooked twice with different reasoning.
<!-- END generated:exercise-ind-5-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** JD-CHSE→5, CK-METH→5, IN-VEG→5

**Aim:** reason from structure to method for ingredients with no prior experience, and explain the choice well enough for
someone else to follow.

**Conditions:** brief support: the brief only. Three unfamiliar ingredients, at least two of them vegetables, each
cooked twice with different reasoning. The variable is the ingredient (Phase 1 §9.2). The extension: a short written
guide to the method choice for one ingredient group, as the `CK-METH` level 5 row asks.

**Inputs:** three ingredients new to the learner, bought in season (about 300–500 g of each vegetable, one whole fish or
two fillets); the pantry.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the analysis for each ingredient: structure, the chosen methods and the rejected ones with their risks, the
   flavour and texture plan, the cue and the reading.
2. Cook each twice; photograph both versions together; probe the fish.
3. Collect household cards; write what the analysis got right and wrong.
4. Write the guide to the method choice for one ingredient group (for example the roots, or the leafy brassicas): the
   structure, the methods that suit it, the ones that fail and why.

**Evidence:** written, photo, tasters:
- **`JD-CHSE` level 5, brief one of five:** the written analysis naming alternatives and risks, and the result to
  standard. The further briefs are `IND-5.M4.X1`, `IND-5.M4.X2`, the written analysis of round 3 of `IND-5.M2.X1`, and
  the capstone (`CAP-5.M1.L1`).
- **`CK-METH` level 5, in part:** method choices reasoned from principles with the rejected alternatives named, and the
  written guide. The capstone brief is the third brief the row asks for (`CAP-5.R1`).
- **`IN-VEG` level 5, the unfamiliar-vegetable condition:** two vegetable-led dishes reasoned from structure. The
  without-recipe condition is in `IND-5.M2.X1` and `IND-5.M3.X1`, the vegetable-led main and the guide in
  `RDV-5.M2.X1`, and the guests condition in the capstone's vegetable course.

**Time:** 2.5 h, plus the written guide.

#### IND-5.M2.L1 — The mystery basket method

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `MNU-4.M3.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→4, KM-PLAN→5*

*Objectives:* [S] inventory, constraints, menu, timeline in 15 minutes; then execute
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The basket:** chosen by someone else from a list of what is in season and in budget, with the pantry available; the
  chooser notes any allergen or diet of the people who will eat.
- **The first 15 minutes,** written down:
  1. **inventory:** every item, its weight and state, what is perishable;
  2. **constraints:** the time, the guests, the equipment, the diet;
  3. **menu:** each item given a role (main, support, accent), with the method chosen from the framework of `IND-5.M1`;
  4. **timeline:** worked back from the serving time, with the longest task first and a control point for each protein
     (`MNU-4.M3.L1`).
- **The fallback:** a simpler version of each dish, decided in the plan, used if a step fails.
- **Everything used, nothing forced:** an item that does not fit is kept for another meal rather than spoiling a dish.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:**
- The plan is written and timed before the first knife cut.
- Every basket item has a role or a reason to keep it.

**Faults:**
- Starting to cook before the plan is complete.
- Using every item in every course.
- No fallback, so one failure sinks the meal.

#### IND-5.M2.L2 — Round 1: vegetables and pantry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `IND-5.M2.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→4, TS-DRY→5*

*Objectives:* [S] a household member picks the basket; two courses within 90 minutes
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The round:** a household member picks a basket of three to five seasonal vegetables; two courses within 90 minutes,
  from the vegetables and the pantry.
- **Dry heat as the backbone:** with no protein, browning, roasting, grilling and frying give depth (`TS-DRY`); the
  method varied between the two courses.
- **Using the whole vegetable:** leaves, stalks and peel as garnish, crisps or a quick stock (`KMG-3`).

**Sessions:**
1. Round 1, basket A (90 min): the 15-minute plan, two courses, timestamps and photos; household cards.
2. Round 1, basket B (90 min): a second basket from another season or with a vegetable not used in A; the plan
   improved from A's log.

**Cues:**
- Both courses on the table within 90 minutes.
- Each course uses a different dry-heat method, to standard.

**Faults:**
- Two courses that taste the same (the same fat, spice and method).
- Pale, steamed vegetables from a crowded tray.

#### IND-5.M2.L3 — Round 2: protein and season

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `IND-5.M2.L2` · moves CR-IMPV→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a basket with one protein and seasonal produce; a main and a side
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The round:** a basket with one protein (a chicken leg, a pork chop, a fish, eggs or a pulse) and seasonal produce;
  a main and a side.
- **The protein first:** its §9 figure, the method from its structure, and the timeline built around its cooking and
  rest.
- **The side as a partner:** contrast in texture and flavour with the main, and timed to finish with it.

**Sessions:**
1. Round 2, basket A (90 min): plan, main and side, probe reading logged; household cards.
2. Round 2, basket B (90 min): a different protein; the plan improved from A.

**Cues:**
- The protein at its target doneness and above its §9 figure, rested.
- The side ready at the same moment.

**Faults:**
- The protein cooked first and left to go cold.
- A side that repeats the main's texture.

**Faults:**
- A dessert left to the end with no time.

<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m6-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The clinic:** a household member introduces a fault mid-cook without warning: too much salt, a split sauce,
  overcooking, too much acid, a burnt base (`FLV-3.M6.L1`, `SAU-2.M7.L1`).
- **[J] Diagnose by the senses first:** taste, look, smell; then the cause from the science; then the fix or a reasoned

- **The rescues:**

  - **burnt base:** moved at once to a clean pan without scraping, and tasted.

1. Clinic, fault one (45 min): a meal with one fault introduced; diagnosed, rescued, logged with before and after photos.
2. Clinic, fault two (45 min): another fault in another dish.
3. Clinic, fault three (45 min): a fault while cooking for guests, who aren't told.

**Cues:**
- The diagnosis names the fault and its cause within a minute.
- The rescued dish passes the household's or guests' card.

**Faults:**
- Masking salt with sugar or acid.
- A rescue that takes so long that the rest of the meal is lost.

- **[J] The senses first:** the cues for each family (`PRO-4.M6.L1`, `JD-CUES`):
  - **proteins:** the press, the colour of the juices, the shrinkage;
  - **vegetables:** the knife's resistance and the colour;

- **The prediction:** before any instrument, the learner writes the doneness and the estimated reading.
- **Then verify:** the probe or the thermometer, every time; for a §9 food (poultry, minced meat, fish, custard) the

  example; framework `JD-CUES` row).

**Sessions:**
1. Proteins (45 min): a roast or several pieces, predictions then readings.
2. Vegetables and breads (45 min): predictions then knife and probe checks; a loaf by sound and weight, then its core.

**Cues:**
- At least 8 of 10 predictions within tolerance.
- No §9 food served on the senses alone.

**Faults:**
- Reading the probe before writing the prediction.
- Trusting a good guess over a low reading.
