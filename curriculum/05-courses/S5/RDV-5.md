## Introduction

`RDV-4` taught the tools of recipe development: ratios, deconstruction, substitution, test plans, writing for others,
and a first original dish. `RDV-5` uses them at scale. The learner develops at least six original dishes across the
categories of a menu, each through at least three documented versions, writes them as reproducible recipes, and
presents them at a tasting day with blind scoring. The portfolio should have a recognisable point of view: the learner's
cooking, not a set of unrelated ideas.

The subject is the learner's choice, so this file follows the pattern of `CUI-4`:
- **one template** for the portfolio plan (`RDV-5.M1.L1`) and **one template** for a development cycle, used for every
  dish (`RDV-5.M2.L1`–`L6`);
- **a short catalogue** of points of view (below), each worked through the six categories.

**Rules for every dish.**
- **Original:** not a course exercise or a published recipe with small changes. The dish starts from an idea written in
  one sentence (`CR-COMP`), and the test log shows how it got from there to the final version.
- **Safety:** assessment §9 applies to every version: proteins to their figures, raw egg and warm egg sauces pasteurised for every guest,
  preserves by a tested method only, and test portions kept as leftovers (at most 2 days).
- **Ingredients:** Dutch seasonal vegetables (velt.nu harvest windows), MSC or seasonal North Sea fish, and Dutch meat
  and dairy; no imported year-round produce. A dish developed in one season is written with its seasonal window.
- **Tasting:** household panel scores for every version, blind where two versions can be compared; the panel's tasters
  have taken the salt triangle test (assessment §5.4).

**In the spiral.**
- `CR-ORIG` reaches level 4: at least three original dishes, one of them in the capstone menu, each with a stated idea
  and a recognisable style, and the guests' cards and the defence showing that the idea was understood. The six dishes
  here are the candidates; the capstone dish and its defence complete the row (`CAP-5.R1`).
- `RD-TEST` 4 and `RD-WRIT` 4 were reached in `RDV-4`; the six test series and recipes here are further evidence, and
  one recipe is cooked by someone else from the text.
- `IN-VEG` 5: the vegetable-led main and the written guide to one vegetable group (`RDV-5.M2.X1`), with the conditions
  of `IND-5` and the capstone.
- `FL-SENS` 5: the tasting day's triangle test, set up for the panel and interpreted, and a short written guide to
  tasting (`RDV-5.M3.X1`). `IND-5.M6.X1` holds the calibration, and the capstone form completes the row.
- `JD-REFL` 5: the reflection on a personal style and its next steps (`RDV-5.M3.X1`), with the agreement log of `REV-5`
  and the capstone defence.

**Pacing.** About 38 hours over at least 8 weeks. Each dish's three versions are spread over about three weeks, so two
or three dishes are in development at once, alongside `IND-5`.

### Point-of-view catalogue

A point of view is what makes six dishes read as one cook's work. Each entry shows the idea, how it shapes the six
categories, and the risk to watch.

- **Season** (for example "one Dutch month, at its peak").
  - *Shapes the six:* every dish built on what is at its best in one or two months; a starter of raw and pickled early
    vegetables, a vegetable-led main of the month's star, a dessert from its dairy and grain.
  - *Risk:* six dishes that all taste of the same two vegetables.
- **Region** (for example the coast of Zeeland, or the sandy soils of the east).
  - *Shapes the six:* the region's produce and techniques (mussels, salt-marsh vegetables, rye, buckwheat, smoked fish);
    a protein main from its fish or meat; a bread from its grain.
  - *Risk:* recreating regional classics instead of making new dishes from them.
- **Technique** (for example fire and smoke, or fermentation).
  - *Shapes the six:* one technique carried through every category, in different roles: a smoked butter for the bread, a
    fermented vegetable as the acid of a main, a charred element in the dessert.
  - *Risk:* the technique taking over, so every dish is smoky or sour.
- **Ingredient** (for example the brassicas, or the onion family).
  - *Shapes the six:* one family explored through every category; the vegetable-led main shows the family at its best,
    the dessert finds its sweetness.
  - *Risk:* a forced use in a category where it doesn't belong.

- The six ideas, read together, sound like one cook.
- Every dish has a first version and a question for round two before any cooking.

**Faults:**
- Six ideas that are really one dish in six forms.
- A dessert or bread left without a plan because it is "the hard one".
- A plan that needs out-of-season produce.

#### RDV-5.M2.L1 — A starter

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l1 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `RDV-5.M1.L1`, `RDV-4.M4.L2` · moves CR-ORIG→4, RD-TEST→4*

*Objectives:* [S] develop, test and finalise an original starter
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The development cycle** (the template for every dish of this module):
  1. **version 1:** from the idea and the starting framework, cooked, photographed in the corner, scored by the panel;
  2. **round two:** one variable changed to answer the plan's first question (a component, a ratio, a method, a
     temperature); scored, blind against version 1 where possible;
  3. **round three:** the next question, or the same variable refined; the final version chosen by the scores and the
     idea, not by habit;
  4. **the final recipe:** weights, cues, timings and the seasonal window, in the format of `RDV-4.M5.L1`.
- **The test log:** for each version, what changed, why, the result, the panel scores, the learner's band and the
  outside band.
- **A starter's job:** small, bright and appetising; it opens the menu, so acid, freshness and texture lead.
- **Safety:** a raw or cured fish starter follows the fish row of assessment §9 (frozen first); a raw-egg dressing is
  made with pasteurised egg for every guest.

**Sessions:**
1. Version 1 (60 min): cooked from the plan; photographed; panel scores.
2. Version 2 (60 min): one variable changed; blind comparison with version 1.
3. Version 3 (60 min): refined; the final chosen; the recipe drafted.

**Cues:**
- Each version answers its question, so the log shows why the final is better.
- The panel scores rise, or the log explains why they did not.

**Faults:**
- Changing several things between versions.
- A starter so large or rich that it would spoil the menu.

#### RDV-5.M2.L3 — A vegetable-led main

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l3 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `VEG-3.M5.L2` · moves CR-ORIG→4, IN-VEG→5*

*Objectives:* [S] develop an original vegetable-led main course
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The cycle:** as in `RDV-5.M2.L1`.
- **Vegetable-led:** the vegetable is the centre, not a side without its meat; built with the component table of
  `VEG-3.M5.L2` (centrepiece, sauce, contrast, depth).

**Cues:**
- The panel doesn't miss meat.
- The vegetable is cooked to its best texture, and the guide explains why.

**Faults:**
- A plate of vegetable sides with nothing at its centre.
- Depth missing, so the main tastes like a starter.

#### RDV-5.M3.L2 — A tasting day

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m3-l2 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `RDV-5.M3.L1` · moves CR-ORIG→4, FL-SENS→5*

*Objectives:* [S] serve a selection to a panel; [J] collect blind scores and act on them
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The panel:** 4–6 people, asked in advance about allergens and diets (`NUT-3`); the menu adjusted or a dish kept
  apart with its own tools; every guest told what each dish contains.
- **Blind scoring:** each dish scored on a card (flavour, texture, appearance, and whether the idea comes through) before
  its idea is told; then the idea told and the panel asked whether they recognised it.
- **[J] The triangle test** (assessment §5, [S20]): for one dish with two close versions, three coded portions, two the
  same and one different, served in random order; each taster picks the odd one out. With three portions, a guess is
  right one time in three, so a difference counts only if clearly more than a third of the panel find it; with a
  panel this small, only a large majority is a clear signal.
- **Acting on the scores:** a change made to the dish whose scores or comments point to a fault, and noted in the
  recipe; scores that don't agree with the learner's own band written in the log.
- **Safety:** dishes held at 57 °C or above, or 5 °C or below, and out of the fridge for at most 2 hours
  (assessment §9).

**Sessions:**
1. The tasting day (3 h): a selection of at least four dishes served to the panel; blind cards collected; the triangle
   test run; results written and one change decided.

**Cues:**
- The panel names the idea of most dishes without being told.
- The triangle result is interpreted correctly, including when it shows no difference.

**Faults:**
- Telling the panel the idea before they score.
- Reading a triangle result of two of six as a clear difference.
- Food standing out too long between courses.

*brief, brief* — A tasting day for 4–6 people with blind scoring.
<!-- END generated:exercise-rdv-5-m3-x1 -->

**Evidences:** RD-WRIT→4, JD-REFL→5, CR-ORIG→4, FL-SENS→5

**Aim:** present the portfolio, collect honest outside judgement, and reflect on a personal style.

**Conditions:** brief support: the brief only. A tasting day for 4–6 people with blind scoring, after the portfolio is
written up. The extensions:
- a triangle test set up for the panel on one dish and interpreted, and a short written guide to tasting, as the
  `FL-SENS` level 5 row asks;
- the reflection on style and next steps, as part of the `JD-REFL` level 5 row.

**Inputs:** the portfolio; ingredients for at least four dishes for the panel; coded cups or plates, a hidden key and
score cards.

**Procedure:**
1. Write up the portfolio and the reflection.
2. Ask the panel about allergens and diets; adjust the menu.
3. Serve the selection; collect blind cards; then tell each idea and ask whether it was recognised.
4. Run the triangle test on one dish; interpret the result.
5. Write the results, the change decided, the agreement between the learner's bands and the panel's, and the tasting
   guide.

**Evidence:** written, tasters, form, photo:
- **`CR-ORIG` level 4, the guests' view:** the panel's cards show whether the idea of each dish was understood. The
  capstone dish and defence complete the row.
- **`FL-SENS` level 5, the triangle test and guide:** the test set up and interpreted correctly, and the guide. With the
  calibration of `IND-5.M6.X1`, the capstone form against the guests' cards completes the row (`CAP-5.R7`).
- **`JD-REFL` level 5, in part:** the reflection on style and next steps, and the learner's bands against the panel's
  for every dish. With the agreement log of `REV-5.M1.X1`, the capstone defence completes the row (`CAP-5.R12`).
- **`RD-WRIT` level 4, further evidence:** the portfolio as one document from which every dish can be cooked.

**Time:** 6 h (the write-up and the tasting day).
