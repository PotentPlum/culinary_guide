## Introduction

Most failures in a home kitchen are failures of organisation, not of technique:
- garlic burns while the onion is still being cut;
- the sauce waits while the potatoes are only just going on;
- a recipe's one critical step is missed because it was hidden in a long paragraph.

`WRK-1` gives the learner a professional way of working before the technique courses ask for speed and precision:
- **mise en place** (*mise en place* / *klaarzetten*);
- **reading a recipe for its intent**, and turning it into a plan and a timeline;
- **the arithmetic of the kitchen**;
- **the vocabulary** of French, Dutch and English kitchens.

It is also the first step of the program's path from recipes to briefs (Phase 1 §6.6). A learner who can read a
recipe critically can later work from a framework, and then from a brief, because they see what the recipe was doing.

**In the spiral.**
- **Mise en place** (`TS-MEP`) is used in every practical from here on, and is re-tested at `MS-S1`.
- **Planning** (`KM-PLAN`) grows into menus and service timelines in S3–S5.
- **Kitchen math** (`KM-MATH`) continues in `KMG-3` (costing) and in the baking courses (baker's percentages).
- **Recipe notes** (`RD-WRIT`) are the start of the recipe development thread.

**Pacing.** About two to three weeks alongside `SAF-1` and `EQP-1`. The timed mise en place and the comparison
cook-twice belong on days with time to cook; the math and the terminology fit short evening slots.

## Modules

### WRK-1.M1 — Mise en place

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-wrk-1-m1 -->
*5.5 h · 3 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches TS-MEP 3, KM-PLAN 1*

*Scope:* the mindset and the physical set-up: read, gather, prepare, clean as you go.

*Objectives:* [S] set up a station and prepare a full mise en place; [J] order prep tasks by how long they keep.

*Check (outline):* tray photo against a checklist; time logged.
<!-- END generated:module-wrk-1-m1 -->

#### WRK-1.M1.L1 — The mise en place mindset

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m1-l1 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `ORI-1.M2.L2` · moves TS-MEP→1*

*Objectives:* [K] why professionals prepare everything first; [J] what can be prepared ahead and what must be last-minute
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Why professionals prepare everything first.** Once the heat is on, steps follow in seconds or minutes. There is no
  time to look for the cumin or dice an onion, and a missing ingredient shows up before it can spoil the dish.
- **The sequence:** read the whole recipe, gather the equipment, gather the ingredients, prepare (wash, peel, cut,
  weigh), then cook and clean as you go.
- **What keeps and what doesn't.**
  - Keeps well: diced onion and carrot, weighed dry goods, mixed sauces, a stock.
  - Prepared last: cut herbs (they bruise and darken), cut apples and potatoes (they brown or need water), dressed
    salads (they wilt), raw protein (it waits in the fridge).

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): analyse two recipes for what can be prepared ahead and what must be last-minute.

**Cues:** a plan that names, for each component, when it is prepared and where it waits.

**Faults:**
- Starting the pan before the knife work is done.
- Prepping everything hours ahead, including the basil.
- Leaving raw protein out on the counter during prep.

#### WRK-1.M1.L2 — Setting up a station

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves TS-MEP→2*

*Objectives:* [S] board stability, bowls, a waste bowl, towel and tray; clean as you go
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The station:**
  - a stable board on a damp towel;
  - a bowl or tray for prepared items, grouped by the moment they go in;
  - a waste bowl;
  - a dry towel;
  - the tools within reach.
- **Clean as you go:** wipe the board between items, empty the waste bowl and wash tools while things cook, so the
  kitchen is clean when the food is ready.
- **Grouping:** items that go into the pan together are placed together, in the order of use.

**Sessions:**
1. Station drill (60 min): set up the station for a simple soup, prepare it, photograph the station before cooking, and
   time how long the clean-up takes after the meal.

**Cues:** nothing on the board but the item being cut; no walking to the bin during prep; the counter clear when the soup
is served.

**Faults:**
- A board that slides.
- Peelings on the board, pushed around with each cut.
- Prepared items in random bowls with no order.

#### WRK-1.M1.L3 — Mise en place practice

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m1-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `WRK-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-MEP→3, KM-PLAN→1*

*Objectives:* [S] prepare a full mise en place for a three-component meal within a time limit
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A time limit makes the order visible.** Clean items come first, then aromatics, and raw protein last on the board.
  Long jobs start first, and the weighing is done while something drains.
- **The prep list:** every item with its cut or measure and the bowl it goes in, written before starting.
- **Reflection:** the time and the bottleneck are logged after each attempt, so the next attempt has a target.

**Sessions:**
1. Timed mise en place (75 min): a three-component meal (for example a pan-fried chicken thigh, roast root vegetables
   and a cabbage salad) with the prep list; time it; cook and eat it.
2. Repeat without the list (75 min), a week later, with a different meal of the same size; compare the times and the
   bottlenecks.

**Cues:** the second attempt faster, with no forgotten item and a clean station at the end.

**Faults:**
- Starting with the raw protein.
- Washing herbs after cutting them.
- Measuring the spices into one bowl when they go in at different moments.

#### WRK-1.M1.X1 — Timed mise en place

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-wrk-1-m1-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — Timed mise en place for a given recipe; photograph the tray.
<!-- END generated:exercise-wrk-1-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-MEP→3

**Aim:** a full mise en place for a two- or three-component meal, before the heat goes on, within a planned time,
without a checklist.

**Conditions:** recipe support: a given recipe for a three-component meal, with a time target set from the first
attempt. Attempt 1 with the checklist; attempt 2 on another day, with another meal and without it.

**Inputs:** for each attempt, a dinner for 2–4: a protein (chicken thighs, an egg dish or a pulse), a vegetable for
the oven (carrot, beetroot or celeriac, by season) and a salad (white cabbage, carrot or chicory).

**Procedure:**
1. Read the recipe.
2. Write the prep list and set the target time.
3. Set up the station.
4. Prepare everything; stop the clock.
5. Photograph the tray and the station.
6. Cook, then clean.
7. Log the time, the bottleneck and the next target.

**Evidence:** log, photo, checklist — the two time logs and tray photos. The second attempt meets the `TS-MEP` level 3
success row: without a checklist, fully prepped before the heat, in a logical order, within the planned time, with the
station clean at the end.

AI review compares the tray photo with the prep list. Reference set for Phase 6:
- *Competent*: grouped bowls in the order of use, an empty board, a waste bowl;
- *Not yet*: the items scattered and the peelings on the board.

**Time:** 2 × 75 min, of which about 40 min is prep each time.

#### WRK-1.M1.X2 — Cook it twice

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-wrk-1-m1-x2 -->
*guided, recipe* — Cook the same recipe twice: once without and once with full mise en place, then compare.
<!-- END generated:exercise-wrk-1-m1-x2 -->

**Evidences:** TS-MEP→2

**Aim:** experience, not just hear, what mise en place changes.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the same simple recipe twice, for example an onion, carrot and lentil soup. The first
time, prep as the recipe goes; the second time, with full mise en place.

**Inputs:** the soup's ingredients twice, for 2–4 portions each (one goes into the freezer).

**Procedure:**
1. Cook the recipe without mise en place; log the total time, the moments of stress and any burning.
2. Another day, cook it with full mise en place; log the same.
3. Compare the two in the journal, with photos of the station during cooking.

**Evidence:** photo, checklist — the station photos during both cooks and the comparison note; prepped with the
checklist, as the `TS-MEP` level 2 success row asks.

**Time:** 2 × 45 min.

### WRK-1.M2 — Recipe literacy

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-wrk-1-m2 -->
*4.5 h · 3 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches CK-RCPT 3, KM-PLAN 2, KM-PURC 1*

*Scope:* how recipes are built, what they leave out, and how to plan from them.

*Objectives:* [K] identify a recipe's key technique and critical points; [S] write a work plan and a shopping list.

*Check (outline):* annotated recipes and a timeline against actual times.
<!-- END generated:module-wrk-1-m2 -->

#### WRK-1.M2.L1 — Anatomy of a recipe

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m2-l1 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · moves CK-RCPT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] parts of a recipe; the implicit knowledge recipes assume; ambiguous terms
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The parts of a recipe:** title and yield, ingredients (ideally by weight, in the order of use), equipment, method,
  cues and times, holding and serving.
- **What recipes assume:**
  - the pan size;
  - the heat level of "medium";
  - the size of "a medium onion";
  - the seasoning at "to taste";
  - the skill to judge "until golden".
- **Ambiguous terms**, and how to resolve them: weights instead of pieces, cues instead of times, and a check of the
  recipe's yield.

**Sessions:**
1. Dissect a recipe (45 min): take a recipe the learner likes, mark each part, list every assumption it makes, and
   cook it with those assumptions written out.

**Cues:** the learner finds at least five assumptions in an ordinary recipe.

**Faults:**
- Trusting the times over what the pan shows.
- Missing the yield, so the dish feeds two instead of four.

#### WRK-1.M2.L2 — Reading for intent

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m2-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `WRK-1.M2.L1` · moves CK-RCPT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] find the key technique, the critical moments and the doneness cues; [J] spot errors and gaps
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Every recipe has a key technique** on which it succeeds or fails: the sear, the emulsion, the reduction.
- **Critical moments** are the points where a mistake cannot be undone, such as adding eggs to a hot liquid or salting
  pasta water.
- **Doneness cues:** what the food looks, smells, sounds and feels like when it is ready; "for 5 minutes" is a guess,
  and "until translucent" is a cue.
- **Errors and gaps:** inconsistent quantities, a missing step, an oven temperature that does not suit the method.
  Comparing two recipes for the same dish shows the essentials.

**Sessions:**
1. Two recipes, one dish (45 min): compare two recipes for the same dish; mark the key technique, the critical moments
   and the cues; note where they disagree and why.

**Cues:** a one-line statement of the recipe's intent ("a quick braise that keeps the vegetables bright").

**Faults:**
- Annotating everything equally.
- Missing that the "secret" of a recipe is one step.

#### WRK-1.M2.L3 — Planning from a recipe

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m2-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `WRK-1.M2.L2` · moves CK-RCPT→3, KM-PLAN→2, KM-PURC→1*

*Objectives:* [S] turn a recipe into a backwards timeline, a prep list and a shopping list
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A backwards timeline:**
  - start from the serving time;
  - subtract each component's cooking and resting time;
  - then subtract its prep;
  - mark the moments of parallel work.
- **The prep list** follows the timeline. The shopping list follows the prep list, scaled to the number of portions and
  grouped by the shop's sections.
- **The plan against the actual:** log both, and note where the plan was wrong, so the next plan is better.

**Sessions:**
1. Plan a dinner (2 h, including cooking): a backwards timeline, a prep list and a shopping list for a two-component
   dinner from a recipe; cook it; log the actual times beside the plan.

**Cues:** the components ready within 10 minutes of each other and of the plan.

**Faults:**
- Forgetting resting and preheating times.
- Two things planned for the same burner.
- A shopping list in recipe order rather than shop order.

#### WRK-1.M2.X1 — Annotate three recipes

<!-- END generated:exercise-wrk-1-m2-x1 -->

**Evidences:** CK-RCPT→3

**Aim:** read unfamiliar recipes for their intent, and fill in what they leave out.

#### WRK-1.M2.X2 — Plan and cook from your own timeline

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-wrk-1-m2-x2 -->
*framework, framework* — Plan and cook a dinner from your own timeline; log the actual against the plan.

**Evidences:** CK-RCPT→3, KM-PLAN→2

**Aim:** a dinner cooked to one's own plan, with the plan tested against the clock.

### WRK-1.M3 — Kitchen math I

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-wrk-1-m3 -->
*3 h · 3 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches KM-MATH 2, RD-ADPT 1*

*Scope:* units, conversions, scaling, percentages.

*Objectives:* [S] convert and scale accurately; [K] explain why some things don't scale linearly.

*Check (outline):* auto-graded calculation quiz.
<!-- END generated:module-wrk-1-m3 -->

#### WRK-1.M3.L1 — Weight, volume and conversions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m3-l1 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · moves KM-MATH→1*

*Objectives:* [K] why weight beats volume; metric conversions; cups and spoons
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Weight against volume.** A cup of flour varies with how it is scooped, and a spoon of salt with the crystal size.
  Grams do not vary.
- **Water and most watery liquids:** 1 ml weighs about 1 g. Oil is lighter, honey heavier.
- **Metric conversions:** kg, g, l, dl, cl, ml. A dl (*deciliter*) is common in Dutch recipes.
- **American measures:** a US cup is about 240 ml, a tablespoon 15 ml, a teaspoon 5 ml; ounces and pounds by weight;
  Fahrenheit to Celsius: subtract 32, multiply by 5/9.

**Sessions:**
1. Weigh the volumes (45 min): weigh a cup of flour scooped three ways, a tablespoon of fine and of flaky salt, and
   100 ml of water and of oil; record the spread.

**Cues:** the learner's own record of how much a cup of flour varies.

**Faults:**
- Converting a cup of flour as if it were water.
- Mixing up tablespoons and teaspoons.
- Not taring the scale.

#### WRK-1.M3.L2 — Scaling a recipe

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m3-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `WRK-1.M3.L1` · moves KM-MATH→2, RD-ADPT→1*

*Objectives:* [S] scale by a factor; [K] what doesn't scale (salt, spices, pan size, evaporation, time)
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The scaling factor** is the new yield divided by the old yield. Every ingredient is multiplied by it and rounded
  sensibly (eggs to whole eggs, or by weight).
- **What does not scale linearly:**
  - salt and spices: start lower, then taste;
  - pan size: a double batch in the same pan steams instead of browning;
  - evaporation: it depends on the surface, not the volume;
  - cooking and cooling times: a bigger pot heats and cools more slowly;
  - baking times and temperatures.
- **Scaling down** has its own limits: a sauce too shallow to whisk, an egg that cannot be halved by eye.

**Sessions:**
1. Scale and cook (45 min): scale a simple recipe from 4 to 6 portions, cook it, and note what had to change beyond the
   multiplication (pan, time, salt).

**Cues:** a scaled recipe that tastes like the original, with the non-linear changes written down.

**Faults:**
- Doubling the salt with everything else.
- Crowding a pan.
- Keeping the original cooking time for a bigger pot.

#### WRK-1.M3.L3 — Percentages

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m3-l3 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `WRK-1.M3.L2` · moves KM-MATH→2*

*Objectives:* [S] salt as a percentage of weight; simple yield (peeled ÷ whole)
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Salt as a percentage of the food's weight.** For example 1–1.5 % for minced meat or a soup's solids, and 1 % in the
  tasting series of `TST-1`. A percentage makes seasoning reproducible at any quantity.
- **Yield** is the usable weight divided by the purchased weight, and the amount to buy is the amount needed divided by
  the yield. It continues in `KMG-3` (costing).
- **A preview of baker's percentages** (`BAK-2`): every ingredient as a percentage of the flour.

**Sessions:**
1. Weigh and season (30 min): peel and weigh a kilo of carrots to find the yield; salt a soup at 1 % of its weight and
   taste it against an unsalted spoonful.

**Cues:** the learner can say the yield of their carrots and what 1 % salt tastes like in a soup.

**Faults:**
- Taking the percentage of the wrong weight (the liquid only instead of the whole).
- Buying for the peeled weight.

#### WRK-1.M4.L1 — Core terminology

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m4-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · moves CK-TERM→2*

*Objectives:* [K] the terms for methods, cuts, tools and stages (e.g. *sauté*/sauteren, *mise en place*, *blancheren*); the start of the glossary
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The S1 glossary** in French, Dutch and English, by group:
  - methods: *sauter* / sauteren, *blanchir* / blancheren, *pocher* / pocheren, *braiser* / smoren, *rôtir* /
    braden or roosteren, *gratiner* / gratineren;
  - cuts: *julienne*, *brunoise*, *macédoine*, *paysanne*, *chiffonade*;
  - tools and stages: *mise en place* / klaarzetten, *bain-marie* / waterbad, *roux*, *fond* / bouillon,
    *al dente*.
- **Why the terms matter.** They name a technique precisely, so a recipe can say much in one word; and the learner
  meets them in shops, books and on menus.
- **The glossary** is the learner's own, growing with every course. Each term gets the three languages, a one-line
  meaning and a reference to the lesson where it is practised.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): build the first 40 terms of the glossary, with a flash-card review.

**Cues:** the learner reads a French or Dutch recipe and knows what each term asks them to do.

**Faults:**
- Learning the words without the technique.
- Confusing *braiser* (with liquid, covered) with *rôtir* (dry heat).
- Treating *julienne* as any thin strip.

#### WRK-1.M4.L2 — Writing recipe notes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-wrk-1-m4-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `WRK-1.M2.L3` · moves RD-WRIT→2, JD-REFL→2*

*Objectives:* [S] write a clear note: quantities by weight, steps in order, cues; [J] review it after cooking
<!-- END generated:lesson-wrk-1-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A reproducible note** has:
  - the yield;
  - the ingredients by weight, in the order of use;
  - the equipment and pan size;
  - numbered steps with cues and times;
  - holding and serving notes.
- **Cues over times:** "until the onion is soft and just colouring at the edges (about 8 min)".
- **The review after cooking:** what was unclear, what was missing, what the reader had to guess. Someone else cooking
  from the note is the real test.

**Sessions:**
1. Write and test (60 min, plus the household cook): write a note for a dish cooked this week; a household member cooks
   from it while the learner watches without helping; revise it.

**Cues:** the other cook finishes without asking a question, and the result matches.

**Faults:**
- "A handful", "a splash", "cook until done".
- Ingredients that appear in the method but not in the list.
- Steps out of order.
