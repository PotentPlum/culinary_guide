## Introduction

The same recipe gives different results on a gas ring and on induction, in thin aluminium and in cast iron, in an oven
that runs hot and one that runs cold. Professional kitchens hide this behind standard equipment; a home kitchen does
not. `EQP-1` makes the learner's own equipment **known and measured**:
- how pan materials behave;
- where the hob's hot spots are;
- what the oven really does at each setting;
- how knives and measuring tools are kept accurate.

This is the first applied science in the program. Conduction and heat capacity explain why a cast-iron pan sears and a
thin pan scorches. The numbers in the oven table explain why a recipe's 40 minutes became 55. `HEA-1` builds on both.

**In the spiral.**
- `KM-EQPT` continues with the calibration log kept from here on, the tier 1 purchases in S2 and the specialist
  equipment of S3–S4.
- `SC-HEAT` reaches level 2 here and continues in `HEA-1` and `SCI-2`.
- The honing habit is a prerequisite of `KNF-1`.

**Pacing.** About a week and a half alongside `SAF-1`. The hob map and the oven calibration need an evening each; the
pan comparison doubles as a dinner.

#### EQP-1.M1.L1 — Pan materials

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves SC-HEAT→1, KM-EQPT→1*

*Objectives:* [K] stainless, aluminium, copper, cast iron, carbon steel, enamel, non-stick: conductivity, heat capacity, reactivity
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Conductivity** is how fast heat spreads through the metal, which gives even heat and a quick response. Copper and
  aluminium conduct well; iron and stainless steel poorly.
- **Heat capacity** is how much heat the pan stores, which lets it hold its temperature when cold food goes in. Thick
  cast iron stores a lot; thin aluminium little.
- **Reactivity:** bare aluminium, iron and copper react with acid (tomato, wine, vinegar), giving a metallic taste and
  discolouring the food. Stainless steel and enamel do not.
- **Construction:** clad stainless (an aluminium or copper core) combines even heat with an inert surface; a thick base
  evens out hot spots; non-stick coatings release food but tolerate neither high empty heat nor metal tools.

**Sessions:**
1. Feel the difference (45 min): heat a thin and a heavy pan on the same setting, sprinkle a little flour, and watch
   where and how fast it browns; note the response when the heat is turned down.

**Cues:** the learner predicts which pan will scorch first and which will recover after cold food goes in.

**Faults:**
- Thinking "heavy" always means "better".
- Simmering tomato sauce for hours in a bare cast-iron pan.
- Preheating a non-stick pan empty on full heat.

#### EQP-1.M1.L2 — The right pan for the job

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m1-l2 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `EQP-1.M1.L1` · moves KM-EQPT→2*

*Objectives:* [J] choose by task (searing, sauce, braise, eggs); size versus burner; Good/Best choices
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The task decides the pan:**
  - searing: high heat capacity (cast iron, carbon steel, heavy stainless);
  - sauces and reductions: even heat, an inert surface, a wide pan for fast evaporation;
  - braising: a heavy lidded pot;
  - eggs and delicate fish: non-stick or a well-seasoned carbon-steel pan.
- **Size against burner:** a pan much wider than the burner has a hot centre and cold edges; too small and the flame
  licks the handle. A crowded pan steams instead of browning.
- **Good and Best:** which pans are worth more money, and which tasks a single Good pan can cover.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): match the learner's own pans to ten tasks, and note the gaps the S1 courses will feel.

**Cues:** a pan chosen for each task, with a one-line reason based on material and size.

**Faults:**
- One non-stick pan for everything, including searing.
- A 28 cm pan on the smallest ring.

#### EQP-1.M1.L3 — Seasoning and care

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m1-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EQP-1.M1.L1` · moves KM-EQPT→2*

*Objectives:* [S] season and maintain cast iron and carbon steel; protect non-stick; clean burnt pans
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Seasoning** is a thin layer of oil polymerised by heat into a hard, slick coating on cast iron and carbon steel.
  Thin layers, wiped almost dry, baked hot, repeated.
- **Daily care of iron pans:** rinse and scrub while warm, dry on the heat, then a thin film of oil. They rust when left
  wet.
- **Non-stick:** medium heat, wooden or silicone tools, no dishwasher if the maker advises against it; replace it when
  the coating is damaged.
- **Burnt pans:** soak, then simmer water with a little bicarbonate, then scrape with wood. No steel wool on coatings.

**Sessions:**
1. Season and restore (60 min): season (or re-season) a cast-iron or carbon-steel pan, and clean one burnt pan;
   photograph before and after.

**Cues:** an even, dark, slightly glossy surface that is not sticky; a fried egg that releases.

**Faults:**
- A thick oil layer that turns sticky.
- Leaving an iron pan to soak.
- Scouring a seasoned surface back to bare metal.

#### EQP-1.M1.X1 — Two pans, one task

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-eqp-1-m1-x1 -->
*comparison, recipe* — Heat the same amount of water and sear the same item in two different pans; compare.
<!-- END generated:exercise-eqp-1-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** KM-EQPT→2

**Aim:** see conductivity and heat capacity in action, and choose pans for real recipes from what was seen.

**Conditions:** recipe support: a fixed procedure. Two pans of different materials (for example thin aluminium or
non-stick, and cast iron or heavy stainless) on the same burner and setting.

**Inputs:** 1 litre of water; two similar pieces for searing, for example two slices of halloumi, two thick slices of
celeriac or two burgers; oil.

**Procedure:**
1. Time how long 1 litre of water takes to boil in each pan, on the same burner.
2. Preheat each pan, sear one piece in each, and photograph both sides.
3. Note the temperature drop and the recovery, by sound and colour.
4. Choose and justify a pan for three recipes the learner will cook in S1.

**Evidence:** photo, written — the times, the side-by-side photos of the sear, and the three pan choices with reasons.
With this module's quiz at 80 %, this meets the `KM-EQPT` level 2 success row.

AI review compares the evenness and colour of the two sears. Reference set for Phase 6:
- *Competent*: an even, deep golden crust;
- *Not yet*: pale in the middle and burnt at the edge.

**Time:** 45 min; the seared pieces are part of a meal.

#### EQP-1.M2.L1 — Gas, ceramic and induction

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m2-l1 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `EQP-1.M1.L1` · moves SC-HEAT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] response speed, power, pan compatibility; [J] how each changes technique
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Gas** heats the pan from a flame. It responds at once and heats the pan's sides too; the power differs by burner.
- **Ceramic and electric** plates heat by radiation and contact, and respond slowly: the plate stays hot after it is
  turned down. The cure is to lift the pan, or to keep a second plate at a lower setting.
- **Induction** heats the pan itself through a magnetic field. It is very fast and precise, needs a magnetic base (test
  it with a magnet), and heats only where the pan touches the coil zone.
- **How technique changes:** on ceramic, plan the heat changes ahead; on induction, beware of very fast scorching and of
  thin pans that warp.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): identify the learner's hob type and power, test the pans with a magnet if it is
induction, and write the three adjustments it calls for.

**Cues:** the learner can say how fast their hob responds and what they do about it.

**Faults:**
- Expecting a ceramic plate to cool like gas.
- A non-magnetic pan on induction.
- A pan wider than the induction zone.

#### EQP-1.M2.L2 — Mapping hot spots

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m2-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · moves KM-EQPT→2*

*Objectives:* [S] the flour or toast test on each burner; record the map
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Hot spots** come from the shape of the flame or coil and from thin pan bases. The flour test (an even layer of flour
  in a dry pan, browning in a pattern) or a sheet of bread (toasting in a pattern) makes them visible.
- **The map:** for each burner, the pattern, the time to brown, and which pan to use where.

**Sessions:**
1. Flour test (45 min): run the flour test with the main pan on every burner at one setting; photograph each pattern;
   draw the map.

**Cues:** clear patterns; the learner knows where in the pan food will brown first.

**Faults:**
- Testing at different settings.
- A greasy pan that spreads the flour unevenly.
- Forgetting to note the pan used.

#### EQP-1.M2.L3 — Oven calibration

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m2-l3 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · moves KM-EQPT→2, SC-HEAT→2*

*Objectives:* [S] measure the true temperature at settings; fan versus conventional; rack positions and their effect
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The true temperature:** an oven cycles around its setting and its dial can be off by tens of degrees. An oven
  thermometer on the middle rack, read after the oven has stabilised, gives the real figure.
- **Fan against conventional** (*hetelucht* against *boven- en onderwarmte*): moving air transfers heat faster, so fan
  settings are often set lower than conventional ones. The table shows by how much for this oven.
- **Rack positions:** the top browns more, the bottom heats the base more, the middle is the most even. The oven door and
  the back can be hotter or cooler.

**Sessions:**
1. Calibration table (90 min, mostly waiting): measure the true temperature at 150, 180 and 220 °C, with fan and
   conventional, after 20 minutes at each; note the time to preheat; test the top and bottom racks at 180 °C.

**Cues:** a table the learner trusts, and the corrected setting for each recipe temperature.

**Faults:**
- Reading the thermometer through a steamed-up door.
- Reading before the oven has stabilised.
- Opening the door every two minutes.

#### EQP-1.M3.L1 — Knife anatomy and choice

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m3-l1 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · moves KM-EQPT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] blade shapes, steels, edges; the three essential knives; Good/Best
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Anatomy:** tip, edge, belly, heel, spine, bolster, tang and handle; a full tang and a balanced weight make control
  easier.
- **Steels:** stainless (forgiving, holds an edge less long), carbon steel (keener edge, stains and rusts); Western
  knives usually have a wider edge angle than Japanese ones, which are harder and more brittle.
- **The three essential knives** (tier 0): a 20 cm chef's knife, a paring knife, a serrated bread knife. Good and Best
  for each. Other knives (boning, slicing, a cleaver) come when a course needs them.
- **Choosing:** the grip, the weight and the length matter more than the brand.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): hold and compare the learner's knives, identify their parts and steel, and note the
gaps.

**Cues:** the learner can say why their chef's knife suits or does not suit their hand.

**Faults:**
- A block of ten mediocre knives.
- A chef's knife that is too short to rock.
- Carbon steel left wet.

#### EQP-1.M3.L2 — Honing and sharpening

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m3-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EQP-1.M3.L1` · moves KM-EQPT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] honing versus sharpening; [S] hone safely; an introduction to the whetstone
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Honing** realigns an edge that has rolled over in use. A honing steel is used often, every session or before a
  long cutting job.
- **Sharpening** removes metal to make a new edge, on a whetstone or a sharpening service. It is needed when honing no
  longer restores the bite.
- **Safe honing:**
  - the steel vertical, tip down on a damp towel;
  - the blade drawn down and towards the body at a constant angle (about 15–20°, depending on the knife);
  - alternating sides;
  - light pressure.
- **The paper test:** a sharp edge slices a hanging sheet of paper cleanly; a dull one tears it.
- **The whetstone** is introduced here and bought with tier 1 in S2.

**Sessions:**
1. Hone and test (45 min): the paper test, honing on the steel (filmed), the paper test again; then cut a tomato with
   the honed and an unhoned knife.

**Cues:** the knife slides into a tomato skin without pressure; the strokes are slow and even, at one angle.

**Faults:**
- Waving the knife at the steel at a changing angle.
- Honing towards the hand that holds the steel.
- Heavy pressure.
- Expecting a steel to fix a chipped edge.

#### EQP-1.M3.L3 — Small tools and measuring

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-eqp-1-m3-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M3.L2` · moves KM-EQPT→2*

*Objectives:* [S] check a scale with a known weight; care of thermometers, peelers, graters, sieves
<!-- END generated:lesson-eqp-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Measuring tools drift.** A scale is checked against a known weight: ten €1 coins weigh 75 g, a calibration weight is
  better. A thermometer is checked in ice water monthly and after a drop (`SAF-1.M3.L2`).
- **Care:**
  - peelers and graters are cleaned with a brush straight away and dried;
  - sieves are rinsed before the starch dries;
  - wooden tools are washed by hand and oiled now and then;
  - probe thermometers are wiped, never soaked unless waterproof.
- **The calibration log** records each check with its date. It is kept from now on, and level 3 of `KM-EQPT` asks for
  it.

**Sessions:**
1. Check and care (45 min): check the scale with coins, the thermometer in ice water, and start the calibration log;
   clean and sort the small tools.

**Cues:** a log with dated readings; tools clean, dry and in their place.

**Faults:**
- A scale that is never checked because it is digital.
- A probe dunked in the washing-up water.
- A grater put away dirty.

#### EQP-1.M3.X1 — Paper test, honing and scale check

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-eqp-1-m3-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — The paper test before and after honing; a scale check.
<!-- END generated:exercise-eqp-1-m3-x1 -->

**Conditions:** recipe support: the honing procedure and the check list are given.

**Procedure:**

4. Check the scale with the coins.

set for Phase 6:
- *Competent*: a side view of safe, even strokes;
- *Not yet*: a steel held in the air, a changing angle, strokes towards the hand.

**Time:** 30 min.
