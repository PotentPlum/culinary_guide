# SAF-1 — Food safety and hygiene I

<!-- BEGIN generated:header -->
**Stage:** S1 · **Hours:** 15.5 h active, 9 practical sessions · **Modules:** 4 · **Lessons:** 10

**Prerequisites:** `ORI-1` · **Required by:** `EQP-1`, `KNF-1`, `HEA-1`, `MS-S1`

**Purpose (outline):** prevent foodborne illness and kitchen accidents at home. This is the non-negotiable basis of every practical. Follows the Dutch Voedingscentrum guidance [S21][S22], with the FDA *Food Code* where a measurable figure is needed [S25] (Phase 4 review).
<!-- END generated:header -->

## Introduction

`SAF-1` makes safe practice a habit before the first knife or pan lesson. Every later practical assumes it, and every
milestone re-tests it as a gate that no score can make up for. It is the only course whose lessons the `ORI-1`
diagnostic can never waive.

**What it covers.**
- **Why food makes people ill:** microbes, toxins, high-risk foods and vulnerable people.
- **The four core rules** of the Voedingscentrum: clean, separate, heat and chill [S21].
- **Each rule turned into a routine:** a hygienic station, the fridge order, a thermometer that is trusted because it
  is calibrated, and fast cooling.
- **Allergens and accidents:** the 14 EU allergens, and the actions for burns, cuts and fat fires.

**Where the figures come from.** Every figure is taken from [assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment):
- the Dutch home rules where they exist [S21][S22];
- the FDA *Food Code* where a gate needs a measurable number [S25];
- the stricter of the two where they differ.

The course teaches the rule and the number together, so the learner can apply both at home and later in a guest
dinner.

**In the spiral.**
- **Hygiene** (`SA-HYG`) reaches level 3 here. It is applied in `KNF-1` and `HEA-1`, then in `POU-2`, `MEA-2` and
  `FSH-2` with raw protein.
- **Temperature control** (`SA-TEMP`) is re-tested in every milestone and climbs to level 5 at the capstone.
- **Allergens** (`SA-ALLG`) return when cooking for guests in S3 and S4.
- **Physical safety** (`SA-PHYS`) is practised in `KNF-1`.

**Pacing.** About two to three weeks, with the work spread out:
- the fridge log runs for a week;
- the cleaning routine is repeated after two weeks;
- the cooling lesson needs two days.

`WRK-1` and `EQP-1` can run alongside it.

### SAF-1.M1 — How food makes people ill

*Check (outline):* scenario quiz (which step is risky, and why).
<!-- END generated:module-saf-1-m1 -->

#### SAF-1.M1.L1 — Microbes, toxins and high-risk foods

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · moves SC-MICR→1, SA-HYG→1*

*Objectives:* [K] bacteria, viruses, toxins; high-risk foods (poultry, minced meat, eggs, rice, shellfish); vulnerable groups
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Bacteria** multiply in food given time, warmth, moisture and nutrients. Examples are *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter*,
  *Listeria* and *Bacillus cereus*.
- **Viruses** such as norovirus do not grow in food. They are carried to it by hands and surfaces, so nobody cooks for
  others while ill with vomiting or diarrhoea.
- **Toxins** are made by some bacteria in the food. Some survive heating, so reheating cannot rescue food that was left
  warm too long.
- **Spores** of *B. cereus* and *Clostridium perfringens* survive cooking and grow when cooked food cools slowly. This
  is why cooling matters (`SAF-1.M3.L3`).
- **High-risk foods:** poultry, minced meat, raw and lightly cooked eggs, shellfish, cooked rice and other starches kept
  warm, cooked foods held for later, and soft cheeses and smoked fish for vulnerable people.
- **Vulnerable people:** young children, pregnant people, people over 70, and people with weakened immunity. For them
  a mild illness can be serious.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): read, then sort a list of foods by risk and give the reason for each.

**Cues:** the learner can say, for any food, what could grow in it, under which conditions, and what stops it.

**Faults:**
- Thinking that smell and look reveal unsafe food. Most pathogens change neither.
- Believing that reheating always makes food safe again.
- Overlooking cooked rice and pasta as risks.

#### SAF-1.M1.L2 — The four core rules

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m1-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M1.L1` · moves SA-HYG→2, SA-TEMP→1*

*Objectives:* [K] clean, separate, heat and chill [S21]; [J] apply them to a week of home cooking
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- The four rules [S21]:
  - **clean**: hands, tools and surfaces;
  - **separate**: raw from ready-to-eat;
  - **heat**: right through;
  - **chill**: fast, and cold enough.
- Each rule stops one route. Clean and separate stop transfer; heat kills; chill slows growth.
- The household figures that frame the course [S22]:
  - the fridge set to 4 °C;
  - leftovers out of the fridge for no more than 2 hours;
  - leftovers kept in the fridge for at most 2 days.
- Applying the rules to a real week: where in each meal could each rule fail?

**Sessions:**
1. Rule audit (60 min): take one week of planned home cooking and mark, meal by meal, the step where each rule
   applies; then list the three riskiest moments of the week and what to change.

**Cues:** for each meal, a named control point (for example "chicken last on the board; board washed straight away").

**Faults:**
- Treating the rules as a list to recite instead of moments in a meal.
- Forgetting "separate" in the fridge and the shopping bag.
- Planning leftovers that sit out after dinner.

#### SAF-1.M1.X1 — Risk walk-through of three meals

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-saf-1-m1-x1 -->
*guided, recipe* — Risk walk-through of the learner's last three cooked meals.
<!-- END generated:exercise-saf-1-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** SA-HYG→2

**Aim:** see one's own cooking through the four rules, and fix the weakest moments.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the walk-through template and the four-rule checklist are given. The learner's last
three cooked meals, from memory and the journal.

**Inputs:** the journal; the four-rule checklist.

**Procedure:**
1. For each meal, list the steps from shopping to leftovers.
2. Mark every point where a rule applied, and whether it was kept.

**Evidence:** checklist, written — the three walk-throughs and the ticked checklist for one meal. Together with the
module check at 100 % on the hygiene items, this meets the `SA-HYG` level 2 success row.

**Time:** 1 h active.

#### SAF-1.M2.L1 — Hands, cloths and sponges

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m2-l1 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M1.L2` · moves SA-HYG→2*

*Objectives:* [S] wash hands properly; manage and replace towels, cloths and sponges
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Hand washing:** warm water and soap, all surfaces (backs, between fingers, thumbs, nails), rinsed, dried on a clean
  towel or paper. When: before cooking, after raw meat, fish or eggs, after the bin, the phone, the face or the toilet.
- **Cloths and sponges** stay damp and warm, which is ideal for bacteria. A cloth wiped over a raw-meat spill spreads
  it everywhere it goes next.
- **The routine:**
  - one cloth for the counter and a separate towel for hands;
  - cloths and tea towels replaced daily and washed hot;
  - sponges replaced often, or replaced by a brush that dries;
  - paper for raw-meat spills.

**Sessions:**
1. Hand-washing drill and towel routine (30 min): wash with a timer while someone watches (or on video), set up the
   cloth and towel routine, and note the moments in a usual meal where hands must be washed.

**Cues:** hands washed without reminders at every change from raw to ready-to-eat; a fresh cloth every day.

**Faults:**
- Wiping hands on the apron or the tea towel used for dishes.
- One cloth for everything.
- A sponge kept for weeks.

#### SAF-1.M2.L2 — Separating raw and cooked

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m2-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves SA-HYG→3, IN-STO→2*

*Objectives:* [S] separate boards and utensils; fridge order with raw meat and fish at the bottom [S22]
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Cross-contamination:** raw meat, poultry, fish and unwashed produce can carry pathogens to food that won't be
  cooked again, by hands, boards, knives, cloths and drips.
- **Separate tools:** a separate board for raw protein (another colour), or ready-to-eat food cut first and raw protein
  last. Knives and tongs are changed or washed between them.
- **The fridge order** [S22]:
  - raw meat and fish at the bottom, covered, in a container that catches drips;
  - ready-to-eat and cooked food above them;
  - vegetables in the drawer;
  - the door, the warmest part, for drinks and sauces.

**Sessions:**
1. Fridge and board set-up (45 min): reorganise the fridge by the order, label the raw-protein board, and photograph
   both.

**Cues:** nothing raw above anything ready-to-eat; the raw board recognisable at a glance.

**Faults:**
- The raw mince on the top shelf because it is the newest.
- The same tongs for raw and cooked chicken on the barbecue.
- Washing raw chicken in the sink, which splashes bacteria around it.

#### SAF-1.M2.L3 — Cleaning versus disinfecting

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m2-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 wk · moves SA-HYG→3*

*Objectives:* [K] what each removes; [S] a cleaning routine for boards, counters, the fridge and the sink
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Cleaning** (detergent and hot water, with friction) removes dirt, grease and most microbes.
- **Disinfecting** kills or removes most of the remaining microbes on a surface that is already clean. Dirt shields
  microbes and weakens disinfectants, so cleaning always comes first.
- **Disinfecting at home** is done by heat (a hot dishwasher cycle, boiling water over a board) or by a household
  disinfectant used as its label says.
- **When disinfecting is needed:** after raw meat, poultry, fish or eggs, and after illness in the house. Everyday
  surfaces need cleaning, not disinfectant.
- **The routine:**
  - boards and knives after each use;
  - the counter after each session;
  - the sink and the fridge weekly, as a schedule on the fridge door.

**Sessions:**
1. Routine set-up (40 min): clean the fridge and sink, write the routine, and do the board cycle after a raw-protein
   prep.
2. Repeat after two weeks (20 min): run the routine again from the schedule alone and note what slipped.

**Cues:** a schedule that is actually followed; boards that air-dry upright; no smell from the sink or the cloths.

**Faults:**
- Spraying disinfectant on a dirty board.
- Rinsing a raw-chicken board in cold water.
- Drying clean boards with the hand towel.

### SAF-1.M3 — Temperature control

*Check (outline):* temperature logs (objective evidence) and a quiz.
<!-- END generated:module-saf-1-m3 -->

#### SAF-1.M3.L1 — The danger zone and the fridge

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m3-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 wk · builds on `SAF-1.M1.L2` · moves SA-TEMP→2, IN-STO→2*

*Objectives:* [K] growth between the cold and hot holding limits (5 and 57 °C) [S25]; [S] check that the fridge holds 4 °C and the freezer −18 °C [S22]
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Growth range.** Most pathogens multiply between fridge and cooking temperatures, fastest around body temperature.
  The program uses the measurable limits of [assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment):
  cold food at 5 °C or below, hot food at 57 °C or above [S25]. Between them, time counts.
- **The fridge** is set to 4 °C, and the freezer to −18 °C or colder [S22]. The Dutch fridge rule is stricter than the
  5 °C limit, so 4 °C applies.
- A fridge dial is not a thermometer. The temperature differs by shelf, rises when the door opens and when warm food
  goes in, and is lowest at the back of the bottom shelf.
- **Freezing** stops growth but kills little. Food thaws in the fridge, not on the counter.

**Sessions:**
1. Fridge log (15 min a day for a week): place the fridge thermometer on the middle shelf, read it morning and evening,
   note the door openings and the warm food put in, and adjust the setting until it holds 4 °C. Read the freezer once.

**Cues:** the reading stable at 4 °C or below on the middle shelf, and back within an hour after a shop.

**Faults:**
- Trusting the dial.
- Reading straight after the door was open.
- A fridge packed so full that air cannot circulate.
- Thawing meat on the counter.

#### SAF-1.M3.L2 — Thermometers and core temperatures

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m3-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves SA-TEMP→2, KM-EQPT→1*

*Objectives:* [S] calibrate with ice water; probe correctly; [K] the safe core temperatures of the main protein groups [S25]
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Calibration:** in a slurry of crushed ice and a little water, stirred, the probe reads 0 °C. In boiling water at
  sea level it reads about 100 °C. An offset is noted, or corrected if the thermometer allows it.
- **Probing:** the thickest part, away from bone, fat and the pan; from the side in thin pieces; wait until the reading
  is stable; clean the probe between raw and cooked.
- **The core temperatures** the program uses ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment),
  FDA *Food Code* [S25]):
  - poultry and stuffed foods: 74 °C;
  - minced meat and minced fish: 70 °C instantaneous, or 68 °C held for 17 seconds;
  - fish and whole cuts of meat: 63 °C for 15 seconds;
  - whole roasts follow a time and temperature table, taught in `MEA-2`.

  The Dutch rule "right through" (*door en door gaar*) is the same rule without a number [S21].

**Sessions:**
1. Calibrate and probe (60 min): calibrate the thermometer, then probe a chicken thigh and a burger or meatball as part
   of a meal, logging the readings and where the probe went.

**Cues:** a reading of 0 °C in ice water (or a known offset); a probe placed in the centre of the thickest part; a
reading that has stopped climbing.

**Faults:**
- Probing at the surface.
- Touching the bone or the pan.
- Reading too early.
- Using an uncalibrated probe.
- Judging chicken by the colour of its juice.

#### SAF-1.M3.L3 — Cooling, storing and reheating

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m3-l3 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 d · builds on `SAF-1.M3.L1` · moves SA-TEMP→3, IN-STO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cool food fast in shallow containers; label it; reheat until steaming hot; [K] rice and *Bacillus cereus*
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Cooling fast.** Spores that survive cooking grow while food cools slowly. Large, deep pots cool slowest.
- **At home** [S22]: leftovers go into the fridge within 2 hours, after cooling faster in shallow containers or in a
  pan standing in cold water.
- **The measurable standard** for batches and stocks [S25]: from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours, and to 5 °C or below
  within 6 hours in total. It is used from `STK-2` on and is introduced here.
- **Storing:** covered, labelled with the contents and the date, kept at most 2 days in the fridge, or frozen [S22].
- **Reheating:** until steaming hot right through (*stomend heet*) [S21], measurable as 74 °C [S25], and only once.
- **Rice:** *B. cereus* spores survive cooking. If rice stands warm, the bacteria form a toxin that reheating does not
  destroy. Rice is cooled quickly, kept in the fridge and reheated once only.

**Sessions:**
1. Cool, store, reheat (90 min over two days, plus waiting): cook a batch dish, cool it in shallow containers with
   temperature readings over time, label it and refrigerate it; on day two, reheat a portion to steaming hot and probe
   it.

**Cues:** the food is in the fridge within 2 hours; labels with a date; a reheating reading of 74 °C or above at the
centre.

**Faults:**
- A full pot of soup put in the fridge to cool overnight.
- Containers too deep.
- No date on the label.
- Reheating the same leftovers twice.
- Rice kept warm in the cooker for hours.

#### SAF-1.M3.X2 — Cool, store and reheat a batch

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-saf-1-m3-x2 -->
*guided, recipe* — Cool, store and reheat a batch of leftovers with a temperature log.
<!-- END generated:exercise-saf-1-m3-x2 -->

**Evidences:** SA-TEMP→3, IN-STO→3

**Aim:** the full temperature chain of a home batch, measured: cooked through, cooled fast, stored, reheated once.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the learner's usual recipe for a stew or sauce with minced meat or chicken, for 4–6

**Inputs:** for example 500 g of minced beef or 600 g of chicken thighs, onions, carrots and a tin of tomatoes or a

**Procedure:**
1. Cook the dish and probe the meat to its core temperature; log it.
2. Divide it into shallow containers; cool them in a cold-water bath; log the temperature every 30 minutes until it is

4. Next day, reheat a portion until steaming hot, probe the centre and log it.
5. Freeze or discard the rest within the 2 days.

**Time:** 1.5 h active over two days; the meal is dinner on both days.

#### SAF-1.M4.L1 — The 14 allergens and cross-contact

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m4-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves SA-ALLG→2*

*Objectives:* [K] the EU list and hidden sources; reading labels; [S] prevent cross-contact when cooking for others
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The 14 groups** that EU law requires to be declared [S23]:
  - cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk;
  - nuts (tree nuts), celery, mustard, sesame;
  - sulphites above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/l, lupin, molluscs.
- **Hidden sources:**
  - milk in sausages and bread;
  - celery in stock cubes and spice mixes;
  - mustard in dressings;
  - fish in Worcestershire sauce;
  - sulphites in wine and dried fruit;
  - lupin flour in some breads and gluten-free products;
  - soy in many processed foods.
- **Reading labels:** on a prepacked product the allergens are emphasised in the ingredient list, for example in bold
  [S23]. "May contain" warnings are voluntary.
- **Cross-contact:** a trace of an allergen can harm an allergic guest, and cooking does not remove allergens. Prevent
  it by:
  - cooking the allergen-free dish first and covering it;
  - using clean tools and pans;
  - keeping separate spoons for tasting.

**Sessions:**
1. Label hunt (45 min): read five pantry products, list their allergens and the hidden ones, and plan how to cook one
   household meal free of a named allergen.

**Cues:** every allergen on a label found, including the ones in bold inside compound ingredients.

**Faults:**
- Reading only the "contains" line on the front.
- Assuming a homemade stock is free of celery.
- Toasting nuts in the pan used for the allergen-free dish.

#### SAF-1.M4.L2 — Burns, cuts and fires

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-1-m4-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · moves SA-PHYS→2*

*Objectives:* [K] causes of accidents; [S] smother a fat fire (never water); first aid for cuts and burns
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-1-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Causes of accidents:**
  - burns from steam, handles turned outwards, splashing oil, wet towels used as oven gloves;
  - cuts from dull knives, knives left in the washing-up water, cutting towards the hand;
  - fires from unattended oil.
- **A fat fire:**
  - turn off the heat if it is safe to reach;
  - cover the pan with a lid or a fire blanket;
  - leave it covered until it is cold;
  - never use water, which turns to steam at once and throws burning fat out;
  - never carry the pan;
  - call 112 if it does not go out.
- **First aid:**
  - **burns** are cooled at once under lukewarm running water, for as long as the first-aid guidance says; Phase 6
    cites the Dutch Red Cross for the duration;
  - **cuts** get pressure, are cleaned and covered;
  - medical help for deep cuts and for large or deep burns.
- **Prevention:**
  - a dry towel or oven gloves;
  - handles turned inwards;
  - a lid and a fire blanket within reach whenever there is oil in a pan.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): study the procedures, then place the fire blanket and the first-aid kit and walk
through the fat-fire steps with a cold pan and a lid.

**Cues:** the learner says the fat-fire steps in order without hesitation; the lid and the blanket are within reach of
the hob.

**Faults:**
- Reaching for water.
- Carrying a burning pan to the sink.
- A fire blanket in the cellar.
- Catching a falling knife.
