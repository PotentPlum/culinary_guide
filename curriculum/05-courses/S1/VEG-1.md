## Introduction

Vegetables are the largest part of a home cook's work and the part most often cooked by habit. The usual result is too
soft, grey or watery, or the same three vegetables all year. `VEG-1` treats vegetables as a subject of their own, the
way a professional kitchen does:
- **structure:** cells, pectin and water, and why heat, acid and alkali change texture;
- **groups:** what a root, a brassica or a leaf has in common in the pan;
- **pigments:** why green turns olive and red cabbage turns blue;
- **the season:** buying by the Dutch calendar and storing to keep quality;
- **method by group:** matching the method to the vegetable;
- **potatoes:** the preparations of the Dutch kitchen, from boiled to mashed to roast.

The course ends with a vegetable plate on which three vegetables, cooked three ways, share one clean plate.

The program's content scope applies here more than anywhere: **Dutch locally grown vegetables, by season**. Every
exercise gives seasonal choices, so the course can start in any month, and the seasonal calendar learned here is used
in every later course.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-VEG` reaches level 3 here, and `VEG-3` (S3) takes it to vegetable-led cooking.
- `IN-VEG` and `SC-STRC` continue in `SCI-2`.
- The vegetable plate is the first composed plate, and `TS-PLT` starts here.

**Pacing.** About five weeks. The group dishes of module 4 run over two weeks of dinners; the market visit fits a
Saturday morning.

#### VEG-1.M1.L1 — Cells, pectin and water

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L2` · moves IN-VEG→1, SC-STRC→1*

*Objectives:* [K] cell walls, pectin, turgor, starch; what heat, acid and salt do to them [S16]
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A vegetable is mostly water in cells.** The pressure of that water against the cell walls (**turgor**) gives
  crispness; a limp carrot has lost water.
- **Cell walls** are glued together by **pectin**. Heat dissolves it gradually, so the cells separate and the vegetable
  softens. Starchy vegetables also soften as their starch absorbs water [S16].
- **What changes the speed:**
  - **acid** (tomato, wine, vinegar) slows the softening, so vegetables cooked in it stay firm;
  - **alkali** (bicarbonate) speeds it, so they turn mushy;
  - **calcium** firms the cell walls;
  - **salt** slightly speeds the softening and seasons.

**Sessions:**
1. Three waters (60 min): the comparison of `VEG-1.M1.X1`.

**Cues:** the learner predicts the order of softness before tasting.

**Faults:**
- Adding tomatoes too early to beans that must soften.
- Bicarbonate "for colour" that turns vegetables to mush.

#### VEG-1.M1.L2 — The vegetable groups

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · moves IN-VEG→2*

*Objectives:* [K] roots, tubers, brassicas, alliums, leaves, stems, squash, fruit vegetables, pods and seeds
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The groups**, and what each has in common in the kitchen:
  - roots (carrot, beetroot, parsnip, celeriac);
  - tubers (potato, Jerusalem artichoke);
  - brassicas (cabbages, sprouts, kale, cauliflower, broccoli);
  - alliums (onion, leek, garlic, shallot);
  - leaves (spinach, lettuces, chard, lamb's lettuce);
  - stems and shoots (asparagus, celery, fennel);
  - squash;
  - fruit vegetables (tomato, courgette, pepper, cucumber);
  - pods and seeds (beans, peas, broad beans).
- **The group predicts** density, water, sugar and the risk of overcooking. Dense roots take long, high heat; leaves
  take seconds; brassicas turn sulphurous when overcooked.
- **Basic preparation** by group: scrub or peel roots, trim brassicas, wash leaves well, string beans.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): sort a week's vegetables into groups and write the main cooking consequence for each
group.

**Cues:** any vegetable placed in its group, with the method consequence.

**Faults:**
- Treating all "green vegetables" alike.
- Peeling everything.
- Washing leaves under a running tap in the sink.

#### VEG-1.M2.L1 — Seasons and freshness

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `VEG-1.M1.L2` · moves IN-SEL→2*

*Objectives:* [K] the local seasonal calendar; [J] judge freshness (firmness, colour, smell, cut ends)
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The Dutch seasonal calendar** in outline:
  - **winter:** storage roots and cabbages, kale (*boerenkool*), Brussels sprouts, leeks, chicory (*witlof*);
  - **spring:** asparagus (*asperges*, roughly April to late June), early greens and radishes;
  - **summer:** beans, courgettes, tomatoes, cucumbers, lettuces;
  - **autumn:** squash, cauliflower, the first cabbages, and the start of the storage season.

  In season, produce is cheaper, fresher and better. The learner keeps the calendar in the journal.
- **Judging freshness:**
  - firmness;
  - bright colour;
  - a fresh smell;
  - moist, not dried, cut ends (asparagus, leeks);
  - leaves without yellowing;
  - beans that snap.
- **Choosing by use:** a slightly limp carrot is fine for soup; a salad needs the best.

**Sessions:**
1. The calendar (45 min): build this month's list from a seasonal calendar and mark what the learner already cooks and
   what is new.

**Cues:** a shopping list that matches the season; the learner names the freshness signs of each item.

**Faults:**
- Buying by recipe instead of by season.
- Choosing the biggest instead of the freshest.
- Ignoring the cut ends.

#### VEG-1.M2.L2 — Storing vegetables

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m2-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M3.L1` · moves IN-STO→3, KM-WAST→2*

*Objectives:* [S] fridge, cool and room storage; ethylene; reviving limp vegetables; using them up
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Where each keeps:**
  - **fridge drawer:** most vegetables, in a bag that lets a little moisture out;
  - **cool, dark and dry, not the fridge:** potatoes (the cold turns their starch to sugar), onions and squash;
  - **room temperature:** tomatoes, which lose flavour in the cold.
- **Ethylene:** apples, pears and ripening tomatoes release a gas that yellows broccoli and leaves and makes cucumbers
  soft. Keep them apart.
- **Reviving:** limp roots and leaves regain crispness in cold water, because the cells take water back in.
- **Using them up:** "first in, first out"; soup, stock and stir-fry for the tired ones; a weekly waste log (`KM-WAST`).

**Sessions:**
1. Storage and revival (45 min): store the week's vegetables by the rules, photograph the result, and revive a limp
   carrot and a head of lettuce in cold water.

**Cues:** the revived vegetables crisp again; the fridge drawer organised; nothing forgotten at the back.

**Faults:**
- Potatoes in the fridge.
- Onions and potatoes stored together.
- Tomatoes in the fridge.
- Apples in the vegetable drawer.

#### VEG-1.M3.L1 — Pigments

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m3-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `VEG-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-VEG→2*

*Objectives:* [K] chlorophyll, anthocyanins, carotenoids under acid, alkali and time; [S] keep greens green
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Chlorophyll** (green):
  - turns olive with long cooking and with acid;
  - a lid traps the vegetable's own volatile acids;
  - short cooking in plenty of boiling water, uncovered, then shocking, keeps it bright.
- **Anthocyanins** (red, purple, blue: red cabbage, beetroot, red onion):
  - red in acid, blue or grey in alkali;
  - this is why Dutch red cabbage is cooked with apple or vinegar;
  - hard tap water can turn it bluish.
- **Carotenoids** (orange and yellow: carrot, squash, pepper) are stable to acid and heat. They dissolve in fat, which
  carries their colour.

**Sessions:**
1. Colour tests (45 min): red cabbage cooked with and without acid; green beans cooked 4 minutes and 12 minutes;
   photograph them.

**Cues:** the learner predicts each colour before lifting the lid.

**Faults:**
- Lemon juice in the green beans while they cook.
- Red cabbage with no acid.
- A lid on greens.

#### VEG-1.M3.L2 — Texture and firmness

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m3-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `VEG-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-VEG→2, SC-STRC→2*

*Objectives:* [K] why acid and calcium keep vegetables firm; overcooking; [J] choose a doneness
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Acid and calcium keep vegetables firm:** potatoes in a tomato stew stay firm, apples with calcium keep their
  shape, and pickles stay crisp.
- **Overcooking:** past the doneness wanted, texture is lost for good, and so are colour and flavour, which leach into
  the water. Brassicas turn sulphurous.
- **Choosing a doneness:**
  - tender-crisp for a salad or a side with bite;
  - fully tender for a purée or a soup;
  - just tender for a vegetable that will cook further in a sauce.

  The doneness wanted is decided before cooking, and then cooked to.

**Sessions:**
1. Doneness (45 min): one vegetable (cauliflower, beans or carrots) cooked to three stated textures, each checked with a
   knife and by biting, photographed.

**Cues:** three clearly different textures, each matching its target.

**Faults:**
- One doneness for every use.
- Cooking by the clock only.
- Forgetting the carryover in a vegetable left in the hot pan.

<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Roasting** (`HEA-1.M4.L1`) concentrates and browns.
- **Glazing** (*glacer* / glaceren) cooks small, even pieces in a little liquid with butter and a pinch of sugar,
  partly covered, until the liquid has reduced to a shiny coating just as the vegetables become tender. The ratio of
  vegetable to liquid decides whether the two arrive together.
- **Purée:** cooked fully tender (boiled or steamed), drained well, blended or pressed, enriched with butter or cream,
  and seasoned.
- **Cut size and method:** small, even pieces for glazing; larger chunks for roasting; any size for a purée.

**Sessions:**
1. Glazed carrots (60 min): from the ratio, two batches with different amounts of liquid; compare.
2. Roast and purée (75 min): roast beetroot or parsnip; a celeriac purée.

**Cues:** the glaze coats the carrots as they turn tender; the purée is smooth and holds a soft peak.

**Faults:**
- Too much liquid, so the vegetables are soft before it reduces.
- Too little, so they burn.
- A watery purée from vegetables not drained or dried.

#### VEG-1.M4.L2 — Brassicas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m4-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L2` · moves TS-VEG→2*

*Objectives:* [S] blanch, roast, sauté and braise cabbage, sprouts and kale
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Blanch:** fast, in plenty of salted water, for green brassicas (broccoli, kale).
- **Roast:** halved sprouts cut-side down, cauliflower florets. Browning adds sweetness and limits the sulphur.
- **Sauté:** shredded cabbage or sprouts, quick and hot.
- **Braise:** white or red cabbage cooked slowly with a little liquid, fat and acid until silky.
- **The sulphur problem** is avoided by short cooking, or by long, gentle braising with acid. The middle ground smells
  worst.

**Sessions:**
1. Sprouts and cauliflower (60 min): Brussels sprouts blanched against halved and roasted; cauliflower roasted.
2. Cabbage (60 min): shredded cabbage sautéed; red cabbage braised with apple (or, in summer, pointed cabbage sautéed
   and a kohlrabi slaw).

**Cues:** roasted sprouts browned on the cut side and tender; braised red cabbage glossy and deep red.

**Faults:**
- Sprouts boiled to grey.
- Kale undercooked and tough, or cooked to mush.
- Red cabbage without acid.

#### VEG-1.M4.L3 — Leaves

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m4-l3 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-VEG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] wash and dry; wilt and sauté greens; [J] cook down volume
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m4-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Washing and drying:** wash in a bowl and lift the leaves out (`VEG-1.M1.L2`); dry them in a salad spinner. Wet
  leaves steam in the pan and dilute a dressing.
- **Wilting:** spinach and chard collapse to a small fraction of their raw volume. A pan full of leaves, added in
  handfuls, gives a small portion. Buy accordingly.
- **Sautéing greens:** a hot pan, a little fat and garlic, the leaves turned until just wilted; the excess water
  squeezed out for a side, kept for a soup.
- **Chard:** stems cooked first, leaves added late.

**Sessions:**
1. Spinach (45 min): wilt spinach two ways (in a dry pan, and sautéed with garlic); weigh it raw and cooked.
2. Chard or kale (45 min): stems and leaves cooked separately and combined.

**Cues:** bright green, glossy, just wilted; no puddle on the plate.

**Faults:**
- Wet leaves in the pan.
- Too little bought.
- Overcooking until slimy.
- Stems and leaves cooked together.

#### VEG-1.M4.L4 — Alliums

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m4-l4 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M2.L2` · moves TS-VEG→3, SC-BRWN→2*

*Objectives:* [S] sweat versus brown versus caramelise onions and leeks; [K] what each gives a dish
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m4-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Three ways of cooking onions, and what each gives a dish:**
  - **sweating:** low heat, a little fat, often a lid; soft and translucent, no colour; a sweet, mild base for soups,
    risotto and white sauces;
  - **browning:** medium-high heat, colour on the edges; savoury depth for stews and gravies;
  - **caramelising:** long and slow (`HEA-1.M2.L2`); deep, sweet and jammy, for a topping or a soup.
- **Leeks** sweat sweet and silky and burn easily; they are best sweated, braised or grilled.

**Sessions:**
1. Three onions (60 min): the same onion sweated, browned and caramelised; each tasted in a spoon of stock.

**Cues:** three distinct colours and flavours, none burnt.

**Faults:**
- "Sweating" at high heat, which browns them.
- Browning leeks for a white sauce.
- Rushing the caramelising.

#### VEG-1.M5.L1 — Potato types and starch

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m5-l1 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-STA→1, IN-VEG→2*

*Objectives:* [K] waxy versus floury; starch and texture; storage and greening (solanine)
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Dutch classes:** *vastkokend* (waxy), *iets kruimig* (slightly floury) and *kruimig* (floury).
  - Waxy potatoes have less starch and hold their shape: salads, boiled, gratins.
  - Floury potatoes fall apart into a light, dry texture: mash, roast potatoes, chips.
- **Starch:** in floury potatoes the cells separate easily when cooked; in waxy potatoes they stay together.
- **Storage:** cool, dark and dry, not in the fridge, which turns starch to sugar so they brown too fast and taste sweet.
- **Greening:** light makes potatoes green and raises solanine, a bitter natural toxin. Green parts and sprouts are cut
  away generously; very green potatoes are discarded.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): read the labels of three potato varieties, class them, and plan a use for each.

**Cues:** the learner chooses the right class for mash, salad and roast without looking it up.

**Faults:**
- Waxy potatoes for mash, which turns gluey.
- Floury potatoes for a salad, which falls apart.
- Green potatoes used as they are.

#### VEG-1.M5.L2 — Boiled and mashed

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m5-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-1.M5.L1` · moves TS-VEG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] start in cold water; mash without going gluey; *purée*
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m5-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Boiled potatoes** start in cold, salted water, so the outside and the centre cook evenly. They are done when a knife
  slides in without resistance. Drained and steamed dry for a minute.
- **Mash:**
  - floury potatoes, drained and dried;
  - mashed or riced while hot;
  - warm milk and butter worked in gently.

  Overworking (a blender or food processor) bursts the cells, releasing starch into a gluey paste.
- ***Purée*** (*pommes purée*): riced, then beaten with a lot of butter for a fine, smooth texture.

**Sessions:**
1. Boiled (45 min): waxy potatoes boiled from cold to just tender, steamed dry.
2. Mashed (60 min): a mash from floury potatoes and, the next time, a riced purée.

**Cues:** a knife slides in without resistance; a fluffy, smooth mash with no lumps and no glue.

**Faults:**
- Potatoes into boiling water.
- Cold milk.
- A blender for mash.
- Undrained, watery potatoes.

#### VEG-1.M5.L3 — Roast and pan-fried

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-1-m5-l3 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-1.M5.L1`, `HEA-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-VEG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] parboil and rough up for roasting; pan-fried and sautéed potatoes
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-1-m5-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Roast potatoes:**
  - floury potatoes, parboiled;
  - drained and shaken to rough up the edges;
  - into hot fat in a hot oven.

  The rough, starchy surface makes the crisp crust.
- **Pan-fried potatoes** (*gebakken aardappelen*) from boiled, cooled potatoes, which hold together and brown well;
  sautéed raw slices need a lid, then high heat.
- **Patience:** the potatoes are left until a crust forms before turning.

**Sessions:**
1. Roast (60 min): roast potatoes, parboiled and roughed up, against plain cubes, on the same tray.
2. Pan-fried (45 min): yesterday's boiled potatoes pan-fried in slices.

**Cues:** a deep golden, crisp crust and a fluffy inside; the pan-fried slices golden on both sides and intact.

**Faults:**
- Fat not hot enough.
- A crowded tray.
- Turning too early, which breaks the slices.
