## Introduction

Rice, pasta and grains are cooked so often that their faults go unnoticed:
- rice that is gluey or crunchy;
- pasta that is soft and sits in a pool of sauce;
- a grain salad that is bland because the grain was cooked in plain water.

`STA-1` gives them the same care as any other technique, starting from the science that explains every one of those
faults:
- **gelatinisation:** starch granules absorbing water and softening;
- **retrogradation:** cooked starch firming as it cools.

**The methods:**
- **rice:** the absorption method, pilaf and a basic risotto;
- **pasta:** dried pasta cooked *al dente* and finished in its sauce, emulsified with the starchy cooking water;
- **grains:** couscous, bulgur, barley and other grains.

**Safety:** cooked starches are high-risk when cooled slowly, because of *Bacillus cereus* (`SAF-1.M3.L3`). The last
module makes cooling and storing them a routine.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-STA` reaches level 3 here.
- Pulses and fresh pasta come in `LEG-2` and S3.
- Risotto is revisited with more control in S2.
- The pasta-water emulsion is the first sauce emulsion: it prepares `SAU-2`.

**Pacing.** About three weeks. Nearly every session is a dinner or a packed lunch; the absorption drill runs over three
evenings.

#### STA-1.M1.L1 — Gelatinisation and retrogradation

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L2` · moves SC-STRC→2, IN-STA→2*

*Objectives:* [K] how starch absorbs water and sets; amylose versus amylopectin; [J] rinse or not; why day-old rice fries well
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Gelatinisation:** heated in water, starch granules absorb water, swell and soften; some starch leaks out and
  thickens the liquid. It is what cooks rice and pasta and thickens a sauce [S16].
- **Retrogradation:** as cooked starch cools, its molecules realign and squeeze out water, so the starch firms and dries.
  This happens in stale bread, hard cold rice and a skin on a sauce. Reheating reverses part of it.
- **Amylose and amylopectin:**
  - **long-grain rice**, high in amylose, cooks into separate, fluffy grains and firms more when cold;
  - **short-grain risotto rice**, high in amylopectin, turns creamy and sticky.
- **Rinsing:** it removes surface starch, so grains stay separate (basmati, pilaf); risotto rice is never rinsed,
  because the surface starch makes the creaminess.
- **Day-old rice fries well:** retrograded, drier grains don't clump in the pan. It is cooled fast and kept cold (§9).

**Sessions:**
1. Rinsed against unrinsed (60 min): the comparison of `STA-1.M1.X1`, with a spoonful of each kept overnight in the
   fridge to see the retrogradation.

**Cues:** the rinsed rice in separate grains; the unrinsed rice clumping; both firmer and drier next day.

**Faults:**
- Rinsing risotto rice.
- Leaving cooked rice out to cool on the counter.
- Expecting freshly cooked rice to fry like day-old rice.

*Objectives:* [K] long, medium and short grain; basmati, jasmine, risotto rice, sushi rice; ratios and absorption

**Concepts:**
- **Rice by grain length:**
  - **long grain:** basmati (aromatic, separate grains), jasmine (fragrant, slightly sticky);
  - **medium and short grain:** risotto rice (arborio, carnaroli), sushi rice (sticky), pudding rice.
- **Ratios by weight:** absorption rice needs a set weight of water per weight of rice, which differs by type and by how

- **Brown rice** keeps its bran, so it needs more water and much more time.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): compare the labels and the grains of three rice types, and write a starting ratio for
each.

**Cues:** the right rice chosen for pilaf, risotto and a side of steamed rice.

**Faults:**
- Using one ratio for every rice.
- Cups instead of weight.
- Brown rice cooked like white.

#### STA-1.M2.L2 — The absorption method

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 3 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M2.L1` · moves TS-STA→2*

*Objectives:* [S] ratio by weight, simmer, rest; [J] fix rice that is wet or undercooked
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The absorption method:**
  - rice and a measured weight of water (salted), brought to the boil;
  - covered, at the lowest heat, until the water is absorbed;
  - off the heat, still covered, for 10 minutes, so the moisture evens out.

  No stirring, which breaks the grains and releases starch.
- **A heavy pan with a tight lid** prevents scorching and keeps the steam in.
- **Fixes:**
  - **wet rice:** uncover over low heat for a few minutes, or rest it longer with a towel under the lid;
  - **undercooked and dry:** a few spoons of boiling water, covered, low heat, rest again.

**Sessions:**
1. Basmati (45 min): with the starting ratio; photographed.
2. Jasmine (45 min): with its ratio; photographed.
3. Basmati again (45 min), with the ratio corrected from session 1.

**Cues:** small steam holes on the surface when the water is absorbed; separate, tender grains; no crust stuck to the
base.

**Faults:**
- Lifting the lid.
- Stirring.
- Too high a heat, which scorches the base.
- Skipping the rest.

#### STA-1.M2.L3 — Pilaf

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m2-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STA-1.M2.L2` · moves TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] toast the rice in fat with aromatics, add stock, cook covered
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The pilaf method:**
  - sweat onion in fat;
  - add the rinsed rice and toast it until every grain is coated and translucent at the edges;
  - add hot stock by weight;
  - cover and cook gently;
  - rest.
- **Why toast:** the fat coats the grains and the light toasting firms their surface, which gives separate grains and
  a nutty flavour.
- **Variations:** spices in the fat, a bay leaf, vegetables added at the end. The structure stays the same.

**Sessions:**
1. Pilaf (75 min): a pilaf with stock and onion, served with a vegetable side.

**Cues:** grains glistening and separate; fragrant; no gluey patches.

**Faults:**
- Skipping the toasting.
- Cold stock.
- Unrinsed rice.
- Stirring after the stock goes in.

#### STA-1.M2.L4 — Risotto basics

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m2-l4 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STA-1.M2.L2` · moves TS-STA→2*

*Objectives:* [S] toasting, adding stock in stages, *mantecatura*; [K] why stirring releases starch
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m2-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The steps:**
  - toast the rice in fat with a sweated onion (*tostatura*);
  - add wine, then hot stock a ladle at a time, at a simmer;
  - stir regularly;
  - stop when the grain is tender with a slight bite;
  - beat in cold butter and cheese off the heat (*mantecatura*).
- **Why stirring releases starch:** the friction between grains rubs starch from their surface into the liquid, which
  thickens it into a creamy sauce around grains that stay intact.
- **The result:** a risotto that flows in a gentle wave on the plate (*all'onda*), not a stiff mound.

**Sessions:**
1. Risotto (75 min): a basic risotto (with a seasonal vegetable: pumpkin, leek or peas).

**Cues:** creamy and flowing; the grains separate and *al dente*; glossy after the *mantecatura*.

**Faults:**
- Cold stock, which stops the cooking.
- All the stock at once.
- Rinsed rice.
- Overcooking to a porridge.
- Butter beaten in over the heat.

#### STA-1.M3.L1 — Cooking dried pasta

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m3-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STA-1.M1.L1`, `TST-1.M2.L3` · moves TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] water, salt, timing, *al dente*; [K] myths (oil in the water, rinsing)
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Water and salt:** enough water for the pasta to move; salted to taste, starting around 10 g per litre
  (`TST-1.M2.L3`), because the pasta is seasoned only from the water.
- **Stirring** in the first minutes, while the surface starch is sticky, prevents clumping. Oil in the water does not.
- ***Al dente:*** cooked through with a slight bite and a thin, pale core when cut. Tasted from a few minutes before the
  packet time.
- **Myths:**
  - oil in the water only floats on top;
  - rinsing washes off the starch that helps the sauce cling, so pasta is rinsed only for some cold salads.

**Sessions:**
1. Taste the minutes (45 min): cook a dried pasta and taste a piece every minute from 4 minutes before the packet time;
   cut each and photograph it; mark the *al dente* point.

**Cues:** a thin white dot in the centre of the cut at *al dente*, disappearing a minute later.

**Faults:**
- Unsalted water.
- A small pot.
- Not stirring at first.
- Trusting the packet time.
- Rinsing.

#### STA-1.M3.L2 — Saucing with pasta water

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-STA→3, SC-EMUL→1*

*Objectives:* [S] finish the pasta in the sauce; emulsify with starchy water and fat
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Finishing the pasta in the sauce:** drain a minute before *al dente* and cook it for the last minute in the sauce,
  so it absorbs flavour and the sauce coats it.
- **Pasta water:**
  - the starchy cooking water binds fat and water into a glossy emulsion that clings to the pasta;
  - it is the first emulsion of the program;
  - its theory (`SC-EMUL`) comes in `SAU-2`.
- **Adjusting:** pasta water to loosen; more tossing and heat to tighten; fat (oil, butter, cheese) added off the
  fiercest heat so it doesn't separate.

**Sessions:**
1. *Aglio e olio* (60 min): garlic in oil, pasta finished with pasta water until glossy.
2. A tomato pasta (60 min): a quick tomato sauce, the pasta finished in it with pasta water.

**Cues:** a sauce that clings and shines, with no oily pool or watery puddle under the pasta.

**Faults:**
- Draining all the water away.
- Sauce poured on top of plain pasta.
- Oil that separates because there was no starch or movement.

#### STA-1.M3.L3 — Shapes and sauces

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m3-l3 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M3.L1` · moves IN-STA→2*

*Objectives:* [K] traditional pairings of shape and sauce, and why they work
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The traditional pairings,** and why they work:
  - long, thin pasta (spaghetti, linguine) with smooth sauces and oil-based or seafood sauces;
  - ribbons (tagliatelle) with butter and meat sauces;
  - tubes and ridged shapes (penne, rigatoni) with chunky sauces and ragù, which they trap;

- **Bronze-die pasta** has a rough surface that holds sauce better.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): match six shapes with sauces, and plan two dinners from the pairings.

**Cues:** a pairing explained by how the sauce meets the surface.

**Faults:**
- Delicate oil sauces on thick tubes.
- Chunky ragù on thin spaghetti.

#### STA-1.M4.L1 — Couscous and bulgur

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m4-l1 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STA-1.M1.L1` · moves TS-STA→2*

*Objectives:* [S] the steeping methods and fluffing; seasoning the liquid
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Steeping:** couscous and fine bulgur are already cooked or parboiled, so they only need to absorb hot liquid.
  - Pour boiling, seasoned liquid over them at about equal volume.
  - Cover for 5–10 minutes.
  - Fluff with a fork and a little fat.
- **Season the liquid** (stock, salt, spices), because a steeped grain can't be seasoned evenly afterwards.
- **Coarse bulgur** may need a short simmer.

**Sessions:**
1. Steep and fluff (45 min): couscous with stock and bulgur with water and lemon; taste the difference the seasoned
   liquid makes.

**Cues:** separate, fluffy grains; seasoned throughout.

**Faults:**
- Plain water.
- Too much liquid, which gives a paste.
- Stirring with a spoon instead of fluffing with a fork.

#### STA-1.M4.L2 — Whole grains

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m4-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STA-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-STA→2, TS-STA→2*

*Objectives:* [S] quinoa, pearl barley and farro: boil like pasta versus absorption; [J] check doneness
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Two methods:**
  - **boil like pasta:** in plenty of salted water, then drain. Forgiving, and best for barley, farro and spelt;
  - **absorption:** a measured amount of liquid. For quinoa and for a richer pilaf-style grain.
- **Quinoa** is rinsed to remove its bitter natural coating.
- **Doneness:** tender with a slight chew; the grains begin to split (barley) or show a little tail (quinoa).
  Tasted, not timed.
- **Dutch-grown options:** pearl barley (*parelgort*) and spelt (*spelt*).

**Sessions:**
1. Barley and quinoa (60 min): pearl barley boiled like pasta, quinoa by absorption; tasted at intervals for doneness.

**Cues:** barley plump, tender and chewy; quinoa fluffy with its little tails visible.

**Faults:**
- Unrinsed quinoa.
- Barley undercooked and hard.
- Grains boiled in unsalted water.

#### STA-1.M4.L3 — Cooling and storing starches

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-sta-1-m4-l3 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `SAF-1.M3.L3` · moves SA-TEMP→3, IN-STO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cool rice and grains fast; store and reheat safely (*B. cereus*)
<!-- END generated:lesson-sta-1-m4-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***B. cereus*** (`SAF-1.M3.L3`): its spores survive cooking, grow while cooked rice and grains cool slowly, and form a
  toxin that reheating does not destroy.
- **The routine** ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)):
  - spread the cooked starch on a tray in a thin layer to cool fast;
  - into the fridge within 2 hours, covered, labelled and dated;
  - eaten within 2 days;
  - reheated once, until steaming hot (74 °C).
- **Batch cooking grains** for the week is safe with this routine, and it is how the grain salad is made.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): write the cooling and storing routine onto the fridge door, and apply it to the grain
salad of the exercise.

**Cues:** rice on a tray, not in the pot; a dated label; a reheating reading at 74 °C or above.

**Faults:**
- Rice cooling overnight in the cooker.
- Deep tubs of warm grains in the fridge.
- Reheating twice.
