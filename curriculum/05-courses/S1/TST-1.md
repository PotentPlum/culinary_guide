# TST-1 — Tasting and seasoning I

<!-- BEGIN generated:header -->
**Stage:** S1 · **Hours:** 17.5 h active, 14 practical sessions · **Modules:** 4 · **Lessons:** 13

**Prerequisites:** `ORI-1` · **Required by:** `STA-1`, `MS-S1`

**Purpose (outline):** learn to taste deliberately and describe what you taste, use salt as a tool, balance acid, fat, sweet, bitter and umami, and calibrate your palate [S18][S20].
<!-- END generated:header -->

## Introduction

Recipes say "season to taste", and that hands the most important judgment of a dish to the cook. `TST-1` trains that
judgment directly:
- **tasting with method:** separating taste, aroma, texture and trigeminal sensations, and naming them in a shared
  vocabulary;
- **salt as a measurable tool:** weighing it and finding the right level, then knowing when to add it;
- **the other levers of balance:** acid, fat, sweet, bitter, umami and heat;
- **correction:** fixing the simple faults of a dish.

The course also makes taste **assessable**. A palate can't be photographed, but it can be calibrated
([assessment §5](../../04-assessment.md#5-calibration-tests)):
- **The tests:** ranking tests and triangle tests with coded samples [S20].
- **Why they matter:** from this course on, the learner's own sensory forms count as evidence only while a passed salt
  and acid calibration stands behind them.
- **The tasting thread:** the calibration is repeated monthly in S1, and runs through every later stage.

**In the spiral.**
- `FL-SENS` and `FL-SEAS` reach level 2 here. They are re-tested at `MS-S1` by the salt and acid series and the tasters'
  cards on the meal.
- `FLV-3` (S3) takes flavour building and correction to level 3 and above.
- The fault-diagnosis test of `MS-S3` builds on module 4.

**Pacing.** About three weeks, in short evening sessions of an hour or so. Most sessions need a household member, to
code samples or to taste blind, so plan them on evenings when someone is home.

*Check (outline):* tasting forms (self-assessed) and a vocabulary quiz.
<!-- END generated:module-tst-1-m1 -->

#### TST-1.M1.L1 — Taste, aroma and texture

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `ORI-1.M3.L2` · moves FL-SENS→1, FL-TEXT→1*

*Objectives:* [K] the five tastes, retronasal aroma, trigeminal sensations, texture and temperature [S18]
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The five tastes** sensed by the tongue: salt, sour, sweet, bitter and umami.
- **Flavour** is taste plus aroma. Most of what we call flavour is **retronasal aroma**: volatile compounds that rise
  from the mouth to the nose while chewing and swallowing. With the nose pinched, a strawberry sweet and a lemon one taste
  almost alike [S18].
- **Trigeminal sensations:** the burn of chilli, the cool of mint, the tingle of carbonation, the bite of mustard. They
  are sensed by nerves of touch and pain, not by taste.
- **Texture and temperature** change what we perceive. Warmth increases sweetness and aroma; cold mutes them; fat coats
  and carries aroma.

**Sessions:**
1. Nose-pinch test (45 min): a sweet, a fruit jam, a cheese and a herb tasted with the nose pinched and then open; a
   chilli, a mint leaf and a sparkling water noted for their trigeminal sensations.

**Cues:** the learner says, for each food, what came from the tongue, what from the nose, and what was touch or pain.

**Faults:**
- Calling everything "taste".
- Confusing sour with bitter.
- Tasting while still warm from the previous sample.

#### TST-1.M1.L2 — Tasting method and vocabulary

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M1.L1` · moves FL-SENS→2*

*Objectives:* [S] a structured tasting (look, smell, taste, feel, finish); a shared vocabulary
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A structured tasting:**
  - **look:** colour, sheen, texture;
  - **smell:** orthonasal, before tasting;
  - **taste:** the five tastes and their balance;
  - **feel:** texture, temperature, fat, trigeminal;
  - **finish:** what lingers, and for how long.
- **The tasting form:** one line per stage, the vocabulary list beside it, and a score of the balance from "needs a
  lot" to "just right". The form is used for the rest of the program.
- **A shared vocabulary** lets the learner and the tasters compare their impressions: flat, bright, round, sharp,
  heavy, clean, muddy.
- **Neutral conditions:** water and a plain cracker between samples, samples at the same temperature, no strong smells
  around.

**Sessions:**
1. Five foods with the form (45 min): taste five different foods (for example an apple, a mature Gouda, a dark
   chocolate, a pickled gherkin and a slice of rye bread) using the form, then compare notes with a household member.

**Cues:** every stage of the form filled in, with specific words; the learner and the taster find the same main
features.

**Faults:**
- "Nice" and "good" as the only words.
- Skipping the smell stage.
- Writing the form afterwards.

### TST-1.M2 — Salt

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-tst-1-m2 -->
*5 h · 3 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches FL-SEAS 2, FL-SENS 2*

*Scope:* salt types, measuring, calibration, timing.

*Objectives:* [S] salt by weight and by taste; [J] decide when to salt.

*Check (outline):* calibration result (objective: the % chosen against the reference range) plus the tasters' agreement.
<!-- END generated:module-tst-1-m2 -->

#### TST-1.M2.L1 — Salt types and measuring

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m2-l1 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · moves FL-SEAS→1*

*Objectives:* [K] crystal size and volume versus weight; [S] weigh salt and use pinches consistently
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Salt types:**
  - fine table salt and fine sea salt: dense, dissolve fast;
  - coarse and flaky salts: lighter per spoon, crunchy;
  - salts with iodine or anti-caking agents: the taste difference is small.
- **Volume against weight:** a teaspoon of fine salt weighs about twice a teaspoon of flaky salt. Recipes in grams avoid
  the problem; a pinch needs calibrating.
- **Calibrating the pinch:** the learner's own two-finger and three-finger pinches, weighed ten times, so they know what
  a pinch is in grams.

**Sessions:**
1. Weigh the salts and the pinch (45 min): weigh a teaspoon of each salt; weigh ten pinches of fine salt; record the
   average and the spread.

**Cues:** the learner knows their pinch in grams within a small spread.

**Faults:**
- Swapping one salt for another by the spoon.
- A pinch that varies with the mood.

#### TST-1.M2.L2 — Salt calibration series

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L1` · moves FL-SENS→2, FL-SEAS→2*

*Objectives:* [S] taste a stock at 0.4–1.6 % salt and find "just right"; blind-repeat to check consistency [S20]
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **"Just right":** a stock tasted across a range of salt levels, from 4 to 16 g per litre (0.4–1.6 %), shows where it
  goes from flat to full to too salty. The point the learner prefers is noted and compared with the tasters' choice.
- **The calibration test** that counts as evidence uses the protocol of
  [assessment §5.1](../../04-assessment.md#51-salt-series):
  - five coded samples at 6, 8, 10, 12 and 15 g/l, ranked: pass with at most one swap of neighbours;
  - six triangle trials of 10 against 12 g/l: pass with 4 of 6.
- **Blind repetition** shows whether a preference is stable or a guess [S20].

**Sessions:**
1. The series (90 min): a helper prepares and codes the samples; the learner tastes the 4–16 g/l range for "just right",
   then runs the ranking test and, where time allows, the triangle trials.

**Cues:** the ranking passed; "just right" between about 8 and 12 g/l and close to the tasters' choice.

**Faults:**
- Samples at different temperatures.
- Too little base, so weighing errors swamp the differences: a full litre per sample on a 1 g scale.
- Peeking at the key.

#### TST-1.M2.L3 — When to salt

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m2-l3 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L2` · moves FL-SEAS→2*

*Objectives:* [K] salting water, pre-salting proteins, seasoning in layers, finishing salt; [J] choose the moment
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Salting water** for vegetables and pasta seasons them from the outside in. The level is a choice the learner tastes,
  starting around 10 g/l.
- **Pre-salting proteins** some time ahead lets the salt move in, and changes texture and juiciness. It is a preview:
  the science comes in `SCI-2`.
- **Seasoning in layers:** some salt early, so it penetrates; adjust at the end, because reduction concentrates it;
  finishing salt for crunch.
- **Choosing the moment:**
  - salt early for what must be seasoned through: potatoes, a braise, a pre-salted steak;
  - salt late for what reduces (a sauce or stock) and for what must stay crisp (a salad, crisp skin).

**Sessions:**
1. Early against late (60 min): the comparison of `TST-1.M2.X2`, with green beans or carrots, tasted blind by a household
   member.

**Cues:** the vegetables salted at the start taste seasoned throughout; the ones salted at the end taste salty outside
and flat inside.

**Faults:**
- Salting a stock at the start and reducing it.
- Salting a salad long before serving.
- Forgetting the pasta water, then over-salting the sauce.

#### TST-1.M2.X1 — The calibration series (salt and acid)

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-tst-1-m2-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — The salt calibration series, with household tasters as a blind panel.
<!-- END generated:exercise-tst-1-m2-x1 -->

**Evidences:** FL-SENS→2, FL-SEAS→2

**Aim:** a calibrated palate for salt and acid, measured against a known key, with the household as a blind panel.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the protocols of [assessment §5.1 and §5.2](../../04-assessment.md#5-calibration-tests).
Coded cups and a hidden key, prepared by a helper. The acid series runs on a second evening; it is added to this
exercise so that the level 2 calibration is complete in one module (see the exercise scope notes in the overview, §5).

**Inputs:**
- **Salt:** 5 litres of unsalted vegetable stock (made from trim or a salt-free base) and 51 g of fine salt.
- **Acid:** 5 litres of the same stock salted at 10 g/l, and 50 ml of white-wine vinegar.
- **Tasting:** 15–20 coded cups.

**Procedure:**
1. The helper prepares and codes the salt series and the triangle sets.
2. Run the ranking test, then the triangle test.
3. Record before reading the key.
4. Each household taster takes the triangle test once (assessment §5.4).
5. Repeat with the acid series (0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 ml/l; triangle 10 against 15 ml/l).
6. Note the preferences of the learner and of the tasters.

**Evidence:** calibration, tasters — the ranking and triangle results for salt and acid against the key, and the
tasters' results:
- passing both ranking tests completes `FL-SENS` level 2, with the forms from `TST-1.M1.X1`;
- it is the calibration part of `FL-SEAS` level 2;
- passing the triangle tests starts the 6-month validity of the learner's sensory forms
  ([assessment §5.5](../../04-assessment.md#55-validity)).

**Time:** 2 × 60 min, plus the helper's preparation.

#### TST-1.M2.X2 — Salted early against salted late

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-tst-1-m2-x2 -->
*comparison, recipe* — Vegetables salted at the start versus only at the end.
<!-- END generated:exercise-tst-1-m2-x2 -->

**Evidences:** FL-SEAS→2

**Aim:** taste what the moment of salting does, with one variable changed.

**Conditions:** recipe support: a fixed comparison. The same vegetable, cut, water and total salt; only the moment
differs.

**Inputs:** 500 g of green beans in summer, or carrots in winter; salt.

**Procedure:**
1. Cook half in salted water (10 g/l).
2. Cook half in unsalted water and salt it at the end with the same amount that the first half absorbed (estimated by
   weighing the salt and the water).
3. A household member tastes both blind and says which is better seasoned.
4. The learner writes why.

**Evidence:** tasters, written — the blind taster's choice and the learner's explanation. With `TST-1.M2.X1`, the
vegetables seasoned at the stage the method names and called "neither under- nor over-seasoned" by the tasters meet
`FL-SEAS` level 2.

**Time:** 45 min; the vegetables are a side dish.

*Objectives:* [K] what each lever does; [S] adjust a dish with each one.

*Check (outline):* tasting forms; quiz on what each lever does.
<!-- END generated:module-tst-1-m3 -->

#### TST-1.M3.L1 — Acid

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l1 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L2` · moves FL-SEAS→2*

*Objectives:* [K] vinegars and citrus; how acid brightens and balances; [S] adjust a soup with acid
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Acid brightens:** it lifts a flat dish, cuts richness and sharpens other flavours. Too much tastes sharp or thin.
- **The acids of a Dutch kitchen:**
  - wine and cider vinegars;
  - lemon;
  - the sour of buttermilk, yoghurt and fermented vegetables (sauerkraut);
  - sour apples.

  Each brings its own aroma as well as its acid.
- **Adding in steps:** a few drops, stir, taste; acid added late keeps its freshness.

**Sessions:**
1. Acid in a soup (45 min): a pumpkin, carrot or pea soup tasted with drops of three acids added in steps; note the
   point where it goes from flat to bright to sharp.

**Cues:** the soup suddenly "opens up" with a few drops; the point is noted in drops per bowl.

**Faults:**
- Adding acid in one go.
- Mistaking a lack of salt for a lack of acid.
- Boiling vinegar for long, so its aroma is lost.

#### TST-1.M3.L2 — Fat

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L2` · moves FL-SEAS→2*

*Objectives:* [K] fat as a flavour carrier and for mouthfeel; [S] finish a dish with oil or butter
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Fat carries flavour:** many aroma compounds dissolve in fat, which releases them slowly and makes a dish taste
  rounder and last longer.
- **Mouthfeel:** fat coats the mouth and softens sharp acid and bitterness.
- **Finishing fats:**
  - a raw olive or rapeseed oil;
  - a knob of butter;
  - a spoon of cream or crème fraîche.

  Each changes the dish in a different direction.

**Sessions:**
1. Finish with fat (45 min): the same soup finished with three fats, tasted side by side.

**Cues:** the fat-finished bowls taste rounder and their flavour lasts longer than the plain one.

**Faults:**
- Adding fat to fix a lack of salt.
- Too much fat, which dulls the dish.

#### TST-1.M3.L3 — Sweet and bitter

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l3 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L2` · moves FL-SEAS→2*

*Objectives:* [K] how sugar softens bitterness and acid; [S] balance a bitter leaf dressing
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Sweetness** softens both bitterness and acid; a little sugar or honey rounds a sharp tomato sauce or a dressing.
- **Bitterness** adds depth and appetite, from chicory (*witlof*), endive, radicchio, coffee, cocoa and charred food.
  Salt also suppresses bitterness.
- **The bitter-leaf dressing:** acid, fat, a touch of sweet and enough salt make chicory pleasant without hiding its
  character.

**Sessions:**
1. Balance a bitter leaf (45 min): a chicory or endive salad (a Dutch winter staple) dressed four ways (acid only, plus
   fat, plus sweet, plus salt), tasted in order.

**Cues:** the bitterness present but no longer dominant; the dressing holding the leaf without drowning it.

**Faults:**
- Masking bitterness with a lot of sugar.
- Forgetting salt in a dressing.

#### TST-1.M3.L4 — Umami

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l4 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L2` · moves FL-SEAS→2, IN-ARO→1*

*Objectives:* [K] glutamates and nucleotides; synergy; [S] add umami (tomato, cheese, mushroom, soy)
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Umami** is the savoury taste of glutamate, found in ripe tomato, aged cheese (an old Gouda), soy sauce and stock.
- **Nucleotides** (inosinate in meat and fish, guanylate in dried mushrooms) multiply the effect of glutamate: this is
  **synergy**. Stock with dried mushrooms, or tomato with anchovy, tastes much deeper than either alone.
- **Adding umami** gives depth and length, especially to vegetable dishes and to food with less salt.

**Sessions:**
1. Build depth (45 min): a plain vegetable broth tasted plain, with tomato paste, with old Gouda rind, with dried
   mushrooms, and with tomato and mushroom together.

**Cues:** the combination tastes clearly deeper than either addition alone.

**Faults:**
- Reaching for stock cubes, which add mostly salt.
- Adding so much soy sauce that the dish becomes salty.

#### TST-1.M3.L5 — Heat and pungency

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l5 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L2` · moves FL-SEAS→2*

*Objectives:* [K] capsaicin, pepper, mustard, horseradish; [S] add heat in controlled steps
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m3-l5 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Chilli heat (capsaicin)** dissolves in fat, not water. It builds in the mouth and lingers; dairy and fat soothe it.
- **Black pepper (piperine)** is sharp and warming. Its aroma fades with long cooking, so it is ground fresh and added
  late.
- **Mustard, horseradish and radish** owe their bite to volatile compounds that flash up the nose and fade quickly. They
  weaken with heat.
- **Adding heat in steps:** a little, wait, taste again, because heat builds over a minute.

**Sessions:**
1. Heat in steps (45 min): a soup divided and given chilli, pepper and mustard in measured steps; note when each
   becomes noticeable and too much, and how fast each fades.

**Cues:** the learner can say how each heat behaves (builds, lingers, flashes) and where "just enough" lies.

**Faults:**
- Adding chilli to taste immediately, which overshoots.
- Boiling mustard into a sauce, so its bite is lost.

#### TST-1.M3.X1 — One soup, five ways

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-tst-1-m3-x1 -->
*comparison, recipe* — One base (a simple soup) adjusted five ways; taste side by side.
<!-- END generated:exercise-tst-1-m3-x1 -->

**Evidences:** FL-SEAS→2

**Aim:** feel each lever of balance on the same base, side by side.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the base soup and the five adjustments are given; each lever is added in measured
steps to its own bowl, from the same salted base.

**Inputs:** a simple vegetable soup for 6 (carrot, pumpkin, celeriac or peas by season), salted to the learner's "just

**Procedure:**

**Evidence:** form, tasters, measure — the forms and the amounts, and the taster's choice; each adjustment noted in

### TST-1.M4 — Seasoning in practice

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-tst-1-m4 -->
*4.5 h · 3 lessons · 4 practical sessions · reaches FL-SEAS 2, FL-CORR 1, JD-TRBL 1, FL-SENS 2*

*Scope:* seasoning at every stage; fixing imbalances; blind tests.

*Objectives:* [S] taste and adjust at set points; [J] diagnose and correct simple faults.

*Check (outline):* the correction test (self-assessed and tasters); the calibration is repeated monthly (tasting thread).
<!-- END generated:module-tst-1-m4 -->

#### TST-1.M4.L1 — Tasting at every stage

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m4-l1 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M2.L3` · moves FL-SEAS→2*

*Objectives:* [S] taste checkpoints during cooking; [K] why seasoning early and late differ
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Taste checkpoints:**
  - the raw ingredients;
  - after the aromatics;
  - when the liquid goes in;
  - halfway;
  - before serving;
  - on the plate.

  Each has its own question: is the base seasoned, has the reduction concentrated it, does it need acid at the end?
- **Early and late seasoning taste different:** salt added early seasons through and integrates; salt added at the end
  sits on the surface and tastes saltier per gram.
- **A clean tasting spoon each time**, never back into the pot (`SAF-1`).

**Sessions:**
1. Checkpoints (45 min): cook a simple braise or soup with the checkpoints written on the plan; taste and note at each;
   adjust only once at the end.

**Cues:** the notes change across the checkpoints the way the theory predicts (saltier after reduction).

**Faults:**
- Tasting only at the end.
- Adjusting at every checkpoint, so the dish overshoots.
- Double-dipping the spoon.

#### TST-1.M4.L2 — Correcting a dish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `TST-1.M3.L5` · moves FL-CORR→1, JD-TRBL→1*

*Objectives:* [J] diagnose flat, too salty, too sour, too sweet, too spicy; [S] correct them (dilute, balance, add)
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Diagnose first:** what is wrong, and which lever fixes it?
- **The fixes:**
  - **flat:** salt first, then acid;
  - **too salty:** dilute with unsalted base or add bulk; acid and fat mask a little, but a potato does not "draw out"
    salt;
  - **too sour:** sweet, fat, dilution;
  - **too sweet:** acid, salt, a bitter element;
  - **too hot:** dilution, fat or dairy, a little sweetness.
- **Correct in small steps**, and taste between them.

**Sessions:**
1. Diagnose (45 min): three samples of a broth, each with one fault, prepared and coded by a helper; diagnose each in
   writing before correcting.
2. Correct (45 min): correct each sample in steps; the helper tastes the corrected samples against a correct reference.

**Cues:** the diagnosis written before the first correction, and right; the corrected samples close to the reference.

**Faults:**
- Correcting before diagnosing.
- Fixing too salty with more acid until the dish is sharp and salty.
- One big correction instead of small steps.

#### TST-1.M4.L3 — Blind triangle test

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-tst-1-m4-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M1.L2` · moves FL-SENS→2*

*Objectives:* [S] run a triangle test with household tasters; [K] why blind tests beat opinion [S20]
<!-- END generated:lesson-tst-1-m4-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Why blind tests beat opinion:** knowing which sample is which changes what we taste, through expectation, brand,
  price and effort. A blind test with a known chance level shows whether a difference is real [S20].
- **The triangle test:**
  - three coded cups, two the same and one different;
  - the position of the odd cup varies;
  - with enough trials, a score well above one in three means the difference is detectable.
- **Using it:** to check whether a change in a recipe matters, before adopting it.

**Sessions:**
1. Run a triangle test (60 min): two versions of the same dish that differ in one thing (for example butter or oil,
   salt at 1 % or 1.2 %), tested with household tasters in six trials.

**Cues:** a result the learner can interpret: "the tasters could tell (5 of 6)" or "could not (2 of 6)".

**Faults:**
- Labelled cups.
- The odd cup always in the same position.
- Too few trials to tell a difference from luck.

#### TST-1.M4.X1 — Fix three unbalanced samples

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-tst-1-m4-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — Fix three deliberately unbalanced samples prepared by a household member.
<!-- END generated:exercise-tst-1-m4-x1 -->

**Evidences:** FL-SEAS→2

**Aim:** diagnose and correct simple faults, against a correct reference that the helper keeps.

**Conditions:** recipe support: a helper prepares a base broth, keeps one portion correct, and makes three faulty versions
(for example flat, too sour, too sweet) with a hidden key.

**Inputs:** 2 litres of broth; salt, vinegar, sugar, chilli; coded cups.

**Procedure:**
1. Taste each sample and write the diagnosis.
2. Correct in small steps, recording every addition.
3. The helper tastes each corrected sample against the reference and says whether they can tell them apart.

**Evidence:** form, tasters, measure — the written diagnoses, the corrections in grams or drops, and the helper's
judgment. The corrected samples called neither under- nor over-seasoned by the taster add to `FL-SEAS` level 2; the
diagnosis practice prepares `FL-CORR` and `JD-TRBL`, which this course takes to level 1.

**Time:** 1.5 h.

#### TST-1.M4.X2 — A triangle test on two versions of a dish

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-tst-1-m4-x2 -->
*comparison, recipe* — A triangle test on two versions of the same dish.
<!-- END generated:exercise-tst-1-m4-x2 -->

**Evidences:** FL-SENS→2

**Aim:** settle a cooking question by a blind test instead of by opinion.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the triangle protocol. One variable differs between the versions; household tasters and
the learner take six trials each.

**Inputs:** two versions of a simple dish, for example mashed potato with butter or with oil, or a soup salted at 10 or
12 g/l.

**Procedure:**
1. A helper codes the cups and keeps the key.
2. Run six trials for each taster, varying the position of the odd cup.
3. Record the results and compare them with chance.
4. Write the conclusion and the decision it leads to.

**Evidence:** calibration, written — the trial results against the key, and the conclusion. The learner's own result
(at least 4 of 6 on a detectable difference) is a further calibration point for `FL-SENS` level 2.

**Time:** 1 h.
