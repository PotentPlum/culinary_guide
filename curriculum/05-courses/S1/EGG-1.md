## Introduction

The egg is the program's first protein and its best doneness lab. It is cheap, it cooks in minutes, and every
difference of a few degrees or a minute shows in the cross-section. Learning what an egg does at 62, 65 and 70 °C
teaches the logic that the meat and fish courses depend on:
- proteins set at defined temperatures;
- carryover continues after the heat is off;
- gentle heat gives a tender result and harsh heat a rubbery one.

The course moves through:
- **egg science:** the structure, freshness and safety of the egg, and how its proteins set;
- **the classical preparations,** each to a stated standard: boiled, poached, fried, scrambled and the omelette;
- **custards:** a stirred and a baked custard, the first of the pastry foundations;
- **timing:** a breakfast for several people, served on time.

The evidence is mostly cross-section photos against reference images, which AI review scores reliably.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-EGG` reaches level 3 here.
- `SC-PROT` continues in `SCI-2` and the protein courses of S2.
- Custards return in `BAK-2` and `PST-3`; the emulsifying power of the yolk returns in `SAU-2`.
- Plating starts here at level 1 and is taught properly in `PLT-4`.

**Pacing.** About three weeks. Most lessons are breakfasts or light dinners. The omelette series and the boiled-egg
timing series each need a dozen eggs in one session.

#### EGG-1.M1.L1 — Anatomy, freshness and safety

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-DAI→2*

*Objectives:* [K] shell, white, yolk; sizes; freshness tests; storage; raw-egg guidance; [S] test and grade a box of eggs
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Anatomy:**
  - the shell (porous) and its membranes;
  - the air cell, which grows as the egg ages;
  - the thick and thin white;
  - the yolk, held in place by the chalazae.
- **Sizes:** EU size classes S, M, L and XL by weight. An egg can vary by 20 g, so baking recipes weigh them.
- **Freshness:**
  - **the water test:** a fresh egg lies flat; an older one tilts or stands; one that floats is old;
  - **on a plate:** a fresh egg has a high, domed yolk and a thick white that holds its shape; an old one spreads.
- **Safety:**
  - eggs are a high-risk food (`SAF-1.M1.L1`) because of *Salmonella*;
  - keep them cool and dry, in their box, not washed;
  - vulnerable groups avoid raw and runny eggs [S21];
  - wash hands after cracking them.
- **Storage:** the fridge, in the box. The best-before date assumes cool storage.

**Sessions:**
1. Grade a box (45 min): water-test and crack-test six eggs of different ages; photograph them on a plate; rank them.

**Cues:** the learner ranks the eggs by age correctly from the white and the yolk alone.

**Faults:**
- Washing eggs, which removes the protective layer.
- Cracking eggs on the bowl's rim, which pushes shell inside.
- Serving runny eggs to a vulnerable guest.

#### EGG-1.M1.L2 — How eggs set

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EGG-1.M1.L1` · moves SC-PROT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] protein denaturation; the white and the yolk set at different temperatures; [S] a water-bath test
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Denaturation and coagulation:** heat unfolds the proteins, which then bond into a network that traps water. A
  liquid becomes a gel, then a solid. Too much heat tightens the network, so it squeezes out water: rubbery whites and
  weeping scrambled eggs [S16].
- **Different proteins set at different temperatures:**
  - the white begins to set in the low 60s °C, but its main protein needs about 80 °C to be firm;
  - the yolk thickens from about 65 °C and sets by about 70 °C.

  That is why a soft-boiled egg can have a set white and a runny yolk.
- **Dilution raises the setting temperature:** sugar and milk in a custard let it thicken around 80 °C instead of
  setting at 70 °C.

**Sessions:**
1. Water-bath test (45 min active, about 2 h elapsed): the comparison of `EGG-1.M1.X1`, with the water held at each
   temperature by the method of `HEA-1.M5.X2`.

**Cues:** three clearly different cross-sections in the order the theory predicts.

**Faults:**
- Letting the bath drift by several degrees.
- Cracking the eggs too early.
- Not photographing each egg with its temperature label.

#### EGG-1.M2.L1 — Boiled eggs

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EGG-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-EGG→2, JD-CUES→2*

*Objectives:* [S] soft to hard boiled; peeling; [K] the green ring and how to avoid it
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Into boiling water from the fridge:** eggs lowered into boiling water give consistent timing and peel more easily
  than eggs started cold.
- **Shocking** in ice water stops the cooking (carryover) and helps peeling.
- **The green ring** is iron from the yolk reacting with sulphur from the white when the egg is overcooked. It is
  avoided by not overcooking and by cooling at once.
- **Peeling:** very fresh eggs peel badly because their white sticks to the membrane; eggs a week or more old peel
  more easily. Crack all over, then peel under water from the air-cell end.

**Sessions:**
1. Soft to hard (45 min): eggs boiled for a soft, a jammy and a hard yolk, shocked, peeled and halved; before cutting,
   the learner writes the predicted yolk (cue first, then the cut).

**Cues:** clean whites, no green ring, the yolk as predicted.

**Faults:**
- Eggs cracking because they went in too fast.
- Overcrowding the pan, so the water stops boiling.
- Skipping the ice water.

#### EGG-1.M2.L2 — Timing by size and temperature

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m2-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `EGG-1.M2.L1` · moves TS-EGG→3*

*Objectives:* [J] adjust for size, fridge temperature and altitude; [S] hit a target yolk three times running
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The variables:** egg size; starting temperature (fridge or room); how many eggs against how much water, which
  decides how fast the boil returns; altitude, which hardly matters in the Netherlands.
- **The learner's own table:** times for their eggs, their pan and their hob, built from the series in the exercise.
  Recipes' times are a starting point.
- **Hitting a target** three times running is the test of control. One variable is changed at a time when it misses.

**Sessions:**
1. Build the table (45 min): run the timing series of `EGG-1.M2.X1`.
2. Hit the target (45 min, another day): three runs at the learner's chosen time for a jammy yolk, without the table at
   hand, each halved and photographed.

**Cues:** three matching cross-sections.

**Faults:**
- Changing the size and the starting temperature at the same time.
- Timing from when the eggs went in rather than from the return to the boil (choose one and keep it).

#### EGG-1.M3.L1 — Poached eggs

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `EGG-1.M1.L2`, `HEA-1.M5.L1` · moves TS-EGG→2*

*Objectives:* [S] fresh eggs, a sieve, water temperature, timing; poach ahead and reheat
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Fresh eggs** have more thick white, which holds a compact shape.
- **A sieve** drains off the thin white that makes wisps.
- **The water:** just below a simmer, deep enough for the egg to set before it touches the bottom. A little vinegar
  helps the surface set but is optional with fresh, drained eggs.
- **Timing:** about 3 minutes for a set white and a runny yolk; lifted out with a slotted spoon and checked by touch.
- **Poaching ahead:** into ice water at once, then reheated in hot water for under a minute. This is how restaurants
  serve many at once.

**Sessions:**
1. Poach (60 min): eggs poached one by one, then two at a time; photograph each.
2. Poach ahead (60 min): four eggs poached, held in ice water, reheated and served on toast.

**Cues:** a compact white with no wisps; a runny yolk under a soft, opaque white.

**Faults:**
- A rolling boil, which tears the egg.
- Old eggs.
- Cracking straight into the pan without draining.
- Too many eggs at once.

#### EGG-1.M3.L2 — Fried eggs

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-EGG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] sunny side up, basted, crispy-edged; [J] heat and fat choice
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Three styles and their heat:**
  - **sunny side up:** low to medium heat, butter or oil, a lid to set the top of the white without browning;
  - **basted:** medium heat, hot fat spooned over the white;
  - **crispy-edged:** hot oil, high heat, the edges lacy and brown, the yolk still runny.
- **Fat choice:** butter for flavour at moderate heat; oil for high heat.
- **The white must set** while the yolk stays runny. The thick white around the yolk is the last to set.

**Sessions:**
1. Three ways (45 min): one egg in each style; photograph from above and the side.

**Cues:** no raw white around the yolk; the style's colour (none, light or lacy-brown) as intended.

**Faults:**
- High heat for sunny side up, so the base burns before the top sets.
- Low heat for crispy edges, so the egg stews in oil.

#### EGG-1.M3.L3 — Scrambled eggs

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EGG-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-EGG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] soft French style versus large curds; [K] how heat and stirring shape the curd
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Soft French style:** low heat, butter, constant stirring, small curds, creamy and almost pourable. Taken off the
  heat just before it looks done, because carryover finishes it.
- **Large curds:** medium heat, gentle folding with a spatula at intervals.
- **Why:** stirring breaks the setting network into small pieces; heat decides how tight the network becomes. Salt
  beaten in shortly before cooking gives a more tender curd.
- **Weeping:** overcooking, or wet additions (tomato, mushroom) added raw.

**Sessions:**
1. Two styles (45 min): soft French style and large curds from the same egg mix; photograph and taste.

**Cues:** small, glossy curds that flow slowly in the French style; soft, moist folds in the large-curd style.

**Faults:**
- High heat.
- Serving from the hot pan, so it overcooks on the plate.
- Raw mushrooms stirred in.

#### EGG-1.M3.L4 — Omelettes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l4 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `EGG-1.M3.L3` · moves TS-EGG→3*

*Objectives:* [S] a rolled French omelette and a flat omelette; [J] fix tearing and browning
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m3-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The French omelette:**
  - 2–3 eggs in a 20 cm pan;
  - butter foaming but not browning;
  - stir quickly with a fork while shaking the pan until the eggs are a soft curd;
  - let the base set briefly;
  - roll it out of the pan.

  The result is pale, with no colour, smooth outside and soft (*baveuse*) inside.
- **The flat omelette** (as a frittata or a Dutch *boerenomelet*): cooked slowly with fillings, set throughout, often
  finished under the grill.
- **Fixes:**
  - **browning:** the pan is too hot, so lower the heat and add the eggs while the butter foams, before it browns;
  - **tearing:** the egg is overcooked or sticking, so use a well-seasoned or non-stick pan, enough butter, and roll
    sooner.

**Sessions:**
1. French omelettes (75 min): the series of `EGG-1.M3.X1`.
2. A flat omelette (75 min): with seasonal vegetables cooked first, set slowly, finished under the grill.

**Cues:** a pale, smooth, rolled omelette with a soft centre; a flat omelette set through, golden on top.

**Faults:**
- A brown French omelette.
- A dry, overcooked centre.
- Fillings added raw and wet.

#### EGG-1.M3.X1 — Omelette series

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-egg-1-m3-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — Omelette series: five in one session, each photographed.
<!-- END generated:exercise-egg-1-m3-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-EGG→3

**Aim:** five French omelettes in one session, each photographed, becoming consistent.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the method and the standard are given. Five in a row, with one thing corrected each
time; the last two without looking at the method.

**Inputs:** 15 eggs; butter; salt; a 20 cm non-stick or seasoned pan.

**Procedure:**
1. Make each omelette.
2. Photograph it whole and cut open.
3. Write one correction.

#### EGG-1.M4.L1 — Stirred and baked custards

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-egg-1-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 h · builds on `EGG-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-EGG→2, SC-PROT→2, JD-TRBL→1*

*Objectives:* [S] a stirred custard to the coating stage and a baked custard in a water bath; [J] rescue early curdling
<!-- END generated:lesson-egg-1-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A stirred custard** (*crème anglaise*) is milk or cream, yolks and sugar, cooked gently while stirring until it
  coats the back of a spoon and a finger drawn through it leaves a clean line (*nappe*). That is at about 80–84 °C.
  Above that it curdles.
- **A baked custard** (a *crème caramel* or a *flan*) sets in a water bath in a moderate oven (about 150 °C). The
  water keeps the heat gentle and even, so the custard sets smooth, without bubbles or a curdled edge, and keeps a
  slight wobble in the centre. It needs several hours to chill.
- **Rescuing early curdling:** off the heat at once, into a cold bowl, then a stick blender, and strained. This works
  only when caught early.
- **Safety:** a custard brought to *nappe* is cooked; a baked custard is probed in the centre, every part at least
  71 °C (§9, egg-thickened desserts). Both are cooled quickly and kept in the fridge (§9).

**Sessions:**
1. Both custards (2 h active, plus about 4 h chilling): a stirred custard with its temperature logged, and baked
   custards in ramekins in a water bath; the stirred custard is served with fruit, the baked ones next day.

**Cues:** the custard thickens suddenly near the end; the line on the spoon stays clean; the baked custard trembles as a
whole when shaken gently.

**Faults:**
- High heat.
- Stopping stirring.
- No water bath, so the custards are bubbled and rubbery.
- Leaving the custard in the hot pan after it is done.
