## Introduction

Every cooking method is a way of getting heat into food at a certain speed, through a certain medium, to a certain
temperature. `HEA-1` teaches that idea and then the methods built on it:
- **heat transfer:** how heat moves through pans, ovens and liquids, and why it keeps moving after the heat is off;
- **browning:** why a dry, hot surface browns and a wet one steams;
- **the dry methods** in a pan and in the oven: sautéing, pan-frying, searing and roasting;
- **the moist methods:** boiling, simmering, poaching, steaming and blanching;
- **a first fair experiment**, comparing methods on one ingredient.

Two habits start here and run through the whole program:
- **reading the pan:** sound, smoke, colour and smell, used before the thermometer;
- **cue first, then reading:** the learner writes down what the cue says, then checks it with the probe (`JD-CUES`).

The science is McGee's [S16] and López-Alt's [S17], kept to what explains a result at the stove.

**In the spiral.**
- **Browning** (`SC-BRWN`) and **dry heat** (`TS-DRY`) continue in `HEA-2`, in the protein courses and in `SCI-2`.
- **Moist heat** (`TS-MOI`) reaches level 3 here, and stocks and braising build on it in S2.
- **Choosing a method** (`CK-METH`) becomes the reasoning behind every framework and brief from S2 on.

**Pacing.** About four to five weeks. The protein lessons are dinners; the comparisons and the temperature drill fit
weekday evenings. Buy the fish on the day it is cooked.

#### HEA-1.M1.L1 — Conduction, convection and radiation

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `EQP-1.M1.L1` · moves SC-HEAT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] the three modes in pans, ovens and grills; why a fan oven cooks faster
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Conduction** is heat passing by contact: from the burner to the pan, from the pan to the food, and from the
  surface of the food to its centre. Inside food, conduction is slow, which is why thickness decides cooking time.
- **Convection** is heat carried by a moving fluid: boiling water, hot oil, the air in an oven. A fan moves the air
  faster and strips away the cooler layer around the food, so a fan oven cooks faster at the same setting.
- **Radiation** is heat from a glowing source: an oven grill, charcoal, the walls of a hot oven. It browns surfaces
  facing it and depends strongly on distance.
- **In practice** every method mixes all three; naming the main one explains what to adjust.

**Sessions:** theory only (1.5 h): for six familiar dishes, name the main mode of heat transfer at each step.

**Cues:** the learner explains why the top of a gratin browns under the grill but its base doesn't.

**Faults:**
- Thinking the oven's air is as hot as the pan, in effect.
- Ignoring the distance from the grill.

#### HEA-1.M1.L2 — Water, fat and air

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L1` · moves SC-HEAT→2, CK-METH→1*

*Objectives:* [K] cooking media and their maximum temperatures; why boiled food never browns
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Water** cannot get hotter than its boiling point, 100 °C at sea level, however high the flame. Food surrounded by
  water never browns, because browning needs a hotter surface. Steam that condenses on food releases a lot of heat, so
  steaming is fast.
- **Fat** can reach far higher temperatures before it smokes, so it can brown and crisp.
- **Air** can be very hot but carries heat poorly, so an oven at 200 °C cooks more slowly than water at 100 °C, but it
  browns.
- **The three method families** follow from the medium: dry heat (air, fat, metal), moist heat (water, steam) and
  combination (browning, then moist).

**Sessions:**
1. Hand test of the media (30 min, safely): compare how fast a potato slice cooks in boiling water, in a hot pan with a
   little oil and in a 200 °C oven; note the times and the surfaces.

**Cues:** the learner predicts which medium cooks fastest and which browns.

**Faults:**
- Turning up the flame under boiling water to "cook faster".
- Expecting colour from poaching.

#### HEA-1.M1.L3 — Carryover and resting

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m1-l3 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L1` · moves SC-HEAT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] the heat gradient and carryover; [S] measure carryover on a roast
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The heat gradient:** during cooking the outside is much hotter than the centre. When the food leaves the heat, the
  stored heat in the outer layers keeps flowing inwards, and the core temperature goes on rising: **carryover**.
- **Carryover is larger** for big, dense items cooked at high heat, and small for thin items.
- **Resting** lets the temperature even out and the juices settle; slicing at once loses more juice.
- **Safety:** the safe core temperature ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)) is
  checked after resting with the probe, and carryover is never assumed.

**Sessions:**
1. Measure carryover (45 min, within a meal): probe a roast or a thick piece of meat at removal and every 2 minutes for
   10 minutes; plot the rise.

**Cues:** the core reading rising for several minutes after removal, then levelling off.

**Faults:**
- Probing only once at removal.
- Slicing at once.
- Relying on carryover for poultry to reach 75 °C without checking.

#### HEA-1.M2.L1 — The Maillard reaction

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L2` · moves SC-BRWN→2*

*Objectives:* [K] temperature, dryness, pH and sugar; flavour and colour; [S] dry a surface for browning [S16]
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The Maillard reaction** is a cascade of reactions between amino acids and reducing sugars that makes hundreds of
  flavour compounds and brown colour. It is the flavour of crust, toast, roasted vegetables and seared meat [S16].
- **Conditions:**
  - **temperature:** it is very slow at 100 °C and fast well above it, which is why a wet surface delays it;
  - **dryness:** water must evaporate from the surface first;
  - **pH:** an alkaline surface browns faster (a pinch of bicarbonate on onions);
  - **sugars and proteins:** both are needed, and more sugar browns faster.
- **Practical rules:**
  - dry the surface;
  - salt ahead or just before, not in between;
  - preheat the pan;
  - don't crowd it;
  - don't move the food until a crust forms.

**Sessions:**
1. Dry against wet (45 min): mushrooms or slices of courgette patted dry against rinsed and wet, in the same hot pan;
   photograph the colour at the same time.

**Cues:** a sizzle that stays loud; a deep, even brown on the dry batch while the wet batch is still grey.

**Faults:**
- Washing mushrooms just before cooking.
- A cold pan.
- Moving the food every few seconds.

#### HEA-1.M2.L2 — Caramelisation and burning

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m2-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M2.L1` · moves SC-BRWN→2*

*Objectives:* [K] sugar browning; where flavour turns bitter; [S] caramelise onions versus burn them
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Caramelisation** is sugar alone breaking down with heat into brown, sweet-bitter, nutty compounds, at higher
  temperatures than the Maillard reaction. In practice, caramelised onions brown by both.
- **Browning against burning:** browning smells sweet and roasted; burning smells acrid and tastes bitter, with black
  spots and smoke. The line is crossed quickly at high heat.
- **Caramelised onions** take a long time at moderate heat (often 30–45 minutes), with stirring and a splash of water to
  lift the fond; quick high-heat "caramelised" onions are burnt outside and raw inside.

**Sessions:**
1. Onions three ways (60 min): one pan of sliced onions at high heat for 10 minutes, one at moderate heat for 40
   minutes, one deliberately taken too far; photograph and taste.

**Cues:** the slow onions jammy and deep brown; the fast ones blackened at the edges and sharp.

**Faults:**
- Rushing with high heat.
- Not deglazing the fond, which then burns.
- Thinking "darker is better" past the acrid smell.

#### HEA-1.M3.L1 — Sauté

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M2.L1` · moves TS-DRY→2*

*Objectives:* [S] preheat, fat, pan load, tossing and stirring; [K] why overcrowding steams
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Sautéing:** small, even pieces over high heat in a little fat, kept moving.
- **The sequence:**
  - preheat the pan;
  - add the fat and let it shimmer;
  - add the food in one layer;
  - listen for a steady sizzle;
  - toss or stir only once a side has coloured.
- **Pan load:** a pan holds as much as fits in one layer with space between. Overcrowding releases more water than can
  evaporate, the temperature drops and the food steams. Cook in batches instead.
- **Fat choice:** neutral oil for high heat; butter added late for flavour, because its milk solids burn.

**Sessions:**
1. Sauté vegetables (60 min): a mix of seasonal vegetables (for example carrot, Brussels sprouts and red onion, or
   courgette and peppers in summer) in two batches.
2. Repeat with a different vegetable mix (60 min), this time tossing; compare the colour of the two sessions.

**Cues:** a loud, even sizzle; pieces browned on several sides; no liquid pooling.

**Faults:**
- Food in a cold pan.
- Everything in one batch.
- Stirring constantly.
- Butter on high heat from the start.

#### HEA-1.M3.L2 — Pan-frying and shallow frying

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L1`, `SAF-1.M3.L2` · moves TS-DRY→2, IN-FSH→1, IN-MEA→1*

*Objectives:* [S] pan-fry a fish fillet and a chicken thigh skin-side down; [K] oil depth and temperature
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Pan-frying** cooks larger pieces in a thin layer of fat, without tossing.
- **Shallow frying** uses fat up to about half the height of the food, at 160–180 °C measured with the thermometer.
  Hot enough to crisp, cool enough not to burn the outside before the inside cooks.
- **Skin-side down:**
  - start a chicken thigh skin-side down in a moderate pan so the fat renders and the skin crisps;
  - press a fish fillet gently for the first seconds so it doesn't curl;
  - in both cases, leave it until it releases.
- **Doneness** is checked with the probe ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)):
  chicken 75 °C; fish 63 °C, or opaque and flaking.

**Sessions:**
1. A fish fillet (60 min): a skin-on fillet (mackerel, saithe or farmed sea bass) pan-fried skin-side down; probe and
   photograph the skin.
2. A chicken thigh (60 min): skin-side down from a moderate pan until crisp, turned, probed to 75 °C.

**Cues:** the skin crisp and golden over its whole surface; the fish releasing by itself; the probe reading in range.

**Faults:**
- Turning the fish too early, which tears it.
- Too hot a pan for the chicken, which burns the skin before the fat renders.
- Oil too cool, so the food soaks it up.

#### HEA-1.M3.L3 — Searing and fond

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-DRY→2, JD-CUES→1*

*Objectives:* [S] sear for colour; [K] fond as the base of a sauce; [J] when to turn
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Searing** gives colour and flavour to the surface at high heat; it doesn't "seal in juices".
- **When to turn:** when the piece releases easily and a deep crust shows at the edge. A piece that sticks is not
  ready.
- **Fond** is the browned residue in the pan, full of flavour, and the base of a pan sauce. It is lifted with a liquid
  (deglazing). Burnt fond is bitter and is discarded.

**Sessions:**
1. Sear (60 min): sear pieces of the same item (halloumi, thick slices of celeriac, or burgers) at medium-high heat;
   turn by the cue.
2. Deglaze (60 min): sear again, then deglaze the fond with water or stock and taste it as a simple sauce.

**Cues:** a deep brown crust; the piece releasing with a gentle push; fond golden to brown, not black.

**Faults:**
- Flipping too often.
- A non-stick pan, which leaves little fond.
- Black fond deglazed into a bitter sauce.

#### HEA-1.M3.L4 — Reading the pan

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l4 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L3` · moves JD-CUES→2*

*Objectives:* [J] sound, smoke, colour, smell; adjust the heat before it goes wrong
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m3-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Sound:**
  - a steady sizzle is right;
  - a quiet hiss or bubbling means too cool or too wet;
  - spitting and crackling means too hot or wet fat.
- **Smoke:** a wisp as the fat reaches temperature; heavy smoke means too hot for that fat.
- **Colour:** the edges of the food and the fond show what is happening underneath.
- **Smell:** roasted and nutty is right; acrid is burning.
- **Acting early:** lower the heat or lift the pan before it goes wrong, not after.
- **Cue first, then reading:** the learner writes the cue's verdict, then checks it with a probe or by cutting, and
  compares the two (`JD-CUES`).

**Sessions:**
1. Cue log (45 min): cook a simple dinner and stop at four moments to write the cue, the verdict and the action; then
   probe or cut to check.

**Cues:** at least three of four verdicts confirmed by the check.

**Faults:**
- Watching only the clock.
- Ignoring the smell until there is smoke.
- Turning the heat up when the food is quiet because it is wet.

#### HEA-1.M5.L1 — Boil, simmer and poach

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-MOI→2, CK-METH→2*

*Objectives:* [K] temperature ranges and what the bubbles look like; [S] hold a simmer and a poach
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The temperature ranges, and what they look like:**
  - **boil:** 100 °C, large rolling bubbles that don't stop when stirred; for pasta and green vegetables;
  - **simmer:** about 85–95 °C, small bubbles breaking gently; for stocks, soups and most braises;
  - **poach:** about 70–85 °C, the surface shivering with no bubbles; for eggs, fish and chicken breast.
- **Why it matters:** a boil toughens proteins and breaks up delicate food; a simmer extracts flavour without clouding;
  a poach cooks gently and evenly.
- **Holding a temperature:** the burner setting, a lid on or off, and the volume of liquid.

**Sessions:**
1. Read the bubbles (45 min): bring a pan of water through the three ranges, reading the thermometer and photographing
   the surface at each.

**Cues:** the learner names the range from the surface alone, then confirms it with the thermometer.

**Faults:**
- Calling a boil a simmer.
- A lid left on, so a simmer turns into a boil.
- Adding cold food to a small pot, so the temperature crashes.

#### HEA-1.M5.L2 — Blanching and shocking

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L1` · moves TS-MOI→2*

*Objectives:* [S] blanch green vegetables in plenty of salted water; shock or spread to cool; [K] why
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Blanching** cooks briefly in plenty of well-salted, boiling water. The large volume keeps the temperature up when
  the vegetables go in.
- **Shocking** in ice water (or spreading out on a tray) stops the cooking at once, keeps the texture and sets a bright
  colour.
- **Why green turns olive:** long cooking and acid change chlorophyll. Short cooking, an open pan and acid added only at
  the end keep it green.
- **Uses:** a side dish, preparing vegetables ahead, loosening tomato skins (`KNF-1.M4.L1`).

**Sessions:**
1. Blanch and shock (45 min): green beans or broccoli (or peas and sugar snaps in summer) blanched in batches and
   shocked; one batch left to cool in the pan for comparison.

**Cues:** bright green, tender-crisp, seasoned; the comparison batch dull and soft.

**Faults:**
- Too little water.
- Unsalted water.
- Overloading the pot.
- Skipping the shock.
- Lemon juice added early.

#### HEA-1.M5.L3 — Steaming

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L1` · moves TS-MOI→2*

*Objectives:* [S] steam vegetables and fish; [K] steaming versus boiling (flavour, nutrients)
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Steaming** cooks in the vapour above boiling water. Condensing steam transfers heat fast, and the food is not in
  the water.
- **Against boiling:** fewer water-soluble vitamins and less flavour leach out; the texture stays more intact; but there
  is no seasoning from salted water, so salt after.
- **Set-up:** a steamer insert, a basket or a sieve over a pot with a lid; the water must not touch the food; space for
  the steam to circulate.
- **Fish** steams well in a few minutes: it is done when opaque and flaking, at 63 °C.

**Sessions:**
1. Steam vegetables and fish (60 min): steam a dense vegetable (carrot or celeriac batons) and a fish fillet; compare
   the vegetable with a boiled batch for flavour.

**Cues:** the steamed vegetable tasting more of itself; the fish just flaking.

**Faults:**
- A pot that runs dry.
- A lid that lets the steam escape.
- Pieces stacked so the steam can't reach them.

#### HEA-1.M5.L4 — Gentle poaching

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l4 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-1.M5.L1`, `SAF-1.M3.L2` · moves TS-MOI→3*

*Objectives:* [S] poach chicken breast in a court bouillon below a simmer; [J] check doneness
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A court bouillon:** water with aromatics (onion, carrot, celery, herbs), salt and a little acid, simmered briefly
  to give flavour.
- **Poaching chicken breast below a simmer** cooks it gently and evenly, so it stays juicy.
  - Start the breast in the warm liquid, bring the liquid to about 75–80 °C and hold it there.
  - The breast is done at 75 °C at the core ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)).
- **Doneness by cue and probe:** firm but springy when pressed, juices clear; confirmed with the probe (cue first, then
  reading).

**Sessions:**
1. Poach (75 min): a court bouillon and two chicken breasts poached, with the liquid temperature logged every 5 minutes
   and the core probed at the end.

**Cues:** the liquid held without bubbles; the breast juicy, and 75 °C at the core.

**Faults:**
- Boiling the breast, which makes it stringy and dry.
- Starting it in boiling liquid.
- Not probing.

#### HEA-1.M5.L5 — Choosing dry or moist heat

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l5 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L1` · moves CK-METH→2, JD-CUES→2, JD-CHSE→1*

*Objectives:* [J] choose a method from the ingredient's structure and the result wanted
<!-- END generated:lesson-hea-1-m5-l5 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The ingredient's structure decides the method:**
  - tender, quick-cooking food suits short methods (sauté, blanch, steam);
  - dense roots suit roasting or simmering;
  - delicate fish suits poaching or steaming;
  - tough, collagen-rich cuts need long moist heat (S2).
- **The result wanted decides the medium:** colour and crust need dry heat; a delicate, even texture needs moist heat.
- **The reasoning in one sentence:** "this ingredient is X, I want Y, so method Z". It is the start of `JD-CHSE`.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): for ten ingredients, choose a method for two different results and write the
one-sentence reason.

**Cues:** every choice reasoned from the structure and the wanted result, not from habit.

**Faults:**
- "Always roast vegetables".
- Steaming food that should have colour.
- Choosing by recipe rather than by result.
