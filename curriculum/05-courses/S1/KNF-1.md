## Introduction

Almost every savoury dish starts on the board, and how it is cut decides how it cooks:
- uneven dice brown at the edges and stay raw in the centre;
- a bruised herb darkens;
- a slipped knife ends the session.

`KNF-1` builds the knife work every later course assumes:
- a safe grip and a stable board;
- the basic motions;
- the alliums and herbs that start most dishes;
- the classical cuts to stated sizes;
- awkward vegetables broken down safely;
- the first timed drills.

The order is deliberate: **safety, then accuracy, then speed**. Speed built on a poor grip or on ragged cuts has to be
unlearned later. The program's sizes and tolerances are fixed in the Phase 4 framework
([TS tolerances](../../04-competencies/TS.md)):
- julienne 2–3 mm thick and 5–6 cm long;
- brunoise 2–3 mm;
- small dice 5–7 mm;
- medium dice 12 mm;
- large dice 20 mm;
- "8 of 10" means 8 of 10 random pieces within the range.

The same note gives the sizes of two cuts this course introduced (S1 review):
- *bâtonnet*: 5–7 mm square and 5–6 cm long, the stick that small dice is cut from;
- *paysanne*: 10–12 mm squares or triangles, 2–3 mm thick.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-KNF` reaches level 3 here and is re-tested at `MS-S1`, where the mise en place list is cut against the clock.
- `KNF-2` (S2) raises it to working speed on volume.
- Fine cuts and the *tourné* come in S4.
- The trim habit starts `KM-WAST`, which continues in the stock and whole-animal courses.

**Pacing.** About four weeks of active work. The timed drills of module 5 then run weekly for the rest of S1, about 8
weeks, alongside the other courses. Drill produce becomes soups, stocks and side dishes, so most sessions are also a
meal.

#### KNF-1.M1.L1 — Grip, claw and a stable board

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAF-1.M4.L2`, `EQP-1.M3.L2` · moves TS-KNF→1, SA-PHYS→2*

*Objectives:* [S] pinch grip; guiding-hand claw; a damp towel under the board; [K] why cuts happen
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The pinch grip:** thumb and the side of the index finger pinch the blade just in front of the bolster, and the
  other fingers wrap the handle. It gives control over the angle and the edge; a grip on the handle alone lets the blade
  wobble.
- **The claw:** fingertips of the guiding hand tucked back, the knuckles forward against the flat of the blade, the
  thumb behind the fingers. The blade can only touch knuckles, and it rides along them.
- **A stable board:** a damp towel or a non-slip mat underneath, the board at a comfortable height, and nothing round
  left rolling: a flat side is cut first and the piece laid on it.
- **Why cuts happen:**
  - a dull knife that needs force and slips;
  - fingertips forward;
  - a sliding board;
  - rushing;
  - a round vegetable that rolls.

**Sessions:**
1. Grip and claw (45 min): set up the board, practise the grip and the claw on a cucumber with slow slices, and film
   30 seconds from the side.
2. Repeat the next day (45 min) on a carrot, check the video against the reference, and correct one thing.

**Cues:** knuckles touching the blade; fingertips out of sight from above; the board does not move when pushed.

**Faults:**
- The index finger on the spine.
- Fingertips flat.
- The thumb sticking out.
- The board on a wet counter without a towel.
- Cutting a whole round carrot without a flat side.

#### KNF-1.M1.L2 — The basic motions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m1-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M1.L1` · moves TS-KNF→2*

*Objectives:* [S] rock, push, pull and slice cuts; matching the motion to the food
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The rocking motion:** the tip stays on the board while the heel rises and falls. For herbs and fine chopping.
- **The push cut:** forward and down through the food, the whole edge used. For most vegetables.
- **The pull cut:** back and down, often with the tip. For fine slicing and for scoring onions.
- **The slicing cut:** a long draw with little downward pressure. For tomatoes, cooked meat and bread with the serrated
  knife.
- **The motion follows the food:** firm, round produce gets a push cut on a flat side; soft skins get a long draw, never
  a press.

**Sessions:**
1. Motion drills (45 min): each motion on cucumber and carrot, slowly, then at an even rhythm.
2. Slices, sticks and dice (45 min): cut carrot into slices, sticks and dice with the push cut; measure 10 sticks and
   10 dice against the julienne and small-dice ranges.

**Cues:** a quiet, rhythmic sound; the knife doing the work; slices of even thickness.

**Faults:**
- Chopping straight down with force.
- Sawing.
- Lifting the whole knife high off the board.
- Pressing a tomato flat.

#### KNF-1.M1.L3 — Posture and knife handling

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m1-l3 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · moves SA-PHYS→3*

*Objectives:* [S] stance, carrying, passing, washing and storing knives safely
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Posture:** feet apart, square to the board, the board at hip height or a little above; the elbow free to move.
- **Handling:**
  - carry a knife point down by the side;
  - pass it by laying it on the counter, never hand to hand;
  - never leave it in the washing-up water;
  - wash and dry it straight away;
  - store it in a block, on a magnetic strip or with a blade guard.
- **A falling knife is never caught:** step back.
- **The safe station around the cutting work:** handles of pans on the hob turned inwards, a dry towel for hot handles,
  the fire blanket within reach (`SAF-1.M4.L2`).

**Sessions:**
1. Station safety check (30 min): set up the board and the hob as for a meal, move a pot of hot water from the hob to the
   sink with a dry towel, and photograph the station and the fire blanket.

**Cues:** no knife ever loose in a sink; pan handles never over the edge of the hob.

**Faults:**
- Gesturing with the knife.
- Leaving it at the edge of the board.
- A wet towel used as an oven glove.

#### KNF-1.M2.L1 — Onions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-KNF→2*

*Objectives:* [S] halve, slice and dice (fine, medium, coarse); [K] why cutting releases sulphur compounds and how to limit tears
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The professional onion dice:**
  - halve through the root;
  - peel, keeping the root on;
  - horizontal cuts (optional, and fewer than people think);
  - vertical cuts towards the root;
  - cross-cuts.

  The root holds the layers together until the end.
- **Three sizes:** fine (brunoise, 2–3 mm), small (5–7 mm), medium (about 12 mm), each with its uses: fine for sauces and
  dressings, small for soffritto and stews, medium for roasting.
- **Why onions sting:** cutting breaks cells, and enzymes turn sulphur compounds into a volatile irritant. A sharp knife
  crushes fewer cells, a chilled onion releases less, and the root end, where it is most concentrated, is cut last.

**Sessions:**
1. Fine and small dice (60 min): five onions in fine and small dice, with three measured on the grid.
2. Medium dice and slices (60 min): five onions in medium dice and in slices (along and across the grain), compared
   raw and sweated.

**Cues:** dice falling apart evenly at the last cut; no long strips; dry eyes with a sharp knife.

**Faults:**
- Cutting the root off first.
- Too many horizontal cuts, so the layers slide.
- Crushing with a dull knife.

#### KNF-1.M2.L2 — Garlic

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KNF-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-KNF→2*

*Objectives:* [S] crush, slice, mince, make a paste with salt; [K] how the cut changes the flavour
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Crushing** with the flat of the knife loosens the skin and bruises the clove.
- **Slicing** gives mild, sweet garlic that browns evenly.
- **Mincing** and **paste** (minced, then worked with a pinch of salt under the flat of the blade) give the strongest
  flavour. The more cells are broken, the more of the pungent compounds form.
- **Heat changes it:** burnt garlic turns bitter in seconds, so minced garlic goes in late or into warm, not smoking,
  fat.
- **Garlic in oil** is kept in the fridge and used within a short time ([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)).

**Sessions:**
1. Four ways (45 min): one head of garlic crushed, sliced, minced and made into a paste; each warmed gently in oil and
   tasted on bread.

**Cues:** slices of even thickness; a smooth paste with no lumps; the four tastes clearly different.

**Faults:**
- Minced garlic added with the onions at high heat, which burns it.
- A garlic press used for everything.
- Garlic oil left at room temperature.

#### KNF-1.M2.L3 — Herbs

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l3 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KNF-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-KNF→2*

*Objectives:* [S] chop and *chiffonade*; keep leaves green; [K] tender versus woody herbs
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Tender herbs** (parsley, basil, chervil, dill, chives, coriander) bruise and darken when crushed. They are cut with a
  sharp knife in few strokes, just before use.
- **Woody herbs** (thyme, rosemary, sage, savoury) are stripped from the stem and chopped fine; they tolerate long
  cooking.
- ***Chiffonade***: leaves stacked, rolled tight and sliced into fine ribbons (basil, sorrel, sage).
- **Drying herbs well** before cutting keeps them from clumping and turning to paste.

**Sessions:**
1. Chop and ribbon (45 min): parsley chopped fine, chives cut into rounds, basil or sage in chiffonade; photographed
   after 10 minutes to see the colour.

**Cues:** bright green and dry on the board after 10 minutes; chives in clean rounds, not crushed.

**Faults:**
- Wet herbs.
- Chopping over and over with a dull knife until they are a dark paste.
- Cutting basil hours ahead.

#### KNF-1.M2.L4 — Shallots and leeks

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l4 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M2.L1` · moves TS-KNF→2*

*Objectives:* [S] dice shallots; clean and cut leeks
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m2-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Shallots** are diced like a small onion, and finer, for sauces and dressings.
- **Leeks** (autumn to spring) carry soil between the layers:
  - trim the root and the dark green;
  - halve lengthwise;
  - rinse under running water, fanning the layers;
  - then slice into half-moons, a julienne or a fine dice.
- **The green tops** go into stock (`KM-WAST`).

**Sessions:**
1. Shallots (60 min): ten shallots in fine dice, measured.
2. Leeks (60 min): three leeks cleaned and cut into half-moons and julienne, cooked into a soup with the trim set aside
   for stock.

**Cues:** no grit in the soup; shallot dice 2–3 mm and even.

**Faults:**
- Slicing leeks before washing them.
- Discarding all the green.
- Shallot dice that are uneven because the root was cut off.

#### KNF-1.M3.L1 — Squaring off and planks

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KNF-1.M1.L2` · moves TS-KNF→2, KM-WAST→2*

*Objectives:* [S] square off and cut even planks; [J] put trim to use (stock, purée, soup)
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Squaring off** a carrot, potato or piece of celeriac gives flat sides and a stable block; every classical cut starts
  from planks of a set thickness.
- **The trade-off:** a perfect block wastes more. The trim is not waste when it is planned: it goes into stock, purée,
  soup or mash (`KM-WAST`).
- **Weighing the trim** shows the yield of the cut (`WRK-1.M3.L3`).

**Sessions:**
1. Planks (60 min): square off carrots and potatoes; cut planks of 2–3 mm and of 5–7 mm; weigh the block and the trim;
   the trim goes into a stock or a mash.

**Cues:** planks of even thickness from end to end; a trim bowl with a use.

**Faults:**
- Wedge-shaped planks from a tilted knife.
- Trimming so much that half the vegetable is gone.
- Trim thrown away.

#### KNF-1.M3.L2 — Bâtonnet and julienne

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-KNF→2, CK-TERM→2*

*Objectives:* [S] cut sticks to size; [K] names and dimensions
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The sizes:**
  - *bâtonnet*: sticks of 5–7 mm square and 5–6 cm long, from 5–7 mm planks;
  - *julienne*: 2–3 mm thick and 5–6 cm long, from 2–3 mm planks.
- **The names and their uses:** *bâtonnet* for crudités and as the stick for small dice; *julienne* for garnishes,
  salads and quick stir-fries.
- **Stacking planks** speeds the work, but only if they are even; a stack that slides gives uneven sticks.

**Sessions:**
1. Bâtonnet (75 min): carrot and celeriac (or kohlrabi in summer) into bâtonnet, measured.
2. Julienne (75 min): carrot and leek into julienne, measured; used in a salad or a stir-fry.

**Cues:** sticks that lie flat and parallel; ends square.

**Faults:**
- Stacks too high.
- Planks of mixed thickness.
- Julienne that is really a thin bâtonnet.

#### KNF-1.M3.L3 — Dice: macédoine and brunoise

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M3.L2` · moves TS-KNF→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cut even dice from sticks
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Dice are cut from sticks:** *macédoine*, the small dice of 5–7 mm, from bâtonnet; *brunoise*, 2–3 mm, from julienne.
  The stick decides the dice, so the earlier cuts must be right.
- **Gather the sticks** into a tight bundle and cut across at the same width.

**Sessions:**
1. Macédoine (75 min): carrot, potato and beetroot into small dice; 10 of each measured.
2. Brunoise (75 min): carrot and celeriac into brunoise; 10 measured; used in a sauce base or a soup.

**Cues:** cubes, not flat tiles; the same size from the first to the last cut.

**Faults:**
- Dice from uneven sticks.
- Flattening the sticks, which gives tiles.
- Speeding up and losing the width.

#### KNF-1.M3.L4 — Paysanne, rounds, bias and roll cuts

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l4 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-KNF→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cut flat, round and angled shapes; [K] when each is used
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***Paysanne:*** flat pieces of 10–12 mm, 2–3 mm thick, as squares, triangles or rounds following the vegetable's shape.
  Thin and quick-cooking, for soups and braises.
- **Rounds** (*rondelles*) and **bias cuts** (diagonal): a bias gives more surface, so faster cooking and an attractive
  shape for stir-fries and roasts.
- **The roll cut:** cut on the bias, roll a quarter turn, cut again, for even chunks with many faces, for roasting and
  braising.
- **Choosing the cut** for the method and the time.

**Sessions:**
1. Flat cuts (60 min): paysanne from carrot, leek and turnip (or kohlrabi); rounds and bias cuts from carrot and parsnip.
2. Roll cuts (60 min): roll-cut carrots and parsnips, roasted beside rounds of the same weight; compare.

**Cues:** paysanne of even thickness; roll-cut pieces of similar size with many faces.

**Faults:**
- Paysanne of different thicknesses.
- A bias angle that wanders.
- Roll cuts of random size.

#### KNF-1.M3.L5 — Why uniformity matters

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l5 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KNF-1.M3.L3` · moves TS-KNF→3, SC-HEAT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] size, surface area and cooking time; [S] a comparison of mixed versus uniform dice in a pan
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m3-l5 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Size decides cooking time.** Heat travels from the surface to the centre, so the time to cook through rises steeply
  with thickness, and a small piece has much more surface per gram than a large one. A cube of 5 mm has twice the
  surface per volume of a cube of 10 mm.
- **In a pan of mixed sizes**, the small pieces burn or turn to mush before the large ones are done. The cook has to
  choose between the two.
- This is the first link between knife work and heat (`SC-HEAT`), which `HEA-1` builds on.

**Sessions:**
1. Mixed against even (45 min): sauté a pan of carefully even dice and a pan of mixed dice for the same time; photograph
   and taste.

**Cues:** in the even pan, the pieces are all done at once; in the mixed pan, burnt and raw pieces sit side by side.

**Faults:**
- Changing two things at once, such as size and heat, so the comparison shows nothing.

#### KNF-1.M4.L1 — Peeling and concassé

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m4-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KNF-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-KNF→3*

*Objectives:* [S] knife versus peeler; peel tomatoes (score, blanch, shock) and cut *concassé*
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Knife against peeler:** the peeler for thin skins (carrot, parsnip, potato), with little loss; the knife for thick
  or knobbly skins (celeriac, squash) after the vegetable is stabilised.
- **Tomato concassé:**
  - score a cross;
  - blanch for 10–20 seconds until the skin lifts;
  - shock in iced water;
  - peel, quarter, seed and dice.

  Late-summer Dutch tomatoes are best for this.

**Sessions:**
1. Peel and concassé (75 min): peel carrots and potatoes with a peeler and a knife and weigh the peel; make a tomato
   concassé (in winter, peel a red pepper by charring it instead).

**Cues:** thin, even peel; tomato skin slipping off; concassé in even dice without seeds.

**Faults:**
- Blanching too long, which cooks the flesh.
- Skipping the ice water.
- Thick knife peel on thin-skinned roots.

#### KNF-1.M4.L2 — Round and awkward vegetables

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-KNF→3, SA-PHYS→3*

*Objectives:* [S] celeriac, squash, cabbage, fennel, beetroot: stabilise, then break down safely
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Stabilise, then break down.**
  - Cut a flat base first.
  - Halve large, hard vegetables (squash, celeriac) by placing the knife and pressing with the flat of the hand on the
    spine, never by swinging.
  - Work with the flat side down.
- **Celeriac:** slice off the top and bottom, cut the peel from top to bottom along the curve.
- **Squash:** halve, scoop the seeds, peel the halves on their flat sides.
- **Cabbage:** quarter through the core, then cut out the core.
- **Fennel:** halve, remove the core wedge, slice.
- **Beetroot:** peel after cooking, or raw with gloves.

**Sessions:**
1. Celeriac and cabbage (60 min): break down a celeriac and a white or red cabbage into dice and shreds.
2. Squash and fennel (60 min): break down a squash and a fennel bulb (or, in late summer, a kohlrabi and a fennel).

**Cues:** the vegetable never rocks under the knife; the guiding hand always above the blade, never in front of it.

**Faults:**
- Hacking at a whole squash.
- The hand in front of the blade while pressing.
- A wet board.

#### KNF-1.M5.L1 — Timed drills and logging

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-1-m5-l1 -->
*4 h · 6 practical sessions · elapsed 8 wk · builds on `KNF-1.M3.L3` · moves TS-KNF→3, JD-REFL→2*

*Objectives:* [S] timed onion dice and *julienne*; [J] log time and uniformity, set the next target
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-1-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Speed comes from rhythm and economy of movement, not force:**
  - the knife stays close to the board;
  - the guiding hand moves back steadily;
  - no piece is handled twice.
- **Deliberate practice:** a fixed drill, timed, with uniformity measured each time and one thing to improve. Speed
  never at the cost of the grip or the size: a fast set out of range does not count.
- **The targets:**
  - an onion in small dice in under 3 minutes with 8 of 10 within range (the `TS-KNF` level 3 row);
  - a carrot in julienne at a steady pace.
- **The log** holds the date, the time, the count within range and the next target. It is the start of the knife
  thread that runs through S2.

**Sessions:**
1. Week 1 (40 min): baseline drill — onion small dice and carrot julienne, timed and measured.
2. Week 2 (40 min): the same, with the target from week 1.
3. Week 3 (40 min): the same, a different onion size.
4. Week 4 (40 min): the same, carrot replaced by celeriac (a new variable).
5. Week 6 (40 min): the same, filmed for a grip check.
6. Week 8 (40 min): the final drill, measured for the module check.

**Cues:** the times falling while the count within range stays at 8 of 10 or more; the grip unchanged in the week 6
video.

**Faults:**
- Rushing and losing the claw.
- Measuring only the best pieces.
- Skipping weeks and then cramming.
