## Introduction

`ORI-1` is the door into the program. It changes no cooking skill yet. It sets up the three things every later course
depends on: a **way of working** (plan, cook, evaluate, with evidence), a **kitchen** organised for that work, and a
**baseline** to measure progress against.

The course teaches the program's logic before any technique, for two reasons:
- **The gates.** A learner who does not understand levels, evidence and stage gates will either over-collect evidence or
  arrive at `MS-S1` with gaps they could have closed months earlier.
- **The baseline.** Without a recorded starting point, the progress the program promises cannot be shown: the
  diagnostic cook and the tasting baseline are repeated at `MS-S1` and set side by side.

**In the spiral.**
- The journal habit started here becomes the reflection thread (`JD-REFL`, level 5 by the capstone).
- The kitchen audit feeds `EQP-1`, and the work zones feed `WRK-1`.
- The tasting baseline is the first point of the tasting thread that runs through `TST-1` and every later course.

**For the experienced learner.** The diagnostic cook can show level 3 in knife work, egg cookery or starch cookery, and
waive lessons in `KNF-1`, `EGG-1` or `STA-1`, but never in `SAF-1`.

**Pacing.** The course fits in the first one or two weeks. Module 1 and the audit fit weekday evenings; the diagnostic
cook needs a free afternoon. Start the journal on day one, because every later exercise writes into it.

#### ORI-1.M1.L1 — Learning to cook, not recipes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ori-1-m1-l1 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · moves JD-REFL→1*

*Objectives:* [K] explain competencies, levels, stage gates and the move from recipes to briefs; [J] set a weekly schedule
<!-- END generated:lesson-ori-1-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- A recipe gives one result under one set of conditions. A competency transfers to new ingredients, equipment and
  constraints. The program moves from recipes (S1) through frameworks to briefs (S5) (Phase 1 §6.6).
- The four dimensions: knowledge [K], skill [S], judgment [J], mastery [M], and the 1–5 scale. Level 3 means "without
  instructions, with acceptable results"; level 5 means "consistently, and can teach it".
- The evidence rules: every level needs objective or partly verifiable evidence, and self-assessment never carries a
  level alone. Level 3 needs two demonstrations on different days, level 4 three under at least two conditions
  ([framework §2](../../04-competency-framework.md#2-the-evidence-model)).
- Module checks gate the course; the milestone and the stage targets gate the next stage. Up to two non-safety
  competencies may be carried one level short; safety competencies never.
- Planning: 5–8 hours a week, with weekday slots for theory and drills and a longer weekend slot for lessons with
  elapsed time (stocks, doughs, ferments).

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): read, then draft the weekly schedule in the journal.

**Cues:** the learner can say, for any lesson, which competency it moves and what evidence will show it.

**Faults:**
- Treating the program as a recipe collection, and skipping the evidence.
- A schedule with no slot longer than an hour, so lessons with elapsed time never fit.
- Believing a confident self-score is enough for a level.

#### ORI-1.M1.L2 — The kitchen journal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ori-1-m1-l2 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · moves JD-REFL→2*

*Objectives:* [K] the plan → cook → evaluate cycle; [S] photograph evidence (cross-section, ruler, side by side)
<!-- END generated:lesson-ori-1-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- The plan → cook → evaluate cycle. The plan names the target, the criteria and the timeline. The cook record holds
  times, readings and photos. The evaluation scores against the criteria and names one next step.
- The journal entry template: date, lesson or exercise ID, competencies, conditions (ingredient, quantity, equipment,
  support), evidence, self-score per criterion, causes, next step.
- Photo standards ([assessment §6](../../04-assessment.md#6-ai-review)): daylight or neutral light; a reference object
  (grid board, ruler, spoon); a cross-section where doneness or crumb matters; the whole plate from above and at 45°;
  side-by-side shots for comparisons.
- Description against diagnosis: "the onions were dark" describes; "the onions burnt because the pan was too hot when
  they went in" diagnoses.

**Sessions:** theory only (1 h): set up the template and take three practice photos of a cut vegetable, which the
exercise then uses.

**Cues:** a stranger could reconstruct what was cooked, how, and how it turned out from the entry alone.

**Faults:**
- Entries written days later, from memory.
- Photos without a reference object, at night under yellow light, or cropped so the evidence is out of frame.
- Evaluations that only describe.

#### ORI-1.M2.L1 — Equipment audit

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ori-1-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `ORI-1.M1.L1` · moves KM-EQPT→1*

*Objectives:* [K] the tier 0 kit and why each item matters; [J] prioritise purchases as Good or Best
<!-- END generated:lesson-ori-1-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- The tier 0 kit (Phase 1 §9.4) and what each item does that nothing else does. Knives, pans of different materials, a
  1 g scale, an instant-read thermometer and an oven thermometer: an oven dial can be off by tens of degrees.
- Good and Best: Good is enough to meet every criterion of the program; Best is faster, more precise or more durable.
  A Best item never replaces a skill.
- Priorities: buy what a criterion depends on first (the thermometers and the scale, because safety gates and ratios
  need numbers), then what the next course needs.

**Sessions:**
1. Audit (60 min): go through the tier 0 list; mark each item present, missing or unfit (warped pan, dull knife,
   untested thermometer); test the thermometer in boiling water and in iced water.

**Cues:** a complete list with a reason for every purchase; the thermometer reads close to 100 °C in boiling water at
sea level and close to 0 °C in iced water.

**Faults:**
- Buying a full Best set at once.
- Keeping a warped pan that rocks on the hob.
- Never checking the thermometer.

#### ORI-1.M2.L2 — Work zones and layout

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ori-1-m2-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves TS-MEP→1*

*Objectives:* [S] arrange storage, prep, cooking and cleaning zones to cut walking and crossing
<!-- END generated:lesson-ori-1-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- The four zones: storage, preparation, cooking, cleaning. Food moves one way, from storage through prep to the hob and
  the plate; dirty things move away from it.
- Fewer steps and fewer crossings save time and lower the risk of cross-contamination, which `SAF-1` builds on.
- Things used together are stored together: knives and boards by the prep zone, pans and oil by the hob.

**Sessions:**
1. Re-organise (75 min): sketch the kitchen, mark the zones, move the tools, photograph before and after.

**Cues:** cooking a simple dish needs fewer steps and no crossing of raw and ready-to-eat food.

**Faults:**
- The board placed far from the bin or the sink.
- Raw meat stored above ready-to-eat food.
- Zones that suit the kitchen's look rather than the work.

#### ORI-1.M2.L3 — Pantry baseline

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ori-1-m2-l3 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · moves IN-STO→1*

*Objectives:* [K] core staples (salts, oils, acids, aromatics, starches); [S] organise storage
<!-- END generated:lesson-ori-1-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- The core staples by function: salts (fine for weighing, flaky for finishing), fats (a neutral oil, olive oil,
  butter), acids (wine and cider vinegar, lemon), aromatics (onions, garlic, dried herbs and whole spices), starches
  (rice, pasta, flour), and stock bases.
- Why salt is weighed: crystal size differs, so a spoonful of flaky salt weighs much less than a spoonful of fine salt.
- Storage: oils cool, dark and closed, because light and heat speed oxidation; whole spices keep their aroma longer
  than ground ones, which have more surface; first in, first out.

**Sessions:**
1. Stock-take and storage (45 min): list the staples against the baseline, label and date, arrange by function.

**Cues:** every staple has a place, a label and a date; the learner can name the function of each.

**Faults:**
- Five kinds of salt and no vinegar.
- Ground spices of unknown age.
- Oil stored next to the hob.

#### ORI-1.M3.L1 — Diagnostic cook

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ori-1-m3-l1 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `ORI-1.M1.L2` · moves JD-REFL→2*

*Objectives:* [S] cook a familiar dish with full documentation (time, photos, notes); [J] self-assess against a simple rubric
<!-- END generated:lesson-ori-1-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- Why a baseline: progress is a comparison with a recorded start, not a feeling. The same dish is cooked again at
  `MS-S1`.
- The choice of dish: one the learner cooks often and would serve to guests, that includes knife work, heat and
  seasoning. Examples: a soup, a stew, a pasta dish, an omelette with a salad.
- The simple rubric: mise en place, knife work, heat control, seasoning, timing, cleanliness. Each is scored in the four
  bands with one line of evidence. Score against *Competent*, not against the best one has done before.
- Strengths and gaps: three of each, every one tied to evidence (a time, a photo, a reading), to set the first
  priorities and to check whether lessons can be waived.

**Sessions:**
1. Diagnostic cook (2.5 h, including write-up): plan, cook with timestamps and photos at fixed points (mise en place,
   during cooking, the finished plate, a cross-section where relevant), then score.

**Cues:** a timeline that matches the clock; photos that show what the rubric asks; scores that differ by row.

**Faults:**
- Choosing a showpiece instead of a usual dish.
- Forgetting to record during cooking.
- Every row scored the same, high or low.

#### ORI-1.M3.L2 — Tasting baseline

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ori-1-m3-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · moves FL-SENS→1*

*Objectives:* [S] rank salt and sugar solutions by strength; record the results as a baseline
<!-- END generated:lesson-ori-1-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- Ranking as a sensory test: coded cups in random order, prepared by someone else where possible
  ([assessment §5](../../04-assessment.md#5-calibration-tests)) [S20].
- Bias and how the method removes it: expectation (codes, not labels), order (random), fatigue (rinse with water,
  pause, plain cracker), temperature (all samples at the same temperature, because it changes perceived intensity).
- The baseline is recorded and **not scored**. The calibration that counts as evidence starts in `TST-1`.

**Sessions:**
1. Ranking (60 min): three series in water, five samples each:
   - salt: 6, 8, 10, 12 and 15 g/l;
   - sugar: 10, 20, 30, 40 and 60 g/l;
   - white-wine vinegar: 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 ml/l.

   Rank each series, then read the key and record the result.

**Cues:** the learner tastes slowly, rinses between samples and records before reading the key.

**Faults:**
- Preparing and coding the samples oneself without a hidden key.
- Tasting cold and warm samples side by side.
- Changing the ranking after looking at the key.

#### ORI-1.M3.X1 — Diagnostic cook with a full journal entry

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-ori-1-m3-x1 -->
*project, recipe* — Diagnostic cook with a full journal entry.
<!-- END generated:exercise-ori-1-m3-x1 -->

**Evidences:** JD-REFL→2

**Aim:** a documented starting point, and a first honest self-assessment.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the learner's own usual recipe, with the rubric and the photo points given. Cooked for
the household, without time pressure.

**Inputs:** the ingredients of the chosen dish, for 2–4 portions.

**Procedure:**
1. Plan: target, timeline, photo points.
2. Cook with timestamps.
3. Photograph at the fixed points.
4. Taste and note any corrections.
5. Score each rubric row with evidence; name three strengths and three gaps.
6. Ask the household for one sentence each.

**Evidence:** written, photo, log — the full entry: plan, timestamped log, photos, scores with evidence, strengths and
gaps (the `JD-REFL` level 2 success row). The photos are kept for the side-by-side comparison at `MS-S1`. Where they
show level 3 cuts, eggs or starch cookery, they are the basis for waiving lessons.

**Time:** 2.5 h active.

#### ORI-1.M3.X2 — Taste-ranking baseline

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-ori-1-m3-x2 -->
*drill, recipe* — Taste-ranking baseline (salt, sweet, sour series).
<!-- END generated:exercise-ori-1-m3-x2 -->

**Evidences:** —

**Aim:** a recorded starting point for the palate. The module reaches `FL-SENS` level 1, so the result is a baseline,
not evidence.

**Conditions:** recipe support: concentrations, coding and procedure given. A helper codes the cups if possible.

**Inputs:** 15 litres of water in total (one litre per sample); about 51 g of fine salt, 160 g of sugar, 50 ml of
white-wine vinegar; 15 cups or glasses with codes; a 1 g scale.

**Procedure:**
1. Prepare the samples and code them, with a hidden key.
2. Bring them to room temperature.
3. Rank each series.
4. Record the result, then read the key.
5. Note the swaps.

**Evidence:** written — the three rankings and the number of swaps per series, as the baseline for `TST-1` and `MS-S1`.

**Time:** 1 h active; the samples need about 30 minutes to reach room temperature.
