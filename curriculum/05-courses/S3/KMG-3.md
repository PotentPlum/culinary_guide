## Introduction

A professional kitchen runs on plans, lists, costs and storage; a household kitchen runs on the same things, usually
without writing them down. `KMG-3` makes them explicit, so that cooking well every day becomes sustainable in time and
money:
- **weekly planning:** a balanced, seasonal week of meals around a real schedule, with one prep day;
- **purchasing:** quantities per person, a consolidated shopping list, and where to buy what (Good or Best);
- **cost and yield:** cost per portion from yields (`BUT-3`), and a budget week that keeps its quality;
- **leftovers and the freezer:** cooked food turned into new dishes within the safety limits, and a freezer that is used
  rather than filled;
- **house recipes:** the household's best dishes written so that anyone can cook them.

The safety figures of [assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment) run through every
module: leftovers into the fridge within 2 hours and kept at most 2 days, the freezer at −18 °C or colder, reheated
until steaming hot and only once, batches cooled by the standard.

**In the spiral.**
- `KM-PLAN`, `KM-PURC`, `KM-MATH`, `KM-WAST`, `IN-STO`, `IN-SEL`, `TS-MEP` and `JD-REFL` reach level 4; `CR-IMPV` and
  `RD-WRIT` level 3; `CR-MENU` starts at level 2.
- `MS-S3` is a week plan, a costed shopping list, a prep day and a written review (`MS-S3.M1.L1`, `L2` and `L7`); this
  course is its rehearsal.

**Pacing.** About four weeks, run on the household's real weeks; each module is one week of planning, shopping,
cooking and logging.

#### KMG-3.M1.L1 — A week of meals

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 wk · builds on `WRK-1.M2.L3` · moves CR-MENU→2, KM-PLAN→4*

*Objectives:* [S] balance, variety, season and time across a week; [J] plan around a busy schedule
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Balance across a week:** a range of proteins (legumes, fish at least once a week [S48], poultry, meat, eggs),
  vegetables at every meal, whole grains, and no main technique, colour or dominant taste repeated two days running (the
  balance checklist of `CR-MENU`).
- **Season:** the week starts from what is best and cheapest now (`VEG-3.M1`), not from a fixed rotation.
- **[J] Around a busy schedule:** quick meals on the busy days (15–30 minutes, from prepared components), a longer cook
  when there is time; cook once, eat twice (a braise on Sunday, its second portion on Tuesday within the 2-day limit or
  frozen); a fallback meal from the pantry.
- **One dinner as a menu:** once a week, a three-course dinner planned as a menu and checked against the balance
  checklist.

**Sessions:**
1. Plan the week (60 min): seven dinners and the lunches that come from them, on the planning template, checked against
   the balance checklist and the household's calendar.

**Cues:**
- Every busy day has a quick meal.
- Every long cook feeds two days.
- The checklist passes.

**Faults:**
- A week of pasta.
- A two-hour recipe on the busiest evening.
- No fallback.

#### KMG-3.M1.L2 — A prep day

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m1-l2 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M1.L1` · moves KM-PLAN→4, TS-MEP→4*

*Objectives:* [S] batch components (grains, sauces, roasted vegetables, stock) for the week
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **What to batch:** components that keep and save time later: a pot of grains or legumes, a stock, a sauce base,
  roasted vegetables, washed and dried leaves, a dressing, a marinade, a dough.
- **The task list:** written in a reasoned order: longest and oven tasks first, the stock in the background, washing and
  cutting while things cook, cold tasks last; one oven temperature shared where possible.
- **Clean as you go:** a bin bowl, a clean board between tasks, the sink emptied between stages; timed photos of the
  station show it.
- **Storage:** every component cooled by the standard, in labelled containers (name, date), at most 2 days in the fridge
  unless frozen (§9); the plan says which day each is used.

**Sessions:**
1. The prep day (2.5 h): the components of the week's plan batched from a written task list; timed station photos;
   everything labelled and stored.

**Cues:**
- No forgotten item.
- A clean station at each timed photo.
- Every container labelled with name and date.

**Faults:**
- Prepping for seven days when the fridge limit is 2.
- Warm containers stacked in the fridge.
- A task list in the head.

#### KMG-3.M2.L1 — Quantities per person

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `WRK-1.M3.L2` · moves KM-PURC→4, KM-MATH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] standard portion sizes; quantities from plans; a consolidated shopping list
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Working portions** (adjusted to the household's appetites, recorded once and reused): for example about 125–150 g
  of raw boneless meat or fish per main portion, 75–100 g of dry pasta or rice, 200–250 g of vegetables, 60–80 g of
  dry legumes.
- **From plan to list:** every recipe's quantities scaled to the household, then added up across the week by item, minus
  what is already in the pantry (the inventory).
- **Yield in purchasing:** a whole bird, a vegetable with its trim, a fish on the bone are bought by the as-purchased
  weight that gives the usable portion (`BUT-3.M4`).
- **Not everything scales in a straight line:** seasoning, braising liquid, oven times and frying oil change less than
  the number of portions; the reason is written down.
- **The consolidated list:** grouped by shop and by section, with quantities and units; checked against the plan.

**Sessions:**
1. The list (60 min): the coming week's plan turned into a consolidated list, scaled, with yields and the pantry
   subtracted.

**Cues:** a list that matches the plan item by item.

**Faults:**
- Buying per recipe, not per week.
- Forgetting the trim.
- Doubling the salt for a double batch.

#### KMG-3.M2.L2 — Where and how to buy

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `VEG-1.M2.L1` · moves KM-PURC→4, IN-SEL→4*

*Objectives:* [J] supermarket, market, specialist, online; seasonal buying; Good/Best decisions
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] Where:**
  - the supermarket for staples and convenience;
  - the market and farm shops for seasonal produce at its peak;
  - specialists (butcher, fishmonger, cheese shop, an Asian or Turkish grocer) for quality, advice and particular items;
  - online and vegetable-box schemes for regular deliveries.
- **Seasonal buying:** the cheapest and best produce is the produce in season; the seasonal calendar (`VEG-1`) and the
  week's market set the plan.
- **Good or Best decisions:** where the better product changes the result (fish freshness, bread flour, a cheese), it is
  worth it; where it doesn't (cooking onions, canned tomatoes), Good is good enough; each decision reasoned.
- **Judging at the shop:** look, smell, firmness, dates, origin; a reasoned change when something is not good.

**Sessions:**
1. The shopping round (60 min): the week's list bought across at least two kinds of shop; Good or Best decisions noted
   with reasons; one reasoned change made at the shop.

**Cues:** every main item's quality, price and purpose noted.

**Faults:**
- Buying the plan whatever the quality.
- Paying Best prices for Good uses.
- No receipts kept.

#### KMG-3.M3.L1 — Cost per portion

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `BUT-3.M4.L1` · moves KM-MATH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] cost a recipe with yield factors; [K] where the money goes
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Costing a recipe:**
  1. Each ingredient's quantity as purchased (with its yield factor from `BUT-3.M4`).
  2. Its price per unit.
  3. Its cost.
  4. The total.
  5. Divided by the number of portions.

  Small items (spices, oil, salt) are added as a flat percentage rather than weighed.
- **Yield factors:** usable weight ÷ purchased weight; and the cooking loss for protein served by weight.
- **[K] Where the money goes:** in most home menus, the protein and dairy are most of the cost; vegetables in season,
  grains and legumes are cheap; waste is a hidden cost (food bought and thrown away is paid for too).
- **A costing sheet** kept for each house recipe, updated when prices change.

**Sessions:**
1. Cost three dishes (60 min): three of the week's dishes costed per portion with yield factors, on the sheet.

**Cues:** a costing within 10 % of the receipts.

**Faults:**
- Costing the trimmed weight at the whole price.
- Forgetting the oil and butter.
- Ignoring waste.

#### KMG-3.M3.L2 — Budget menus

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 wk · builds on `KMG-3.M3.L1` · moves KM-PURC→4*

*Objectives:* [J] cheaper cuts, seasonal produce and pantry staples; [S] a budget week without losing quality
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] Cutting cost without cutting quality:**
  - cheaper cuts cooked well (`SLW-3`: shin, cheek, shoulder, chicken legs);
  - whole birds and fish, with every part used (`BUT-3`);
  - legumes and grains as the base, protein as the accent;
  - seasonal produce at its peak;
  - the pantry and preserves used (`PRS-3`);
  - leftovers planned in (`KMG-3.M4`);
  - Best only where it changes the result.
- **Where quality must not be cut:** freshness of fish and meat, and safety.
- **A budget week:** a set amount per person per day, the menu designed to it, costed in advance and checked against
  the receipts.

**Sessions:**
1. A budget week (60 min of planning, then the week): a week of dinners designed to a set budget and costed in advance.

**Cues:** within budget, and the household cards don't drop.

**Faults:**
- Cheap meat cooked fast and tough.
- A budget met by eating the same thing every day.
- Skipping vegetables to save money.

#### KMG-3.M4.L1 — Transforming leftovers

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→3, KM-WAST→4*

*Objectives:* [S] re-use cooked food in new dishes (soups, fritters, gratins, fried rice, salads); [J] safety limits
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **New dishes, not reheated plates:**
  - soups (roasted vegetables blended with stock);
  - fritters and croquettes (mash, grains, vegetables bound with egg and flour);
  - gratins and bakes (vegetables, pasta, a sauce, a crust);
  - fried rice and grain bowls (cold cooked rice is best for frying);
  - salads (grains, legumes, roasted vegetables, a sharp dressing);
  - fillings (a braise in a pie, a roast in a sandwich);
  - *stamppot* and hash.
- **Changing texture and flavour:** a crisp element, a fresh herb, an acid and a new sauce turn yesterday's dish into a
  new one (`FLV-3`).
- **[J] Safety limits** (assessment §9):
  - leftovers into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking, cooled quickly, covered;
  - at most 2 days in the fridge, or frozen;
  - reheated until steaming hot throughout, and only once: a leftover that was already reheated is not reheated again;
  - cooked rice cooled quickly and kept cold (*Bacillus cereus*, `SCI-2.M6`);
  - when in doubt, throw it out.
- **Improvising quickly:** what is at hand photographed first, a framework chosen, a meal on the table in 45 minutes.

**Sessions:**
1. Two transformations (90 min): two leftovers from the week turned into new dishes from frameworks, each within 45
   minutes.
2. A pantry meal (45 min): a meal from what is at hand, photographed first, without a recipe.

**Cues:**
- A new dish, not a reheated plate.
- Within 45 minutes.
- Every leftover within its 2 days.

**Faults:**
- Reheating a dish twice.
- Leftovers kept "until they smell".
- A fritter that falls apart for want of a binder.

#### KMG-3.M4.L2 — Freezer management

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m4-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 wk · builds on `SAF-1.M3.L3` · moves IN-STO→4, KM-WAST→4*

*Objectives:* [K] what freezes well and what doesn't; [S] portion, label, rotate first in, first out
<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] What freezes well:** stocks, soups, braises, cooked legumes and grains, sauces, doughs and baked bread, raw meat
  and fish, blanched vegetables, herbs in oil or butter (kept frozen), fruit for cooking.
- **[K] What freezes badly:** raw salad vegetables and cucumber (they go limp), cooked potatoes in large pieces (grainy),
  dairy-heavy sauces that split, mayonnaise, fried foods (soggy), cooked egg whites (rubbery).
- **Freezing well:**
  - cooled first (by the standard), never hot into the freezer;
  - flat bags or shallow containers, air pressed out (freezer burn);
  - portioned for one meal;
  - labelled with content, date and a use-by date for quality.
- **The freezer at −18 °C or colder** (§9), checked with a thermometer.
- **Thawing:** in the fridge, or cooked from frozen; never on the counter.
- **Rotation:** an inventory on the door or on the phone, first in first out, checked monthly; a quality limit per item
  (for example most cooked dishes within a few months).

**Sessions:**
1. Freezer audit (45 min): everything taken out, listed, labelled and dated; what is past its quality limit used first or
   discarded; the inventory set up.

**Cues:**
- An inventory that matches the freezer.
- Every item labelled and dated.

**Faults:**
- Unlabelled tubs of "brown stew".
- Freezing hot food.
- Thawing on the counter.

#### KMG-3.M5.L1 — Writing house recipes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-kmg-3-m5-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `WRK-1.M4.L2` · moves RD-WRIT→3, JD-REFL→4*

<!-- END generated:lesson-kmg-3-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A house recipe** is a dish the household wants again, written so it comes out the same every time and anyone can
  cook it:
  - a title and one line on what it is;
  - the yield (portions, or a batch size);
  - ingredients by weight in the order of use;
  - numbered steps with cues ("until the onions are soft and golden, about 10 minutes"), temperatures and times;
    - the allergens it contains (the 14 groups [S23]);
  - storage (fridge days within §9, freezing) and reheating notes;
  - a costing line (`KMG-3.M3`).
- **Testing it:** the learner cooks it again a week later from the page only; then a household member cooks it from the
  page while the learner watches without helping; the gaps are written into the next version.
- **[J] Reviewing the course:** which planning, buying and storage habits saved time and money, which didn't stick, and
  why; a pattern across the logs; a practice plan for S4.

**Sessions:**
1. Write and repeat (75 min): three house recipes written; each cooked again from the page a week later and compared.
2. Someone else cooks (75 min): a household member cooks one recipe from the page; the learner notes every question
   they ask.

**Cues:**
- A recipe another person can follow without asking.
- The repeat matches the first.

**Faults:**
- "Season to taste" with no guidance.
- Cups and handfuls instead of weights.
- No storage or reheating notes.
