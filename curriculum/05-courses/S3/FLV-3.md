## Introduction

`TST-1` taught the learner to taste and season; S2 put that to work in sauces, stocks and proteins. `FLV-3` turns
seasoning into design. A dish is built in layers: an aromatic base, a middle of main flavours and umami, and a top of
acid, fresh herbs and texture. The learner learns to plan those layers before cooking, and to diagnose a dish that has
gone wrong in more complex ways than "needs salt".

The course has three parts:
- **the aromatics:** herbs (when each goes in, and how to keep green sauces green) and spices (toasting, blooming,
  grinding, blends, storage and chilli heat);
- **the balance:** umami layered from several sources, acid chosen and timed, texture designed as contrast, and
  pairings that complement, contrast or bridge;
- **the palate:** complex faults (one-dimensional, muddy, heavy, harsh), and the second calibration round, with the
  fault-diagnosis test of [assessment §5.3](../../04-assessment.md#53-fault-diagnosis-from-s3).

The approach follows Segnit's flavour pairings and the tasting methods of sensory science [S18][S20]. Herbs are Dutch
and seasonal (grown in a pot or bought); spices are pantry goods and are bought whole where possible.

**In the spiral.**
- `IN-ARO`, `FL-SEAS`, `FL-CORR` and `FL-SENS` reach level 4, and `FL-ARCH` and `FL-TEXT` level 3. `VEG-3` and
  `CUI-3` use them every day.
- `MS-S3` re-tests the fault-diagnosis test (`MS-S3.M1.L4`) and the aromatics.
- `CR-COMP` (level 2) and `CR-ORIG` (level 1) start here; `GRD-3` and S4 take them further.

**Pacing.** About four to five weeks. Most sessions are a dish for dinner with a tasting form; the calibration and
fault tests need a household member to prepare the samples.

#### FLV-3.M1.L1 — Herb families and timing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STK-2.M1.L2` · moves IN-ARO→3*

*Objectives:* [K] tender versus woody herbs; fresh versus dried; when each goes in
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Tender herbs** (parsley, chervil, dill, chives, tarragon, basil, mint, coriander): their aromas are fragile, so they
  go in at the end or raw. Their stems carry flavour too and can go into stocks and pastes.
- **Woody herbs** (thyme, rosemary, sage, bay, oregano, savory): their aromas survive and need heat and time to come
  out, so they go in early, in fat or in long cooks. Bay leaves are removed before serving.
- **Fresh against dried:**
  - woody herbs dry well; tender herbs mostly lose their character (dried dill and parsley taste of hay);
  - dried herbs are stronger by weight, so roughly a third of the fresh amount, added early;
  - dried herbs fade within months, so they are bought small and smelled before use.
- **Families and partners:** the classic bundles (*bouquet garni*, *fines herbes*), and herbs that go with Dutch
  seasonal produce (dill and potatoes, tarragon and chicken, sage and pumpkin, savory and beans).
- **Handling:** washed and spun dry, tender herbs kept like flowers in water or wrapped in damp paper; cut with a sharp
  knife, not bruised; chopped close to serving.

**Sessions:**
1. Taste and time (60 min): the same quick pan sauce or vinaigrette finished with five herbs, and a slow-cooked pot of
   beans or potatoes with a woody herb added at the start and at the end; tasted side by side and noted on the form.

**Cues:** the learner can say for each herb when it goes in and why, and tell a fresh from a tired bunch.

**Faults:**
- Parsley boiled for an hour.
- Rosemary scattered raw over a finished dish.
- Dried tender herbs used like fresh.
- Basil chopped an hour before serving, black at the edges.

#### FLV-3.M1.L2 — Herb pastes, oils and green sauces

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M1.L1` · moves IN-ARO→4, FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] pesto-style pastes, herb oils, *salsa verde*; [J] keep them green and bright
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The green-sauce ratio:** herbs, an oil, an acid, salt, and a savoury or rich element (cheese, anchovy, capers,
  seeds), varied by family: *pesto*-style pastes (pounded or blended), *salsa verde* (chopped, with vinegar), *gremolata*
  (dry), chimichurri-style (vinegar-forward).
- **Keeping them green:**
  - blanch tough or quick-oxidising herbs for a few seconds and chill them in ice water;
  - blend cold and briefly, because heat from the blade dulls them;
  - add acid at the end, because acid turns chlorophyll olive-brown;
  - cover the surface with oil.
- **Herb oils:** herbs blended with oil, warmed briefly and strained clear. They are stored in the fridge and used
  within days, like garlic in oil (assessment §9).
- **Seasoning:** salt and acid balanced on a neutral carrier (a slice of potato or bread), not from the spoon.

**Sessions:**
1. Two green sauces (60 min): a pounded paste and a chopped vinegar sauce from the same herbs; one half of the paste
   made with blanched herbs, the other raw; colour photographed after 2 hours.
2. A herb oil (45 min): blended, strained and photographed; used on a soup.

**Cues:**
- Bright green after 2 hours.
- Distinct herb flavour, not grassy or bitter.
- Balanced on the carrier.

**Faults:**
- Blending for minutes until warm.
- Lemon juice added at the start.
- Herb oil kept on the shelf.
- A paste that tastes of raw garlic only.

#### FLV-3.M1.X1 — Three green sauces from one ratio

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-flv-3-m1-x1 -->
*framework, framework* — Three green sauces from one ratio, each paired with a different dish.
<!-- END generated:exercise-flv-3-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** IN-ARO→4, FL-ARCH→3

**Aim:** use one ratio to make three different green sauces, each designed for a dish, and identify herbs blind.

**Conditions:** framework support: the green-sauce ratio and the flavour-plan form. Three sauces from different herb
families, each paired with a different dish (for example dill and mustard with potatoes, parsley and caper with fish,
sage and pumpkin-seed paste with roast pumpkin). Each dish gets a written flavour plan (base, layers, finish) before
cooking. The extension: the herb half of the blind aroma test, eight herbs coded by a household member.

**Inputs:** three seasonal herb bunches, oil, vinegar, salt, a savoury element per sauce, and the three dishes as
household meals; eight herbs for the blind test.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the three flavour plans.
2. Make the sauces by the ratio, adjusting each for its dish.
3. Serve each with its dish; tasters fill in cards.
4. Take the blind herb test: smell (and if needed taste) eight coded herbs and name them.
5. Compare plans, forms and cards.

**Evidence:** written, form, tasters, calibration:
- **`FL-ARCH` level 3:** three dishes with a written flavour plan; the tasters find depth and balance.
- **`IN-ARO` level 4, in part:** the herb half of the blind identification (at least 6 of 8). The spice half and the
  blend with a written reason follow in `FLV-3.M2.X2`; together they make 12 of 16. The `MS-S3` test completes the row.

**Time:** 2.5 h over three meals.

#### FLV-3.M2.L1 — Spice technique

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SCI-2.M4.L1` · moves IN-ARO→4*

*Objectives:* [S] toast, bloom in fat, grind; [K] whole versus ground; fat- versus water-soluble flavour
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Whole against ground:** whole spices keep their aroma for a year or more; ground spices lose most of it within
  months, because grinding exposes the volatile oils.
- **Toasting** (dry, in a pan): whole seeds until fragrant and a shade darker, moved constantly, tipped out at once.
  Burnt spices are bitter and are thrown away.
- **Blooming** (*tadka*, *tarka*): whole or ground spices fried briefly in fat at the start or poured over at the end.
  Many aroma compounds dissolve in fat, not in water, so blooming carries them through the dish.
- **Grinding:** a mortar for small amounts and pastes, a blade grinder for larger batches; ground just before use.
- **Order:** whole spices first (they need longer), ground spices after (they burn quickly), and a final bloom or a pinch
  of a fresh-ground spice at the end for top notes.

**Sessions:**
1. Toast and grind (40 min): coriander and cumin toasted, cooled and ground; smelled against the pre-ground jar.
2. Bloom (45 min): a dal or a lentil soup with half the spices bloomed in fat and half stirred into the water; tasted
   side by side.

**Cues:**
- Fragrant, a shade darker, not smoking.
- The bloomed version rounder and more aromatic.

**Faults:**
- Toasting ground spices in a dry pan.
- Walking away from the pan.
- Ground spices added to very hot oil and burnt.
- A jar of paprika from three years ago.

#### FLV-3.M2.L2 — Spice blends of the world

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M2.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→3, CK-CUIS→2*

*Objectives:* [K] *garam masala*, *ras el hanout*, five-spice, *za'atar*, *quatre épices*; [S] make two blends
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A blend is a flavour plan:** warm, sweet, earthy, floral, sharp and hot spices balanced for a use.
- **Five classic blends**, their structure and use (CK-CUIS level 2, the pantry of four traditions):
  - *garam masala* (North India): warm and sweet (cinnamon, cardamom, clove, black pepper, cumin, coriander), added
    near the end;
  - *ras el hanout* (Morocco): many spices, floral and warm, for tagines and couscous;
  - Chinese five-spice: star anise, cassia, clove, Sichuan pepper and fennel, for braises and roasts;
  - *za'atar* (Levant): wild thyme or oregano, sumac and sesame, sprinkled raw or with oil on bread;
  - *quatre épices* (France): pepper, nutmeg, clove and ginger, for *charcuterie* and stews.
- **Making a blend:** whole spices toasted separately (each browns at its own rate), cooled, ground and mixed by weight;
  written down so it can be repeated.

**Sessions:**
1. Two blends (75 min): *garam masala* and *za'atar* (or five-spice), weighed and written up.
2. Use them (60 min): each in a dish, for example a lentil *dal* finished with *garam masala* and flatbread with
   *za'atar* and oil.

**Cues:**
- Each blend smells balanced, with no single spice dominating unless intended.
- The recipe card has weights.

**Faults:**
- All spices toasted together.
- *Garam masala* added at the start and cooked flat.
- A blend not written down.

#### FLV-3.M2.L3 — Buying and storing spices

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l3 -->
*1 h · 0 practical sessions · moves IN-STO→4*

*Objectives:* [J] whole versus ground, freshness, where to buy; [S] storage and rotation
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Buying:** whole where possible, in small amounts, from shops with a high turnover (an Asian, Turkish or Moroccan
  shop often sells fresher spices more cheaply than a supermarket jar).
- **Freshness checks:** colour, smell when rubbed, and the date of purchase on the label.
- **Storing:** airtight, dark, cool and dry, away from the stove; labelled with the name and the date.
- **Rotation:** ground spices used within about six months, whole within about a year; an inventory once a season,
  with what is fading used first or replaced.
- **[J] Whole or ground:** whole for what is toasted or used often; ground for what is used rarely and in small amounts,
  or hard to grind at home (turmeric, ginger, cinnamon bark).

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** a spice drawer where every jar has a name and a date, and nothing is older than its limit.

**Faults:**
- Jars above the stove.
- No dates.
- Buying the large supermarket jar of a spice used twice a year.

#### FLV-3.M2.L4 — Chillies

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l4 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M3.L5` · moves IN-ARO→4*

*Objectives:* [K] varieties, fresh versus dried, heat and flavour; [S] control heat in a dish
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m2-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Heat** comes from capsaicin, mostly in the white ribs and seeds. It dissolves in fat and alcohol, not in water.
- **Flavour** varies: fruity, smoky, grassy; fresh chillies (Dutch greenhouse chillies in summer) taste green and
  bright, dried chillies (*ancho*, *chipotle*, *Kashmiri*) deep and sweet.
- **Forms:** fresh, dried whole, flakes, powders, pastes, fermented sauces.
- **Controlling heat:**
  - remove the ribs and seeds for less heat;
  - add in steps and taste; heat builds over a dish's cooking;
  - balance with fat, sugar, acid and starch; dairy and fat calm it, water doesn't;
  - when too hot, increase the rest of the dish rather than "covering" it.
- **Handling:** gloves or washed hands; never touching the eyes; dried chillies toasted briefly in a ventilated kitchen.

**Sessions:**
1. Heat in steps (45 min): a tomato-free bean or vegetable stew made mild, then raised in three steps with fresh and
   dried chilli; each step tasted and noted.

**Cues:** the learner can predict roughly how hot a dish will end, and fix one that is too hot.

**Faults:**
- All the chilli in at once.
- Seeds left in for a family meal.
- Water to cool the mouth.
- Rubbing the eyes.

#### FLV-3.M2.X1 — Ground against toasted-and-ground cumin

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-flv-3-m2-x1 -->
*comparison, recipe* — Ground versus toasted-and-ground cumin in the same dish.
<!-- END generated:exercise-flv-3-m2-x1 -->

**Evidences:** IN-ARO→4

**Aim:** show by a blind test whether toasting and fresh grinding changes a dish.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the comparison recipe Phase 6 writes. The same dish (for example a carrot or pumpkin
soup) made twice, one with pre-ground cumin and one with cumin seed toasted and ground fresh, at the same weight;
coded and tasted blind.

**Inputs:** pre-ground cumin, whole cumin, the soup ingredients (Dutch seasonal carrots or pumpkin, onion, stock).

**Procedure:**
1. Write the prediction.
2. Make both soups the same way.
3. Code them; tasters choose and describe.
4. Write the result and whether it changes how the learner buys cumin.

**Evidence:** calibration, written, tasters — the blind comparison and its written conclusion; part of the `IN-ARO`
level 4 evidence, completed in `FLV-3.M2.X2`.

**Time:** 1.5 h.

#### FLV-3.M2.X2 — Your own spice blend, used in two dishes

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-flv-3-m2-x2 -->
*framework, framework* — Your own spice blend, used in two dishes.
<!-- END generated:exercise-flv-3-m2-x2 -->

**Evidences:** IN-ARO→4, FL-ARCH→3, IN-STO→4

**Aim:** compose a blend for a stated purpose, use it in two dishes, store it for quality, and identify spices blind.

**Conditions:** framework support: the blend framework and the flavour-plan form. A blend of five to eight spices,
designed for a stated dish (for example a winter root-vegetable roast, or a braise of beans and savoy cabbage), with a
written reason for each spice. Two dishes, each with a flavour plan. The extensions:
- the spice half of the blind aroma test (eight spices coded by a household member);
- a storage plan for the spice drawer and the blend: container, label, date and a use-by date chosen for quality; the
  blend's quality noted again on the day it is next used.

**Inputs:** whole spices, the two dishes as household meals, jars and labels.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the purpose and the reason for each spice.
2. Toast, grind and weigh the blend; write it down.
3. Cook the two dishes with flavour plans; tasters fill in cards.
4. Take the blind spice test.
5. Store the blend and audit the spice drawer; write the storage plan.
6. Note the blend's quality on its next use.

**Evidence:** calibration, written, tasters, form, photo, log:
- **`IN-ARO` level 4:** the blend for a stated dish with a written reason, judged balanced by the tasters; the spice
  half of the blind identification (with `FLV-3.M1.X1`, at least 12 of 16 in total). The `MS-S3` test completes the
  row.
- **`FL-ARCH` level 3:** the two dishes with flavour plans, and the cumin soup of `FLV-3.M2.X1`, make three; the
  tasters find depth and balance.
- **`IN-STO` level 4, in part:** the storage plan with method, container, label and use-by date per item, and the
  quality note on the day of use. The batch-cooking part of the row follows in `KMG-3.M4.X1` and `PRS-3.M6.X1`.

**Time:** 2.5 h over two meals.

### FLV-3.M3 — Umami, salt and acid in depth

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-flv-3-m3 -->
*4.5 h · 2 lessons · 3 practical sessions · reaches FL-SEAS 4*

*Scope:* layering umami; choosing and timing acid.

*Objectives:* [S] layer umami sources; [J] choose the acid and the moment.

*Check (outline):* blind household panel against the plain version.

#### FLV-3.M3.L1 — Layering umami

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `TST-1.M3.L4` · moves FL-SEAS→4*

*Objectives:* [S] ferments, aged cheese, dried mushrooms, anchovy, miso, tomato paste; synergy in practice
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Two kinds of umami:** glutamate (aged cheese, tomato paste, miso, soy sauce, seaweed, mushrooms) and the
  nucleotides (dried mushrooms, anchovy, meat and fish). Together they are much stronger than either alone (synergy,
  `TST-1.M3.L4`).
- **Sources and how to use them:**
  - tomato paste fried in fat until brick-red;
  - dried mushrooms soaked, the liquid strained and used;
  - Parmesan or aged Gouda rinds in a soup;
  - anchovy melted into fat until it disappears;
  - miso stirred in off the boil;
  - kombu steeped below the boil;
  - soy or fish sauce as part of the salt.
- **Layering:** one source in the base (fried with the aromatics), one in the middle (in the liquid) and one at the
  end (grated cheese, a few drops of soy), so that depth comes from several places.
- **Salt in the umami sources:** soy, miso, anchovy and cheese are salty, so the added salt drops.

**Sessions:**
1. A layered stock (60 min): a vegetable stock with kombu and dried mushrooms, against one without; tasted blind.
2. A layered dish (60 min): a mushroom and barley risotto-style dish built in three umami layers, written as a plan.

**Cues:** "deep" or "round" on the tasters' cards, without any single ingredient being named.

**Faults:**
- Miso boiled.
- Anchovy in lumps.
- Salt added before the soy.
- One source in a large dose instead of three small ones.

#### FLV-3.M3.L2 — Choosing and timing acid

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M3.L1` · moves FL-SEAS→4*

*Objectives:* [J] vinegars, citrus, wine, fermented acids; cooked versus raw; finishing
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Acids differ in flavour:** wine vinegar (sharp), cider vinegar (fruity), sherry or balsamic vinegar (deep, sweet),
  rice vinegar (mild), lemon (bright, perfumed), wine (cooked into the base), fermented acids (yoghurt, buttermilk,
  sauerkraut juice, pickling liquid).
- **Cooked against raw:** acid cooked in (wine reduced with the aromatics) becomes part of the base and loses its
  edge; acid added at the end lifts and sharpens.
- **[J] Choosing:** match the acid to the dish's character (cider vinegar with apples and pork, sherry vinegar with
  mushrooms, lemon with fish and herbs, buttermilk with potatoes) and to its richness (fatty dishes need more).
- **Finishing:** a few drops, tasted, then more; acid also reduces the salt the dish seems to need.

**Sessions:**
1. Five acids (45 min): the same braised lentils finished with five acids in five bowls; each judged and the best match
   reasoned on the form.

**Cues:** the learner can name the acid that fits a dish and explain why.

**Faults:**
- Balsamic vinegar on everything.
- Lemon juice simmered for an hour.
- A rich dish with no acid at all.

#### FLV-3.M4.L1 — Texture design

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `TST-1.M1.L1` · moves FL-TEXT→3*

*Objectives:* [K] crisp, crunchy, creamy, chewy, tender; why contrast keeps a dish interesting
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The vocabulary:** crisp (breaks cleanly, often thin and dry), crunchy (breaks with resistance, often thick), creamy,
  smooth, chewy, tender, juicy, silky, gelatinous.
- **Why contrast matters:** the palate adapts to a single texture within a few bites, and the dish becomes boring. A
  contrast (soft and crisp, creamy and crunchy, tender and chewy) keeps each bite interesting.
- **Keeping textures until the plate:**
  - crisp elements are added at the last moment, or kept apart from wet ones;
  - steam ruins crispness, so fried things rest on a rack, not on paper in a pile;
  - salads are dressed just before serving.
- **Planning:** a texture line in the flavour plan: the main texture and the contrast named before cooking.

**Sessions:**
1. A texture tasting (60 min): one soft base (a purée or a soup) served with four toppings of different textures;
   each pairing described with the vocabulary on the form.

**Cues:** the form uses precise texture words; the learner predicts which contrast works best.

**Faults:**
- "Crunchy" for everything.
- A crisp topping added an hour before serving.
- Three soft elements on one plate.

#### FLV-3.M4.L2 — Crunchy elements

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M4.L1` · moves FL-TEXT→3, CR-COMP→2*

*Objectives:* [S] toasted crumbs, seeds and nuts, crisps, fried shallots; [J] when to add them
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The usual crunchy elements:**
  - toasted breadcrumbs (*pangrattato*) with garlic and herbs;
  - toasted seeds (pumpkin, sunflower, sesame) and buckwheat;
  - vegetable crisps (thin slices of root vegetables or kale, baked or fried);
  - fried shallots or onions;
  - croutons, crackling, crisp-fried capers.
- **Nuts** are a classic crunch, but the program's produce scope uses seeds and grains instead; where a learner uses
  nuts, the allergen rule applies (`SAF-1.M4`).
- **Frying small elements:** shallow oil, a thermometer, small batches, drained on a rack, salted at once (`HEA-2.M4`).
- **[J] When to add them:** at the last moment, on top, where they won't sit in sauce; and only where they serve the
  dish (a crunchy element that fights the dish is a fault, not a contrast).
- **Storing:** cooled completely and airtight; most keep a few days.

**Sessions:**
1. Three crunchy elements (60 min): *pangrattato*, toasted seeds and fried shallots, made, cooled and stored airtight;
   checked for crispness the next day.

**Cues:**
- Evenly golden, crisp after cooling.
- Fried shallots golden, not dark brown (they keep cooking off the heat).

**Faults:**
- Shallots fried until dark brown in the pan.
- Crumbs burnt at the edges.
- Elements stored warm and gone soft.

### FLV-3.M5 — Pairing and composition

<!-- BEGIN generated:module-flv-3-m5 -->

#### FLV-3.M5.L1 — Pairing principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FLV-3.M3.L2` · moves FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [K] complementing, contrasting and bridging; classic pairings and why they work
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Three ways to pair** (Segnit [S18]):
  - **complement:** flavours that share character (mushroom and thyme, beetroot and dill, pork and apple);
  - **contrast:** flavours that oppose and balance (rich and sharp: mackerel and rhubarb; sweet and salty; fatty and
    bitter: duck and chicory);
  - **bridge:** a third ingredient that links two that don't meet (a herb or spice shared by both).
- **Classic pairings and why they work,** with Dutch seasonal examples: potatoes and dill, white asparagus and egg and
  butter, kale and smoked sausage, pumpkin and sage, leeks and mustard, red cabbage and apple and clove.
- **Regional logic:** "what grows together goes together", a useful start, not a rule.
- **Testing a pairing small:** taste the two together on a spoon before building a dish.

**Sessions:**
1. Pairing tests (60 min): six small spoon tests of two ingredients each (two complements, two contrasts, two bridged),
   each judged on the form.

**Cues:** the learner can name the pairing type and the reason it works.

**Faults:**
- Pairing by novelty alone.
- A bridge that becomes a third main flavour.
- Not tasting the pair before cooking.

#### FLV-3.M5.L2 — Building flavour in layers

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m5-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FLV-3.M5.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→3, CR-ORIG→1*

*Objectives:* [S] base, middle and top notes in a dish; [J] create your own combination
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m5-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Three layers:**
  - **the base:** the aromatics, fat and browning that cook in (a *soffritto*, a *mirepoix*, toasted spices);
  - **the middle:** the main ingredient, its liquid, the umami layers;
  - **the top:** what goes in at the end and is tasted first: acid, fresh herbs, a finishing fat, texture.
- **A flavour plan** names each layer before cooking; the tasting form checks it after.
- **[J] An own combination:** start from a pairing (`FLV-3.M5.L1`), give it a base and a top, and test it small before
  cooking a whole dish.

**Sessions:**
1. A dish in three layers (75 min): a dish of the learner's design from a pairing tested in `FLV-3.M5.L1`, with a
   written plan of base, middle and top; tasted against the plan.

**Cues:** the tasters can describe a beginning, a middle and an end to each mouthful.

**Faults:**
- No top layer (the dish tastes flat).
- Too many layers fighting.
- The plan written after cooking.

#### FLV-3.M6.L1 — Complex faults

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-flv-3-m6-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M5.L2` · moves FL-CORR→4, JD-TRBL→4*

*Objectives:* [J] one-dimensional, muddy, heavy, harsh: causes and fixes
<!-- END generated:lesson-flv-3-m6-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Beyond salt and acid:** faults that the S1 checklist doesn't name.
  - **One-dimensional:** all middle, no base or top. Fix: a top layer (acid, herbs), or more depth (umami, browned
    aromatics).
  - **Muddy:** too many flavours, none clear; often brown and over-cooked herbs and spices. Fix: brighten with acid and
    fresh herbs, and next time use fewer elements.
  - **Heavy:** too much fat or starch, no lift. Fix: acid, a fresh or bitter element, a lighter garnish; thin a sauce.
  - **Harsh:** raw garlic or alcohol, burnt spices, too much acid or bitterness. Fix: cook longer if raw, round with fat
    or a little sweetness, dilute; burnt cannot be fixed.
- **The diagnosis order:** salt first (most faults are salt), then acid, then the layers, then texture; one change at a
  time, tasted on a carrier.
- **[J] Fix or restart:** burnt, scorched and badly over-salted dishes are often better restarted or turned into
  something else.

**Sessions:**
1. Diagnose (60 min): four small portions of one dish, each spoiled in a different way by a household member; named
   and written up.
2. Fix (60 min): two of them fixed and tasted blind against the correct version.

**Cues:** the fault named in one or two words, with the fix and its reason.

**Faults:**
- Adding salt to every fault.
- Changing three things at once.
- Covering a burnt taste with more spice.
