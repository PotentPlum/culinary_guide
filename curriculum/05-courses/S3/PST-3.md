## Introduction

`BAK-2` taught short pastry, cakes, pastry cream and the first tart. `PST-3` adds the pastry work that most home bakers
avoid because it seems difficult, and shows that it is mostly a matter of temperature and timing:
- **choux:** a cooked paste leavened by steam, for *éclairs*, profiteroles and savoury *gougères*;
- **laminated dough:** how layers of butter and dough rise, made as rough puff and used in tarts, pies and *palmiers*;
- **tarts:** fruit tarts, baked fillings and savoury pies, all with a crisp base;
- **creams, ganache and mousses:** *crème anglaise* variations, ganache as an emulsion, mousses set with gelatine or
  chocolate;
- **chocolate:** types, melting without seizing, and tempering introduced by the seed method;
- **planning:** a dessert built from components made over three days.

The method follows the CIA's baking and pastry fundamentals [S9]: weighed formulas, temperatures checked with a
thermometer, and each element judged in cross-section. Fruit is Dutch and seasonal (rhubarb and strawberries in early
summer, plums in late summer, apples and pears from autumn); chocolate, vanilla and citrus are pantry goods.

Pastry has its own safety points (assessment §9): pastry cream and custards cooled by the cooling standard and kept at
most 2 days; raw-egg mousses made with pasteurised egg for every guest and kept at most 24 hours; cream fillings kept
in the fridge and out of it for at most 2 hours.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-BAK` reaches level 4 across the course: six of its seven elements, with laminated pastry, two adapted formulas and
  a diagnosed failure.
- `SC-STRC` and `KM-PLAN` reach level 4; `IN-BAK`, `SC-EMUL` and `RD-RATO` level 3.
- `MS-S3` re-tests a pastry item if the learner chose bread at `MS-S2` (`MS-S3.M1.L5`).
- `PST-4` (S4) builds plated desserts, ice cream (from the *crème anglaise* base made here) and advanced lamination.

**Pacing.** About five to six weeks. Laminated dough and multi-day desserts need several short sessions over a few days;
most bakes are household desserts or are frozen.

#### PST-3.M1.L1 — Choux paste

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BAK-2.M1.L2` · moves TS-BAK→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cook the *panade*, add eggs to the right consistency, pipe; [K] steam leavening
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The *panade*:** water (or milk and water), butter and salt brought to the boil, all the flour added at once and
  stirred over the heat until the paste comes away from the pan and a thin film forms on the bottom. Cooking the flour
  gelatinises the starch so it can hold the steam.
- **Adding the eggs:** the paste cooled slightly (so the eggs don't cook), then beaten egg added in parts until the
  paste is glossy and falls from the spoon in a "V" that holds. The amount of egg varies with how much water evaporated,
  so it is judged, not fixed.
- **[K] Steam leavening:** in a hot oven, the water in the paste turns to steam and puffs the shell; the egg proteins
  and starch set around the hollow.
- **Piping:** a plain round nozzle, the bag held upright for rounds, at an angle for *éclairs*; points smoothed with a
  wet finger; an egg wash.

**Sessions:**
1. The paste (60 min): a batch of choux paste, the egg added to the "V"; piped into rounds and *éclair* lengths; baked.
2. Consistency (45 min): one batch split, with too little, just enough and too much egg; baked side by side and photographed.

**Cues:**
- A film on the pan base.
- A glossy "V" off the spoon.
- Even piping.

**Faults:**
- Adding all the egg at once.
- A runny paste that spreads flat.
- Eggs into a hot paste, scrambled.

#### PST-3.M1.L2 — *Éclairs*, profiteroles and *gougères*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m1-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `PST-3.M1.L1` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] shapes, baking and drying out; fillings; [J] collapse and hollowness
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Baking:** a hot oven to puff (around 200 °C), then lower to dry and set; the door kept shut for the first two
  thirds, because the steam escapes and the shells collapse.
- **Drying out:** the shells pierced or split and returned to a cooling, ajar oven so the inside dries; a hollow, crisp
  shell that doesn't go soft.
- ***Gougères*:** grated cheese (aged Gouda works well) beaten into the paste; savoury, served warm.
- **Fillings:** pastry cream (`BAK-2.M5`), whipped cream, a *crème diplomate*; filled close to serving so the shell
  stays crisp; cream-filled pastries in the fridge, out for at most 2 hours (§9).
- **[J] Collapse and hollowness:**
  - collapsed shells: underbaked, the door opened early, or too much egg;
  - doughy inside: not dried out;
  - cracked, uneven shells: oven too hot or paste too stiff;
  - small and dense: too little egg or a cool oven.

**Sessions:**
1. Profiteroles and *éclairs* (75 min): baked, dried out, filled with pastry cream, glazed.
2. *Gougères* (60 min): cheese choux for a starter; one shell cut in cross-section.

**Cues:**
- Golden, light for their size.
- Hollow and dry inside.
- Still crisp an hour after filling.

**Faults:**
- Opening the oven at 10 minutes.
- Filling hours ahead.
- Soft, wet insides.

#### PST-3.M2.L1 — How lamination works

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M3.L2` · moves SC-STRC→4, IN-DAI→4*

*Objectives:* [K] layers of dough and butter, steam and gluten; butter temperature
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Layers:** a dough and a fat are folded so they form many thin, separate layers. In the oven, the water in the
  dough and butter turns to steam and pushes the layers apart; the gluten holds them; the butter fries them crisp.
- **Folds:** each "turn" multiplies the layers (a letter fold triples them); rough puff uses fewer, quicker turns than
  classic puff.
- **[K] Butter temperature:** the butter must be cold and pliable at the same time, about as firm as the dough: too
  warm and it melts into the dough (no layers); too cold and it shatters (broken layers). Rests in the fridge between
  turns keep it there and relax the gluten.
- **The butter itself:** butter with a higher fat content (often sold as baking or "extra" butter) and a waxy texture
  laminates more easily; the dairy choice is reasoned (`IN-DAI`).
- **Gluten:** a lean dough with moderate gluten; overworked dough shrinks and toughens.

**Sessions:**
1. Fold and see (60 min): a small dough laminated with cold butter, cut after one, two and three turns; the layers
   photographed under the light.

**Cues:**
- Visible, separate layers in the cut.
- The butter pliable, not greasy or brittle.

**Faults:**
- Butter melting through the dough.
- Skipping the rests.
- Flouring heavily between folds.

#### PST-3.M2.L2 — Rough puff

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 4 h · builds on `PST-3.M2.L1` · moves TS-BAK→3*

*Objectives:* [S] make rough puff with folds and rests; [J] manage butter temperature
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The method:** cold butter in cubes or grated, mixed loosely into flour and salt with iced water to a shaggy dough
  with visible butter; rolled into a rectangle; letter-folded; turned 90°; rolled and folded again; rested in the fridge
  between pairs of turns, for four to six turns in total.
- **[J] Managing butter temperature:** a cool kitchen, a cold surface, quick work; when the butter shows signs of
  softening (greasy, sticky patches), straight back into the fridge.
- **Storing:** wrapped in the fridge for a day or two, or frozen.
- **Shrinkage:** rested before cutting and again after shaping, so it doesn't shrink in the oven.

**Sessions:**
1. A batch of rough puff (90 min active, 4 h in total): made with the full number of turns and rests; logged.
2. A second batch (60 min): made with the corrections from the first; compared.

**Cues:**
- Streaks of butter in a smooth dough.
- Square corners and even thickness.

**Faults:**
- Butter breaking through the surface.
- Rolling in one direction only.
- No rest before baking.

#### PST-3.M2.L3 — Using puff pastry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m2-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `PST-3.M2.L2` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] tarts, pies, *palmiers*; [J] bake to full rise and colour
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Cutting:** a sharp knife or cutter straight down (dragging or twisting seals the layers); egg wash kept off the cut
  edges.
- **Shapes:** a free-form tart (*tarte fine*) with a border; a pie lid with a vent; *palmiers* rolled in sugar;
  cheese straws.
- **[J] Full rise and colour:** a hot oven (around 200–220 °C) to puff; baked until deep golden through, not pale (pale
  puff is soggy inside); a docked or weighted base for tarts that should stay flat.
- **Savoury and sweet:** an apple or pear *tarte fine*; a leek and cheese pie; a mushroom pie lid.

**Sessions:**
1. Sweet (60 min): *palmiers* and an apple *tarte fine*.
2. Savoury (60 min): a pie with a puff lid; the lid cut and photographed in cross-section.

**Cues:**
- Even, tall rise.
- Deep golden and crisp throughout.

**Faults:**
- Egg wash on the edges, gluing the layers.
- Pale, underbaked layers.
- A tart that puffed into a dome.

#### PST-3.M3.L1 — Fruit tarts

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 h · builds on `BAK-2.M5.L1` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] pastry cream base, fruit arrangement, glaze
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The components:** a blind-baked sweet pastry case (`BAK-2.M3`), a layer of pastry cream (`BAK-2.M5`), fresh fruit
  arranged, and a glaze (warmed apricot jam or a fruit jelly) for shine and to keep the fruit from drying.
- **Keeping the base crisp:** the case baked fully and sealed (a thin brush of melted chocolate or egg wash baked on);
  assembled close to serving.
- **Fruit arrangement:** fruit dried, cut evenly, arranged in overlapping rows or circles from the outside in; one or two
  fruits, not a fruit salad.
- **Seasonal choices:** strawberries in June, plums and berries from Dutch growers in summer, poached pears or apples in
  autumn and winter.
- **Safety:** pastry cream cooled by the cooling standard (§9) and the tart kept in the fridge, out for at most 2 hours.

**Sessions:**
1. A fruit tart (90 min active, 4 h in total): case blind-baked, pastry cream made and cooled, assembled and glazed.

**Cues:**
- A crisp base when sliced.
- Neat rows of fruit.
- An even shine.

**Faults:**
- A soggy base under the cream.
- Wet fruit.
- Assembling a day ahead.

#### PST-3.M3.L2 — Baked fillings

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `BAK-2.M3.L3` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *frangipane*, custard, lemon; [J] judge the set
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***Frangipane*:** butter, sugar, eggs and ground almonds (or, nut-free, ground toasted seeds or a mix of flour and
  semolina), baked with fruit (pears, plums, apples); set and golden.
- **Custard fillings:** a baked custard (eggs, cream or milk, sugar) in a blind-baked case, at a low oven so it sets
  without bubbling or curdling (`EGG-1`); a lemon tart (citrus as a pantry good) is the classic sharp version.
- **[J] Judging the set:** a custard is set when the edge is firm and the centre wobbles like jelly as a whole, not in
  waves; it finishes setting as it cools. A *frangipane* is set when a skewer comes out clean and the top springs back.
- **Safety:** the custard probed in the centre, every part at least 71 °C (the egg-thickened row of §9), as in
  `BAK-2.M5.L2`.
- **Cooling:** at room temperature briefly, then into the fridge within the 2-hour limit (§9).

**Sessions:**
1. A custard tart (90 min): a baked custard or lemon tart; the set judged by the wobble; sliced when cold.

**Cues:**
- A smooth, glossy surface without cracks.
- A clean slice.

**Faults:**
- An oven too hot: bubbles, cracks and curdling.
- Taking it out with a liquid centre.
- Leaving the tart out overnight.

*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BAK-2.M3.L2` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] savoury fillings, double-crust pies, venting; [J] a crisp base under a wet filling
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Savoury fillings:** a *quiche*-style custard with leeks, onions or greens; a pie filling of braised meat (`SLW-3`) or

#### PST-3.M4.L2 — Ganache

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SCI-2.M4.L2` · moves SC-EMUL→3, TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] ratios for glaze, filling and truffles; [K] ganache as an emulsion; fix a split ganache
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Ganache as an emulsion:** chocolate (cocoa butter and solids) and cream (water and milk fat) emulsified into a
  smooth, glossy mass; the ratio sets its use:
  - about 1 part chocolate to 1 part cream (dark chocolate) for a glaze;
  - about 2 to 1 for a firmer filling and truffles;
  - more cream for a whipped ganache;
  - milk and white chocolate need more chocolate for the same firmness, because they contain less cocoa solids.
- **Making it:** hot cream poured over chopped chocolate, left a minute, then stirred from the centre outwards into an
  emulsion; or blended with a stick blender, the head kept below the surface.
- **[K] Fixing a split ganache:** oily and grainy means the fat has separated: a spoon of warm milk or cream blended in
  from the centre brings it back; a ganache too hot or overworked splits most easily.

**Sessions:**
1. Three ganaches (60 min): glaze, filling and truffle ratios with dark chocolate; textures compared when cool.
2. Split and fix (30 min): a ganache split on purpose (too hot or over-stirred) and repaired; photographed at each step.

**Cues:**
- Glossy and smooth.
- The ratio matching its use.
- The repair photographed.

**Faults:**
- Boiling cream on fine chocolate, splitting it.
- Whisking in air.
- Using compound chocolate for a fine ganache.

#### PST-3.M4.L3 — Mousses

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m4-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 4 h · builds on `PST-3.M4.L2` · moves TS-BAK→4, RD-RATO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] base, aeration, setting; [K] gelatine: blooming, dosing by weight, setting and melting points (taught here, deepened in `MOD-4.M3`); ratios
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m4-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The three parts:** a flavoured base (melted chocolate, a fruit purée, a *crème anglaise*), aeration (whipped cream,
  whisked egg whites or an Italian meringue), and a setting agent (chocolate itself, or gelatine).
- **Folding:** the aeration folded into the base in thirds, the first third stirred in to loosen it; the base and the foam
  at compatible temperatures, so the chocolate doesn't seize and the gelatine doesn't set in lumps.
- **[K] Gelatine:**
  - **blooming:** sheets soaked in cold water, or powder sprinkled over a measured cold liquid;
  - **dosing by weight:** a percentage of the liquid it sets, by the recipe; sheets differ in strength, so weight,
    not the number of sheets, is the reliable measure;
  - **setting and melting:** gelatine dissolves in warm liquid (never boiled), sets in the fridge over hours, and melts
    again close to body temperature, which gives a melt-in-the-mouth texture;
  - some fruits (fresh pineapple, kiwi) contain enzymes that stop it setting unless cooked first;
  - gelatine is an animal product (pork or beef), which matters for vegetarians and some faiths (`NUT-3`).
- **Ratios:** a mousse by ratio (base, foam, setting agent), written down so it can be varied.
- **Safety:** a mousse with raw egg is made with pasteurised egg for every guest, or on a base cooked to 71 °C (a *crème
  anglaise*; §9). A mousse with raw egg is kept in the fridge at most 24 hours (the raw-egg row of §9), one on a cooked
  base at most 2 days.

**Sessions:**
1. A chocolate mousse (60 min active, 4 h in total): chocolate as the setting agent; folded; set.
2. A fruit mousse (60 min active, 4 h in total): a seasonal fruit purée with gelatine dosed by weight and whipped cream;
   set and unmoulded.

**Cues:**
- Light and smooth.
- No streaks of white.
- Set enough to hold a spoon's shape, melting in the mouth.

**Faults:**
- Gelatine boiled or added to a cold base, setting in strings.
- Deflating the foam by stirring.
- Counting sheets instead of weighing.

#### PST-3.M5.L1 — Chocolate types and melting

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · moves IN-BAK→3*

*Objectives:* [K] cocoa content, couverture, compound; [S] melt without burning or seizing
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Types:**
  - **cocoa content:** the percentage of cocoa solids and cocoa butter together; more means more bitter and intense;
  - **couverture:** extra cocoa butter, flows well, for tempering and coating;

  lower.

  tools; a seized chocolate can still go into a ganache or a sauce with more liquid.

**Sessions:**

   comparison bake of the same brownie or ganache with two chocolates, photographed in cross-section and explained.

- Smooth and glossy melted chocolate.
- No grain.
- The learner can read a chocolate label.

**Faults:**
- Steam in the bowl.

#### PST-3.M5.L2 — Tempering introduced

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-pst-3-m5-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `PST-3.M5.L1` · moves TS-BAK→4*

*Objectives:* [S] the seed method with a thermometer; [K] why tempering matters
<!-- END generated:lesson-pst-3-m5-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Why tempering matters:** cocoa butter can set in several crystal forms; only one (form V) gives a glossy
  surface, a clean snap, shrinkage that releases from moulds, and stability at room temperature. Untempered chocolate
  sets dull, streaky (bloom) and soft.
- **The seed method:**
  1. Melt two thirds of the chocolate fully (dark to about 45–50 °C).
  2. Stir in the remaining third, finely chopped, as "seed" crystals in the right form.
  3. Stir until it cools to the working temperature (dark about 31–32 °C, milk about 29–30 °C, white about 28–29 °C;
     the maker's figures on the pack take precedence).
  4. Remove any unmelted seed.
- **Testing:** a knife tip or a strip of paper dipped and left at room temperature sets glossy and snaps within a few
  minutes.
- **Keeping it in temper:** working at the temperature, warmed briefly if it thickens.

**Sessions:**
1. Temper dark chocolate (60 min): by the seed method with a thermometer; tested; used for dipping.
2. Temper again (60 min): a second batch, logged, used for a few simple bars or shards.

**Cues:**
- The test strip glossy and snapping.
- The temperatures logged.
- No streaks.

**Faults:**
- Overheating the melt.
- Skipping the test.
- A cold, humid kitchen with a fan blowing on the chocolate.
