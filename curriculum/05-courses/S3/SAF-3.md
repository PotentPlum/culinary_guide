## Introduction

S1 taught the rules of a safe kitchen; S2 applied them to stocks, poultry, meat and fish. S3 adds processes where the
rules are not enough on their own: large batches, food kept without oxygen, food preserved by acid, salt or sugar,
and food served below the usual core temperatures. In each, the hazard cannot be seen, smelled or tasted, and a mistake
can be serious (botulism is rare and deadly). `SAF-3` comes first in the stage because `SLW-3`, `GRD-3`, `PRS-3`,
`FIR-3` and `CUI-3` lean on it.

The course has two ideas:
- **Some processes need a tested method.** The learner learns to recognise them, to find the method in a trustworthy
  source, and to refuse a recipe that improvises where it must not.
- **Control points.** HACCP, the system professional kitchens use, is reduced to its idea: find the steps where a hazard
  is controlled, name the limit, and log it. The learner writes such a plan for the `MS-S3` week and uses it for every
  guest production from here on.

Every figure comes from [assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment), which was extended
for this stage: the poultry table for low-temperature cooking, cook-chill storage, parasites in raw fish, risk groups,
acidity, home canning, fermentation salt and nitrite curing salt. Each has a checked source; where the Dutch home advice
is stricter than the professional rule, the stricter one is taught.

**In the spiral.**
- `SA-TEMP` reaches level 4 here: a large batch cooled to the measurable standard, a skill `SCI-2` started on a smaller
  batch.
- `SA-HRPR` reaches level 4: the knowledge in this course, and the logs in `SLW-3`, `FIR-3`, `GRD-3` and `PRS-3`.
- `SA-HYG` and `SA-PHYS` reach level 5: the plan is written here, and the five productions it asks for are logged
  across S3 and S4, with the capstone among them.

**Pacing.** About a week. The cooling exercise needs a day: the stock is made, cooled and logged, then checked the next
morning.

#### SAF-3.M1.L1 — Cooling, holding and reheating batches

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-3-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `SCI-2.M6.L2` · moves SA-TEMP→4, SA-HRPR→2*

*Objectives:* [S] cool large volumes fast (shallow trays, ice bath, dividing); hold hot and reheat safely
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Why batches are different.** A small pan of soup cools in minutes; 4 litres in a deep pot can stay warm for hours,
  in the range where spores that survived cooking germinate and grow (`SCI-2.M6`). Time in that range is the hazard.
- **The standard** (assessment §9): from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours, and to 5 °C or below within 6 hours in total.
  The first stage is the critical one, because growth is fastest there.
- **The methods** (Food Code §3-501.15):
  - divide into shallow containers (a few centimetres deep);
  - an ice-water bath, stirred, with the water topped up with ice;
  - ice as an ingredient: part of the water of a stock replaced by ice after reducing;
  - lids off until cold, then covered;
  - never a hot pot in the fridge, which warms everything else.
- **Holding hot:** at 57 °C or above, checked with the probe; a low oven or a bain-marie, not a pan switched off.
- **Reheating:** until steaming hot throughout, and once only. For food that is then held hot, every part reaches 74 °C
  for 15 seconds within 2 hours (§9).
- **Storing:** at most 2 days in the fridge at 4 °C, covered and labelled; freeze what will be kept longer.

**Sessions:**
1. Cool a batch (2 h active over a day): make the 4 litres of stock for `SAF-3.M1.X1`, split it, cool it two ways and
   log both; next morning, check the fridge and reheat one portion to 74 °C.

**Cues:**
- Readings logged every 30 minutes, with the time.
- 21 °C reached within 2 hours.
- Containers shallow and uncovered while cooling.

**Faults:**
- One deep pot in the fridge.
- The lid on while hot.
- Logging only the end temperature.
- Reheating the whole batch for one bowl.

#### SAF-3.M1.L2 — Botulism, acidity and oxygen

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-3-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `SCI-2.M6.L2` · moves SA-HRPR→3*

*Objectives:* [K] why low-acid, low-oxygen storage is dangerous (garlic in oil, home canning); pH 4.6; [J] only tested methods [S27]
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***Clostridium botulinum*** forms spores that survive boiling. Where there is no oxygen, low acid, enough moisture and
  warmth, they can germinate and make a toxin that kills in tiny amounts. The food can look, smell and taste normal.
- **The pH line** (§9, NCHFP [S27]): foods at pH 4.6 or below are acid foods (fruit, pickles, sauerkraut, jams); above
  4.6 is low-acid (meat, fish, poultry, milk, and all fresh vegetables except most tomatoes). Mixtures are usually
  low-acid unless a tested recipe adds enough acid.
- **Where the risk hides at home:**
  - garlic, herbs or vegetables in oil (no oxygen, low acid): refrigerated, used soon, never kept on the counter (§9);
  - home canning of low-acid foods: only a pressure canner reaches the needed temperature, and the program does not
    teach it; a boiling-water canner is for acid foods only;
  - vacuum bags and sous vide: no oxygen, so the cook-chill rules apply (`SAF-3.M1.L3`);
  - foil-wrapped baked potatoes left warm.
- **The judgment: only a tested method.** A tested recipe comes from a source that measured its acid, time and jar size
  (NCHFP, or an equivalent authority). A blog that says "my grandmother did it" is not a test. Signs of an unsafe recipe:
  low-acid vegetables in a water bath, "adjust the vinegar to taste" in a canned recipe, oven canning, no processing time,
  garlic oil kept in the cupboard.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** the learner can sort ten foods into acid and low-acid, and explain why a canned recipe can't be changed.

**Faults:**
- Trusting the smell.
- "Topping up" a tested pickle with extra vegetables.
- Water-bath canning beans or a vegetable soup.
- Garlic oil on the shelf.

#### SAF-3.M1.L3 — Curing salts, fermentation and sous vide rules

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-3-m1-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L2` · moves SA-HRPR→4*

*Objectives:* [K] nitrite dosage, salt % for ferments, time and temperature for pasteurisation; when to throw away
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-3-m1-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Curing salts** (§9). Nitrite stops *C. botulinum* in cured meats that are not cooked through or are kept long
  (air-dried, cold-smoked, fermented sausages). It is toxic in excess, so:
  - it is used only where a tested recipe needs it;
  - the added nitrite stays at or below 80 mg per kg of meat (the EU limit, as the nitrite ion);
  - the dose is calculated from the nitrite content on the salt's label and weighed on a 0.01 g scale;
  - curing salt is labelled and kept apart from table salt.

  The cures of `PRS-3` are cooked or frozen before eating and use plain salt; the nitrite rule is taught so the learner
  can read a recipe that asks for it.
- **Fermentation** (§9). Salt at 2.25–2.5 % of the vegetables' weight, the vegetables under the brine, and a cool room
  (sauerkraut ferments in about 3–4 weeks at 21–24 °C). Discard a ferment with fuzzy or coloured mould, a slimy or soft
  texture, or a rotten smell; a flat white film (*kahm* yeast) is skimmed. `PRS-3.M3` practises it.
- **Sous vide and low-temperature cooking** (§9):
  - poultry follows the poultry table: for example 60 °C for 35 minutes or 65.6 °C for 5 minutes at the core; nothing
    below 60 °C;
  - whole meat roasts follow the roast table; intact beef steaks follow the steak row; other intact meat and fish reach
    63 °C for 15 seconds;
  - fish served below 60 °C at the core is treated as raw: frozen first (see below), and not served to risk groups;
  - the time starts when the **core** reaches the bath temperature, not when the bag goes in;
  - food kept for later is chilled in its bag by the cooling standard and kept as leftovers (2 days) or frozen. The Food
    Code's professional limits are not used at home.
- **Raw and lightly cooked fish** (§9): frozen first to −20 °C or below in all parts for at least 24 hours (fish sold
  with that treatment qualifies), or in a home freezer shown by a thermometer to hold −20 °C or below, for 7 days. A
  freezer at −18 °C does not qualify. Salt, sugar and acid do not kill the parasites; heat to 60 °C at the core for
  1 minute does.
- **Risk groups** (§9): pregnant people, young children, older people and people with weakened immunity are not served
  raw or lightly cooked fish, rare meat or soft eggs from raw eggs. Raw-yolk sauces and raw-egg desserts are made with
  pasteurised egg for every guest.
- **When to throw away:** a bulging lid or a jar that spurts, a broken seal, mould on a sugar or acid preserve beyond
  what its tested method allows, sous vide food kept beyond 2 days in the fridge, a cooling log that missed its limit.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** the learner can say, for each process, which figure controls it and where it comes from.

**Faults:**
- Starting the sous vide timer when the bag goes in.
- Chicken at 57 °C "because it's on the internet".
- Curing salt guessed by the spoon.
- Ceviche from fresh, unfrozen wild fish.

#### SAF-3.M1.X1 — Cooling a 4-litre batch

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-saf-3-m1-x1 -->
*framework, framework* — Cool a 4-litre batch of stock with a temperature log; compare two cooling methods.
<!-- END generated:exercise-saf-3-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** SA-TEMP→4, SA-HRPR→4

**Aim:** cool a large batch to the measurable standard, find out which method works in the learner's own kitchen, and
judge a high-risk recipe.

**Conditions:** framework support: the cooling-log template and the method framework. Four litres of stock, split
into two equal halves cooled by two methods (for example a shallow tray in the fridge against a stirred ice-water bath).
The extension: one preserving or oil recipe from a magazine or website, judged against the checklist of
`SAF-3.M1.L2`, and refused or corrected with the reason.

**Inputs:** bones or vegetables for 4 litres of stock (a chicken carcass and Dutch seasonal vegetables such as carrot,
onion, leek and celeriac), a thermometer, two shallow containers, ice, labels.

**Procedure:**
1. Make the stock and strain it at the boil.
2. Split it in two; start both logs at 57 °C.
3. Cool each by its method, reading every 30 minutes until 21 °C, then every hour until 5 °C.
4. Label and refrigerate; next morning, reheat one portion to 74 °C and log it.
5. Compare the two curves, and write which method the learner will use for batches.
6. Judge the recipe against the checklist: unsafe or not, why, and what a tested method would change.

**Evidence:** log, measure, quiz, written:
- **`SA-TEMP` level 4:** both halves cooled from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours and to 5 °C within 6 hours, logged; a
  portion reheated to 74 °C.
- **`SA-HRPR` level 4, in part:** the scenario items at 100 % (the module check) and one unsafe recipe identified and
  corrected or refused with the reason. The production plan with control points follows in `SAF-3.M2.X1`, and the logs
  of high-risk steps in `SLW-3.M5.X2`, `GRD-3.M3.X1` and `PRS-3.M2.X2`.

**Time:** 2 h active, plus the cooling and the next morning's check.

#### SAF-3.M2.L1 — Critical control points

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-3-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L1` · moves SA-HYG→5*

*Objectives:* [S] find the hazards and control points in a three-course menu; [K] the HACCP idea
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The HACCP idea**, in three questions per step of a menu:
  1. What can go wrong here (a hazard: bacteria growing or surviving, cross-contamination, an allergen, a physical
     hazard)?
  2. Is this the step that controls it (a control point), and what is the limit (a figure from §9, or a rule)?
  3. How is it checked and logged, and what happens if the limit is missed (the corrective action)?
- **The usual control points at home:**
  - receiving and storing (the fridge at 4 °C, raw protein on the bottom shelf);
  - preparing (raw protein last, separate boards, hands washed);
  - cooking (core temperatures, the poultry and roast tables);
  - cooling (the 2-hour and 6-hour limits);
  - holding and reheating (57 °C; 74 °C);
  - allergens (the guest's dish first, with clean tools);
  - high-risk processes (the tested method, the log).
- **Corrective actions** are decided in advance: cook longer, cool faster in smaller containers, discard.
- **A plan is only as good as its log.** A tick without a reading is not a log.

**Sessions:**
1. A plan for a three-course menu (60 min): take a menu with a cold starter, a poultry main and a custard dessert, list
   every step, mark the control points with their limits and corrective actions, and compare with the model plan.

**Cues:**
- Every control point has a figure or a rule, a check and an action.
- Not every step is a control point.

**Faults:**
- Twenty control points nobody can keep.
- A limit with no source.
- No corrective action.
- A plan written after the meal.

#### SAF-3.M2.L2 — Equipment hazards

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-saf-3-m2-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M4.L1` · moves SA-PHYS→5*

*Objectives:* [S] mandoline, pressure cooker, blowtorch, deep fryer, immersion circulator: risks and safe use
<!-- END generated:lesson-saf-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Mandoline:** always the guard or a cut-resistant glove; stop before the last few centimetres; the blade cleaned
  with a brush, never a cloth.
- **Pressure cooker:** as most manuals say, never more than two-thirds full (half for beans and grains, which foam); the valve checked
  before use; pressure released as the manual says, never by forcing the lid; the gasket replaced when it cracks.
- **Blowtorch:** used away from the body and from anything that burns, on a stable surface, the gas closed after use.
- **Deep fryer or frying pot:** as in `HEA-2.M4` (a third full, the thermometer, a lid ready, never water).
- **Immersion circulator:** clipped to a stable container on a heat-resistant surface, the water level within its marks,
  the cable kept dry, unplugged before it is lifted out.
- **A safety check before use:** the manual, the setup, the space around, and what to do if it fails.

**Sessions:**
1. Film the setups (45 min): set up two pieces of equipment the learner owns or will use in S3 (for example the
   mandoline and the pressure cooker), and film each setup and its use with the checklist read aloud.

**Cues:** the guard always on; the valve checked aloud; nothing burnable near the torch.

**Faults:**
- Slicing the last stub of a vegetable.
- Forcing a pressure-cooker lid.
- A torch pointed towards the hand.
- A circulator clipped to a wobbly pan.
