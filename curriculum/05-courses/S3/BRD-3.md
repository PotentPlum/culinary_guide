## Introduction

`BRD-2` baked lean and enriched yeast breads with commercial yeast, a first shaped loaf in a Dutch oven, and a bread
log. `BRD-3` adds what makes bread a lifelong craft: **time and culture**.
- **preferments** (*poolish*, *biga*) for flavour and a flexible schedule, and wetter doughs;
- **a sourdough culture:** started, kept, read and used for a first sourdough loaf;
- **shaping and scoring** with high-hydration dough, for tension and a controlled opening (an "ear");
- **whole grain and rye**, which behave differently from white wheat;
- **a schedule around a working week**, and diagnosing loaves from the crumb.

The method follows Hamelman's *Bread* [S13]: formulas in baker's percentages, dough temperatures logged, and each loaf
judged by its crumb. Flour from a Dutch mill (*molenmeel*, stone-ground wholemeal, rye) suits the course and is widely
sold; Dutch *roggebrood* is the rye tradition of the country.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-BRD` reaches level 3 with the sourdough loaf; the lean and enriched loaves of the row were baked in `BRD-2`.
- `JD-CUES` and `JD-TRBL` reach level 4 in reading the starter, the dough and the crumb.
- `KM-PLAN` reaches level 4 with a four-week baking schedule; `SC-STRC`, `RD-RATO`, `RD-TEST` and `SC-MICR` add further
  evidence.
- `MS-S3` re-tests a sourdough loaf if the learner chose pastry at `MS-S2` (`MS-S3.M1.L5`).

**Pacing.** About eight weeks: the starter takes about two weeks to become reliable, and the final module is four weeks
of baking for the household alongside other courses.

#### BRD-3.M1.L1 — *Poolish* and *biga*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 16 h · builds on `BRD-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-BRD→3*

*Objectives:* [K] flavour and scheduling; [S] a *poolish* bread
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A preferment** is part of the flour, water and a little yeast mixed hours before the final dough:
  - ***poolish*:** equal weights of flour and water (100 % hydration), a pinch of yeast, 12–16 hours at room
    temperature; extensible dough, a creamy, slightly nutty flavour;
  - ***biga*:** a stiff preferment (about 50–60 % hydration), more yeast-forward and stronger dough;
  - ready at its peak: domed, bubbly, just beginning to recede.
- **[K] Flavour:** the long, slow fermentation produces organic acids and aromatic compounds that a straight dough has
  no time to make; the bread also keeps longer.
- **[K] Scheduling:** the preferment made in the evening, the final dough mixed the next morning; the yeast in the final
  dough is reduced; the timing adjusts with the room's temperature.
- **Baker's percentages** (`BRD-2`): the preferment's flour and water counted in the totals, so the overall hydration is
  known.

**Sessions:**
1. The *poolish* (15 min in the evening): mixed, covered, left at room temperature; its peak photographed.
2. The bread (2 h active over the day): the final dough mixed from the *poolish*, fermented, shaped and baked; a straight
   dough of the same formula baked alongside for comparison.

**Cues:**
- The *poolish* domed and bubbly at its peak.
- The *poolish* loaf more aromatic, with a more open crumb.

**Faults:**
- A collapsed, over-fermented *poolish* that smells of alcohol.
- Forgetting to count its flour and water.
- The same yeast in the final dough as a straight dough.

#### BRD-3.M1.L2 — Hydration

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `BAK-2.M1.L1` · moves TS-BRD→3, RD-RATO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] handle 70–80 % hydration doughs; [K] hydration by flour type
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Hydration** is water as a percentage of flour weight. More water gives a more open, irregular crumb and a thinner,
  crisper crust, but a stickier dough.
- **[K] By flour type:** stronger white flour takes more water; wholemeal and rye absorb much more (bran and pentosans);
  a weak or old flour takes less. The same hydration feels different from one flour to the next.
- **Handling wet dough (70–80 %):**
  - wet hands and a bench scraper instead of extra flour;
  - stretch-and-folds or coil folds in the bowl instead of kneading;
  - an autolyse (flour and water rested before salt and yeast) for extensibility;
  - adding some of the water late (*bassinage*) once the gluten has formed.
- **Dough temperature:** aimed at about 24–26 °C by the water's temperature, measured and logged, because it sets the
  pace of the fermentation.

**Sessions:**
1. Two hydrations (75 min): the same formula at 68 and 76 % hydration, mixed with folds, logged and baked.
2. A high-hydration loaf (75 min): a 78 % dough handled with coil folds and an autolyse; baked; crumb photographed.

**Cues:**
- A smooth, extensible dough after the folds.
- The dough temperature in range.
- A more open crumb at the higher hydration.

**Faults:**
- Flouring the bench heavily.
- Kneading a wet dough on the bench.
- Water at any temperature.

#### BRD-3.M2.L1 — Starting and keeping a starter

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 wk · builds on `BRD-2.M1.L1` · moves SC-MICR→3*

*Objectives:* [S] feed ratios, temperature, storage; [K] wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] What lives in a starter:** wild yeasts (which raise the bread) and lactic acid bacteria (which sour it and make
  flavour), in a stable community. Their acid lowers the pH, which keeps unwanted organisms out (`SCI-2.M6`).
- **Starting one:** wholemeal or rye flour and water mixed and fed daily; after a few days of erratic activity it settles
  into a predictable rise and fall; usually reliable within about two weeks.
- **Feeding ratios:** starter, flour and water by weight (for example 1:1:1, or 1:5:5 for a slower rise); a stiffer or
  colder starter is more sour and slower.
- **Temperature:** warm (24–26 °C) for activity; the fridge between bakes, fed once a week.
- **Storage and hygiene:** a clean jar with a loose lid, the level marked with a band; discard used in pancakes or
  crackers; a pink or orange tint, or fuzzy mould, means it is thrown away and started again.

**Sessions:**
1. Start it (20 min, then daily for two weeks): the starter mixed and fed; the log of `BRD-3.M2.X2` begun.

**Cues:**
- A predictable doubling within 4–8 hours of feeding.
- A pleasant sour, yoghurty smell.

**Faults:**
- Giving up on day 3.
- A sealed jar.
- Chlorinated or very cold water slowing it.

#### BRD-3.M2.L2 — Reading the starter

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m2-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · builds on `BRD-3.M2.L1` · moves JD-CUES→4*

*Objectives:* [J] peak, smell, bubbles, float test; when it's ready
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] The cues of a ready starter:**
  - at or just after its peak: domed, risen to about double, full of bubbles through the jar;
  - a pleasant sour, fruity smell (not of nail varnish, which means hungry);
  - a spoonful floats in water (a rough guide, not always reliable for wholemeal or rye starters);
  - a web of gluten when pulled.
- **Hungry or over-ripe:** collapsed, liquid (*hooch*) on top, an acetone smell: fed again before use.
- **Cue, then check:** the learner predicts "ready" from the look and smell, then checks with the band (rise) and the float
  test, and logs both.

**Sessions:**
1. Read it (60 min over a day): the starter photographed and smelled every two hours after a feed; the peak predicted,
   then checked; logged.

**Cues:** predictions within an hour of the observed peak.

**Faults:**
- Using it at the bottom of its curve.
- Trusting only the float test.

#### BRD-3.M2.L3 — A first sourdough loaf

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m2-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 1 d · builds on `BRD-3.M2.L2` · moves TS-BRD→3*

*Objectives:* [S] levain, mix, bulk, shape, retard, bake
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The stages:**
  1. **Levain:** some starter fed and left to peak.
  2. **Mix:** flour and water (autolyse), then the levain and salt.
  3. **Bulk fermentation:** with folds, until the dough has grown by roughly half, is domed and jiggly, with bubbles at the
     sides; the time depends on dough temperature (often 4–6 hours at 24–26 °C).
  4. **Pre-shape and shape** with tension.
  5. **Retard:** the shaped loaf in a basket in the fridge overnight (flavour, schedule, easier scoring).
  6. **Bake** cold from the fridge in a preheated Dutch oven, lid on, then off.
- **Judging bulk by the dough, not the clock:** volume, a domed surface, bubbles, a jiggle.
- **Safety:** the Dutch oven at about 250 °C; dry oven gloves on both hands, the loaf lowered in on baking paper
  (`BRD-2.M3`).

**Sessions:**
1. Mix and bulk (90 min active over a day): levain, mix, folds, bulk judged by the dough; shaped and retarded.
2. Bake (60 min the next morning): scored and baked; the crumb photographed after cooling.

**Cues:**
- A domed, jiggly dough at the end of bulk.
- A tall loaf with an open, even crumb.

**Faults:**
- Ending bulk by the clock.
- Under-fermented: a tight, dense crumb with a few large holes.
- Slicing it hot.

#### BRD-3.M3.L1 — Advanced shaping

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 3 practical sessions · builds on `BRD-2.M2.L3` · moves TS-BRD→3*

*Objectives:* [S] tension with high-hydration dough; *bâtard* and *boule*
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Tension:** a tight skin on the surface of the loaf holds its shape, directs the oven spring upwards and helps the
  scoring open.
- **Pre-shape and bench rest:** the dough divided and loosely rounded, rested 20–30 minutes so the gluten relaxes.
- **The *boule*:** the dough turned seam up, folded in from the edges, flipped and dragged towards the body on an
  unfloured patch of bench to tighten the surface.
- **The *bâtard*:** folded into a letter, rolled from the top with the thumbs pressing in, the seam sealed; even, with
  tapered or blunt ends.
- **High hydration:** a lightly floured surface, a bench scraper, quick confident movements; a well-floured basket (rice
  flour sticks least).

**Sessions:**
1. *Boules* (45 min): three pre-shaped and shaped; the tension checked by a poke and photographed.
2. *Bâtards* (45 min): three shaped; filmed for AI review.
3. Wet dough (45 min): a 78 % dough shaped into both forms; compared.

**Cues:**
- A taut, smooth surface.
- The loaf holds its shape when turned out.
- A sealed seam.

**Faults:**
- Tearing the surface by over-tightening.
- Too much flour, so the dough slides instead of tightening.
- No bench rest.

#### BRD-3.M3.L2 — Scoring

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 3 practical sessions · builds on `BRD-2.M3.L2` · moves TS-BRD→3*

*Objectives:* [S] blade angle and depth; patterns; [K] why scoring directs the spring
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Why scoring directs the spring:** the loaf expands fast in the first minutes of the bake; a cut gives it a planned
  place to open, instead of bursting at a weak point.
- **The ear:** a single long cut on a *bâtard*, the blade held at a shallow angle (about 30° to the surface) and about
  5–10 mm deep, in one confident stroke; the flap lifts and bakes into a crisp ridge.
- **Patterns:** a cross on a *boule*; parallel cuts; decorative shallow cuts plus one deep expansion cut.
- **Conditions:** a cold, retarded loaf is easier to score; steam (the Dutch oven's lid) keeps the surface soft so the
  cut can open.

**Sessions:**
1. Single cut (30 min): the ear cut on three *bâtards* at different angles; the openings compared.
2. Patterns (30 min): three *boules* scored with different patterns.
3. Combined (30 min): the best shape and cut on one bake, photographed.

**Cues:**
- A clean cut in one stroke.
- An even, raised ear.
- The bloom where it was planned.

**Faults:**
- Sawing the blade.
- Cutting straight down (no ear).
- Too shallow on a warm, slack loaf.

#### BRD-3.M4.L1 — Whole wheat

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `SCI-2.M3.L2` · moves SC-STRC→4*

*Objectives:* [K] bran and water absorption; [S] a whole wheat loaf
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Bran and water:** the bran and germ absorb much more water, and the bran's sharp edges cut gluten strands; a
  wholemeal dough needs more water, a longer autolyse (or a soaker) and gentle handling.
- **Flavour:** nutty, fuller; it pairs well with a preferment or sourdough; the germ's oils make the flour go stale and
  rancid faster, so it is bought fresh and kept cool.
- **Expectations:** a denser, less open crumb than white bread; good wholemeal bread is moist and well risen, not heavy.
- **Blends:** a share of wholemeal (20–50 %) in a white loaf as a step up.

**Sessions:**
1. A wholemeal loaf (75 min active, 1 day in total): 100 % wholemeal with a long autolyse at a higher hydration; crumb
   photographed.

**Cues:**
- A moist, risen crumb.
- Not crumbly, not gummy.

**Faults:**
- White-bread hydration for wholemeal, giving a dry, dense loaf.
- Over-kneading.
- Rancid flour from the back of the cupboard.

#### BRD-3.M4.L2 — Rye

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m4-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 d · builds on `BRD-3.M2.L3` · moves TS-BRD→3*

*Objectives:* [K] pentosans and acidity; [S] a rye bread with different handling
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Pentosans and acidity:** rye has little gluten-forming protein; its structure comes from pentosans (gums) that
  hold water and make a sticky paste. Rye flour is rich in enzymes that break down starch, so without acid the crumb turns
  gummy; a sourdough's acidity tames them. This is why rye breads are traditionally sourdough breads.
- **Handling:** a paste rather than a dough; mixed, not kneaded; shaped with wet hands or baked in a tin; scored little or
  not at all.
- **Baking and resting:** baked long and often lower; rested for a day before slicing so the crumb sets.
- **Traditions:** Dutch *roggebrood* (dark, dense, long-baked), German and Scandinavian ryes with seeds; a *pain de
  seigle*-style mixed loaf as an easier start.

**Sessions:**
1. A rye loaf (75 min active, 2 days in total): a sourdough rye in a tin, baked, rested a day, sliced and photographed.

**Cues:**
- A moist but set crumb after a day.
- No gummy streak at the base.

**Faults:**
- Kneading rye like wheat.
- Slicing it warm.
- A yeasted rye without acid, gummy.

#### BRD-3.M5.L1 — Sourdough around a working week

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 6 wk · builds on `BRD-2.M5.L1` · moves KM-PLAN→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a weekly schedule with retarding; [J] adjust to the season's temperature
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The fridge as a tool:** the starter kept in the fridge and fed the evening before; the shaped loaf retarded overnight
  or up to a day or two, so bake day can move.
- **A weekday schedule** (one example): feed the levain in the morning; mix after work; bulk in the evening; shape before
  bed; retard overnight; bake before work or the next evening.
- **[J] Adjusting to the season:** in winter, warmer water, a warm spot (an oven with the light on) and longer bulk; in
  summer, cooler water, less levain and shorter bulk; the dough temperature logged and the schedule adjusted from it.
- **A control point on time:** if the dough is ready before the cook is, the fridge pauses it; if it isn't, the bake
  moves.

**Sessions:**
1. Plan (30 min): a four-week schedule written around the learner's actual week.
2. First week (2 h active over the week): baked to the schedule; deviations logged.
3. Adjust (30 min): the schedule changed from the first week's log.

**Cues:**
- A schedule that fits the real week.
- Changes made from the log, not guessed.

**Faults:**
- A schedule that needs someone at home at noon.
- Ignoring the room temperature.
- Baking an over-proofed loaf rather than adjusting.

#### BRD-3.M5.L2 — Diagnosing loaves

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-brd-3-m5-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `BRD-3.M2.L3` · moves JD-TRBL→4, RD-TEST→3*

*Objectives:* [J] under- and over-proofing, dense crumb, flat loaf: cause and fix; [S] test one variable
<!-- END generated:lesson-brd-3-m5-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] Reading the crumb and the shape:**
  - **under-proofed:** dense, tight crumb with a few large irregular holes under the crust; a burst side; a pale, gummy
    feel;
  - **over-proofed:** flat, spreading loaf; a weak ear; an even, large-holed crumb that collapsed; sour;
  - **dense crumb:** too little fermentation, too little water, a weak starter or too much whole grain without
    adjustment;
  - **flat loaf:** weak shaping, over-proofing, too slack a dough, or a cold oven.
- **From cause to fix:** one cause chosen from the evidence (log, photos), one variable changed, the next loaf compared.
- **[S] Testing one variable:** for example bulk extended by an hour, or the retard shortened, with a blind tasting or a
  crumb comparison.

**Sessions:**
1. Diagnose and test (90 min over a bake): two loaves of the same dough with one variable changed; crumbs compared and
   tasted blind.

**Cues:** a cause named from the evidence, and a fix that works on the next loaf.

**Faults:**
- Changing everything at once.
- Blaming the flour every time.
- No photos to compare.
