## Introduction

`VEG-1` taught the vegetable families, cuts and basic methods; S2 used vegetables as sides, soups and braises. `VEG-3`
puts the vegetable at the centre of the plate. It was added after the Phase 1 review (§13.2 Q7), because a home cook
who follows the seasons cooks vegetables more than anything else, and because a vegetable-led main is where technique,
flavour architecture and composition meet.

The course has five parts:
- **thinking vegetable-first:** the anatomy of a vegetable main, and one vegetable followed through its whole season,
  used whole (leaves, stems, peels and tops);
- **high heat:** charring and blistering without bitterness, whole-roasting and salt-baking;
- **gentle heat:** glazing, confit and basting; silky purées and vegetable emulsions;
- **satisfying plant-based mains:** umami, fat, texture and protein without meat; vegetable steaks, gratins and layered
  bakes;
- **the vegetable lab:** one vegetable tested five ways, and an original vegetable-led main.

The produce is Dutch and in season, which is the point of the course: asparagus, peas and young carrots in spring;
courgettes, beans, tomatoes, sweetcorn and peppers in summer; pumpkin, celeriac, beetroot, cauliflower, cabbages and
leeks in autumn; kale, savoy, chicory, salsify (*schorseneren*), Jerusalem artichoke (*aardpeer*), parsnip and stored
roots in winter. Plant proteins come from legumes (`LEG-2`), eggs and dairy, and where the learner chooses, tofu or
tempeh.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-VEG`, `IN-VEG`, `IN-SEL` and `SC-BRWN` reach level 4; `RD-TEST`, `FL-ARCH`, `CR-COMP` and `JD-CHSE` level 3.
- `CR-ORIG` starts at level 1 with the learner's own dish.
- `MS-S3` asks for a vegetable-led main in its week plan (`MS-S3.M1.L1`) and vegetable garnishes in its two-course
  evening (`MS-S3.M1.L3`).

**Pacing.** About four weeks of active time; the seasonal project of `VEG-3.M1.X1` runs alongside for about eight weeks,
so the course is best started at the beginning of a vegetable's season.

#### VEG-3.M1.L1 — Thinking vegetable-first

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FLV-3.M5.L1` · moves CR-COMP→3, IN-VEG→4*

*Objectives:* [K] the anatomy of a vegetable-led dish: centrepiece, sauce, contrast, richness
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The anatomy of a vegetable-led main:**
  - **a centrepiece:** a vegetable cooked to show its best (a whole-roasted celeriac, a charred cabbage wedge, a
    vegetable steak);
  - **a sauce** that brings richness or depth (a brown butter, a vegetable emulsion, a nut-free *romesco*, a yoghurt
    sauce);
  - **a contrast:** crunch, acid, bitterness or freshness;
  - **richness and satiety:** fat, a starch or legume, cheese or eggs, umami (`FLV-3.M3`).
- **Why vegetable-led dishes fail:** they feel like a side without its main, because they lack richness, umami, protein
  or a texture contrast, or because the vegetable was cooked timidly.
- **Composition:** the component table of `FLV-3.M4`, with the vegetable as the main.

**Sessions:**
1. Rebuild a side into a main (60 min): a familiar vegetable side (for example roasted carrots) rebuilt as a main with a
   sauce, a contrast and richness; the component table written.

**Cues:** the household calls it a main, not a side.

**Faults:**
- A plate of steamed vegetables.
- No fat or umami.
- Six vegetables competing, none as the centre.

#### VEG-3.M1.L2 — One vegetable, whole and through its season

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m1-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 8 wk · builds on `VEG-1.M2.L1` · moves IN-SEL→4, KM-WAST→4*

*Objectives:* [S] leaves, stems, peels and tops; [J] how a vegetable changes from early to late season
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The whole vegetable:**
  - leaves: beetroot and kohlrabi leaves, cauliflower leaves, carrot tops (in a pesto or a soup);
  - stems: broccoli and chard stems peeled and sliced;
  - peels: roasted potato peels as crisps, celeriac peel in stock;
  - tops and trimmings: leek greens in stock, pumpkin seeds toasted.
- **[J] How a vegetable changes through its season:**
  - **early:** small, tender, sweet, thin-skinned; little cooking, raw or barely cooked (young carrots with tops, new
    potatoes);
  - **peak:** full flavour, cheapest, most abundant; any method;
  - **late and stored:** larger, starchier or more fibrous, thicker skin; long cooking, roasting, purées, soups (stored
    carrots, late celeriac).
- **Buying at the market:** what is best this week (look, smell, firmness, weight for size), its price, and its
  purpose; the week planned from it, with a fallback (`IN-SEL`).

**Sessions:**
1. Whole use (60 min): one vegetable bought whole with its leaves and stems; every part used in a meal; the waste weighed.

**Cues:**
- Nothing edible in the bin.
- The method chosen for the stage of the season.

**Faults:**
- Carrot tops in the bin.
- Old stored carrots served raw and woody.
- Early vegetables overcooked.

#### VEG-3.M2.L1 — Charring and blistering

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-2.M3.L1` · moves SC-BRWN→4, TS-VEG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] cabbage, leeks, peppers, onions over high heat; [K] where char stops being pleasant
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **High, dry heat** browns the surface by the Maillard reaction and caramelisation (`SC-BRWN`), and at the extreme
  chars it: a cast-iron pan, a grill, the oven's top heat or a gas flame.
- **What chars well:** cabbage wedges, leeks, spring onions, peppers, chicory, broccoli, sweetcorn, onion halves cut side
  down; dense vegetables are part-cooked first.
- **[K] Where char stops being pleasant:** a thin, blistered char on part of the surface is smoky and sweet; a thick
  black crust over the whole surface is bitter and acrid. Charred skins that are peeled (peppers, aubergine) can go
  black; eaten surfaces should be dark brown with patches of black at most.
- **Steering browning:** dry surface, the right fat, salt, the pan not crowded, the cut face down and left alone; sugar
  (a glaze) browns faster, acid slows it; more browning for a sweet vegetable, less for a delicate one.

**Sessions:**
1. Cabbage and leeks (60 min): a pointed-cabbage wedge and leeks charred in cast iron; one less, one more; tasted.
2. Peppers and onions (60 min): blistered peppers peeled; onion halves charred cut side down and then softened in the
   oven.

**Cues:**
- Deep brown with blistered patches.
- A sweet, smoky taste with no acrid bitterness.

**Faults:**
- A wet vegetable that steams.
- Moving it constantly.
- A fully black, bitter surface.

#### VEG-3.M2.L2 — Whole-roasting and salt-baking

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `VEG-1.M4.L1` · moves TS-VEG→4, CK-METH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] whole celeriac, beetroot, cauliflower, cabbage; salt crust; [J] judge doneness inside
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Whole-roasting:** a whole celeriac, cauliflower, beetroot, pumpkin or small cabbage roasted slowly until tender to
  the centre, basted with butter or oil and aromatics; the outside caramelised, the inside soft and concentrated.
- **Salt-baking:** a whole vegetable (celeriac, beetroot, potatoes) enclosed in a crust of salt and egg white or salt
  and flour, baked; the crust seals in moisture and seasons gently; cracked open at the table.
- **[J] Judging doneness inside:** a skewer or a thin knife slides through to the centre with no resistance; the core
  temperature of a dense root around 95 °C is a guide; a large celeriac can take two hours or more.
- **Finishing:** sliced into wedges or steaks, browned in butter, sauced.

**Sessions:**
1. A whole-roasted vegetable (75 min active, 2 h in total): a whole celeriac or cauliflower roasted and basted.
2. Salt-baked beetroot (45 min active, 1.5 h in total): beetroot in a salt crust; cracked and served.

**Cues:**
- Skewer-soft to the centre.
- Caramelised outside.
- Seasoned but not salty.

**Faults:**
- Raw in the centre.
- An oven too hot, burnt outside.
- A salt crust that cracks open during baking.

#### VEG-3.M3.L1 — Glazing, confit and basting

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-1.M4.L1` · moves TS-VEG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] glaze to a shine; confit in oil or butter; baste whole vegetables
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Glazing** (*glacer*): vegetables (carrots, turnips, small onions, radishes) cooked in a little water or stock with
  butter, salt and a little sugar, partly covered, until tender just as the liquid reduces to a syrupy glaze that coats
  them. *À blanc* (no colour) or *à brun* (taken on to colour).
- **Confit:** vegetables cooked slowly submerged in oil or butter at a low heat until meltingly soft (fennel, shallots,
  tomatoes, garlic, potatoes); kept in the fridge and used within 2 days (the leftovers row), because vegetables in oil
  are a botulism risk (§9).
- **Butter-basting:** a whole or halved vegetable (a cabbage wedge, a celeriac steak, a carrot) browned in a pan, then
  butter and aromatics added and spooned over continuously as the butter foams.
- **Timing:** the cut size decides the time; the liquid is measured so that it is gone when the vegetable is tender.

**Sessions:**
1. Glazed vegetables (45 min): carrots or turnips glazed *à blanc* and *à brun*.
2. Confit and basting (60 min): fennel or shallots in olive oil confit; a celeriac steak butter-basted.

**Cues:**
- A shiny glaze that coats, no liquid left in the pan.
- Tender to the knife.
- Confit soft, not browned.

**Faults:**
- Liquid gone before the vegetables are tender (burnt).
- Tender vegetables in a pool of liquid.
- Confit on the shelf.

#### VEG-3.M3.L2 — Purées and vegetable emulsions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STK-2.M4.L1` · moves FL-TEXT→3, TS-SAU→4*

*Objectives:* [S] silky purées (blend, pass, mount); vegetable-based sauces
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A silky purée:**
  1. Cook the vegetable fully tender (in milk, stock or water, or roasted for depth).
  2. Drain well; blend hot in a jug blender with a little of the liquid (vent the lid, `STK-2.M4`).
  3. Mount with cold butter or cream while blending.
  4. Pass through a fine sieve.
  5. Season.

  Starchy vegetables (potatoes) go gluey in a blender and are pressed instead; non-starchy ones (celeriac, cauliflower,
  carrot, parsnip, pumpkin) blend silky.
- **Vegetable emulsions and sauces:** a vegetable juice or purée emulsified with butter or oil (a carrot-butter sauce, a
  beurre blanc made with vegetable juice, a red-pepper emulsion), or a vegetable reduction as a sauce base.
- **Consistency:** a purée holds a soft peak and spreads slowly; a sauce reaches *nappe*.
- **Holding:** warm in a covered pan or a bain-marie at 57 °C or above if held (§9); a skin is prevented with butter or a
  lid.

**Sessions:**
1. Two purées (60 min): celeriac and carrot, blended and passed; one mounted with butter, one with cream; compared.
2. A vegetable sauce (45 min): a carrot or red-pepper emulsion with butter, at *nappe*.

**Cues:**
- Smooth on the tongue.
- Glossy, holding a soft peak.
- The sauce at *nappe*, not splitting.

**Faults:**
- Gluey potato purée from the blender.
- Not passing.
- A watery purée from undrained vegetables.

### VEG-3.M4 — Satisfying plant-based dishes

*Scope:* umami, fat, texture and protein without meat; gratins and vegetable steaks.

#### VEG-3.M4.L1 — Building a satisfying plant dish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FLV-3.M3.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→3, IN-VEG→4*

*Objectives:* [K] umami, fat, texture and protein in plant cooking; [S] layer them in one dish
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] What makes a meal satisfying, and the plant sources:**
  - **umami:** mushrooms (fresh and dried), tomato paste, miso, soy, aged cheese, seaweed, fermented vegetables
    (`FLV-3.M3`);
  - **fat:** butter, oils, cheese, eggs, seeds and tahini;
  - **texture:** something to chew (a seared vegetable steak, mushrooms, lentils, grains), something crisp;
  - **protein:** legumes, eggs, dairy, tofu and tempeh, grains; combined over the day rather than in one bite.
- **Layering them in one dish:** a base of browned aromatics and umami, a middle of the vegetable and legume, a top of
  acid, herbs and crunch (`FLV-3.M5`).
- **Brown, don't boil:** browning (Maillard) gives savoury depth that boiled vegetables lack.

**Sessions:**
1. A layered dish (75 min): a mushroom and lentil ragù or a bean and cabbage bake with three umami sources, fat, a chewy
   element and a crisp top, written as a flavour plan.

**Cues:** "I didn't miss the meat" on the household card.

**Faults:**
- Boiled vegetables with no browning.
- No fat.
- One soft texture throughout.

#### VEG-3.M4.L2 — Vegetable steaks, gratins and layered dishes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m4-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-3.M2.L2` · moves TS-VEG→4, JD-CHSE→3*

*Objectives:* [S] thick-cut vegetable "steaks", gratins and layered bakes; [J] choose method by vegetable
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Vegetable steaks:** thick slices (3–4 cm) of celeriac, cauliflower, cabbage or pumpkin, part-cooked if dense (steamed
  or roasted), then seared and butter-basted; served with a sauce like a steak.
- **Gratins:** thin, even slices (a mandoline with its guard, `SAF-3.M2.L2`) layered with cream, stock or a *béchamel*
  and cheese, baked until tender and browned: potato, celeriac, pumpkin, leek, fennel.
- **Layered bakes:** vegetable lasagne-style bakes, a *tian*, a shepherd's-pie-style bake with a lentil base.
- **[J] Choosing the method by vegetable:** density (dense roots part-cooked first), water content (watery vegetables like
  courgette salted and drained or roasted first), and flavour (sweet vegetables brown more).

**Sessions:**
1. A vegetable steak (60 min): a celeriac or cauliflower steak, part-cooked, seared and basted, with a sauce.
2. A gratin (75 min): a celeriac and potato gratin, sliced on the mandoline, baked until tender and browned.

**Cues:**
- The steak browned and tender to the centre.
- The gratin tender, creamy, browned on top, not watery.

**Faults:**
- A raw-centred steak.
- Uneven slices.
- A watery gratin from courgettes not drained.

#### VEG-3.M5.L1 — One vegetable, five ways

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-veg-3-m5-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M1.L1` · moves RD-TEST→3*

*Objectives:* [S] a structured five-method test with blind tasters; [J] conclude and record
<!-- END generated:lesson-veg-3-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A structured test** (`SCI-2.M1`): one vegetable, five methods (for example raw, steamed, roasted, charred and
  confit, or salt-baked); everything else held constant (variety, batch, cut, salt); coded samples tasted blind by at
  least two tasters on a form with the same questions.
- **What to record:** time, yield (weight before and after), texture, flavour and preference; photos of each.
- **[J] Concluding:** which method suits which purpose (a side, a main, a purée), what the data support, and what the
  next question is (a follow-up test of one variable on the best method).

**Sessions:**
1. The test (2 h): the five methods cooked side by side, coded and tasted blind; the results tabled.

**Cues:** a plan another person could repeat; conclusions no bigger than the data.

**Faults:**
- Different cuts per method.
- Unblinded tasting.
- Concluding "roasted is best" for every purpose.

#### VEG-3.M5.X1 — The five-method test
