## Introduction

A cook who feeds a household and guests needs to know what a balanced meal is, how to adapt a dish for someone who eats
differently, and how to cook for someone with an allergy without putting them at risk. `NUT-3` is applied: it doesn't
teach dietetics, it teaches cooking decisions.
- **applied nutrition:** what protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre do in a dish and for satiety; a balanced plate and a
  balanced week; what cooking methods do to nutrients;
- **adapting to diets:** vegetarian and vegan (replacing the functions of meat, egg and dairy), gluten-free,
  lactose-free and lower-salt cooking, without losing quality;
- **allergens:** the 14 groups of EU Regulation 1169/2011 [S23] in depth, their hidden sources, and a cross-contact plan
  from shopping to plate for an allergic guest.

The reference for a balanced diet in the Netherlands is the Voedingscentrum's *Schijf van Vijf*; Phase 6 cites its
current page. The course gives no medical or dietary advice for individuals: a guest's doctor or dietitian decides what
they may eat, and the cook follows it.

**In the spiral.**
- `SA-ALLG` reaches level 4 with a menu for a guest with an allergen; `RD-ADPT` level 3; `CR-COMP` level 2.
- `MS-S3` re-tests the 14 allergen groups and cross-contact in its safety quiz (`MS-S3.M1.L6`, a gate).
- `CR-MENU` level 4 (S4) asks for a menu with a dietary need, which builds on this course.

**Pacing.** About three weeks, after `VEG-3` and `KMG-3`; the dishes are household meals and one guest dinner.

#### NUT-3.M1.L1 — Macronutrients in cooking

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M2.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre: their roles in satiety and in cooking
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Protein:** satisfies for longest; in cooking it sets, binds, browns and holds structure (eggs, meat, legumes,
  dairy) (`SCI-2.M2`).
- **[K] Carbohydrate:** starches give energy, body and texture (thickening, crisping, the base of a meal); sugars sweeten,
  brown and preserve.
- **[K] Fat:** carries flavour and aroma (fat-soluble compounds), gives richness and a satisfying mouthfeel, browns and
  crisps; energy-dense, so the amount matters.
- **[K] Fibre:** from vegetables, legumes, whole grains; adds bulk and satiety, slows digestion; changes texture
  (wholemeal dough, `BRD-3.M4`).
- **Satiety in a dish:** protein, fibre and some fat together make a meal satisfying; a plate of only starch or only
  vegetables leaves people hungry soon.
- **A substitution from a guide:** for example wholemeal pasta for white, or yoghurt for some of the cream, with the
  effect on taste and texture noted.

**Sessions:**
1. One dish, three ways (60 min): a pasta dish made with white pasta and a cream sauce, with wholemeal pasta, and with
   half the cream replaced by yoghurt and more vegetables; satiety and taste noted on the form.

**Cues:** the learner can say what each macronutrient contributes to a dish, in cooking and on the plate.

**Faults:**
- Removing all fat and losing the flavour.
- A "healthy" plate that leaves everyone hungry.

#### NUT-3.M1.L2 — The balanced plate

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M1.L1` · moves CR-COMP→2*

*Objectives:* [K] balanced meals; how cooking methods affect nutrients; [S] check a week's menu
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] A balanced meal:** about half the plate vegetables (and fruit across the day), a quarter whole grains or
  potatoes, a quarter protein (legumes, fish, eggs, dairy, poultry, meat), with a modest amount of healthy fats; water as
  the drink. The *Schijf van Vijf* frames a balanced week in the same way.
- **A balanced week:** legumes and fish more often, red and processed meat less often, vegetables at every meal, variety
  across the week (`KMG-3.M1`).
- **[K] How cooking methods affect nutrients:**
  - water-soluble vitamins (C, several B vitamins) leach into cooking water: steaming or using the cooking water in a
    soup keeps more;
  - long cooking and keeping warm destroy more vitamin C;
  - fat-soluble carotenoids (carrots, pumpkin, tomatoes) are absorbed better with a little fat;
  - deep-frying adds fat; grilling and roasting don't need much.
- **Checking a menu:** the week's plan against the plate model and the week guidelines; one or two changes, not a
  rebuild.

**Sessions:**
1. Check a week (60 min): the last week's meals checked against the plate model; one meal rebuilt and plated.

**Cues:** a menu check that names specific, small changes.

**Faults:**
- Boiling vegetables long and pouring the water away.
- Counting ketchup as a vegetable.
- Rebuilding the whole week instead of adjusting it.

#### NUT-3.M2.L1 — Vegetarian and vegan

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-3.M4.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] replace meat, egg and dairy functions (protein, umami, binding, richness)
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Replacing functions, not ingredients:** ask what the meat, egg or dairy does in the dish, then replace that function:
  - **protein and substance:** legumes, tofu, tempeh, eggs and cheese (vegetarian), seitan (wheat gluten, not for
    gluten-free guests);
  - **umami and depth:** mushrooms, miso, soy, tomato paste, seaweed, browned onions (`FLV-3.M3`);
  - **binding** (egg): a flax or chia "egg", aquafaba (chickpea water), starch, mashed legumes;
  - **richness** (butter, cream): olive oil, plant creams, tahini, coconut milk, a purée of white beans or cauliflower;
  - **stock:** a vegetable stock with mushrooms and kombu (`STK-2`).
- **Hidden animal products:** gelatine (`PST-3.M4`), fish sauce and anchovy, meat stock, rennet in some cheeses, lard in
  pastry, honey (for vegans); labels checked.
- **Vegan baking:** egg and butter functions replaced by tested formulas; a straight swap often fails.

**Sessions:**
1. A vegetarian version (60 min): a meat dish adapted to vegetarian with its functions replaced; compared.
2. A vegan version (75 min): the same dish adapted further to vegan; compared by the household.

**Cues:** the adapted dish has the depth, richness and texture of the original.

**Faults:**
- Removing the meat and adding nothing.
- Meat stock in a "vegetarian" soup.
- Gelatine in a vegan dessert.

#### NUT-3.M2.L2 — Gluten-free, lactose-free and lower salt

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `NUT-3.M2.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] adapt thickening, baking and seasoning; [J] what to tell guests
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Gluten-free:**
  - thickening with cornflour, potato or rice starch instead of a flour *roux*;
  - baking with gluten-free flour blends by a tested formula (structure from starches, eggs and gums);
  - hidden gluten: soy sauce (tamari is usually gluten-free; check the label), stock cubes, breadcrumbs, beer, malt,
    spice blends;
  - oats only if labelled gluten-free;
  - for coeliac guests, gluten is also an allergen-style cross-contact risk (`NUT-3.M3`).
- **Lactose-free:** lactose-free milk and cream behave like normal ones in cooking; aged hard cheeses (old Gouda,
  Parmesan) are naturally very low in lactose; butter contains little. A milk *allergy* is different: every milk protein
  is excluded (`NUT-3.M3`).
- **Lower salt:**
  - reduce gradually (taste adapts within weeks);
  - build flavour with acid, herbs, spices, umami and browning;
  - salt at the end on the surface, where it is tasted most;
  - count the salt in stock cubes, soy, cheese, cured meats.
- **[J] What to tell guests:** what the dish contains and what was replaced, clearly, without promising more than was
  done ("made without gluten-containing ingredients, in a kitchen that also uses flour").

**Sessions:**
1. Gluten-free and lactose-free (75 min): a sauce thickened without flour and a gratin made lactose-free; compared.
2. Lower salt (45 min): a soup made with a third less salt and built up with acid, herbs and umami; tasted blind against
   the original.

**Cues:**
- A gluten-free sauce with the same body.
- A lower-salt soup the tasters don't call flat.

**Faults:**
- Soy sauce in a gluten-free dish.
- Telling a coeliac guest "it's gluten-free" when it was cooked next to flour.
- Simply leaving the salt out.

#### NUT-3.M3.L1 — The 14 allergens in depth

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M4.L1` · moves SA-ALLG→4*

*Objectives:* [K] hidden sources (sauces, stocks, spice mixes, processed foods); labelling for guests
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The 14 groups** (EU Regulation 1169/2011, Annex II [S23], `SAF-1.M4`): cereals containing gluten, crustaceans,
  eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk, tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphites (above a threshold), lupin and
  molluscs.
- **[K] Hidden sources:**
  - **sauces and condiments:** soy sauce (soy, wheat), Worcestershire sauce (fish), mayonnaise (egg, mustard), pesto
    (nuts, milk), *romesco* (nuts);
  - **stocks and bouillon:** celery, milk, gluten;
  - **spice mixes:** mustard, celery, gluten as a carrier;
  - **processed foods:** milk in sausages and bread, egg in pasta, sesame in breads, lupin flour in some baked goods,
    sulphites in dried fruit and wine.
- **Labels:** the 14 groups are emphasised in the ingredients list (for example in bold); "may contain" warnings are
  voluntary and count for a severe allergy; a recipe changed by the maker needs a new check.
- **Labelling for guests:** a menu card or a word at the table that names the allergens in each dish.

**Sessions:**
1. A label hunt (60 min): ten pantry products read for the 14 groups and hidden sources; a dish's ingredient labels
   photographed and its allergens listed.

**Cues:** the learner finds every allergen on ten labels.

**Faults:**
- Reading only the front of the pack.
- Ignoring "may contain" for a severe allergy.
- Trusting last year's label.

#### NUT-3.M3.L2 — Cooking for an allergic guest

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `NUT-3.M3.L1` · moves SA-ALLG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] plan the menu, shopping, prep order and serving to prevent cross-contact; communicate clearly
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Ask first:** the allergen, how severe (traces or only ingredients), and what the guest usually does; the guest's
  word decides, and the cook doesn't guess.
- **The cross-contact plan, from shopping to plate:**
  - **menu:** the whole meal free of the allergen where possible, so nothing can be mixed up; otherwise the guest's dish
    designed to be as good as the others;
  - **shopping:** every ingredient's label read and photographed; no products with a "may contain" warning for a severe
    allergy;
  - **storage:** the guest's ingredients kept apart and labelled;
  - **preparation:** the guest's dish first, on cleaned surfaces with washed tools, pans and boards; hands washed; no
    shared oil, spoons or tasting spoons;
  - **cooking and serving:** covered, labelled, plated first and served first; no garnish from a shared bowl;
  - **communication:** the guest told what is in each dish.
- **If something goes wrong:** a dish that may be contaminated is not served; for a reaction, the guest's own plan
  (medication) and 112.

**Sessions:**
1. Plan (60 min): a cross-contact plan written for a guest with a stated allergy (for example milk or celery), checked
   against the checklist.
2. Cook (2 h): the meal cooked by the plan; the guest's dish and the others tasted by the household.

**Cues:**
- Every step of the plan done in order.
- The guest's dish as good as the others.

**Faults:**
- Picking the allergen off a plate.
- The same spoon for both pots.
- A stock cube nobody checked.
