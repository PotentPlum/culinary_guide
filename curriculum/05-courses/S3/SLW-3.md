## Introduction

S2 braised a first stew and roasted joints to a core temperature. `SLW-3` is about **time** as the main tool:
- why tough, collagen-rich cuts become tender only with long, moist, gentle heat, and how to choose the cut and method
  for the time available;
- the families of braise (brown, white, vegetable and legume), the pot-roast, and the braising liquid turned into a
  finished sauce;
- the same dish moved between the pot, the pressure cooker, the slow cooker and a low oven, with the changes predicted;
- **sous vide**: precise temperature, equilibrium cooking, and the difference between doneness and pasteurisation, by
  the tables of assessment §9.

Safety is part of the method, not a footnote. The low-temperature cooks follow the time-temperature tables of
[assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment) (the poultry table, the roast table, the
steak row, and the fish and risk-group rows), taught in `SAF-3.M1.L3`. A cook below a table is a gate *Fail*. Food
cooked sous vide and kept for later is chilled by the cooling standard and kept as leftovers, at most 2 days.

The cuts are Dutch: beef shin (*schenkel*), cheek (*wang*), chuck and brisket; pork shoulder (*schouder*) and belly;
lamb shoulder and neck; chicken legs. The vegetables are seasonal: leeks, fennel, savoy and red cabbage, celeriac and
root vegetables in winter, and beans from `LEG-2`.

**In the spiral.**
- `SC-PROT`, `SC-HEAT`, `CK-METH`, `TS-CMB`, `TS-PRO`, `TS-SAU`, `TS-DRY` and `JD-CUES` reach level 4 here or with
  `FIR-3`, which does the same with fire.
- `SA-HRPR` gets its sous vide log; `RD-ADPT` reaches level 3.
- `MS-S3` re-tests a braise in its week plan (`MS-S3.M1.L1`) and the combination cooking of its two-course evening
  (`MS-S3.M1.L3`).

**Pacing.** About four to five weeks. The braises run in the background on weekend afternoons; each feeds the household
for two meals, and the second portion is frozen or reheated within 2 days.

#### SLW-3.M1.L1 — Collagen to gelatin

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `SCI-2.M2.L1` · moves SC-PROT→4*

*Objectives:* [K] time and temperature; why braised meat can be dry and tender at once
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Two kinds of muscle protein, two behaviours** (`SCI-2.M2`):
  - the muscle fibres (myosin, actin) tighten and squeeze out water as they heat, from about 50 °C, more and more above
    about 65–70 °C;
  - the collagen of the connective tissue shrinks at about 60–65 °C and only slowly dissolves into gelatin, faster the
    hotter it is, over hours.
- **Why braised meat can be dry and tender at once:** by the time the collagen has melted, the fibres have lost much of
  their water. The gelatin coats them and the sauce, so the meat feels moist; but cooked too long or too hot, the fibres
  turn stringy and dry.
- **Time and temperature together:** the same tenderness comes from shorter at a hotter simmer or longer at a gentler
  one; a gentle oven around 140–160 °C, or a liquid barely trembling, gives the widest window between tender and
  stringy.
- **Resting in the liquid:** cooling in the braising liquid lets the meat reabsorb some of it; a braise is often better
  the next day (cooled by the standard, `SAF-3.M1.L1`).

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** the learner can explain from the two proteins why a shin needs three hours and a fillet five minutes.

**Faults:**
- A hard boil.
- Lifting the meat out and leaving it to dry.
- Judging by time only.

#### SLW-3.M1.L2 — Choosing cuts and methods

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `MEA-2.M2.L1` · moves IN-MEA→3, CK-METH→4*

*Objectives:* [J] shin, cheek, shoulder, brisket, legs; method by cut and by time available
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The braising cuts** and what they give:
  - beef: shin (gelatinous, falls apart), cheek (silky, very gelatinous), chuck and brisket (beefy, slices or shreds),
    oxtail;
  - pork: shoulder (forgiving, shreds), belly (rich, layered), hock;
  - lamb: shoulder and neck (sweet, fatty);
  - chicken and duck legs: shorter braises (about an hour).
- **[J] Method by cut:** a large piece of shoulder or brisket pot-roasts or slow-roasts; shin and cheek braise in
  liquid; cubed chuck stews; chicken legs braise briefly.
- **[J] Method by the time available:** a weekday (pressure cooker, or chicken legs), a weekend afternoon (a braise), a
  slow cooker while at work, or overnight in a low oven.
- **Buying:** the cuts are cheap; ask the butcher, buy on the bone where possible (the bone adds gelatin and flavour),
  and look for marbling and visible connective tissue.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** for a given cut and time available, the learner names a method and the expected time.

**Faults:**
- A lean cut (topside) in a long braise, which dries.
- A shin in a quick stir-fry.
- Ignoring the time available.

#### SLW-3.M2.L1 — Brown braise

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m2-l1 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 h · builds on `MEA-2.M6.L1` · moves TS-CMB→3*

*Objectives:* [S] sear, deglaze, braise in wine or stock; skim; [J] doneness
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The steps:**
  1. Dry and season the meat; sear it in batches until deeply brown (`MEA-2.M4`); set aside.
  2. Brown the aromatics; add tomato paste if used and fry it brick-red.
  3. Deglaze with wine, beer or cider and reduce.
  4. Return the meat; add stock to come about two-thirds up; a *bouquet garni*.
  5. Cover and cook gently in a 140–160 °C oven, turning once.
  6. Skim the fat; check with a fork from the planned time.
- **Liquid:** too much dilutes the sauce and boils the meat; too little scorches. A lid with a sheet of parchment
  (*cartouche*) slows evaporation.
- **[J] Doneness:** a fork slides in and comes out without lifting the meat; the meat pulls apart along its fibres but
  holds its shape.
- **Regional examples:** *hachee* (Dutch beef and onions with vinegar and bay), *carbonade flamande* (beer), *boeuf
  bourguignon* (red wine), *stoverij*.

**Sessions:**
1. A brown braise (2 h active, 4 h in total): the braise of `SLW-3.M2.X1`.

**Cues:**
- A deep brown sear.
- A liquid that barely trembles.
- Fork-tender, holding its shape.

**Faults:**
- A crowded pan that steams.
- The meat covered with liquid.
- The fat not skimmed.

#### SLW-3.M2.L2 — White braise

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 3 h · builds on `SAU-2.M3.L3` · moves TS-CMB→3*

*Objectives:* [S] *fricassée* or *blanquette*: no browning, a velvety sauce
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **No browning:** the meat (veal, chicken, rabbit, pork) is blanched or sweated without colour, so the sauce stays pale.
- ***Blanquette*:** the meat simmered in white stock with aromatics; the liquid thickened at the end with a *roux*
  (`SAU-2.M3`) and finished with cream and egg yolk (*liaison*), not boiled after the yolks go in.
- ***Fricassée*:** the meat sweated in butter without colour, dusted with flour, then simmered in stock; finished with
  cream.
- **The velvety sauce:** strained, reduced and skimmed; seasoned with a few drops of lemon.
- **Doneness:** chicken to 74 °C at the core (assessment §9), veal and pork tender.

**Sessions:**
1. A *fricassée* (90 min active, 3 h in total): a chicken-leg or pork *fricassée* with mushrooms and leeks; the sauce at
   *nappe*.

**Cues:**
- An ivory sauce at *nappe*.
- No colour on the meat.
- The yolk liaison smooth, not scrambled.

**Faults:**
- Browning the meat.
- Boiling after the liaison.
- A grey, cloudy sauce from unskimmed scum.

#### SLW-3.M2.L3 — Braising vegetables and legumes

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m2-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `LEG-2.M1.L2` · moves TS-CMB→4*

*Objectives:* [S] fennel, leeks, cabbage, beans; [J] adapt time and liquid
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Vegetables that braise well:** fennel, leeks, chicory (*witlof*), celery, savoy and red cabbage, celeriac, carrots,
  onions, endive; sturdy, with structure that softens into silkiness.
- **The method:** browned (or not, for a white braise), a little liquid (stock, wine, butter and water), covered, gently
  cooked until tender, then uncovered to reduce the liquid to a glaze.
- **Legumes in a braise:** pre-cooked beans (`LEG-2.M1`; dried common beans by the §9 bean rule) added for the last
  30–60 minutes so they take on flavour without bursting.
- **[J] Adapt time and liquid:** by the vegetable's density and cut size; a dense celeriac wedge needs longer than a
  leek half.

**Sessions:**
1. Braised chicory or fennel (45 min): halves browned, braised with butter and stock, glazed.
2. A cabbage and bean braise (75 min): savoy or red cabbage braised with cooked beans, apple and spices; the time and
   liquid predicted and logged.

**Cues:**
- Tender to a knife, holding shape.
- A glossy glaze, not a puddle.

**Faults:**
- Too much liquid, so the vegetables boil.
- Beans added at the start and burst.
- Uneven pieces.

#### SLW-3.M3.L1 — Pot-roasting

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 h · builds on `SLW-3.M2.L1` · moves TS-CMB→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a whole joint or bird in a covered pot; [K] how it differs from a braise
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **A pot-roast** (*poêlé*) cooks a whole joint or bird in a covered pot with little liquid, on a bed of aromatics and
  fat, in a moderate oven. The meat steams and roasts at once.
- **[K] How it differs from a braise:**
  - little liquid (a few centimetres, or only the juices);
  - often a tender or medium cut (a whole chicken, a pork loin, a rolled shoulder), served sliced and juicy;
  - the lid comes off at the end to colour the skin.
- **Doneness by the §9 figures:** a chicken to 74 °C in the thickest part of the thigh; pork to 63 °C for 15 seconds; a
  rolled shoulder cooked longer, until tender.
- **Carryover in a pot:** a covered pot holds heat, so the core keeps rising after the oven; the rise is predicted and
  logged (`HEA-2.M2`).
- **Dutch examples:** a pot-roast of pork with apple and sage, *gestoofde kip* in a *braadpan*.

**Sessions:**
1. A pot-roast (90 min active, 4 h in total): a whole chicken or a pork loin pot-roasted on leeks and root vegetables;
   the core logged every 5 minutes after the oven.

**Cues:**
- Juicy slices.
- Coloured skin.
- The carryover predicted within 2 °C.

**Faults:**
- Too much liquid, so the joint boils.
- No colour.
- Pulling a chicken below 74 °C and hoping.

#### SLW-3.M3.L2 — From braising liquid to sauce

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAU-2.M2.L2` · moves TS-SAU→4*

*Objectives:* [S] degrease, reduce, thicken or mount; [J] balance gelatin, acid and salt
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Degrease:** skim the fat with a ladle, or chill the liquid and lift off the set fat (the next-day braise).
- **Reduce:** strain, then reduce in a wide pan to concentrate; taste as it goes, because salt concentrates too.
- **Thicken or mount** (`SAU-2.M2`):
  - reduction alone, if the liquid is rich in gelatin;
  - a little starch slurry, or the puréed vegetables of the braise;
  - cold butter whisked in at the end for gloss (*monter au beurre*).
- **[J] Balance gelatin, acid and salt:** a gelatin-rich sauce coats well but can feel sticky; a few drops of vinegar or
  wine lift it; salt is adjusted last, after reducing.
- **The target:** *nappe*: a line drawn through the sauce on the back of a spoon stays clean.

**Sessions:**
1. A sauce from the braise (45 min): the liquid of `SLW-3.M2.X1` (or the pot-roast) degreased, reduced, finished and
   tasted against the plan.

**Cues:**
- *Nappe* on the spoon.
- Glossy.
- Balanced, not sticky or salty.

**Faults:**
- Salting before reducing.
- Flour stirred straight in, lumpy.
- A greasy film on the plate.

#### SLW-3.M4.L1 — Pressure cooking

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m4-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAF-3.M2.L2` · moves SC-HEAT→4, RD-ADPT→3*

*Objectives:* [K] pressure and boiling point; [S] safe use; [J] adapt times and liquid
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Pressure and boiling point:** sealed, the pot builds pressure, and water boils hotter (about 115–120 °C at full
  pressure). Collagen dissolves much faster, so a braise takes about a third of the time.
- **What changes:**
  - almost no evaporation, so less liquid (the minimum the manual names) and a thinner sauce, reduced after opening;
  - browning still happens before the lid goes on;
  - flavours meld less than in a long braise; a short simmer with the lid off helps.
- **[S] Safe use** (`SAF-3.M2.L2`): never more than two-thirds full, half for beans and grains; the valve checked; the
  pressure released as the manual says (natural release for meat and foaming foods); never forcing the lid.
- **[J] Adapting a recipe:** time to about a third, liquid to the minimum, the vegetables that would turn to mush added
  after a first cook.

**Sessions:**
1. A pressure-cooked braise (60 min): the braise of `SLW-3.M2.X1` adapted to the pressure cooker; the time and liquid
   predicted.
2. Stock or beans (45 min): a chicken stock or soaked chickpeas in the pressure cooker; compared with the pot version.

**Cues:**
- Tender in about a third of the time.
- The sauce reduced to *nappe* after opening.

**Faults:**
- Overfilling.
- A quick release on a foaming pot.
- The sauce served thin.

#### SLW-3.M4.L2 — Slow cooker and low oven

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m4-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 8 h · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→3*

*Objectives:* [J] adapt a braise to a slow cooker or a low oven; [K] the safety limits
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The slow cooker** heats a ceramic pot gently for 4–10 hours. Little evaporation, so less liquid; no browning, so
  brown first in a pan.
- **[K] The safety limits** (assessment §9):
  - the food must still end at the §9 figures (poultry 74 °C, braises until tender and hot throughout);
  - dried common beans are never cooked in a slow cooker (the bean row of §9): they are cooked first by the bean rule;
  - frozen meat is thawed in the fridge first, so that it does not spend hours in the growth range;
  - leftovers are cooled by the standard, not left in the switched-off pot.
- **The low oven** (about 120–150 °C) is the slow cooker's alternative: a heavy casserole with a lid, often better
  because the oven browns the top and the heat is even.
- **[J] Adapting:** less liquid than on the stove; the time roughly doubled on low; thickening at the end.

**Sessions:**

   slow cooker; the liquid and time predicted.

- The liquid level predicted.
- The meat tender and hot throughout at the end.

**Faults:**
- Dried kidney beans in the slow cooker.
- Frozen meat straight in.
- A watery sauce left unreduced.

#### SLW-3.M5.L1 — Sous vide principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m5-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 h · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L3` · moves SC-HEAT→4, TS-MOD→1*

*Objectives:* [K] precise temperature, equilibrium, time versus pasteurisation; the stove-top method without a circulator
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m5-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Equilibrium cooking:** the food sits in water at the exact temperature wanted at its core, so it cannot overcook
  past that point. Doneness depends on the temperature; the time makes it hot through and, for safety, long enough at
  that temperature.
- **Time to the core:** the thicker the piece, the longer it takes to reach the bath temperature (roughly with the
  square of the thickness: twice as thick takes about four times as long). A probe through closed-cell foam tape on the
  bag shows when the core is there.
- **[K] Doneness against pasteurisation** (`SAF-3.M1.L3`, assessment §9): at 60 °C a chicken breast is cooked to its
  texture as soon as the core gets there, but it is safe only after 35 minutes at that core temperature (the poultry
  table). Hotter means shorter: at 65.6 °C, 5 minutes.
- **Bags:** food-safe zip bags with the air pressed out by lowering them into water, or vacuum bags; no metal clips
  that touch the food.
- **Without a circulator:** a heavy pot of water on the lowest flame, an instant-read thermometer in the water, the
  heat adjusted every few minutes to stay within ±1 °C. It works for cooks of up to about an hour; a longer cook needs a
  circulator.
- **The program's limits:** poultry from 60 °C upwards; other foods by their §9 row; nothing kept warm in the bath
  after the time.

**Sessions:**
1. Eggs (60 min active, 2 h in total): eggs at 63 and 65 °C, compared; the bath held on the stove-top or with a
   circulator, its temperature logged.

**Cues:**
- The water within ±1 °C of the target throughout (log).
- The yolks as predicted.

**Faults:**
- Air pockets in the bag.
- Starting the clock when the bag goes in.
- A bath that drifts 3 °C.

#### SLW-3.M5.L2 — Sous vide doneness

**Concepts:**

#### SLW-3.M5.L3 — Finishing and vegetables

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-slw-3-m5-l3 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SLW-3.M5.L2` · moves TS-DRY→4, JD-CUES→4*

*Objectives:* [S] dry and sear after the bath without overcooking; vegetables sous vide; [J] when sous vide is worth it
<!-- END generated:lesson-slw-3-m5-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Finishing without overcooking:**
  - out of the bag, patted very dry;
  - seared in a very hot pan (or under a hot grill) for the shortest time possible, about 30–60 seconds a side;
  - the edges too, for a steak (the steak row);
  - chilling the surface briefly in ice water before searing gives more time for a crust without a grey band.
- **Vegetables sous vide:** at about 83–85 °C, where plant cell walls soften: carrots, beetroot, celeriac, leeks, cooked
  in the bag with butter and salt keep their flavour and colour. Firmer and more intense than boiled.
- **[J] When sous vide is worth it:** thick, even pieces where precision matters (a thick steak, a chicken breast, a
  pork tenderloin), cooking ahead for guests, and dense vegetables; not for thin pieces, browning-led dishes, or braises
  that want reduction.

**Sessions:**
1. Finishing (45 min): a steak or chicken breast from the bath seared two ways (straight from the bath, and chilled
   first); the grey band measured in the cross-section.
2. Vegetables (60 min): carrots or beetroot sous vide against roasted and boiled; texture and colour compared.

**Cues:**
- A deep crust in under a minute a side.
- No grey band, or one under 2 mm.

**Faults:**
- A wet surface that steams.
- Searing for minutes.
- Vegetables at 60 °C, still raw.
