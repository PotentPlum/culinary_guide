**Purpose (outline):** cooking over a charcoal or wood fire: building and controlling the fire; direct grilling of meat, fish, vegetables and bread; indirect roasting with the lid on; low-and-slow barbecue (collagen, bark, the stall); cooking in the embers; a live-fire meal for guests. ## Introduction

`HEA-2.M3` grilled on a grill pan and under the oven grill. `FIR-3` moves outdoors to a charcoal fire, where the cook
controls the heat source itself:
- **the fire:** fuel, airflow, zones, the lid; reading heat by hand and by thermometer; safety around fire, coals, ash
  and carbon monoxide;
- **direct grilling:** meat, fish, vegetables, fruit and flatbread, seared hot and finished in the cool zone;
- **indirect cooking:** the lidded grill as an oven, and wood smoke as a flavour (not as preservation: cold smoking and
  smoked cures stay outside the program's core, Phase 3 §2);
- **low-and-slow barbecue:** a pork shoulder or ribs over many hours, through the stall, to a target texture, with sauces
  and sides;
- **embers and the plancha:** vegetables in the coals, cast iron over the fire;
- **a live-fire meal** for guests, planned like a service.

Every protein reaches its figure in [assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment):
poultry 75 °C, minced meat 75 °C, fish and intact meat 63 °C for 15 seconds, intact beef steaks by the steak row. Fire
gives uneven heat, so the probe matters more outdoors than indoors.

**Without outdoor space.** A cast-iron grill pan and the oven grill teach the browning and the doneness, but not fire
control. The learner then reaches `TS-FIRE` level 2 at most, and the record says so; `MS-S3` accepts the oven grill for
its live-fire element on the same terms.

The produce is seasonal: grilling vegetables in summer (courgette, peppers and aubergine from Dutch greenhouses, sweet
corn, spring onions), roots and onions for the embers in autumn and winter; Dutch pork, beef and chicken; North Sea fish
(mackerel, herring) whole for the grill.

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-FIRE` reaches level 3; level 4 (three low-and-slow cooks and three live-fire meals in two conditions) starts here
  and continues in S5: the second live-fire meal in `IND-5.M5.X1`, the remaining cooks logged as household cooks.
- `TS-PRO`, `TS-DRY`, `TS-SAU`, `SC-PROT`, `JD-CUES`, `SA-HRPR` and `KM-PLAN` reach level 4 with `SLW-3`, which does the
  same indoors.
- `MS-S3` cooks one element of its two-course evening over live fire (`MS-S3.M1.L3`).

**Pacing.** About five weeks in the grilling season (May to September), mostly at weekends; the low-and-slow cook takes
a whole day.

#### FIR-3.M1.L1 — Fuel, grills and safety

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-FIRE→1, SA-PHYS→4*

*Objectives:* [K] briquettes, lump charcoal, wood and gas; kettle, kamado and open grills; [K] fire, burns and carbon monoxide (never indoors or in a closed space); [K] flare-ups, charring and smoke compounds, and how to limit them
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Fuel:**
  - **lump charcoal:** lights fast, burns hot and unevenly, little ash;
  - **briquettes:** even and long-burning, more ash; choose ones without lighter fluid added;
  - **wood:** chunks for smoke or a wood fire; only dry, untreated hardwood (beech, oak, fruit woods), never painted or
    treated wood;
  - **gas:** clean and convenient, but no fire to manage and little smoky flavour.
- **Grills:** the kettle (a lid and two vents: a versatile oven), the kamado (thick ceramic, very stable heat), the
  open grill or brazier (direct heat only).
- **How a fire cooks:** mostly by radiant heat from the coals (felt on the hand), plus convection under the lid; heat
  falls off fast with distance.
- **Safety:**
  - **carbon monoxide:** a charcoal grill is never used indoors, in a garage, a tent or a closed porch, and never
    brought inside while warm;
  - the grill on a stable, non-flammable surface, away from walls, hedges and parasols;
  - no lighter fluid, petrol or spirits on a lit fire;
  - heat-resistant gloves, long tongs, a bucket of water or sand and the fire blanket nearby;
  - children and pets kept away;
  - ash only into a metal bucket with a lid, and only when completely cold (embers can stay hot for a day).
- **Flare-ups, charring and smoke compounds:** fat dripping on coals flares and deposits soot and smoke compounds on the
  food; charred, blackened parts contain compounds best limited. Ways to limit them: trim excess fat, cook over the
  two-zone fire and move the food away from flames, avoid blackening, and cut off burnt bits.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** the learner can choose a fuel for a cook and set up a safe place for the grill.

**Faults:**
- Lighter fluid on a lit fire.
- The grill under a parasol.
- Warm ash in a plastic bin.
- A grill in the garage "because it's raining".

#### FIR-3.M1.L2 — Building and controlling the fire

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `FIR-3.M1.L1` · moves TS-FIRE→2, JD-CUES→4, SA-PHYS→5*

*Objectives:* [S] light with a chimney, set two- and three-zone fires, control with vents and lid; [J] read heat by the hand test and the lid thermometer; [S] handle hot grates, coals and ash safely
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The chimney starter:** paper or natural firelighters underneath, charcoal on top; ready when the top coals are
  covered in grey ash (15–25 minutes).
- **Zones:**
  - **two-zone:** all coals on one side (direct, hot) and none on the other (indirect, cool);
  - **three-zone:** a thick hot bank, a thinner medium area, and an empty cool zone;
  - the cool zone is where food goes when it flares or browns too fast.
- **Vents and lid:** the bottom vent feeds air to the fire (more air, more heat); the top vent lets smoke and heat out
  and draws air through. Small changes, then wait 10–15 minutes before judging.
- **Reading heat:**
  - **the hand test:** the palm held about 10 cm above the grate; seconds before it must be moved: about 2 for very hot,
    4–5 for medium, 7 or more for low;
  - **the lid thermometer**, checked against an oven thermometer on the grate (lid thermometers read the dome, not the
    grate);
  - the hand-test estimate is written before the reading (cue, then reading).
- **Handling hot grates and coals:** heat-resistant gloves, long tongs, the grate lifted with its handles, coals
  moved with tongs or a small shovel, never with hands.

**Sessions:**
1. Light and zone (60 min): a chimney lit, a two-zone fire built, a few sausages and vegetables grilled over the zones,
   hand-test estimates logged against the grate thermometer.
2. Hold temperatures (90 min active, 2 h in total): the lid held at 120 °C and then 180 °C for 20 minutes each by the
   vents, logged every 5 minutes (`FIR-3.M1.X1`).

**Cues:**
- Coals grey-ashed before spreading.
- The lid within ±15 °C of the target for 20 minutes.
- Hand-test estimates close to the readings.

**Faults:**
- Spreading black coals.
- Opening the lid every two minutes.
- Big vent changes and impatience.
- No cool zone.

#### FIR-3.M1.X1 — Two zones, two temperatures

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-fir-3-m1-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — Light a chimney, build a two-zone fire, and hold the lid at 120 °C and at 180 °C for 20 minutes each; log the vent positions.
<!-- END generated:exercise-fir-3-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-FIRE→2, SA-PHYS→5, JD-CUES→4

**Aim:** build and control a fire by the vents, and read heat by hand before the thermometer.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the fire-control sheet. A chimney lit, a two-zone fire built, and the lid held at
120 °C and at 180 °C (±15 °C) for 20 minutes each, with the vent positions logged. The extensions:
- vegetables and a simple protein (sausages or chicken thighs) grilled over the two zones to their §9 figures;
- hand-test estimates before each reading;
- the setup, the lighting and the handling of the grate and coals filmed, with the fire-safety checklist.

**Inputs:** charcoal, a chimney starter, firelighters, an oven thermometer for the grate, sausages or chicken thighs,
two seasonal vegetables.

**Procedure:**
1. Set up the grill safely; tick the checklist; film it.
2. Light the chimney; build the two-zone fire.
3. Grill the vegetables and the protein; probe and log.
4. Hold 120 °C, then 180 °C, for 20 minutes each; log vents and readings every 5 minutes, with hand estimates.
5. Close the grill down safely; dispose of the ash when cold.

**Evidence:** photo, measure, log, video, checklist:
- **`TS-FIRE` level 2:** a chimney lit, a two-zone fire built, vegetables and a simple protein grilled to doneness. The

#### FIR-3.M2.L1 — Grilling meat

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `MEA-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-FIRE→3, TS-PRO→4*

*Objectives:* [S] steaks, chops, sausages and skewers; [J] manage flare-ups; sear hot, finish in the cool zone; reverse sear
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Sear hot, finish cool:** thick cuts seared over the hot zone, then moved to the cool zone (lid on) until the core is
  near the target; thin cuts cooked over direct heat only.
- **Reverse sear:** a thick steak cooked in the cool zone with the lid on until about 10 °C below the target, then
  seared hard over the coals; even inside, a crust outside.
- **The cuts:** steaks (by the steak row of §9: seared on every surface, intact), pork chops (63 °C for 15 seconds),
  sausages and burgers (minced meat: 75 °C), chicken thighs on skewers (75 °C).
- **[J] Managing flare-ups:** move the food to the cool zone, close the lid briefly to starve the flames; never water.
- **Doneness:** cue first (feel, colour of the juices), then the probe in the thickest part; carryover after the grill.

**Sessions:**
1. Steaks and chops (60 min): a steak by direct heat and a pork chop seared and finished cool; cue then probe; logged.
2. Sausages and skewers (60 min): sausages slowly in the medium zone and chicken skewers hot, each to its §9 figure.

**Cues:**
- A crust without black char.
- The core within 3 °C of the target after resting.
- Sausages browned without bursting.

**Faults:**
- Sausages over the hottest zone, burnt and raw.
- Pressing burgers flat with the spatula.
- Pinkish chicken skewers served "because they look done".

#### FIR-3.M2.L2 — Grilling fish and seafood

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FSH-2.M3.L5` · moves TS-FIRE→3, IN-FSH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] whole fish, skin-on fillets and prawns without sticking; [K] a clean, hot, oiled grate
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] A clean, hot, oiled grate:** brushed clean, heated well, and the fish (not the grate) oiled lightly. Fish sticks
  when the grate is dirty or not hot enough, or when it is turned too soon: it releases when the skin has crisped.
- **Whole fish:** a whole mackerel or herring, slashed on both sides, cooked over medium heat, turned once with a wide
  spatula (or in a fish basket).
- **Fillets:** skin-on, skin side down for most of the time, turned once; or on a plank or foil.
- **Prawns and mussels:** prawns on skewers over high heat for a few minutes; mussels in a pan on the grill until they
  open (closed ones discarded, `FSH-2.M4`).
- **Doneness:** 63 °C for 15 seconds at the thickest part (assessment §9); the flesh opaque and flaking at the bone.
- **Buying for the grill:** firm, oily fish suit it best; freshness judged at purchase (clear eyes, red gills, a fresh
  sea smell; `FSH-2.M1`).

**Sessions:**
1. A whole fish (60 min): a whole mackerel or a sea bream bought whole (freshness photographed), grilled, probed and
   served.

**Cues:**
- The skin crisp and intact.
- The fish releasing without tearing.
- 63 °C at the backbone.

**Faults:**
- A cold or dirty grate.
- Turning the fish too early.
- Thin delicate fillets straight on the grate.

#### FIR-3.M2.L3 — Vegetables, fruit and flatbreads

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m2-l3 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-2.M3.L2` · moves TS-FIRE→3, TS-VEG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] grilled and charred vegetables, fruit for dessert, flatbread on the grate; [J] which vegetables need blanching first
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Straight on the grate:** courgette, peppers, aubergine, spring onions, leeks, mushrooms, sweet corn, chicory halves;
  oiled, salted, cut thick enough to stay on the grate.
- **[J] Blanch first:** dense vegetables (potatoes, carrots, beetroot, celeriac, fennel) are parboiled or steamed until
  nearly tender, then grilled for colour.
- **Charring:** a deep char on peppers and aubergine is meant (skins peeled after); on most vegetables, colour without
  black.
- **Fruit for dessert:** Dutch apples, pears, plums or rhubarb in season, grilled until caramelised, with a dairy or a
  crumble.
- **Flatbread on the grate:** a simple yeasted or yoghurt dough rolled thin, grilled for a minute or two a side, brushed
  with herb oil.

**Sessions:**
1. Vegetables (60 min): three vegetables, one blanched first, grilled to tender and coloured.
2. Flatbread and fruit (60 min): flatbreads on the grate; a grilled fruit dessert.

**Cues:**
- Vegetables tender through, coloured outside.
- Flatbread puffed and spotted.

**Faults:**
- Raw potato slices burnt outside.
- Thin courgette ribbons falling through the grate.
- A thick flatbread raw inside.

#### FIR-3.M2.X2 — Direct sear against reverse sear

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-fir-3-m2-x2 -->

<!-- END generated:exercise-fir-3-m2-x2 -->

**Aim:** compare two ways of grilling a thick steak, and judge doneness by cue first.

**Conditions:** framework support: the comparison form. Two thick steaks (about 4 cm) from the same cut: one seared over

**Procedure:**
1. Write the target, the predicted carryover and the predicted difference.

3. Photograph the cross-sections side by side for AI review.
4. Write which method suits which cut and why.

**Evidence:** measure, photo, written — four proteins (the two steaks, the chop and the skewers) judged by cue first,
then probed, within 2 °C, the cue noted before the reading; carryover predicted. This meets the `TS-PRO` level 4 row

#### FIR-3.M3.L1 — Indirect roasting

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 h · builds on `POU-2.M3.L1` · moves TS-FIRE→3, TS-DRY→4*

**Concepts:**
- **The grill as an oven:** the food in the cool zone, the lid on, the top vent over the food so the heat and smoke draw
  across it; a drip tray under the food.
- **Spatchcocking:** the backbone cut out with shears, the bird pressed flat; it cooks faster and more evenly than a
  whole bird.
- **[J] Holding the temperature:** about 180–200 °C for a chicken, by the vents; a few lit coals added from a chimney
  for a long cook.

  and the breast, and must read 75 °C (assessment §9); the rise after the grill is predicted and logged, but not relied
  on for safety.

  if needed.

1. A spatchcocked chicken (90 min active, 2 h in total): the bird of `FIR-3.M3.X1`.

**Cues:**
- The lid within ±15 °C of the target.
- 75 °C in thigh and breast.
- Crisp, golden skin.

**Faults:**
- The bird over the coals, burnt underneath.
- Opening the lid constantly.
- Probing only the breast.

#### FIR-3.M3.L2 — Wood smoke as a flavour

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m3-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 h · builds on `FIR-3.M3.L1` · moves TS-FIRE→3, FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [K] clean versus heavy smoke; wood chunks, chips and planks; [S] add smoke to a roast or to vegetables; [J] how much is enough
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Clean against heavy smoke:** a thin, blue, almost invisible smoke tastes sweet and aromatic; thick white or grey
  smoke from smouldering, damp or starved wood tastes bitter and acrid.
- **Wood:** chunks (slow, steady smoke), chips (quick, short), planks (gentle, for fish); dry hardwoods: beech, oak, and
  fruit woods (apple, cherry) are mild; never treated or resinous wood.
- **Using it:** a chunk or two on the coals at the start of an indirect cook; the vents open enough for a clean burn;
  smoke is absorbed best by cool, moist surfaces, so most flavour goes on early.
- **[J] How much is enough:** a little smoke is a seasoning; too much makes food bitter and dominates; start with one
  chunk.
- **Not preservation:** hot smoke here flavours food that is cooked to its §9 figure; cold smoking and smoked cures are
  outside the program.

**Sessions:**
1. Smoked vegetables (90 min active, 2 h in total): onions, beetroot or celeriac roasted indirectly with one chunk of
   wood, against the same without smoke; tasted side by side, the smoke judged on the form.

**Cues:**
- Thin blue smoke.
- A smoky note the tasters notice but don't find harsh.

**Faults:**
- Thick white smoke for hours.
- Wet chips smouldering.
- Smoke added at the end only.

#### FIR-3.M4.L1 — Low-and-slow principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m4-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `SLW-3.M1.L1`, `SAF-3.M1.L1` · moves SC-PROT→4, SA-HRPR→4*

*Objectives:* [K] collagen and time, the stall and evaporation, bark, rubs, mops and wrapping; [K] long-cook safety: keep the meat out of the danger zone, rest, cool and reheat safely
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Collagen and time** (`SLW-3.M1`): a pork shoulder or ribs at a grill temperature of 110–130 °C for many hours, until
  the collagen has melted and the meat pulls apart (a core around 90–95 °C for pulled pork; ribs that bend and crack on
  the surface).
- **The stall:** the core stops rising for hours around 65–75 °C, because evaporation from the surface cools the meat as
  fast as the fire heats it. It ends by itself, or sooner when the meat is wrapped.
- **Bark:** the dark crust from the rub, smoke and drying surface; wrapping softens it.
- **Rubs, mops and wrapping:** a rub of salt, sugar and spices, ideally the day before; a vinegar mop to keep the surface
  moist; wrapping in paper or foil to push through the stall.
- **[K] Long-cook safety** (assessment §9):
  - the meat goes on cold from the fridge onto a fire already at temperature, and the cook is not paused and resumed;
  - the finished meat is far above every §9 cooking figure;
  - resting and holding: wrapped in a warm, insulated box, held at 57 °C or above, checked with the probe;
  - leftovers cooled by the standard (57 to 21 °C within 2 hours, to 5 °C within 6 hours), kept at most 2 days or
    frozen, reheated until steaming hot, once.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** the learner can explain the stall and plan a timeline that allows for it.

**Faults:**
- Raising the fire to "beat" the stall.
- Pulling the shoulder at 75 °C because the timer says so.
- A shoulder left warm on the counter overnight.

#### FIR-3.M4.L2 — Pork shoulder or ribs

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-fir-3-m4-l2 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 10 h · builds on `FIR-3.M4.L1` · moves TS-FIRE→3, TS-CMB→4*

*Objectives:* [S] trim, rub, cook at a steady 110–130 °C, wrap or not, rest; [J] judge doneness by probe feel, not only by temperature
<!-- END generated:lesson-fir-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Trimming:** excess hard fat and loose bits removed; for ribs, the membrane on the bone side pulled off.
- **The cook:**
  1. Rub the day before.
  2. Set an indirect fire with a chunk of wood; hold 110–130 °C.
  3. Add lit coals as needed from a chimney; log fire and meat temperatures every 30–60 minutes.
  4. Mop or spray if drying; wrap at the stall if the timeline needs it.
  5. Finish when the probe slides in like into soft butter.
  6. Rest wrapped for at least an hour.
- **[J] Doneness by probe feel:** the temperature guides, the feel decides; a probe that meets resistance means more
  time, whatever the number.
- **Ribs** cook in roughly 5–6 hours; a shoulder of 3–4 kg in 10–12 hours.

**Sessions:**
1. The cook (3 h active over about 10 h): the shoulder or ribs of `FIR-3.M4.X1`.

**Cues:**
- A steady 110–130 °C.
- A dark bark.
- Probe-tender.
- Meat that pulls apart in long strands.

**Faults:**
- Fire swinging between 90 and 180 °C.
- Opening the lid too often.
- Slicing a shoulder straight off the grill.

**Concepts:**
- **[J] The fire plan:**
  - the zones needed for each dish, in order;
  - when to refuel (a second chimney lit 20 minutes before it's needed);
  - which dishes cook ahead (ember vegetables, a low-and-slow element) and which at the last moment (steaks, fish,

- **[K] Safety around guests and children:** a cordon around the grill, nobody running near it, alcohol and fire kept
