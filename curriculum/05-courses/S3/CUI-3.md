## Introduction

`CUI-3` is the largest course of the program: eight culinary traditions, each studied through the same lens and cooked
in four lessons (history and pantry, two signature techniques, and a meal). It is one overarching *World cuisines* course,
taught in two sections that each count as a unit of progress (Phase 3, review answer 3):
- **`CUI-3.SEC1` Europe and the Mediterranean** (`CUI-3.M1`–`M5`, 51 h): the lens, then French regional, Italian,
  Iberian, and Levantine and North African cooking;
- **`CUI-3.SEC2` Asia and Mexico** (`CUI-3.M6`–`M9`, 48 h): the Indian subcontinent, Chinese, Japanese and Mexican
  cooking.

A section is complete when the assessments of its modules are passed; `MS-S3` and `CUI-4` need the whole course.

**The lens.** Every tradition is studied with the same five questions (`CUI-3.M1.L1`):
1. **The pantry:** the fats, starches, aromatics, acids and condiments that recur.
2. **The core techniques:** the methods that define the cooking.
3. **The flavour profile:** the balance of tastes and aromas that makes a dish "taste of" the tradition.
4. **The meal structure and drinks:** how a meal is put together, served and eaten, and what is drunk with it.
5. **History and geography:** why the tradition is as it is (climate, trade, religion, migration).

**Respect and adaptation.** Each tradition is regional and alive; the course teaches representative dishes and the
logic behind them, not a single "authentic" version. Adaptations to a Dutch kitchen are allowed and named:
- **produce:** Dutch seasonal vegetables stand in where the original produce is out of season or flown in (for example
  pointed cabbage or chard for Chinese greens, Dutch greenhouse peppers and tomatoes in summer, pumpkin and roots in
  winter); every substitution is noted in the exercise, with its effect;
- **pantry goods** (spices, rice, noodles, soy sauce, miso, *masa harina*, chillies, olive oil) are bought from
  specialist shops, which `CUI-3.M1` maps;
- **meat and fish** are Dutch or North Sea, by the rules of the earlier courses.

Safety follows [assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment) in every tradition: minced
fillings to 75 °C, poultry to 75 °C, fish to 63 °C (no raw-fish dishes are taught here; `GRD-3.M3` covers the rules),
cooked rice cooled fast and kept cold, soft-set egg dishes and cured meats not served to risk groups, allergens (sesame,
peanuts, tree nuts, soy, fish, crustaceans, gluten) named for guests.

**In the spiral.**
- `CK-CUIS` reaches level 3: for each tradition, the tradition quiz at 80 % and two dishes from frameworks that the style
  description and the tasters recognise.
- `CK-RCPT` and `IN-SEL` reach level 4 in `CUI-3.M1`; `TS-STA`, `IN-STA`, `TS-STK`, `TS-MOI`, `TS-DRY`, `TS-SAU`,
  `TS-CMB`, `TS-EGG`, `FL-SEAS` and `KM-PLAN` add level-4 evidence in the traditions; `FL-ARCH`, `FL-TEXT`, `CR-COMP`
  and `JD-CHSE` add level-3 evidence.
- `CUI-4` (S4) adds further traditions and cooking within one tradition from principles (`CK-CUIS` level 4).

**Pacing.** About twelve to fifteen weeks in all, at roughly one tradition every one and a half weeks, with the
section boundary as a natural pause. Most dishes are household meals; each tradition ends with a meal, several with
guests.

#### CUI-3.M1.L1 — The lens

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M2.L2` · moves CK-CUIS→2, JD-CHSE→3, CK-BEVR→1*

*Objectives:* [K] pantry, core techniques, flavour profile, meal structure and drinks, history and geography
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The five questions** of the lens (introduction), applied first to the cooking the learner knows best: Dutch home
  cooking (butter, potatoes and *stamppot*, the *hachee* braise, mustard and vinegar, the evening meal of meat,
  potatoes and vegetables, coffee and a *koekje*), so the lens is clear before it meets the unfamiliar.
- **[K] The pantry as a fingerprint:** a tradition can often be recognised from its fat, its starch, its aromatic base and
  its acid: butter, wheat, shallot and wine; olive oil, wheat or rice, garlic and tomato; ghee, rice and wheat, onion,
  ginger and spices, tamarind or yoghurt; neutral oil, rice, ginger, garlic and spring onion, soy and rice vinegar;
  lard or oil, corn, chilli and lime.
- **[K] Meal structure and drinks:** courses in sequence (France, Italy), many dishes at once (China, India, the
  Levant), a set tray (Japan); water, wine, beer, tea or fresh drinks with the meal (`CK-BEVR` level 1: knowing what is
  usually drunk).
- **History and geography** as explanation: trade routes (spices, chillies from the Americas), religion (pork, beef,
  fasting), climate (preserving, fats).
- **[J] Reading for the lens:** a cookbook or a recipe from the tradition read for what it assumes the cook knows.

**Sessions:** theory only.

**Cues:** for any tradition in the course, the learner can name its fat, starch, aromatic base and acid.

**Faults:**
- "Chinese food" as one cuisine.
- Judging a tradition by its restaurant versions abroad.
- History learned as dates rather than as reasons.

#### CUI-3.M1.L2 — Reading and sourcing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `WRK-1.M2.L2` · moves CK-RCPT→4, IN-SEL→4*

*Objectives:* [J] interpret implicit technique in recipes from other traditions; [S] find ingredients and sensible substitutes for two recipes
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[J] Implicit technique:** recipes from other traditions leave out what every home cook there knows: "fry the
  *masala* until the oil separates", "cook the rice", "make a *sofrito*", "add *dashi*". The learner recognises the gap
  and fills it from the lens and the course.
- **Converting conventions:**
  - cups and spoons to grams (by ingredient, because a cup of flour and a cup of sugar weigh differently);
  - Fahrenheit to Celsius, gas marks, fan ovens;
  - ingredient names that differ (US cilantro is coriander leaf; "scallions" are spring onions);
  - pan sizes and equipment (a wok, a *paella* pan, a *tawa*).
- **Finding errors:** a recipe whose ratio, time or quantity doesn't make sense (three tablespoons of salt for 500 g of
  rice; 10 minutes for a beef shin) is checked against the ratios and times the learner knows.
- **Sourcing:** which ingredients are found in a Dutch supermarket, which at an Asian, Turkish, Moroccan, Surinamese or
  Mexican shop, which online; sensible substitutes and their effect; what to buy whole and what to buy small (`FLV-3.M2`).

**Sessions:**
1. Two recipes (45 min): two recipes from different traditions (for example an American and an Indian one) read,
   converted to the program's conventions, and annotated with the implicit steps.
2. A shop visit (60 min): one specialist shop near the learner visited; the shelves photographed and the key pantry items
   for two traditions noted with prices.

**Cues:**
- Every cup turned into grams.
- Every implicit step written in.
- A sourcing list with shops and substitutes.

**Faults:**
- Converting cups of flour as 250 g.
- Missing "fry until the oil separates".
- Buying a large bag of a spice used once.

#### CUI-3.M1.X1 — A pantry map and a sourcing list

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-cui-3-m1-x1 -->
*guided, recipe* — A pantry map of the eight traditions; a sourcing list for the shops near you.
<!-- END generated:exercise-cui-3-m1-x1 -->

**Evidences:** JD-CHSE→3, CK-RCPT→4, IN-SEL→4

**Aim:** map the pantries of the eight traditions, and prepare to source them near home.

**Conditions:** recipe support: the pantry-map template. A pantry map of the eight traditions (fat, starch, aromatic

- three unfamiliar pantry ingredients from the map (for example sumac, *katsuobushi* and *masa harina*) analysed in
  writing (what they are, how they taste, how they are used) and cooked in a small dish;
- two recipes from other countries' conventions adapted to the program's (weights, Celsius, names, equipment), one with
  three planted errors found and corrected with a reason.

**Inputs:** the course's tradition notes; shop visits; small amounts of the three ingredients.

**Procedure:**
1. Fill in the pantry map.
2. Visit or browse the shops; write the sourcing list.
3. Analyse and cook the three unfamiliar ingredients; photograph.
4. Adapt the two recipes; find and correct the planted errors.

**Evidence:** written, photo, quiz, measure:
- **`JD-CHSE` level 3:** three unfamiliar ingredients analysed in writing and cooked by a chosen approach; each result
  acceptable.
- **`CK-RCPT` level 4:** at least two of three planted errors found and corrected with a reason (and the items at 80 %);
  a recipe adapted from another country's conventions.
- **`IN-SEL` level 4, further evidence:** sourcing with quality, price and purpose noted; the row was met in
  `VEG-3.M1.X1`.

**Time:** 3 h, plus the shop visits.

#### CUI-3.M2.L1 — The regions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] Normandy, Burgundy, Provence, the South-West, Alsace: fat, produce and signature dishes
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] France as regions:** the classical cuisine of `SAU-2` and the *brigade* grew from regional home and inn cooking;
  each region is shaped by its fat, produce and neighbours:
  - **Normandy:** butter, cream, apples and cider, Calvados; *poulet vallée d'Auge*, mussels in cider;
  - **Burgundy:** red wine, beef, mustard, snails; *bœuf bourguignon*, *œufs en meurette*;
  - **Provence:** olive oil, garlic, tomatoes, herbs, fish; *ratatouille*, *aïoli*, *bouillabaisse*;
  - **the South-West:** duck and goose fat, confit, beans; *cassoulet*, *pommes sarladaises*;
  - **Alsace:** lard, cabbage and sauerkraut, pork, Riesling; *choucroute garnie*, *tarte flambée*.
- **Terroir:** the idea that place shapes taste (cheese, wine, butter, poultry with a named origin).
- **The lens applied:** pantry (butter or oil or duck fat, shallots, wine, herbs), techniques (braising, *sauté*,
  emulsions, pastry), flavour profile (rich, rounded, herb-scented), meal structure (*entrée*, *plat*, cheese, dessert),
  drinks (regional wine or cider, water).
- **Dutch kitchen notes:** Normandy and Alsace match Dutch produce closely (apples, cabbage, pork, dairy); Provence
  is best cooked in summer with Dutch greenhouse tomatoes, courgettes and peppers.

**Sessions:**
1. A regional tasting (60 min): a simple dish from two regions side by side (for example mussels in cider and in white
   wine with garlic and tomato), tasted and described with the lens.

**Cues:** the learner can place a French dish in its region from its fat and produce.

**Faults:**
- "French food" as restaurant food only.
- Olive oil in a Normandy dish without a reason.

#### CUI-3.M2.L2 — A regional dish project

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M2.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cook a regional dish and its accompaniments; [J] adapt to local ingredients
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Choosing a dish:** one regional dish with its accompaniments, researched with the lens: its region, its fat, its

- **[J] Adapting to local ingredients:** what can be bought in the Netherlands in season, what is substituted (cider

**Sessions:**

2. Cook (2 h): the dish and its accompaniments cooked; household cards.

**Cues:** a dish a French home cook would recognise, with every adaptation named.

**Faults:**
- A dish chosen for its fame, not its fit with the season.
- Unnamed substitutions.

#### CUI-3.M2.L3 — Butter, cream, olive oil and duck fat

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M2.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cook one dish from a butter-and-cream region and one from an oil or duck-fat region; [K] how the cooking fat shapes a regional cuisine
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] How the cooking fat shapes a cuisine:**
  - **butter and cream** (the north and west): rich, rounded sauces; browned butter; gentle heat, because butter burns;
  - **olive oil** (the south): fruity, peppery, high-heat friendly; raw as a finish;
  - **duck and goose fat** (the South-West): savoury, crisp potatoes and confit;
  - **lard** (Alsace and the centre): pork-based dishes and pastry.
- **One dish from each side:** a butter-and-cream dish (for example chicken in cider and cream, leeks in cream, a
  *gratin dauphinois*) and an oil or duck-fat dish (for example a *ratatouille* in summer, *pommes sarladaises* in duck
  fat).
- **The fat in the flavour profile:** what the fat carries (aromatics in butter, garlic and herbs in oil) and what it
  needs to balance it (acid, wine, mustard).

**Sessions:**
1. The butter dish (75 min): a dish from Normandy or Burgundy.
2. The oil or duck-fat dish (75 min): a dish from Provence or the South-West.

**Cues:** each dish tastes of its fat and region.

**Faults:**
- Burnt butter where browned was meant.
- Olive oil heated past its smoke point.

#### CUI-3.M2.L4 — A regional French meal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M2.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, JD-CHSE→3*

*Objectives:* [S] *entrée*, *plat* and cheese or dessert from one region; [J] the order, portions and drink of a French home meal
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The French home meal:** an *entrée* (a salad, a soup, a terrine), a *plat* with its garnish, then cheese or dessert
  (or both); bread on the table; water and a regional wine, or cider.
- **[J] Order, portions and drink:** a light *entrée* before a rich *plat*; modest portions per course; the cheese before
  the dessert; the drink from the same region.
- **Planning:** most of the meal prepared ahead (a braise, a terrine from `GRD-3`, a tart from `PST-3`), so the cook sits
  at the table.

**Sessions:**
1. The meal (3 h): a three-course meal from one region for the household or guests.

**Cues:** portions that leave room for the next course; the courses flowing.

**Faults:**
- Three rich courses.
- A huge *entrée*.
- Cheese after the dessert.

#### CUI-3.M2.X2 — The same vegetable in butter and in olive oil

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-cui-3-m2-x2 -->
*comparison, framework* — The same vegetable cooked in butter and in olive oil, the regional way.
<!-- END generated:exercise-cui-3-m2-x2 -->

**Evidences:** CK-CUIS→3, FL-ARCH→3

**Aim:** taste how the regional fat changes a dish.

**Conditions:** framework support: the comparison form. One seasonal vegetable (for example leeks, carrots or
courgettes) cooked two regional ways: in butter (a northern style, perhaps with cream or cider) and in olive oil (a
southern style, with garlic and herbs); tasted side by side; each described with the lens and a flavour plan.

**Inputs:** the vegetable, butter, olive oil, the regional aromatics.

**Procedure:**
1. Write the two flavour plans.
2. Cook both; taste side by side; tasters describe.
3. Write which region each belongs to and why.

**Evidence:** quiz, photo, tasters, written, form — the second French dish of `CK-CUIS` level 3, recognised by its style
and the tasters; a further dish with a flavour plan for `FL-ARCH` level 3.

**Time:** 1 h.

#### CUI-3.M3.L1 — Regional Italy

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] north versus south; *cucina povera*; pasta and sauce logic; meal structure
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] North against south:** the north with butter, rice (*risotto*), polenta, fresh egg pasta and cured meats; the
  south with olive oil, dried durum-wheat pasta, tomatoes, vegetables and fish.
- ***Cucina povera*:** cooking from few, humble ingredients made good: bread soups, beans, greens, pasta with breadcrumbs,
  using everything.
- **Pasta and sauce logic:** the shape matches the sauce (long and flat with butter and cream or ragù; tubes and ridges
  with chunky sauces; small shapes in soups); the pasta finished in the sauce with a little cooking water; sauced, not
  drowned.
- **Meal structure:** *antipasto*, *primo* (pasta, rice or soup), *secondo* (meat or fish) with a *contorno*
  (vegetables), *dolce*; at home often only *primo* and *secondo*. Wine and water at the table, an espresso after.
- **Few ingredients, high quality:** the best olive oil, cheese and tomatoes the cook can find; Dutch summer tomatoes
  and winter greens (*cavolo nero* grows well in the Netherlands) fit.

**Sessions:**
1. Two pastas (60 min): a southern dried-pasta dish (for example with tomato, garlic and olive oil) and a northern
   butter-and-cheese pasta; compared with the lens.

**Cues:** the pasta finished in the sauce, glossy and clinging.

**Faults:**
- Sauce poured over plain pasta.
- Rinsing pasta.
- Ten ingredients in one sauce.

#### CUI-3.M3.L2 — Fresh egg pasta

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M3.L2` · moves TS-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] mix, knead, rest, roll and cut by hand or machine; filled pasta introduced
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The dough:** flour (Italian "00" or a mix with semolina) and eggs by weight (the classic is roughly one egg per 100 g
  of flour, adjusted by feel); mixed in a well, kneaded 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic, wrapped and rested 30
  minutes.
- **Rolling:** by machine (through the settings, folded and passed a few times on the widest setting first) or by hand
  with a long pin; to an even thickness (thin enough to see the shadow of a hand for tagliatelle, thinner for filled
  pasta); checked against a ruler.
- **Cutting:** tagliatelle, *pappardelle*, *lasagne* sheets; dusted with semolina so they don't stick.
- **Cooking:** in plenty of well-salted boiling water for one to three minutes; finished in the sauce.
- **Storage:** fresh pasta dried in nests, or frozen; cooked fresh pasta treated as leftovers (§9).

**Sessions:**
1. Make and roll (75 min): a batch of dough made and rested, rolled to two thicknesses, photographed against a ruler.
2. Cut and cook (60 min): tagliatelle cut and cooked, finished in a simple butter sauce; the thickness and bite judged.

**Cues:**
- A smooth, elastic dough.
- Even sheets that show the hand's shadow.
- Cooked in minutes, with bite.

**Faults:**
- A sticky dough with too much egg.
- Uneven sheets that cook unevenly.
- Cutting without dusting, so the strands clump.

#### CUI-3.M3.L3 — Filled pasta and *gnocchi*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M3.L2` · moves TS-STA→4, FL-TEXT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] ravioli and tortellini shapes, sealed without air; potato or ricotta *gnocchi*; [K] why floury potatoes and little flour make light *gnocchi*
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Filled pasta:** a thin sheet, a filling that is dry and well seasoned (ricotta and greens, pumpkin, mushrooms, a
  braise), sealed without air pockets (air expands and bursts them), with water or egg wash; shapes: ravioli,
  *tortellini*, *agnolotti*.
- **Fillings with raw meat** are avoided at this level; cooked fillings (a braise from `SLW-3`) are cooled before use.
- **[K] Light *gnocchi*:** floury potatoes, baked or steamed (not boiled wet), riced while hot, with as little flour as
  will hold them, mixed briefly. Waxy potatoes and extra flour make them dense and gluey (`STA-1`). Ricotta *gnocchi*
  use drained ricotta, egg and a little flour.
- **Cooking:** in gently boiling water, lifted out a moment after they float; finished in butter and sage or a light
  sauce.

**Sessions:**
1. Ravioli and *tortellini* (75 min): a ricotta-and-greens or pumpkin filling; two shapes; cooked and served.
2. *Gnocchi* (60 min): potato *gnocchi* from floury potatoes; a test dumpling cooked before the batch; finished in brown
   butter.

**Cues:**
- Filled shapes that hold, no bursts.
- Light *gnocchi* that keep their shape.

**Faults:**
- Air pockets.
- A wet filling that soaks the dough.
- *Gnocchi* kneaded like bread.

#### CUI-3.M3.L4 — An Italian meal in courses

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M3.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, JD-CHSE→3*

*Objectives:* [S] *primo*, *secondo* and *contorno*; [J] proportions between the courses, and when one simple sauce is enough
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l4 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The Sunday meal:** a *primo* of pasta or *risotto*, a *secondo* of meat or fish (a roast, a braise, grilled fish) with
  a *contorno* of seasonal vegetables, and fruit or a simple *dolce*.
- **[J] Proportions between the courses:** a modest *primo* (about 80 g of dried pasta or 100 g of fresh per person) so
  that the *secondo* is still welcome; the *contorno* served on its own plate or beside, not as a garnish.
- **[J] When one simple sauce is enough:** a *primo* with one sauce of few ingredients, done well, rather than a complex
  one competing with the *secondo*.
- **Timing:** the *secondo* in the oven while the *primo* is eaten; the pasta cooked at the last moment.

**Sessions:**
1. The meal (3 h): a *primo*, a *secondo* with *contorno*, and a *dolce* for the household or guests.

**Cues:** a light *primo*, a welcome *secondo*, the courses timed.

**Faults:**
- A huge plate of pasta before a roast.
- The *contorno* forgotten.
- A rich sauce on both courses.

#### CUI-3.M4.L2 — Rice in a wide pan

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M2.L3` · moves TS-STA→4, IN-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *paella*-style rice: *sofrito*, stock, no stirring, *socarrat*
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***Paella*-style rice:** a wide, shallow pan; a *sofrito*; the main ingredients browned; a well-flavoured stock (fish,
  chicken or vegetable) with saffron; the rice scattered in an even, thin layer; **no stirring** after the rice goes in;
  cooked until the liquid is absorbed and the grains are tender but separate.
- **The *socarrat*:** the thin, caramelised crust on the bottom, made in the last minutes by raising the heat and
  listening for a crackle; crisp and toasty, not burnt.
- **The rice:** short, round, absorbent rice (*bomba*, *calasparra*) absorbs a lot of liquid and stays firm; risotto rice
  releases more starch and goes creamy, which is wrong here. The variety is chosen with a reason (`IN-STA`).
- **A home stove:** a pan wider than the burner heats unevenly; the pan is moved and turned, two burners are used, or the
  rice finishes in the oven; the adaptation is noted.
- **Safety:** chicken browned and cooked through (75 °C), seafood to 63 °C; leftover rice cooled fast (`SCI-2.M6`).

**Sessions:**
1. A vegetable rice (75 min): a wide-pan rice with seasonal vegetables (for example green beans, artichokes in season,
   or pumpkin and chickpeas), the *socarrat* made.
2. A comparison (60 min): the same rice dish made with *bomba* and with risotto rice; the texture compared and
   photographed.

**Cues:**
- An even, thin layer of rice.
- Separate grains.
- A crackling, golden *socarrat*.

**Faults:**
- Stirring.
- Too thick a layer of rice.
- A burnt, black base.

- A quick *sofrito*, raw-tasting.
- Fried, crisp potatoes in the *tortilla* (they should be soft).
- Salt cod soaked on the counter.

#### CUI-3.M5.L3 — Tagines and couscous

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `MEA-2.M6.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-CMB→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a tagine with layered spices and fruit or preserved lemon; couscous steamed in two stages; [K] how the pot shapes the braise
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The tagine** is both the pot (a conical lid that returns condensed steam to the dish) and the braise: meat (lamb
  shoulder, chicken legs) or vegetables cooked slowly with onion, spices (ginger, saffron, cinnamon, cumin, *ras el
  hanout*), little liquid, and fruit (dried apricots or prunes, or Dutch apples or pears), preserved lemon and olives.
- **[K] How the pot shapes the braise:** little liquid and the cone's condensation give a concentrated sauce; on a home
  stove, a heavy casserole (`HEA-2`) with a tight lid works the same way; a heat diffuser protects a clay tagine.
- **Layering spices:** some bloomed with the onions at the start, some (*ras el hanout*, fresh herbs) at the end
  (`FLV-3.M2`).
- **Couscous steamed in two stages:** the grains moistened and rubbed with oil, steamed over the stew (or in a steamer)
  for about 15 minutes, turned out, sprinkled with salted water and broken up, then steamed again until fluffy; quick
  "pour-over" couscous is the shortcut, and the difference is tasted.
- **Doneness:** lamb tender to a fork; chicken to 75 °C.

**Sessions:**
1. A tagine (75 min active, 2 h in total): lamb shoulder or chicken legs with fruit or preserved lemon, in a casserole or a
   tagine.
2. Couscous two ways (45 min): steamed in two stages and the quick method; compared.

**Cues:**
- A concentrated sauce that coats.
- Tender meat.
- Fluffy, separate couscous grains.

**Faults:**
- Too much liquid, a soup.
- Couscous gluey and lumpy.
- All spices at the start, the top notes gone.

#### CUI-3.M6.L2 — A curry from whole spices

**Concepts:**
- **Building a *masala*:**
  1. Whole spices (cumin, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, bay) bloomed in oil or ghee.

  3. Ginger-garlic paste fried until the raw smell goes.
  4. Ground spices (turmeric, coriander, chilli) fried briefly with a splash of water so they don't burn.

- **The sauce:** the main ingredient (chicken thighs, lamb, paneer, chickpeas, seasonal vegetables) simmered in the
  *masala* with water, stock, yoghurt or coconut milk; finished with *garam masala* and fresh coriander.
- **[J] Balancing heat and acid:** chilli in steps (`FLV-3.M2.L4`); acid from tomato, yoghurt, tamarind, lemon or

- **Safety:** chicken to 75 °C; yoghurt added off the boil so it doesn't split.

**Sessions:**
1. A *masala* (60 min): the base built step by step; the "oil separates" stage photographed.
2. The curry (90 min): a chicken, paneer or vegetable curry from the *masala*, balanced and finished with a *tadka* or
   *garam masala*.

**Cues:**
- Deep golden onions.
- The oil separating at the edges.
- Balanced heat and acid.

**Faults:**
- Pale onions and a raw-tasting sauce.
- Ground spices burnt in hot oil.
- Yoghurt boiled and split.

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m6-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***Tadka dal*:** split red lentils, yellow split peas (*toor*, *chana*) or mung, simmered with turmeric until soft and

#### CUI-3.M7.L1 — Regional China

*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Regional cuisines** (traditionally eight great cuisines; five as a start):
  - **Cantonese** (Guangdong): fresh, light, steaming and quick stir-frying, *dim sum*, roast meats;

- **[K] *Wok hei*:** the "breath of the wok": smoky, seared flavour from very high heat, small batches and constant
  movement; on a home stove only partly possible, by heating the wok until it smokes and cooking small amounts.
- **The pantry:** light and dark soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, rice vinegar (and black Chinkiang vinegar), oyster sauce,
  sesame oil (a finishing oil), cornflour, ginger, garlic, spring onion, dried mushrooms, fermented black beans, chilli
  bean paste, Sichuan pepper, star anise.

  restaurant food in the Netherlands is its own tradition, related but distinct.

**Sessions:**

   tasted; one simple dish (smashed cucumber salad, or a quick-braised greens dish) seasoned two regional ways.

**Faults:**

#### CUI-3.M7.L2 — Stir-frying

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-DRY→4*

*Objectives:* [S] prep everything first, velvet the protein, wok heat on a home stove, the sauce at the end
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Prep everything first** (`WRK-1`): the cooking takes minutes, so every ingredient is cut to a similar size, the sauce
  is mixed in a bowl, and everything is lined up in the order it goes in.
- **Velveting:** thin slices of chicken, pork or beef marinated briefly in soy, Shaoxing wine, cornflour and a little egg
  white or oil, then flashed in oil or water; tender, silky meat.
- **Wok heat on a home stove:**
  - the wok heated until it smokes before the oil goes in;
  - small batches, so the wok stays hot (a crowded wok steams);
  - protein seared and lifted out, vegetables cooked, everything back together;
  - a flat-bottomed carbon-steel wok or a large heavy pan on the biggest burner; on induction, a flat wok.
- **The sauce at the end:** the premixed sauce (soy, wine, stock, sugar, cornflour) poured in at the end and tossed until
  glossy and coating.
- **Safety:** a smoking wok and oil: no water near the oil, a lid at hand, the extraction on (`HEA-2.M4`); chicken to
  75 °C, pork and beef slices cooked through for a family meal.

**Sessions:**
1. A stir-fry (75 min): a velveted chicken or pork stir-fry with seasonal vegetables (for example pointed cabbage, leeks,
   green beans, mushrooms), the batches timed.
2. A vegetable stir-fry (45 min): greens with garlic, and a second vegetable dish, on the same hot wok.

**Cues:**
- Seared, not steamed.
- Vegetables bright and crisp-tender.
- A glossy sauce that coats.

**Faults:**
- A crowded, steaming wok.
- Cutting during cooking.
- Cornflour in lumps.

#### CUI-3.M7.L3 — Dumplings and steaming

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M7.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-MOI→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a dumpling dough, a filling and two folds; steamed fish with ginger and spring onion; [K] hot-water versus cold-water dough
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Hot-water against cold-water dough:** boiling water partly cooks the starch and weakens the gluten, giving a
  soft, tender dough that is easy to roll thin and suits steamed and pan-fried dumplings; cold water keeps the gluten
  strong, giving a chewier dough that holds up to boiling.
- **The filling:** minced pork (or chicken, or a vegetarian mix of mushrooms, cabbage and tofu) with ginger, spring
  onion, soy, Shaoxing wine and sesame oil, beaten until sticky; cabbage salted and squeezed to remove water.
- **Two folds:** a half-moon with pleats, and a simple pinched fold; sealed well.
- **Cooking:** boiled (added to boiling water, brought back to the boil with a splash of cold water, until they float
  and the filling is cooked); or pan-fried (*guotie*: browned, then steamed with water under a lid, then crisped). A
  minced-pork filling reaches 75 °C (§9); one dumpling is cut and probed.
- **Steamed fish with ginger and spring onion:** a whole fish (a North Sea fish or a Dutch-farmed trout) steamed over
  boiling water until it reaches 63 °C at the bone (§9), then topped with shredded ginger and spring onion, hot oil and a
  light soy dressing.

**Sessions:**
1. Dumplings (90 min): a hot-water dough, a filling, two folds; boiled and pan-fried.
2. Steamed fish (60 min): a whole fish steamed, probed and dressed.

**Cues:**
- Thin, sealed wrappers.
- A juicy, cooked filling at 75 °C.
- Fish just opaque at the bone.

**Faults:**
- Watery filling that bursts the wrapper.
- Thick, doughy pleats.
- Overcooked, dry steamed fish.

#### CUI-3.M8.L1 — Dashi and Japanese principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l1 -->

<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l1 -->

**Concepts:**

- The umami obvious once salted.

**Faults:**
- Boiling the *kombu* (slimy, bitter).

#### CUI-3.M8.L3 — Simmering and grilling the Japanese way

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M8.L2` · moves CK-CUIS→3, FL-SEAS→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *nimono* (simmered vegetables in seasoned dashi) and *yakimono* (salt-grilled fish or teriyaki); [K] the *sa-shi-su-se-so* seasoning order
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- ***Nimono*:** vegetables (daikon or turnip, carrot, pumpkin, potato, mushrooms; Dutch seasonal roots fit well) simmered
  gently in seasoned *dashi* under a drop lid (*otoshibuta*: a lid that rests on the food, keeping it submerged and the
  pieces from tumbling); they absorb the seasoning and stay intact.
- **[K] The *sa-shi-su-se-so* order:** sugar (*sa*), salt (*shi*), vinegar (*su*), soy sauce (*se*, old spelling) and miso
  (*so*) added in that order: sugar first because its larger molecules penetrate slowly and salt firms the food; soy
  and miso last because their aroma fades with heat.
- ***Yakimono*:** salt-grilled fish (*shioyaki*: a whole mackerel or fillets salted 20–30 minutes ahead, grilled until
  crisp and cooked to 63 °C at the bone), or *teriyaki* (chicken thighs or fish glazed with soy, *mirin* and sugar, the
  glaze added near the end so it doesn't burn; chicken to 75 °C).
- **Seasoning by taste:** each dish's balance adjusted and noted (`FL-SEAS`).

**Sessions:**
1. *Nimono* (60 min): seasonal roots simmered under a drop lid, seasoned in order.
2. *Yakimono* (60 min): salt-grilled mackerel or a chicken *teriyaki*, probed.

**Cues:**
- Vegetables intact and seasoned through.
- Crisp-skinned fish at 63 °C.
- A glossy glaze, not burnt.

**Faults:**
- Soy sauce at the start of a long simmer.
- No drop lid, broken vegetables.
- The teriyaki glaze burnt.

**Concepts:**

#### CUI-3.M9.L1 — The Mexican pantry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] fresh and dried chillies, corn and *nixtamal*, beans; regional styles
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Chillies, fresh and dried:** fresh (jalapeño, serrano, *poblano*) for brightness and heat; dried (*ancho*,
  *guajillo*, *pasilla*, *chipotle*) toasted, soaked and blended for depth, colour and sweetness. A dried chilli is a
  different ingredient from its fresh form (*ancho* is a dried *poblano*).
- **[K] Corn and *nixtamal*:** dried corn cooked with lime (calcium hydroxide, *cal*), which loosens the hulls, makes the
  dough hold together and improves its nutrition; ground into *masa* for tortillas and *tamales*.
- **Beans:** black, pinto, cooked slowly with onion and herbs (*epazote*), often refried; dried beans follow the bean rule
  of §9.
- **Regional styles:** the north (wheat tortillas, grilled beef), Oaxaca (moles, cheese), the Yucatán (achiote, sour
  orange, pit-cooking), central Mexico (street food, *tacos*).
- **Meal structure and drinks:** the main meal at midday; tortillas at every meal; salsas on the table; *agua fresca*
  (fruit or flower water), water, beer.

**Sessions:**
1. A chilli tasting (60 min): three dried chillies toasted, soaked and blended into small salsas; a fresh salsa
   alongside; tasted for heat, sweetness and smoke.

**Cues:** the learner can tell *ancho*, *guajillo* and *chipotle* apart by taste and use.

**Faults:**
- Burning dried chillies while toasting (seconds per side).
- Using chilli powder blends for everything.

#### CUI-3.M9.L2 — Salsas and a braise

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 3 h · builds on `FLV-3.M2.L4` · moves FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] toasted-chilli and fresh salsas; a braise in a chilli sauce
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Fresh salsas:** *pico de gallo* (tomato, onion, chilli, coriander, lime) in summer with Dutch tomatoes; a raw green
  salsa; seasoned and balanced; kept in the fridge and out for at most 2 hours (§9).
- **Cooked salsas:** tomatoes, tomatillos (or green tomatoes from a Dutch late-season glut as a stand-in), chillies, onion
  and garlic charred on a dry pan (`VEG-3.M2`) and blended; a toasted-dried-chilli salsa.
- **A braise in a chilli sauce:** dried chillies toasted, soaked and blended with aromatics and spices into a sauce,
  fried briefly to deepen it, then pork shoulder or chicken legs braised in it until tender (`SLW-3`); chicken to 75 °C.
- **Balance:** heat against acid, salt and a little sweetness; the salsa chosen to contrast the braise.

**Sessions:**
1. Two salsas (60 min): a fresh salsa and a charred, blended salsa.
2. The braise (90 min active, 3 h in total): a pork or chicken braise in a dried-chilli sauce.

**Cues:**
- Salsas bright and balanced.
- A deep red, glossy braising sauce.
- Tender meat.

**Faults:**
- Burnt, bitter chillies.
- A raw-tasting sauce not fried after blending.
- A salsa left out all afternoon.

#### CUI-3.M9.L3 — *Masa* and tortillas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M9.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] corn tortillas from *masa harina*, pressed and cooked on a dry pan; tacos and a simple *tamal*; [K] *nixtamalización*
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] *Nixtamalización*:** the lime treatment of corn (`CUI-3.M9.L1`), which is why tortillas can be made from corn
  alone; *masa harina* is dried, ground *nixtamal* corn, mixed with water to a dough.
- **Corn tortillas:**
  - *masa harina* and warm water mixed to a soft, smooth dough like play-dough, rested, adjusted with water until the
    edges don't crack when pressed;
  - balls pressed between two sheets of plastic in a tortilla press (or with a heavy pan);
  - cooked on a dry, hot cast-iron pan (a *comal*): turned once, then again; a good tortilla puffs;
  - kept warm wrapped in a cloth.
- **Tacos:** a warm tortilla, a filling, a salsa, onion and coriander; simple.
- **A simple *tamal*:** *masa* beaten with fat and stock, spread on a corn husk, filled, folded and steamed until it comes
  away from the husk.

**Sessions:**
1. Tortillas (75 min): a batch pressed and cooked; the puff photographed; tacos.
2. *Tamales* (75 min active, 2 h in total): a small batch with a vegetable or the braise as filling, steamed.

**Cues:**
- Tortillas that puff and stay pliable.
- *Tamales* that come away from the husk.

**Faults:**
- A dry dough that cracks.
- A pan not hot enough, stiff tortillas.
- Stacking hot tortillas uncovered, so they dry.
