## Introduction

The *garde manger* is the cold station of a classical kitchen: salads, dressings, cold sauces, cured fish, terrines,
cheese and charcuterie, cold starters and canapés. For a home cook it is the station of **preparing ahead**: almost
everything here is made hours or days before it is served, which makes it the backbone of cooking for guests.

The course builds on the vinaigrettes and mayonnaise of `SAU-2` and the fish of `FSH-2`:
- **salads** as main courses, leafy and composed, with grains and legumes;
- **dressings and cold sauces** by ratio, across cuisines;
- **cured and marinated fish**, with the parasite rule of assessment §9;
- **a terrine**, the first forcemeat cooked to a core temperature and pressed;
- **cold platters**: a cheese and charcuterie board, a plated cold starter, sandwiches, toasts and canapés.

Food held cold is still food at risk. The rules of
[assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment) apply throughout: the fridge at 4 °C,
platters out of the fridge for at most 2 hours, leftovers kept at most 2 days. Raw and lightly cooked animal foods
(cured or marinated fish, raw-yolk sauces, raw-milk cheese and raw cured meats) are not served to risk groups, and fish
eaten raw or cured is frozen first.

Leaves and vegetables follow the Dutch seasons: lettuces, rocket and spinach from spring to autumn; chicory (*witlof*),
lamb's lettuce (*veldsla*), winter purslane, red cabbage, kale and roots in winter. Cheeses are Dutch where possible
(farmhouse Gouda, aged Gouda, goat's cheese, a Dutch blue).

**In the spiral.**
- `TS-GRD` reaches level 3 across three modules: the cured fish, the terrine and the cold starter.
- `TS-SAU` reaches level 4 with `SLW-3` and `FIR-3`; this course adds the cold sauces and the repaired emulsion.
- `CR-COMP`, `FL-TEXT` and `RD-RATO` reach level 3, and `IN-DAI` level 4.
- `MS-S3` serves a cold starter in its two-course evening (`MS-S3.M1.L3`).

**Pacing.** About four weeks. The cure takes two days and the terrine two (cooked, then pressed overnight); the salads
are the week's main courses.

#### GRD-3.M1.L1 — Leafy salads

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M6.L1` · moves TS-GRD→2*

*Objectives:* [S] wash, dry, dress just before serving; [J] match leaves and dressing
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m1-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Washing and drying:** leaves soaked in plenty of cold water, lifted out (grit sinks), spun completely dry; wet
  leaves dilute the dressing and won't hold it.
- **Keeping:** dried leaves rolled in a damp towel in a container in the fridge stay crisp for a day or two.
- **Dressing at the last moment:** the dressing in the bottom of a wide bowl, the leaves on top, tossed by hand just
  before serving, with just enough to coat. Seasoned leaves taste better than a seasoned dressing poured over.
- **[J] Matching leaves and dressing:**
  - tender leaves (butter lettuce, lamb's lettuce) take a light vinaigrette;
  - sturdy and bitter leaves (chicory, radicchio, kale, frisée) take a robust, creamy or warm dressing;
  - peppery leaves (rocket, watercress) like acid and a little sweetness.
- **Adding interest:** herbs as leaves, shaved raw vegetables, a crunchy element (`FLV-3.M4`), a cheese.

**Sessions:**
1. Three leaves, three dressings (45 min): a tender, a bitter and a peppery leaf, each dressed two ways; the best
   match reasoned on the form.
2. A side salad for dinner (30 min): washed, dried and dressed at the table, photographed within a minute.

**Cues:**
- Leaves glossy, not wet or soggy.
- No dressing pooling.
- Each leaf seasoned.

**Faults:**
- Dressing an hour ahead.
- Wet leaves.
- A heavy dressing on tender leaves.

#### GRD-3.M1.L2 — Composed, grain and legume salads

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m1-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `GRD-3.M1.L1` · moves TS-GRD→3, CR-COMP→3*

*Objectives:* [S] balance base, contrast, dressing, garnish; dressing warm grains and legumes
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m1-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The parts of a composed salad:** a base (leaves, grains, legumes, potatoes), a main element (a protein or a roasted
  vegetable), a contrast (crunch, acid, bitterness), a dressing that ties it, and a garnish (herbs, seeds).
- **Grains and legumes:** dressed while still warm, because they absorb the dressing (and salt) as they cool; then the
  tender elements added cold.
- **Balance:** taste, texture, temperature and colour; the component table names what each part brings.
- **Main-course salads:** enough protein and starch to satisfy; dense enough to be a meal.
- **Seasonal examples:** a warm potato and smoked-mackerel salad with mustard dressing; a lentil, roasted beetroot and
  goat's cheese salad; a barley, kale and apple salad; a *salade niçoise*-style plate in summer.

**Sessions:**
1. A grain or legume salad (75 min): dressed warm, finished cold; tasted after an hour.
2. A composed plate (75 min): a composed salad assembled from its components, photographed.

**Cues:**
- Grains seasoned through.
- Each component distinct.
- A clear contrast.

**Faults:**
- Grains dressed cold, tasting flat inside.
- Everything chopped together into a mush.
- No acid.

#### GRD-3.M2.L1 — Emulsified and creamy dressings

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAU-2.M4.L1` · moves TS-SAU→4, RD-RATO→3*

*Objectives:* [S] mustard, yoghurt, buttermilk and tahini dressings; [K] ratios and stability
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m2-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] Ratios:**
  - a vinaigrette: about three parts oil to one part acid by volume, varied to taste (`SAU-2.M6`);
  - a mustard-emulsified dressing: mustard as the emulsifier, holding the oil longer;
  - creamy dressings: yoghurt, buttermilk, crème fraîche or mayonnaise as the base, thinned with acid and water, with
    herbs; tahini dressing: tahini, lemon, water and garlic.
- **[K] Stability:** a temporary emulsion (vinaigrette) separates and is re-shaken; an emulsifier (mustard, egg yolk,
  tahini, honey) holds it; dairy bases are stable but thin with acid and salt over hours.
- **Repairing a broken emulsion:** a fresh yolk or a spoon of mustard in a clean bowl, the broken sauce whisked in drop
  by drop (`SAU-2.M4`).
- **Storing:** dressings with dairy, egg or fresh garlic in the fridge, used within 2 days (assessment §9); a plain
  vinaigrette keeps longer in the fridge.

**Sessions:**
1. Four dressings (45 min): a mustard vinaigrette, a buttermilk-herb, a yoghurt and a tahini dressing, by ratio;
   stability checked after 30 minutes.
2. Break and repair (30 min): a mayonnaise-based dressing broken on purpose (oil added too fast) and repaired; filmed.

**Cues:**
- Each dressing at coating consistency.
- The repaired emulsion glossy and stable.

**Faults:**
- No emulsifier in a dressing meant to hold.
- Tahini that seizes because the water went in too fast.
- A creamy dressing kept a week.

#### GRD-3.M2.L2 — Cold sauces

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `GRD-3.M2.L1` · moves TS-SAU→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *gribiche*, *salsa verde*, *tzatziki*, *chimichurri*, *romesco*; [J] pair them with dishes
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m2-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **Five cold sauces and their logic:**
  - ***gribiche*** (France): a mayonnaise-like emulsion on cooked egg yolk, with capers, gherkins and herbs; for fish,
    asparagus, cold meat;
  - ***salsa verde*** (Italy): chopped herbs, capers, anchovy, oil and vinegar (`FLV-3.M1`);
  - ***tzatziki*** (Greece): strained yoghurt, salted and drained cucumber, garlic, dill;
  - ***chimichurri*** (Argentina): parsley, oregano, garlic, chilli, vinegar and oil; for grilled meat;
  - ***romesco*** (Catalonia): roasted peppers and tomatoes, bread, olive oil, vinegar; traditionally with almonds or
    hazelnuts, here made with toasted bread and seeds, or with nuts only where no guest is allergic.
- **[J] Pairing:** rich with sharp (*gribiche* with cold beef), grilled with herby and acid (*chimichurri* with steak),
  spicy with cooling (*tzatziki* with lamb).
- **Base ratios:** two families cover most cold sauces: an **emulsion base** (yolk or mustard and oil) and an **herb-acid
  base** (herbs, acid, oil, salt). The variation is in the flavourings.

**Sessions:**
1. Two sauces of each family (75 min): *gribiche* and *tzatziki*; *salsa verde* and *chimichurri*.
2. Pair them (45 min): each with a dish (cold beef, asparagus or a winter vegetable, grilled meat, roasted lamb), the
   choice reasoned.

**Cues:**
- Each sauce balanced and at its consistency.
- The pairings reasoned.

**Faults:**
- Watery *tzatziki* from unsalted cucumber.
- *Chimichurri* blended to a purée.
- A sauce that drowns the dish.

#### GRD-3.M3.L1 — Gravad salmon

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 d · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L3`, `FSH-2.M2.L1` · moves TS-GRD→3, SA-HRPR→4*

*Objectives:* [S] salt and sugar ratio, time, pressing; [K] freezing to kill parasites
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m3-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **What a cure does:** salt draws out water and seasons the flesh; sugar balances the salt and adds to the firming;
  the flesh becomes denser, silkier and translucent. A cure seasons and changes texture; it does **not** make the fish
  safe from parasites.
- **[K] Freezing first** (assessment §9): fish eaten cured or raw is frozen to −20 °C or below in all parts for at least
  24 hours, or bought with that treatment (ask the fishmonger for fish frozen for raw use), or frozen at home only in a
  freezer shown by a thermometer to hold −20 °C or below, for 7 days. A freezer at −18 °C does not qualify. Thawed in the
  fridge.
- **The cure by ratio:** salt and sugar weighed as a percentage of the fish's weight (the framework's ratio, written in
  the log), with dill and pepper; the fillet skin-on, covered, pressed under a light weight in the fridge at 4 °C for
  about two days, turned once a day; the liquid poured off.
- **Finishing:** the cure rinsed or scraped off, the fillet dried, sliced thin at an angle off the skin.
- **Storing and serving:** wrapped in the fridge at 4 °C and eaten within 2 days of finishing the cure (the program
  applies the leftovers rule of §9, having no checked figure for longer); not served to risk groups (§9).

**Sessions:**
1. The cure (45 min, then 2 days): the salmon of `GRD-3.M3.X1` weighed, cured and pressed; turned daily; finished and
   sliced.

**Cues:**
- Firm, translucent, evenly coloured flesh.
- Thin slices that hold together.

**Faults:**
- Unfrozen fresh salmon.
- The cure guessed, not weighed.
- Slicing thick and against the grain.

#### GRD-3.M3.L2 — Acid-marinated fish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 h · builds on `GRD-3.M3.L1` · moves TS-GRD→3*

*Objectives:* [S] *ceviche*-style fish; [K] acid doesn't kill pathogens the way heat does
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m3-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **What acid does:** it denatures the surface proteins, so the fish turns opaque and firm as if cooked. It is not cooked:
  **[K] acid does not kill pathogens the way heat does, and it does not kill parasites**.
- **The rules** (assessment §9): fish frozen first as for the cure; very fresh, kept cold, marinated briefly and served
  at once; not served to risk groups.
- ***Ceviche*-style:** small cubes or thin slices of firm white fish (previously frozen), lime or lemon juice, salt,
  onion, chilli and herbs; marinated 10–20 minutes for a tender bite, longer for a firmer texture.
- **Other traditions:** soused herring (*zure haring*) and pickled fish use vinegar and are sold frozen-treated by law;
  the home version starts from fish frozen first.

**Sessions:**
1. A *ceviche*-style starter (45 min active, 1 h in total): previously frozen white fish marinated two times (10 and 30
   minutes) and compared for texture.

**Cues:**
- Opaque outside, slightly translucent inside at the shorter time.
- Bright and fresh.

**Faults:**
- Fresh unfrozen wild fish.
- Marinating for hours until chalky.
- Serving it to a pregnant guest.

#### GRD-3.M3.X1 — Gravad salmon by ratio

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-grd-3-m3-x1 -->
*framework, framework* — Gravad salmon by ratio, with a curing log.
<!-- END generated:exercise-grd-3-m3-x1 -->

**Evidences:** TS-GRD→3, SA-HRPR→4

**Aim:** cure a salmon fillet by a weighed ratio, safely, with a complete log.

**Conditions:** framework support: the cure framework and the curing log. A salmon fillet frozen first by the §9 rule
(the proof kept: the label or the freezer log), thawed in the fridge, cured by weight ratio (salt and sugar to fish
logged), pressed for about two days, sliced and served; the log shows every control point (freezing, thawing,

**Procedure:**
1. Freeze (or buy frozen-treated) and log it; thaw in the fridge.
2. Weigh the fish; calculate and weigh the cure; log it.
3. Cure, press and turn; log the fridge temperature daily.
4. Finish, slice, photograph the slices; serve to non-risk-group eaters.
5. Store in the fridge and use within 2 days.

**Evidence:** photo, measure, log, quiz, written:
- **`TS-GRD` level 3, the cured-fish part:** a cured fish by weight ratio, salt and sugar to fish logged, sliced thin
  (photographed). The terrine and the cold starter follow in `GRD-3.M4.X1` and `GRD-3.M5.X1`.
- **`SA-HRPR` level 4, further evidence:** a high-risk process (raw fish) by a tested rule, every control point logged;
  the scenario items at 100 %.

**Time:** 1 h active over two days.

#### GRD-3.M4.L1 — Forcemeat principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MEA-2.M5.L2` · moves TS-GRD→3, RD-RATO→3*

*Objectives:* [K] meat, fat and salt ratios; binding; temperature during mixing
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m4-l1 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] A forcemeat** (*farce*) is ground meat and fat, seasoned and bound so it slices cleanly when cold:
  - **meat and fat:** fat carries flavour and keeps it moist; a country terrine is coarse, with a high proportion of
    pork fat;
  - **salt:** weighed as a percentage of the meat, it seasons and dissolves the proteins (myosin), which bind the mix
    (`MEA-2.M5`);
  - **binders:** egg, a *panade* (bread soaked in milk or cream);
  - **flavour:** shallots, garlic, herbs, *quatre épices* (`FLV-3.M2`), a little alcohol.
- **[K] Temperature during mixing:** the meat, fat and equipment kept very cold (below about 4 °C, the fridge), so the
  fat doesn't smear and the bind holds; a fried test patty seasons it before it goes into the mould.
- **Hygiene:** minced meat is a high-risk raw food; the grinder parts and bowls chilled and washed hot afterwards.

**Sessions:**
1. A forcemeat and a test (60 min): a small pork forcemeat mixed cold by a weighed ratio; a test patty fried, tasted and
   the seasoning adjusted; the bind checked.

**Cues:**
- The mix sticky and bound.
- The fat in distinct pieces, not smeared.
- The test patty seasoned well.

**Faults:**
- Warm meat and a smeared, greasy mix.
- Tasting raw forcemeat.
- Salt by eye.

#### GRD-3.M4.L2 — A terrine or pâté

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m4-l2 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 2 d · builds on `GRD-3.M4.L1` · moves TS-GRD→3*

*Objectives:* [S] a country terrine or chicken-liver pâté; bake in a water bath to a core temperature; press and rest
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m4-l2 -->

**Concepts:**
- **The mould:** a terrine mould or a loaf tin, lined with streaky bacon or baking paper, packed without air pockets,
  covered.
- **Baking in a water bath:** the mould in a roasting tin of hot water in a moderate oven (about 150–160 °C), so the edges
  don't overcook before the centre is done.
- **The core temperature** (assessment §9): a terrine of minced pork reaches 70 °C at the centre; one containing poultry
  or chicken livers reaches 74 °C. The probe goes into the very centre.
- **Pressing and resting:** weighted overnight in the fridge (a board and tins) so it slices cleanly; cooled by the
  cooling standard first (out of the water bath, onto a rack, into the fridge within the 2-hour limit).
- **A chicken-liver pâté** is the smooth alternative: livers cooked in butter to 74 °C at the centre, blended with butter
  and a little alcohol, set in ramekins and sealed with butter.
- **Storing:** in the fridge, at most the 2 days of the leftovers rule once cut, unless the guided recipe's tested
  method says otherwise.

**Sessions:**
1. The terrine (90 min active, 2 days in total): the terrine of `GRD-3.M4.X1` packed, baked to its core temperature,
   cooled, pressed and sliced.

**Cues:**
- The core at its §9 figure.
- A clean slice that holds.
- A thin layer of set jelly round the edge.

**Faults:**
- No water bath, dry edges.
- Probing near the edge.
- Slicing before it has set overnight.

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m5-l1 -->

*Objectives:* [K] cheese families and serving temperature; [S] a balanced board with accompaniments

**Concepts:**

**Sessions:**

- Varied families, textures and milks.
- Each cheese with its own knife.
- Served at temperature.

**Faults:**
- Five Goudas.
- Cheese straight from the fridge.
- A board left out all evening.

#### GRD-3.M5.L3 — Sandwiches, toasts and canapés

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-grd-3-m5-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `GRD-3.M2.L1` · moves CR-COMP→3, FL-TEXT→3*

*Objectives:* [K] structure of a sandwich (bread, barrier, filling, contrast); [S] hot and cold sandwiches, open toasts and bite-sized canapés; [J] what holds up for an hour on a platter
<!-- END generated:lesson-grd-3-m5-l3 -->

**Concepts:**
- **[K] The structure of a sandwich:** bread, a barrier (butter, a thick spread or cheese that keeps moisture from the
  bread), the filling, and a contrast (crunch, acid, a fresh herb); in that order.
- **Hot and cold sandwiches:** a toasted cheese sandwich (*tosti*) with even colour and a melted centre; a club or a
  *broodje* with a layered filling; an open sandwich on rye (*smørrebrød*-style).
- **Open toasts and canapés:** a sturdy base (toasted bread, rye crisps, cucumber or chicory leaves, a blini), a
  topping, a garnish; one or two bites each.
- **[J] What holds up for an hour on a platter:**
  - firm bases, barriers, toppings that don't weep;
  - leaves and crisp elements added at the last moment;
  - nothing hot that should be eaten hot;
  - everything within the 2-hour limit out of the fridge (§9).
- **Amounts:** for a gathering, about six to eight canapés per person per hour before dinner, more if they replace it.

**Sessions:**
1. A sandwich platter and canapés (75 min): two sandwiches and three canapés; photographed at once and after an hour.

**Cues:**
- Bases still crisp after an hour.
- Each canapé one or two bites.
- Clean edges.

**Faults:**
- Soggy bread from an unbarriered filling.
- Canapés too big to eat in two bites.
- A platter out for the whole afternoon.
