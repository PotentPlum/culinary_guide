**Before you start.** This course comes last in S4 because it brings the stage together: the plating of `PLT-4`, the
recipe development of `RDV-4` and the kitchen management of `KMG-3`, applied to a dinner for guests. You will learn to:

- **make them feasible:** portions and a consolidated order for six to eight, a make-ahead plan, and where to buy what;
- **run the kitchen:** a timeline from a week before (T−7) to service, several courses on four burners and one oven,

- **pair drinks:** wine, beer, cider and fortified wines, the non-alcoholic drinks, the principles of pairing, and

- **deliver:** a dinner project for four to six guests, which rehearses `MS-S4`.

**Drinks and guests** (assessment §9):
- every menu with drinks has a non-alcoholic pairing for every course;
- no alcohol for anyone under 18, none in pregnancy, and the advice not to drink while breastfeeding;
- a dish cooked with alcohol is not alcohol-free: 85 % of the alcohol remains when it is stirred into a boiling liquid
  and taken off the heat, 75 % when flamed, still 5 % after 2.5 hours of simmering.

The course teaches tasting and pairing, not drinking: tastings are small sips, spitting is normal, and the alcohol-free
route through the module is complete on its own.

Produce is Dutch and in season. The drinks include Dutch wines (Limburg, Gelderland, Zeeland), Dutch beers and ciders,
and Dutch alcohol-free drinks and juices.

Plan about five weeks at 6.5 hours a week. Every module builds towards the dinner project: the order list

### MNU-4.M1.L1 — Progression and balance

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M1.L1` · moves CR-MENU→3*

*Objectives:* [K] light to rich, variety of technique, colour, texture and temperature; avoiding repetition
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** good courses can still make a tiring menu. A menu that rises and falls, varies its techniques and
never repeats itself leaves guests wanting the next course, and that is a skill separate from cooking each dish.

**You need:** three published menus (restaurant websites, cookbooks or menus you kept); the checklist below; a pen in
two colours.

**Explanation:**

*Light to rich.* A menu rises from light, fresh and acidic towards the richest course, usually the main, then steps down
to a dessert that refreshes or comforts without overwhelming.

*Variety:*
- **technique:** not two braises or two fried courses; a raw or cured element, a roast or sear, a slow cook;
- **colour:** each course with its own colours; the menu not all brown or all white;
- **texture:** crisp, creamy, tender and chewy spread across the courses (`FL-TEXT`);
- **temperature:** a cold starter, a hot main, a frozen or warm dessert.

*Avoiding repetition.*
- No main ingredient twice (cream in three courses, tomato in two).
- No flavour that tires (garlic in every course).
- No two courses with the same structure (a mousse and a terrine).

*Portions across the menu* (`PLT-4.M1.L2`). The sum matters: a four-course menu has smaller courses than a two-course
one.

*The checklist:*

| Point | Ask |
|---|---|
| Progression | Does it rise to the main and step down? |
| Technique | Is each course cooked a different way? |
| Colour, texture, temperature | Does each course bring something new? |
| Repetition | Any ingredient, flavour or structure twice? |
| Portions | Does the sum fit the number of courses? |

**Practice:**
1. **Menus marked (45 min).** Mark three published menus against the checklist for progression, variety and
   repetition. Rewrite one.

**Look for:** you can mark the progression, the variety and any repetition on a given menu.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The main feels heavy and dull | A rich starter that blunts it | A light, acidic starter |
| Guests tire by the main | Cream, butter or cheese in every course | One rich course; acid and freshness around it |

**Safety:** a menu planned for guests notes every allergen per course from the start (`NUT-3`), so the checklist covers
it before the shopping does.

**Record:** the three marked menus; your rewrite, with what you changed and why.

### MNU-4.M1.L2 — Seasonal and themed menus

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `MNU-4.M1.L1` · moves CR-MENU→4*

*Objectives:* [S] design menus around a season or a theme; [J] what to cut
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a season or a theme gives a menu its reason. Planning the drinks and a guest's need from the start,
and cutting what doesn't serve the idea, turn a list of dishes into a menu.

**You need:** the Dutch seasonal calendar (`VEG-1`); your repertoire from S1–S4; the checklist of `MNU-4.M1.L1`; paper.

**Explanation:**

*A seasonal menu.* Build it from what is at its best (the Dutch seasonal calendar, `VEG-1`):

| Season | At its best |
|---|---|
| Spring | Asparagus, new potatoes, rhubarb, lamb |
| Summer | Tomatoes, beans, courgettes, strawberries |
| Autumn | Pumpkin, mushrooms, game, apples and pears |
| Winter | Roots, cabbages, chicory, kale, mussels |

*A themed menu.* A tradition (`CUI-4`), a region, a memory, a colour or a technique, carried by every course without
forcing it.

*Drinks and a dietary need.* Give each menu a drink idea per course, alcoholic and non-alcoholic, and plan one guest's
need (an allergen, vegetarian, no alcohol) in from the start rather than adding it later.

*The rationale.* A short written reason for each course and for the order.

*What to cut.* Cut a course or component that repeats, overloads the stove, or doesn't serve the theme. A menu is often
better with one element fewer.

A worked example, an autumn menu:

| Course | Dish | Drink | Non-alcoholic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | Raw celeriac and apple salad, toasted seeds | A dry Dutch white | Cloudy apple juice with soda |
| Main | Braised venison, roasted pumpkin, red cabbage | A light red | A smoked tea, cooled |
| Dessert | Pear poached in cider, its sorbet | A sweet cider | A pear shrub |

The guest's need: a dairy-free guest, so the pumpkin is roasted in oil and the dessert has no cream. Cut: a mushroom
soup between starter and main (a second earthy course).

**Practice:**
1. **A seasonal menu (45 min).** Write one with its rationale, drinks and a dietary need; check it against the criteria.
2. **A themed menu (45 min).** The same; cut one element and write the reason.

**Look for:**
- menus that pass the progression and variety checklist;
- a cut that makes the menu clearer.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A "seasonal" winter menu with summer produce | Imported produce | Use the calendar |
| A gimmick | A theme forced on every course | Let the theme lead, not dictate |
| A half-empty plate for one guest | The dietary need solved by leaving things out | Plan their dish as a full dish |

**Safety:** the guest's need written into the menu from the start; a dish cooked with alcohol not offered as
alcohol-free (assessment §9).

**Record:** both menus with rationales; the element you cut and why.

### MNU-4.M2.L1 — Quantities and procurement

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M2.L1` · moves KM-PURC→4*

*Objectives:* [S] portions for 6–8 across courses; a consolidated order list; [J] where to buy what
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a dinner for eight fails at the shop as easily as at the stove: too little of the main, fish bought
too early, a budget blown. A consolidated order list, worked out from yields, prevents all three.

**You need:** the chosen menu; your yield records (`BUT-3`, `KMG-3`); shop prices; the portion table of `PLT-4.M1.L2`; a
spreadsheet or the planning template.

**Explanation:**

*Portions for 6–8 across courses.* Multiply the portion per course (`PLT-4.M1.L2`) by the guests and add a margin: about
10 % on the main protein, none on the dessert. Turn portions into purchase weights with your own yields.

A worked example, a main of 170 g of cooked venison for eight:
- 170 g × 8 = 1,360 g cooked;
- plus 10 % margin: about 1,500 g cooked;
- your braising yield is 65 % (from your records): 1,500 ÷ 0.65 = about 2.3 kg raw to order.

*A consolidated order list.* Sum every recipe's ingredients across the courses. Group them by shop and by day of
purchase, and cross off what is already in the pantry.

*Where to buy what.*
- The butcher or fishmonger: ordered a few days ahead for the main item.
- Produce: from a market or farm shop the day before.
- Pantry goods and drinks: early.
- Good and Best: chosen where the choice changes the result.

*The budget.* Work out the expected cost per guest from the list; compare the receipts afterwards.

**Practice:**
1. **The order list (60 min).** Write the quantities and the consolidated list for the chosen menu, with the shops,
   days and budget.

**Look for:** a list a second person could shop from, with the reason for each quantity.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too little or far too much | A recipe for four multiplied by two without yields | Portions × guests ÷ yield |
| Fish not fresh on the day | One trip for everything | Fish and produce last, the day before or on the day |

**Safety:** chilled food bought last and taken home cold; the fridge space checked before the big shop; allergen-free
products' labels photographed for the allergen plan (`MNU-4.M5`).

**Record:** the list with quantities, shops, days and budget.

### MNU-4.M2.L2 — Make-ahead analysis

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M1.L2` · moves KM-PLAN→4*

*Objectives:* [J] which components keep and which must be last-minute; [S] a make-ahead plan
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m2-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a dinner for guests is cooked over several days, or the cook spends the evening at the stove.
Knowing what keeps, and placing every component on a day with a control point, is what makes the evening calm and safe.

**You need:** the chosen menu with its recipes; the planning template; the §9 storage rows; labels.

**Explanation:**

*What keeps and what doesn't:*
- **keeps or improves:** stocks, braises, sauce bases, doughs, pickles, ice cream, terrines, many desserts;
- **must be last-minute:** searing, frying, dressing leaves, emulsions, plating, anything crisp;
- **in between:** blanched vegetables (refreshed and finished at service), par-cooked starches.

*The make-ahead plan.* Give each component the day it is made, how it is stored and how it is finished. Work through
the menu one course at a time, then read the plan by day to see whether any day is overloaded. Storage is
labelled, dated, cooled by the cooling rule, and kept in the fridge at most 2 days or frozen (assessment §9).

*Control points.* The moments where something is checked, each with its target and what to do if it fails:

| Control point | Target | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Stock cooled | 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours, to 5 °C within 6 in total (§9) | Smaller containers, an ice bath; log it and act at once |
| Braise reheated | 74 °C in every part (§9) | Keep heating; stir; probe again |
| Dough risen | Doubled, springs back slowly | More time somewhere warmer |

**Practice:**
1. **The plan (60 min).** Write the make-ahead plan for the chosen menu, with storage, control points and fallbacks.

**Look for:** every component placed on a day, with storage and a control point where it needs one.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A frantic last day | Everything left to the day | Move what keeps to T−2 and T−1 |
| Food past its time at the dinner | Made too early | At most 2 days in the fridge, or frozen (§9) |

**Safety:** every make-ahead item labelled, dated, cooled by the cooling rule and kept within 2 days or frozen;
reheated to 74 °C before hot holding (assessment §9).

**Record:** the plan with days, storage, control points and fallbacks.

### MNU-4.M3.L1 — Backwards timelines

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MNU-4.M2.L2` · moves KM-PLAN→4, TS-MEP→5*

*Objectives:* [S] a timeline from T−7 days to service, with buffers
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a dinner runs on time only if it is planned back from the moment the guests sit down. A timeline
from a week before, with buffers and a named first thing to drop, absorbs the slips every dinner has.

**You need:** the menu, the order list and the make-ahead plan of `MNU-4.M2`; the planning template; the timeline
checklist below; labels and the log (`KMG-3`).

**Explanation:**

*Working backwards from service.* Start with the time the guests sit down. Set each course's serving time, then count
each component's finishing, cooking and prep times back from there.

*From T−7 days:*

| When | What |
|---|---|
| T−7 to T−5 | Menu final, orders placed, pantry and drinks bought, equipment checked |
| T−3 to T−2 | Stocks, doughs, desserts that freeze, pickles |
| T−1 | Braises, sauce bases, desserts, vegetables prepped, table planned, fish and produce collected |
| The day | Mise en place by the early afternoon, the oven plan, plating stations, a rest before guests arrive |
| Service | A minute-by-minute plan for the last hour |

*Buffers.* Leave 15–20 minutes of slack before each course, and name a "first thing to drop" if time runs out (a
garnish, an amuse-bouche, a second sauce).

*Labels and the log* (`KMG-3`). Label and date every make-ahead item; record each control point in the log.

*The timeline checklist:* buffers before each course; a first thing to drop; no two last-minute steps at once; a rest
before the guests arrive; every control point on the timeline.

**Practice:**
1. **The timeline (60 min).** Write the full timeline from T−7 for the dinner project's menu and check it against the
   checklist.

**Look for:** a timeline with buffers and a named first thing to drop.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A tired, rushed host | No rest before guests arrive | Mise en place done by the early afternoon |
| Two courses go wrong at once | Their last-minute steps at the same moment | Move one step earlier, or change it |

**Safety:** the cooling and reheating control points of `MNU-4.M2.L2` placed on the timeline with times; chilled
deliveries into the fridge at once.

**Record:** the timeline; the first thing to drop.

### MNU-4.M3.L2 — Scheduling the stove and oven

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `EQP-1.M2.L3` · moves KM-EQPT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] fit several courses into four burners and one oven; [J] reorder to avoid clashes
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a home kitchen has four burners and one oven, and a four-course dinner wants more. A grid of time
against equipment shows the clashes on paper, where they cost nothing to fix.

**You need:** the timeline of `MNU-4.M3.L1`; squared paper or a spreadsheet; the dinner's pans; water for the
rehearsal; a timer.

**Explanation:**

*The equipment plan.* A grid of time against equipment (four burners, one oven, the circulator, the grill, the fridge
and freezer space), with each component placed. Clashes show at a glance.

| Time | Burner 1 | Burner 2 | Burner 3 | Burner 4 | Oven |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18:30 | Sauce reheating | | Water for vegetables | | Venison braise, 74 °C check |
| 19:00 | Sauce held low | Pan for searing | Vegetables | | Plates warming |

*Reordering to avoid clashes:*
- one oven temperature at a time: group dishes by temperature, or cook one ahead and reheat it;
- free burners by moving components to the oven, the circulator or a vacuum flask;
- use resting time for the oven's next job;
- change a component (roasted vegetables to glazed on the hob) when it can't fit.

*Reading the grid.* Two entries in one cell are a clash. A long run of one burner is a component that could move to
the oven or a flask. An empty oven during the main course is room for warming plates.

*Workarounds.* Replace a missing tool with a reason (`MOD-4.M2.X1`); a borrowed induction plate is a plan B for a fifth
burner.

**Practice:**
1. **The equipment plan (60 min).** Draw the grid for the dinner project; find and resolve two clashes. Rehearse the
   last hour with water in the pans.

**Look for:** a grid without clashes and a rehearsal that runs to time.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Three dishes need the oven at three temperatures | Not grouped | Group by temperature; cook one ahead |
| Make-ahead dishes with nowhere to go | Fridge space forgotten | Put the fridge and freezer on the grid |

**Safety:** a vacuum flask or held pan kept at 57 °C or above, probed (assessment §9); pan handles turned in when every
burner is in use.

**Record:** the grid with the two clashes and how you resolved them; the rehearsal times.

### MNU-4.M3.L3 — Holding, reheating and plating for several

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l3 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-3.M1.L1`, `PLT-4.M3.L1` · moves SA-TEMP→5, TS-PLT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] hold safely, reheat without loss, plate 6–8 consistently
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m3-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** at a dinner, food waits: held hot, reheated, plated eight times. Doing that safely and without
losing quality is where a guest dinner most often goes wrong.

**You need:** one course for eight (household and neighbours, or frozen portions); eight plates; a low oven; a water
bath or a covered pan; the probe; the plating tools; a timer; the sketch of the plate.

**Explanation:**

*Holding safely* (assessment §9). Hot food at 57 °C or above (a low oven, a covered pan on the lowest heat, a water
bath); cold food at 5 °C or below. Probe held food.

*Reheating without loss.*
- Braises and sauces: brought to 74 °C in every part within 2 hours, gently and stirred.
- Vegetables: refreshed earlier, finished in butter at service.
- Meat: rested and sliced at the last moment, not reheated in slices.

*Plating 6–8 consistently* (`PLT-4`). Warm the plates and line them up. Add each component to all plates in turn, with
a second pair of hands if possible. Check the first plate against the sketch and match the rest to it. Sauce
and the frozen or crisp element go on last, just before the plates leave.

*The cooling plan for leftovers.* Write before the dinner what is cooled, how, and by when: by the cooling rule, in
shallow containers, into the fridge.

**Practice:**
1. **A rehearsal for eight (60 min).** Hold, reheat and plate one course for eight, timed and probed. Photograph the
   plates together.

**Look for:**
- eight matching plates in under ten minutes;
- every held and reheated item probed in range.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Food in the danger zone | "Kept warm" at 45 °C for an hour | Hold at 57 °C or above; probe it |
| Grey, dry meat | Sliced meat reheated | Rest whole; slice at the last moment |

**Safety:** hot holding at 57 °C or above, cold at 5 °C or below; reheating to 74 °C in every part within 2 hours;
leftovers cooled by the cooling rule and kept at most 2 days (assessment §9).

**Record:** the probe readings; the plating time; the photo of the eight plates; the cooling plan.

## MNU-4.M4 — Beverage basics and food–drink pairing

### MNU-4.M4.L1 — Wine basics

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `FLV-3.M6.L2` · moves CK-BEVR→2*

*Objectives:* [K] main grapes and styles; acidity, tannin, body, sweetness; serving temperature; [S] structured tasting
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** wine is described by its structure (acidity, tannin, body, sweetness), and structure, not colour,
decides a pairing. A structured tasting turns "I like it" into notes you can pair with.

**You need:** three wines of different structure (for example a crisp white, a light red and a fuller red), or their
alcohol-free equivalents; a glass per wine; the tasting form (`TST-1`); the probe or a drinks thermometer; water,
plain bread and a spittoon (a jug or bowl).

**Explanation:**

*Main grapes and styles:*
- **white:** Sauvignon Blanc (crisp, herbaceous), Riesling (high acid, dry to sweet), Chardonnay (unoaked and fresh, or
  oaked and rich), Pinot Gris and Grüner Veltliner. The Dutch whites from Limburg and Gelderland (Johanniter, Solaris,
  Auxerrois) fit here.
- **red:** Pinot Noir (light, red fruit), Gamay, Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot (fuller, tannic), Syrah (spicy),
  Tempranillo and Sangiovese.
- **rosé, sparkling** (traditional method and others) **and sweet wines.**

*Structure:*
- **acidity:** mouth-watering;
- **tannin:** drying, from the skins and oak;
- **body:** light to full, mostly from alcohol and extract;
- **sweetness:** dry to sweet.

*Serving temperature:*

| Style | Serve at |
|---|---|
| Sparkling and sweet whites | Cold, about 6–8 °C |
| Most whites | Cool, 8–12 °C |
| Light reds | Slightly cool, 12–14 °C |
| Fuller reds | A cool room temperature, 16–18 °C |

*Structured tasting.*
1. **Look:** colour and clarity.
2. **Smell:** fruit, flowers, spice, oak.
3. **Taste:** acidity, tannin, body, sweetness, length.
4. **Conclude:** what the wine is, and what it would go with.

Take small sips; spitting is normal; water and bread between wines. The same method works for alcohol-free wines.

**Practice:**
1. **A structured tasting (60 min).** Taste three wines of different structure (or their alcohol-free equivalents)
   with the form, and measure their serving temperatures.

**Look for:** tasting notes that describe structure, not only flavour.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Notes that say "red, fruity" | Judging by colour | Write acidity, tannin, body, sweetness |
| A soft, hot-tasting red | Served warm from a heated room | Cool it to 16–18 °C |
| A muddled palate | Drinking rather than tasting | Small sips; spit |

**Safety:** no alcohol for anyone under 18, none in pregnancy, and the advice not to drink while breastfeeding
(assessment §9); small sips and spitting; the alcohol-free route is complete on its own.

**Record:** the three tasting forms; the serving temperatures.

### MNU-4.M4.L2 — Beer, cider, fortified wines and cooking with alcohol

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MNU-4.M4.L1` · moves CK-BEVR→2*

*Objectives:* [K] styles and uses; [S] cook with wine, beer and spirits (reduction, deglazing, flambé safety)
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** beer, cider and fortified wines pair with food that wine struggles with, and they cook well. Cooking
with alcohol also brings two safety facts: the alcohol stays in the dish, and a flambé is a real fire.

**You need:** a beer, a cider and a fortified wine (or alcohol-free versions); glasses; the tasting form; for the pan
sauce, a piece of pork or chicken, 150 ml of cider and stock; for the flambé, 30 ml of a spirit in a small jug, a pan
with a lid, a long lighter; the fire blanket within reach.

**Explanation:**

*Beer.* Lager and pilsner (crisp, bitter), wheat beer (soft, spicy), pale ale and IPA (hoppy, bitter), brown ales,
*bokbier* in autumn, stouts and porters (roasted), Belgian and Dutch abbey beers (*dubbel*, *tripel*), sour beers.
Bitterness, carbonation and malt sweetness are what pair.

*Cider.* Dry to sweet, still or sparkling; Dutch and Normandy ciders; apple and pear.

*Fortified wines.* Sherry (*fino* dry and salty, *oloroso* nutty, *Pedro Ximénez* sweet), port (ruby, tawny), madeira.
Small glasses, with specific foods: *fino* with salty snacks, tawny with cheese.

*Cooking with alcohol:*
- reduce wine before stock or cream goes in, so it doesn't taste raw (`CLS-4.M3`);
- deglaze with wine, beer or cider;
- beer goes into braises (*stoofvlees*) and batters;
- spirits are added at the end, or flamed;
- the alcohol stays after cooking (assessment §9), so a dish cooked with alcohol is not served as alcohol-free.

*Flambé safety:*
1. The extractor hood off; nothing flammable above the pan; children away.
2. The spirit measured into a jug, never poured from the bottle into a hot pan.
3. The pan taken off the heat to add it.
4. Lit with a long lighter, at arm's length.
5. A lid ready to put out the flame.

**Practice:**
1. **Beer, cider and a fortified wine, with cooking (60 min).** Taste one of each with the form. Deglaze a pan sauce
   with cider. Flambé by the safety steps.

**Look for:**
- the flambé done with the hood off and the lid in hand;
- a pan sauce without a raw-wine taste.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Flame runs up into the bottle | Spirit poured from the bottle into a flaming pan | Always measure into a jug first |
| Flames drawn upwards | The hood running | Hood off before you light |
| A sharp, raw sauce | Wine or cider not reduced | Reduce before the stock goes in |

**Safety:** the flambé steps above; the lid in hand and the fire blanket in reach (`SAF-1`); alcohol retained in cooked
dishes, so they are not served as alcohol-free (assessment §9).

**Record:** the three tasting forms; the sauce photo; the flambé steps ticked off.

### MNU-4.M4.L3 — Non-alcoholic pairing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l3 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `MNU-4.M4.L1` · moves CK-BEVR→3*

*Objectives:* [K] teas, infusions, kombucha and ferments, juices and shrubs, alcohol-free wines and beers; [S] make a shrub or an infusion
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** every guest gets a drink chosen for their course, and for many guests that drink has no alcohol.
Non-alcoholic drinks are described and paired by structure, like wines, and some are best made at home.

**You need:** for the shrub, 300 g of seasonal Dutch fruit (rhubarb, red currants or apples), 150 g of sugar and 150 ml
of cider vinegar; a clean jar with a lid; a sieve and a clean bottle; sparkling water; three non-alcoholic drinks to
taste (a tea, a kombucha or Dutch juice, an alcohol-free wine or beer); glasses; the tasting form.

**Explanation:**

*The non-alcoholic drinks:*
- **teas:** green (grassy, light tannin), black (tannic), oolong, smoked teas; herbal infusions (mint, verbena,
  chamomile);
- **kombucha and other ferments** (water kefir): acidity and fizz;
- **juices:** Dutch apple and pear juices (cloudy, single-variety), vegetable juices;
- **shrubs:** fruit, sugar and vinegar, diluted with sparkling water; acid like a wine;
- **alcohol-free wines and beers:** improving, and best chosen by structure (acidity, bitterness), not by imitation.

*Building structure without alcohol:*

| Structure | From |
|---|---|
| Acidity | Vinegar, citrus, ferments |
| Tannin | Tea |
| Bitterness | Hops or herbs |
| Body | Sugar, or a little salt |
| Fizz | Carbonation |

*A shrub.* Steep seasonal Dutch fruit with sugar and vinegar in the fridge for a few days, strain it and keep it in the
fridge. Dilute about one part shrub with three or four parts sparkling water, and taste for the balance of sweet and
sharp. Or make a cold-brewed tea or herb infusion.

**Practice:**
1. **A shrub and a tasting (60 min).** Make a seasonal shrub. Taste three non-alcoholic drinks with the form, by
   structure.

**Look for:** non-alcoholic drinks described by structure, like wines.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Guests without alcohol get only cola | Sweet soft drinks as the only option | Plan a drink per course |
| A cloying or harsh shrub | No acidity balance | Adjust sugar or vinegar; dilute more |

**Safety:** the shrub made in a clean jar and kept in the fridge; fruit washed; every drink offered as alcohol-free
checked on its label.

**Record:** the shrub's proportions and dilution; the three tasting forms.

### MNU-4.M4.L4 — Pairing principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l4 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `MNU-4.M4.L3` · moves CK-BEVR→3, FL-SEAS→5*

*Objectives:* [K] match and contrast; acid, tannin, sweetness, salt and heat; [S] pair one menu with both alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks
<!-- END generated:lesson-mnu-4-m4-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** a pairing works or fails for reasons you can name: acid, tannin, sweetness, salt and heat. Knowing
them lets you pair a whole menu, with and without alcohol, and adjust the dish to the drink.

**You need:** one dish (for example a pan-fried mackerel with a sharp beetroot salad); four drinks (two alcoholic, two
not; for example a crisp white, a tannic red, a rhubarb shrub, a smoked tea); the tasting form; the menu of the dinner
project; blind tasters for the second session.

**Explanation:**

*Match and contrast.* A drink matches the dish's weight and intensity (light with light). Then it either echoes a
flavour (an earthy Pinot Noir with mushrooms) or contrasts it (a crisp, acid wine with a rich, fatty dish).

*The interactions:*

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Acid | The drink needs at least the dish's acidity, or it tastes flat |
| Tannin | Softened by fat and protein (red meat); harsh with oily fish, and made metallic by it |
| Sweetness | The drink at least as sweet as the dessert, or it tastes sour |
| Salt | Softens tannin and bitterness; loves acid and sweet (salty cheese with sweet wine) |
| Heat (chilli) | Made hotter by alcohol and tannin; cooled by low alcohol, a little sweetness and fizz |

*Pairing a menu with both.* Give each course an alcoholic and a non-alcoholic drink with the same reason, for example
an acidic white and a rhubarb shrub with a rich fish.

*Seasoning for the drink* (`FL-SEAS`). Adjust the dish's acid and salt slightly when a pairing is planned: a touch more
acid in the dish for a high-acid wine. The tasters judge it blind.

**Practice:**
1. **Principles tested (60 min).** Taste one dish with four drinks (two alcoholic, two not) to show the acid, tannin,
   sweetness and heat interactions; note each.
2. **A menu paired (60 min).** Pair one menu course by course with both kinds of drink. Adjust the seasoning for the
   pairing and have it judged blind.

**Look for:** pairings explained by structure; the blind tasters judge the adjusted seasoning balanced.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A metallic taste | A tannic red with oily fish | A white, or a low-tannin drink |
| The wine tastes sour | A dry wine with a sweet dessert | A drink at least as sweet |
| A burning mouth | A strong alcoholic drink with a hot chilli dish | Low alcohol, a little sweetness, fizz |

**Safety:** small sips and spitting; a non-alcoholic pairing for every course (assessment §9).

**Record:** the four-drink notes; the paired menu with reasons; the blind tasters' forms.
