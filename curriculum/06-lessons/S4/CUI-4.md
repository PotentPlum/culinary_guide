**Before you start.** `CUI-3` studied eight traditions through one lens, a few dishes each. This course goes deep into
**three traditions you choose**, from `CUI-3` or new ones. In each you move from cooking dishes to cooking *from
principles*: the extended pantry and its regions, two signature techniques practised to a high standard, and a regional
meal designed and cooked without recipes.

Every deep dive follows the same 14-hour template, one module per tradition:
1. **Pantry and regions in depth** (`L1`): the extended pantry, regional variation, seasonality, history and where to
   buy it in the Netherlands.
2. **Signature technique I** (`L2`) and **II** (`L3`): the techniques that define the tradition, each practised twice.
3. **A regional menu** (`L4`): a three-dish meal of one region, designed and cooked without recipes, with a written
   rationale through the `CUI-3` lens.

The three modules differ in what they add. `CUI-4.M1` uses the deep-dive catalogue of the
[course file](../../05-courses/S4/CUI-4.md) as its guide. `CUI-4.M2` adds a written comparison of the first two
traditions. `CUI-4.M3` is planned **without the catalogue**: you write your own catalogue entry from sources before
cooking, which also opens a tradition outside the catalogue.

**Respect and adaptation,** as in `CUI-3`. Each tradition is regional and alive. Adaptations to a Dutch kitchen are
allowed, and named in every exercise with their effect:
- Dutch seasonal vegetables stand in for produce that is out of season or flown in;
- pantry goods (spices, rice, noodles, pulses, dried chillies, *masa harina*, olive oil, citrus) come from specialist
  shops: Turkish, Moroccan and Surinamese shops, Asian supermarkets and *tokos*, Italian and Spanish delicatessens, and
  online;
- meat and fish are Dutch or North Sea, by the rules of the earlier courses;
- nuts are replaced by seeds by default, as in S3, with the allergen rule where you use them.

**Safety** follows assessment §9 in every tradition:
- forcemeats and minced fillings to the minced row (68 °C for 17 seconds, or a row of its table);
- poultry to 75 °C or the poultry table, fish to 63 °C;
- raw fish in *sushi* only if frozen first by the fish row, and never for risk groups;
- cooked rice and stocks cooled by the cooling rule; frying by `HEA-2.M4`; allergens named for guests.

A probe reading below the §9 figure is a gate **Fail** in every exercise.

Plan about seven weeks at 6.5 hours a week, roughly two weeks per tradition, with the regional meal at the end of
each, often with guests. You can spread the three traditions through S4 between other courses, so each is cooked in a
season that suits its region.

**Have ready:** the `CUI-3` lens and your `CUI-3` notes; the course file's catalogue; the equipment of the traditions
you choose (only those: the catalogue names it per tradition); the instant-read thermometer and, for frying, the frying
thermometer; tasting forms, the calibrated form and tasters' cards; a budget of about €30 per extended pantry.

### CUI-4.M1.L1 — Pantry and regions in depth

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→4, IN-ARO→4*

*Objectives:* [K] the extended pantry, regional variation, seasonality, history; sourcing
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** cooking from principles starts with knowing what the tradition cooks with, where, and when. A
pantry map and a blend of your own are the base the rest of the deep dive stands on.

**You need:** the catalogue entry of your first tradition; your `CUI-3` notes on it; a notebook; a budget of about €30
for the pantry; a mortar and pestle or a spice grinder; whole spices for the blend; small jars and labels; the
ingredients for one stated dish to taste the blend in.

**Explanation:**

*The extended pantry.* Beyond the core pantry of `CUI-3`, the ingredients that make regional cooking possible. For
the Italian entry, that is *Carnaroli* rice, *polenta*, *guanciale*, anchovies, dried porcini. Write each on your map
with three facts: what it does in the food, how it is stored, and how long it keeps.

*Regional variation.* The tradition is a set of regions with their own climate, produce, fats and dishes. Normandy
cooks with butter, cream and apples; Provence with olive oil and herbs. Choose one region now for the menu of
`CUI-4.M1.L4`.

*Seasonality.* Every tradition has its own calendar of what is cooked when. Map it onto the Dutch seasons, and place the
deep dive in a season that suits the region: a Provençal summer menu in August, an Alsatian menu with Dutch white
asparagus in spring.

*History.* Trade, migration, religion and climate shape a region's food. Trace one dish to its origins from at least
two sources (the reading method of `CUI-3.M1.L2`), and write a short paragraph.

*Sourcing.* For each pantry item: where you buy it in the Netherlands, at what price and quality (Good and Best), and
what you substitute when it is not found. Buy only what the menu needs, not the whole list.

*The spice blend.* The tradition's characteristic blend or seasoning (*herbes de Provence*, *ras el hanout*, a
*garam masala*, *shichimi*, a *mole* spice base), composed by you for a stated dish:
1. write the dish and what the blend must do in it (warmth, freshness, smoke, heat);
2. toast the whole spices that are toasted in the tradition; grind them;
3. mix in parts by weight, starting from a small batch; taste it in the dish, adjust, and write the final parts and
   the reason for each.

**Practice:**
1. **Pantry and blend (60 min).** Taste the pantry items you bought, raw and cooked (a little oil in a pan, a spoon of
   the cooking liquid). Compose your blend for the stated dish and taste it in the dish.

**Look for:**
- a written pantry map with sources and substitutions;
- a blend whose reason is written and whose taste is balanced in the dish: no single spice shouting.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A map that reads like one national cuisine | Regions ignored | Name the region of each item and dish |
| Money spent on unused jars | The whole pantry list bought first | Buy for the menu |
| A blend you can't explain | Copied from a jar label | Write the job of each spice |

**Safety:** allergens in the pantry named (sesame, mustard, celery, gluten in some blends and sauces; nuts replaced by
seeds by default). Toast spices over moderate heat and stay with the pan.

**Record:** the pantry map, the region chosen and why, the history paragraph, and the blend with its parts and reasons.

### CUI-4.M1.L2 — Signature technique I

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-4.M1.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] the tradition's defining technique (e.g. *mole*, hand-pulled noodles, *tagine*, *tandoor* adapted for home)
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** the defining technique is what makes a tradition's food taste like itself. Practised twice, once
as the tradition makes it and once with a predicted new combination, it becomes something you can cook from
principles rather than from a recipe.

**You need:** the catalogue entry; two sources on the technique; the extended pantry; Dutch seasonal produce; the
tradition's equipment (for example a wide heavy pan for risotto, a couscoussier, a *comal*); the thermometer; tasting
forms and tasters' cards.

**Explanation:**

*The defining technique.* The catalogue names it for each tradition: a regional braise, risotto and polenta, a slow
pulse stew, couscous by hand, the *tandoor* adapted for home, hand-made noodles, *sushi*, *mole*.

*The method and its cues.* Study it from at least two sources. Write the steps, then mark the critical points, the
moments that decide the result, and the faults to expect. For risotto:
- the rice toasted until translucent at the edges;
- the hot stock added in stages, stirring;
- the *mantecatura*, butter and cheese beaten in off the heat;
- the wave (*all'onda*) when the pan is shaken.

*The high standard.* Before cooking, describe in writing what the dish looks, smells and tastes like when it is right.
After cooking, check the result against it, point by point. The description is your judge, not the clock.

*Practising twice.* The first time, follow the tradition closely. The second time, cook a new combination: a Dutch
seasonal ingredient (pumpkin in a risotto in autumn), a new filling or a new garnish. Write the predicted effect on
taste and texture before you cook it, then let tasters judge it with the form.

*Safety.* Name the §9 row that applies to the technique in your plan: a *coq au vin* is poultry (75 °C or the poultry
table); a *fabada* uses common beans (the bean row); a *tandoori* chicken goes to 75 °C.

**Practice:**
1. **The technique as the tradition makes it (90 min).** Cook it from the method. Check the result against your written
   standard and note each point met or missed.
2. **A new combination (90 min).** The same technique with a seasonal Dutch ingredient or a new element. Write the
   prediction first; tasters' cards and the form after.

**Look for:**
- the result matches the written description of the standard;
- the prediction for the new combination is checked honestly, including where it was wrong.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A result short of the standard | A critical point skipped because the clock said so | Cook to the cue, not the time |
| A different dish, not a new combination | The technique itself changed | Change an ingredient, keep the method |

**Safety:** the §9 row of the technique named in the plan and logged (probe readings for meat, poultry and fish).
Hot stock and long-simmered sauces spit: a lid ready and long sleeves (`SAF-1`).

**Record:** the written standard; the checklist after the first cook; the prediction and the tasters' verdict for the
second; photos; the probe log.

### CUI-4.M1.L3 — Signature technique II

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l3 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-4.M1.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a second technique or a preparation family (e.g. dumplings, *sushi* rice, *mezze* breads)
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** the second technique is learned as a family, so that several dishes follow from it. It is also
where most of the tradition's safety rows sit: forcemeats, frying, raw fish, a reused stock.

**You need:** the catalogue entry; two sources; the pantry; the equipment of the technique (a terrine mould or loaf
tin, a baking steel, a frying pot and frying thermometer, a steamer, a *tawa*); the instant-read thermometer; tasting
forms and tasters' cards.

**Explanation:**

*The second technique or a preparation family,* from the catalogue: *pâtés* and *terrines*, pizza and focaccia, salt
cod and *escabeche*, flatbreads, fermented batters, red-braising, *tempura*, *tamales*.

*A family, not a dish.* Learn the technique so that several dishes follow: several fillings, shapes or sauces. A
forcemeat by ratio (meat, fat, salt at about 1.5–1.8 %, a panade or egg) gives a country *terrine*, a game *terrine*
and a vegetable-studded one. A *tamal* dough gives a pork, a chicken and a bean filling.

*Practising twice,* as in `CUI-4.M1.L2`: once close to the tradition, once as a new combination with a written
prediction.

*Safety by family.* Name the §9 row for the technique:

| Family | §9 row |
|---|---|
| *Terrines* and *pâtés* | The minced row at the core (68 °C for 17 seconds; 75 °C with poultry); cooled by the cooling rule; kept at most 2 days or frozen |
| Salt cod and *escabeche* | Desalted in the fridge for 24–48 hours, water changed several times; *escabeche* kept cold, eaten within 2 days |
| *Tempura*, *pakora* | Frying by `HEA-2.M4` |
| Red-braising, master stock | The stock brought to the boil, cooled by the cooling rule and frozen between uses |
| *Sushi* | Raw fish only if frozen first by the fish row (−20 °C in all parts for 24 hours, or bought so treated); never for risk groups |
| *Tamales* | Meat fillings by the minced or poultry rows |

Taste a forcemeat before it is baked by poaching a spoonful: seasoning is fixed once it sets.

**Practice:**
1. **The technique as the tradition makes it (90 min).** Cook it, photograph it, check it against the written
   standard.
2. **A new combination (90 min).** A second version of the family with a predicted change; tasters' cards and the form.

**Look for:**
- two dishes of the family to standard;
- the safety row met and logged where there is one.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Only one dish possible | The family learned as a single recipe | Write what is fixed and what varies |
| An unsafe forcemeat or filling | Not probed | Probe the centre; log it |

**Safety:** the row in the table above, named in the plan and logged. A *terrine* is probed in the very centre.

**Record:** the family's fixed and variable parts; the probe readings; photos of both dishes; the tasters' verdict.

### CUI-4.M1.L4 — A regional menu

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l4 -->
*4.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `CUI-4.M1.L3` · moves JD-CHSE→4, CK-CUIS→4*

*Objectives:* [J] design and cook a three-dish regional meal without recipes
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m1-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** a regional meal designed without recipes is the proof of cooking from principles. Recreating a dish
from a description alone, with a plan and a fallback, is the judgment `JD-CHSE` level 4 asks for.

**You need:** the pantry; seasonal Dutch produce; descriptions of the dishes (from a regional cookbook's text, a menu,
or a memory), but not their recipes; the day plan; the alcohol-free drinks; tasters' cards with the descriptions on
them.

**Explanation:**

*Designing a regional meal without recipes:*
- three dishes from one region, chosen for balance through the `CUI-3` lens: pantry, techniques, flavour profile, meal
  structure;
- at least two of them recreated from a description alone, each with a written plan and a fallback for its riskiest
  step;
- the seasonal Dutch substitutions named, with their effect;
- the drinks the region serves with it, and an alcohol-free option for every guest (assessment §9).

*Balance.* Three rich braises compete. A Burgundian menu might be a vegetable *velouté*, a *bœuf bourguignon* with its
garnish, and a fruit tart: light, rich, sharp.

*Recreating from a description.* Read the description; list the components it names; work out the technique for each
from what you know; write the steps and the cues. Then write a fallback for the step most likely to fail: "if the
sauce is thin when the meat is done, lift the meat out and reduce the sauce alone".

*The rationale.* One page through the lens: why these dishes, how they fit the region, and what was adapted.

*The day plan.* What is made the day before (a braise, which can wait; a *terrine*, which needs pressing),
what on the day, and the order of service.

**Practice:**
1. **The regional meal (2–3 h on the day, prepared the day before).** Cook it from the plan for the household or guests.
   Photograph each dish. Give the tasters cards with the descriptions to hand, and ask whether they recognise each
   recreated dish.

**Look for:**
- the tasters recognise each recreated dish from its description;
- the rationale names the pantry, the techniques, the flavour logic and every adaptation.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A heavy, monotonous meal | Three dishes that compete | One rich dish, two that contrast |
| Not really recreated | A recipe found and followed later | Plan from the description only; note what you looked up |
| A failed step with nothing to do | No fallback | Write one for the riskiest step |

**Safety:** a braise made the day before is cooled by the cooling rule and reheated until steaming hot throughout, once
(assessment §9); every protein to its row; allergens named on the cards; an alcohol-free option for every guest.

**Record:** the menu, the rationale, the plan with fallbacks, the photos, the cards, and the adaptations with their
effect.

### CUI-4.M2.L2 — Signature technique I

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m2-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-4.M2.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] the tradition's defining technique
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m2-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** the second defining technique tests whether the habits of the first deep dive help or mislead. The
method (standard first, critical points, two practices) carries over; the seasoning reflexes don't.

**You need:** the catalogue entry; two sources on the technique; the second pantry; Dutch seasonal produce; the
technique's equipment (for example a couscoussier, or a steamer basket over a pot with the seam sealed with a cloth);
the thermometer; tasting forms and tasters' cards.

**Explanation:**

*The defining technique* of the second tradition, from the catalogue, studied and practised as in `CUI-4.M1.L2`: the
method, its critical points, the written standard and the §9 row that applies.

*An example: couscous by hand.* Semolina is rolled with water into grains, then steamed two or three times over a stew
in a couscoussier, and fluffed with butter or oil between steamings. Its critical points are the feel of the grains
after rolling (separate, not pasty), a steamer seam sealed so the steam goes through the grains, and the fluffing that
keeps them apart. The standard you write might read: "light, separate grains, tender with no chalky centre, tasting of
the stew's steam".

*What carries over:*
- **helps:** writing the standard first; reading cues, not the clock; the probe log;
- **misleads:** the first tradition's seasoning reflexes. A cook fresh from Italian food may reach for butter and
  cheese to finish; a Moroccan couscous wants butter or oil, *ras el hanout* and the stew's own broth. Taste against
  the new standard, not the old one.

*Practising twice:* once as the tradition makes it; once as a new combination (Dutch pumpkin, carrots and turnips in
the couscous stew in autumn), with a written prediction.

**Practice:**
1. **The technique as the tradition makes it (90 min).** Cook it; check it against the written standard.
2. **A new combination (90 min).** Predict, cook, and have tasters judge it with the form.

**Look for:** the result meets the standard; the prediction is checked.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A dish that tastes of the first tradition | Seasoning by its reflexes | Taste against this tradition's standard |
| A new combination that is a different dish | The technique changed | Change an ingredient only |

**Safety:** the §9 row of the technique named and logged; a stew with chicken or lamb to its row (poultry 75 °C);
steam burns: lift the lid away from you (`HEA-1`).

**Record:** the standard; the first-cook checklist; the prediction and the tasters' verdict; a line on which habits
from the first deep dive helped and which misled.

### CUI-4.M2.L3 — Signature technique II

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m2-l3 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-4.M2.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a second technique or a preparation family
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m2-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** the second family of the second tradition adds more dishes you can cook without a recipe. Its
safety row is named in the plan before you start, because frying, fermenting and forcemeats each carry one.

**You need:** the catalogue entry; two sources; the pantry; the equipment (a *tawa* or cast-iron skillet, a baking
steel, a deep heavy pot and frying thermometer, a blender for batters); the instant-read thermometer; tasters' cards
and the form.

**Explanation:**

*The second technique or preparation family* of the second tradition, from the catalogue, practised as in
`CUI-4.M1.L3`.

*An example: flatbreads.* One dough family, several breads:
- **pita:** a hot steel makes the pocket puff;
- ***msemen*:** the dough laminated with butter or oil, folded into squares and cooked on a hot pan;
- ***manakish*:** a flat round topped with *za'atar* and oil.

Write what is fixed (the dough, its hydration, the rest) and what varies (the shape, the fat, the topping, the heat).

*A second example: fermented batters.* *Idli* and *dosa* from soaked, ground rice and *urad* dal, fermented overnight
in a warm place, then steamed or spread thin on a hot pan. The batter is the family; the steamer and the *tawa* give two
dishes from it.

### CUI-4.M3.L2 — Signature technique I

<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-4-m3-l2 -->

### CUI-4.M3.L3 — Signature technique II

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-4-m3-l3 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-4.M3.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a second technique or a preparation family

**Why it matters:** in your own entry, nobody hands you the safety row. This lesson finds it: every technique is matched

*Finding the safety row.* Ask these questions of the technique, in order, and write each answer into the entry:
1. **Is there raw or lightly cooked fish?** The fish row: frozen first (−20 °C in all parts for 24 hours, or bought so
   treated), and never for risk groups. A home freezer at −18 °C does not qualify.

   minutes, never in a slow cooker.

7. **Is it fermented or preserved?** The preserving rows: tested methods only.
