**Before you start.** `RDV-4` gave you the tools of recipe development: ratios, deconstruction, substitution, test
plans, writing for others, and a first original dish. Here you use them at scale. You develop at least six original
dishes across the categories of a menu, each through at least three documented versions, write them as reproducible
recipes, and present them at a tasting day with blind scoring. The six should read as one cook's work: yours.

The course has one template for the portfolio plan (`RDV-5.M1.L1`) and one template for a development cycle, used for
every dish (`RDV-5.M2.L1`). The other five dish lessons say what is particular to their category.

**Rules for every dish.**
- **Original.** Not a course exercise, and not a published recipe with small changes. The dish starts from an idea
  written in one sentence (`CR-COMP`), and the test log shows how it got from there to the final version.
- **Safety.** Assessment §9 applies to every version: proteins to their figures, raw egg and warm egg sauces pasteurised
  for every guest, preserves by a tested method only, and test portions kept as leftovers (at most 2 days).
- **Ingredients.** Dutch seasonal vegetables (velt.nu harvest windows), MSC or seasonal North Sea fish, and Dutch meat
  and dairy; no imported year-round produce. A dish developed in one season is written with its seasonal window.
- **Tasting.** Household panel scores for every version, blind where two versions can be compared. The panel's tasters
  have taken the salt triangle test (assessment §5.4).

**The point-of-view catalogue.** A point of view is what makes six dishes read as one cook's work:

| Point of view | How it shapes the six | Risk to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Season ("one Dutch month, at its peak") | Every dish built on what is best in one or two months | Six dishes that taste of the same two vegetables |
| Region (the Zeeland coast, the sandy soils of the east) | The region's produce and techniques: mussels, salt-marsh vegetables, rye, buckwheat, smoked fish | Recreating regional classics instead of making new dishes |
| Technique (fire and smoke, fermentation) | One technique in a different role in every category | Every dish smoky or sour |
| Ingredient (the brassicas, the onion family) | One family explored through every category | A forced use where it doesn't belong |

Plan about 38 hours over at least 8 weeks. Each dish's three versions are spread over about three weeks, so two or
three dishes are in development at once, alongside `IND-5`.

**Have ready:** the full kitchen of S1–S4; the probe; the 1 g scale; the photo corner (`PLT-4`); the test-plan template
and recipe format of `RDV-4`; score cards and coded cups or plates; a folder or document for the portfolio.

### RDV-5.M1.L1 — Planning the portfolio

*2 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `RDV-4.M5.L2` · moves CR-ORIG→3*

*Objectives:* [J] six dishes across categories; a point of view (season, region, technique, ingredient); a test plan per dish
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** six good dishes are not yet a portfolio. A point of view, six balanced categories and a test plan
for each turn eight weeks of cooking into a body of work that shows who you are as a cook.

**You need:** your record (the dishes and techniques you do best and want to develop); the seasonal calendar; the
catalogue above; the test-plan template of `RDV-4.M4.L1`; a calendar for the next eight weeks.

**Explanation:**

*The point of view.* One sentence that says what your cooking is about, chosen from the catalogue or written fresh.
Test it: could a stranger tell the six dishes came from one cook?

*The six categories:*
1. a starter;
2. a soup or small plate;
3. a vegetable-led main;
4. a protein main;
5. a bread or pastry element;
6. a plated dessert.

Together they could form a menu, so plan them for balance across courses (`MNU-4.M1`): no repeated main ingredient,
technique or texture.

*Per dish, the plan holds:*
- the idea in one sentence;
- the starting framework or ratio (`RDV-4.M1`);
- the first version;
- the question each test round must answer, and the variable for that round;
- how it is scored (household panel, blind where possible);
- the seasonal window.

A worked line, for the point of view "the onion family, through the year":

| Category | Idea | Start | Round 2 question | Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vegetable-led main | Leeks as the centre: charred, then braised in their own stock | Braise ratio of `RDV-4.M1` | Char before or after the braise? | Leeks, autumn–winter |

*The calendar.* The template of `RDV-4.M4.L1` for every dish, and a calendar that spreads the 18 versions over the
weeks.

*Cost.* Test the protein main and the dessert in small portions first; expensive items only after cheaper practice.

**Practice:** None: theory only.

**Look for:**
- the six ideas, read together, sound like one cook;
- every dish has a first version and a question for round two before any cooking.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Six ideas that are one dish in six forms | The point of view applied too narrowly | Give it a different role in each category |
| The dessert or bread left unplanned | "The hard one" put off | Plan it first |
| A plan that needs out-of-season produce | The calendar not checked | Move the dish to its window |

**Safety:** for each dish, the plan names the §9 rows that apply (the protein, egg, fish eaten raw, preserves), so every
version is cooked to them.

**Record:** the plan, ready for `RDV-5.M1.X1`.

### RDV-5.M1.X1 — The portfolio plan

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-rdv-5-m1-x1 -->
*framework, framework* — The portfolio plan, using the template from `RDV-4`.
<!-- END generated:exercise-rdv-5-m1-x1 -->

**Task:** define what your cooking is about and plan six dishes that show it: a point of view, six dishes across the
categories, a test plan for each, and a calendar.

**You need:** your record; the seasonal calendar; the catalogue; the portfolio checklist and the test-plan template.

**Framework:** the portfolio checklist and the test-plan template from `RDV-4`.

| Part | Fixed | Your decisions |
|---|---|---|
| Point of view | One sentence, with the reason | Season, region, technique, ingredient, or your own |
| Categories | Starter, soup or small plate, vegetable-led main, protein main, bread or pastry, plated dessert | The idea for each |
| Test plan | Three rounds; one variable per round; panel scores, blind where possible | The questions and variables |
| Balance | No repeated main ingredient, technique or texture | Which dish changes if two repeat |

*Procedure:*
1. Write the point of view in one sentence, with the reason.
2. For each category, write the idea, the starting framework, the first version, the round questions and variables, and

3. Check the six against each other for balance; change any that repeat.
4. Put the 18 versions on the calendar.

**Show:**
- **`CR-ORIG` level 3:** the plan against the checklist: a point of view, six ideas across the categories, a test plan

AI review reads the plan against the checklist: *competent* is a point of view in one sentence, six ideas that read as
one cook, a question and variable for every round, no repeats across courses, and every dish in its window; *not yet*
is a missing category, a round without a variable, or a dish out of season.

**Time:** 2 h.

### RDV-5.M2.L1 — A starter

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l1 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `RDV-5.M1.L1`, `RDV-4.M4.L2` · moves CR-ORIG→4, RD-TEST→4*

*Objectives:* [S] develop, test and finalise an original starter
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** this lesson sets the development cycle you use for all six dishes. A starter is a good first
subject: small, quick to cook, and honest about balance, so each version shows clearly what changed.

**You need:** the plan for your starter; seasonal ingredients for three versions; the photo corner; the panel and score
cards; coded plates for blind comparison.

**Explanation:**

*The development cycle.* The template for every dish of this module:
1. **Version 1:** from the idea and the starting framework; cooked, photographed in the corner, scored by the panel.
2. **Round two:** one variable changed to answer the plan's first question (a component, a ratio, a method, a
   temperature); scored, blind against version 1 where possible.
3. **Round three:** the next question, or the same variable refined. Choose the final version by the scores and the
   idea, not by habit.
4. **The final recipe:** weights, cues, timings and the seasonal window, in the format of `RDV-4.M5.L1`.

*The test log.* For each version:

| Version | Changed | Why | Result | Panel | Your band | Outside band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Pickled beetroot instead of roasted | Version 1 lacked acid | Brighter; the panel preferred it blind | 7.5 | Competent | Competent |

*A starter's job.* Small, bright and appetising. It opens the menu, so acid, freshness and texture lead.

**Practice:**
1. **Version 1 (60 min).** Cooked from the plan; photographed; panel scores.
2. **Version 2 (60 min).** One variable changed; blind comparison with version 1.
3. **Version 3 (60 min).** Refined; the final chosen; the recipe drafted.

Space the versions about a week apart, so you can think between them.

**Look for:**
- each version answers its question, so the log shows why the final is better;
- the panel scores rise, or the log explains why they didn't.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The log can't say why a version improved | Several things changed at once | One variable per round |
| The starter fills the panel up | Too large or rich | Smaller; acid and texture lead |

**Safety:** a raw or cured fish starter follows the fish row of assessment §9: frozen first, at −20 °C or below in all
parts for at least 24 hours. A raw-egg dressing is made with pasteurised egg for every guest.

**Record:** the test log; the photos of three versions; the panel scores; the draft recipe.

### RDV-5.M2.L2 — A soup or small plate

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l2 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `RDV-5.M1.L1` · moves CR-ORIG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] develop an original soup or small plate
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a soup or small plate concentrates one idea into a few mouthfuls. It tests whether the idea is
strong enough to stand almost alone, with only a base and one contrast to support it.

**You need:** the plan for your soup or small plate; stock from the freezer or the ingredients to make one; seasonal
ingredients for three versions; the photo corner; the panel.

**Explanation:**

*The cycle.* As in `RDV-5.M2.L1`: three versions, one variable each, the test log, the final recipe.

*A soup.* A base (stock, purée or broth) and a garnish of contrast. The soup should taste of its main ingredient, not
its stock.

*A small plate.* One main element and two supports: one for contrast in texture, one for balance (acid, fat or
bitterness).

*The variable.* Often the base, or the garnish's texture:

| Round question | Variable | Compared |
|---|---|---|
| Does the stock help or hide the vegetable? | The base | A light stock against water |
| Roasted or raw purée? | The method of the base | Depth against freshness |
| What does the garnish add? | Its texture | Crisp against soft |

*Making ahead.* A soup is a natural make-ahead dish, so plan the cooling and storage into the recipe.

**Practice:**
1. **Version 1 (60 min).** From the plan; photographed; scored.
2. **Version 2 (60 min).** One variable changed; blind comparison.
3. **Version 3 (60 min).** Refined; the final chosen; the recipe drafted.

**Look for:**
- the idea is clear in one spoonful;
- the garnish adds a contrast the base lacks.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The soup tastes of stock | Too much or too strong a stock | A lighter stock, or water, and more of the main ingredient |
| The garnish disappears | No contrast in texture or temperature | A crisp, raw or acid garnish |

**Safety:** a stock or soup made ahead is cooled by the cooling rule (from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2 hours, and to 5 °C
or below within 6 hours in total) and kept at most 2 days (assessment §9).

**Record:** the test log; the photos; the panel scores; the draft recipe with its make-ahead note.

### RDV-5.M2.L3 — A vegetable-led main

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l3 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `VEG-3.M5.L2` · moves CR-ORIG→4, IN-VEG→5*

*Objectives:* [S] develop an original vegetable-led main course
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** a vegetable-led main is where this program's produce shows what it can do. Built from structure and
given real depth, it should satisfy a panel that doesn't miss the meat.

**You need:** the plan for your main; the component table of `VEG-3.M5.L2`; the decision framework of `IND-5.M1.L1`;
seasonal vegetables for three versions; the photo corner; the panel.

**Explanation:**

*The cycle.* As in `RDV-5.M2.L1`.

*Vegetable-led.* The vegetable is the centre, not a side without its meat. Build it with the component table of
`VEG-3.M5.L2`:

| Part | Role | Example: a celeriac main |
|---|---|---|
| Centrepiece | The vegetable at its best | Celeriac salt-baked whole, carved at the table |
| Sauce | Fat and depth | Brown butter with its roasting juices |
| Contrast | Texture and acid | Quick-pickled shallots and crisp celeriac skin |
| Depth | Browning, umami | A mushroom *duxelles* |

*Reasoned from structure.* Choose the method from the vegetable's water, starch and fibre (`IND-5.M1.L1`). Add depth by
browning, umami and fat. A dense root takes a long, dry heat that concentrates it; a watery leaf wants a quick, hot
method that keeps it bright.

*The written guide.* A short guide to one vegetable group's behaviour (the roots, or the brassicas, for example):
- its structure;
- the methods that suit it;
- its faults and their fixes;
- your dish as the worked example.

**Practice:**
1. **Version 1 (60 min).** From the plan; photographed; scored.
2. **Version 2 (60 min).** One variable changed; blind comparison.
3. **Version 3 (60 min).** Refined; the final chosen; the recipe drafted; the guide written.

**Look for:**
- the panel doesn't miss meat;
- the vegetable is cooked to its best texture, and the guide explains why.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A plate of sides with nothing at the centre | No centrepiece | One vegetable, large, cooked to its best |
| It tastes like a starter | Depth missing | Browning, umami and fat |

**Safety:** if the dish uses dried beans, the bean row of assessment §9 applies: soaked at least 5 hours, the water
poured away, then boiled in fresh water at least 30 minutes.

**Record:** the test log; the photos; the panel scores; the draft recipe; the vegetable guide.

### RDV-5.M2.L4 — A protein main

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l4 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `RDV-5.M1.L1` · moves CR-ORIG→4, RD-TEST→4*

*Objectives:* [S] develop an original meat or fish main course
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** a protein main is the most expensive dish to develop and the least forgiving of a wrong doneness.
Testing it economically, and probing every version, makes the development honest and safe.

**You need:** the plan for your main; a cheaper cut or smaller portion for early versions where the variable allows; the
full protein for the final; the probe; the photo corner; the panel.

**Explanation:**

*The cycle.* As in `RDV-5.M2.L1`.

*A protein main.* Meat or fish cooked to an exact doneness, with a sauce and garnishes that serve the idea. Choose the
protein's method from its structure (`PRO-4`).

*Testing economically.* Early versions on a cheaper cut or a smaller portion, where the variable allows; the full
protein in the final version. But the sauce is tested on the final protein: a sauce built on chicken thigh may not suit
a duck breast.

| Round | Variable | Protein used |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The whole idea | A small portion of the final protein |
| 2 | The sauce's acid | The final protein |
| 3 | The garnish's texture | The final protein, full portion |

*Doneness within §9.* Choose the doneness only within the rows of assessment §9. Write the target core temperature
into the recipe.

**Practice:**
1. **Version 1 (60 min).** From the plan; probed; photographed; scored.
2. **Version 2 (60 min).** One variable changed; blind comparison.
3. **Version 3 (60 min).** Refined; the final chosen; the recipe drafted with the target core temperature.

**Look for:**
- the protein at the exact doneness in every version, with the reading logged;
- the sauce makes the protein taste more of itself.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The sauce doesn't suit the final protein | Tested on a different one | Test the sauce on the final protein |
| A doneness below the §9 row | It "looks better" | Only doneness within the row |

**Safety:** every version probed against its §9 row: poultry to 75 °C or the poultry table; intact meat and fish to
63 °C for 15 seconds; minced meat and offal to their rows; wild game to 75 °C.

**Record:** the test log with every reading; the photos; the panel scores; the draft recipe with the target core
temperature.

### RDV-5.M2.L5 — A bread or pastry element

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l5 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `RDV-5.M1.L1` · moves CR-ORIG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] develop an original bread or pastry
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l5 -->

**Why it matters:** in a bread or pastry, one change shifts everything else, so the discipline of one variable per
round matters most here. It is also where the point of view can live in a grain or a technique.

**You need:** the plan for your bread or pastry; flours, fat and leaven for three versions; the 1 g scale; the photo
corner; the panel.

**Explanation:**

*The cycle.* As in `RDV-5.M2.L1`, with baker's percentages as the framework (`BAK-2`, `BRD-2`).

*One variable at a time.* The candidates are hydration, the flour, the fat, the proof and the bake. A change in one
shifts the others: more whole-grain flour absorbs more water, so a flour change can look like a hydration change.

| Round | Variable | Held constant |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | The flour: 30 % rye in place of 30 % wheat | Hydration, salt, leaven, proof, bake |
| 3 | Hydration: +5 points | The new flour, the rest as round 2 |

*Its place in the menu.* A bread for the table or a pastry element in a dish. It carries the point of view: a Dutch
grain such as rye or buckwheat, or a technique.

*Photographs that compare.* The crumb, crust or layers photographed in the corner, cut the same way each time.

**Practice:**
1. **Version 1 (60 min active, longer in total).** From the plan; crumb or layers photographed; scored.
2. **Version 2 (60 min active).** One variable changed; compared.
3. **Version 3 (60 min active).** Refined; the final chosen; the recipe drafted in baker's percentages.

**Look for:**
- the crumb, crust or layers match the idea;
- the photos show the change between versions.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The log can't explain the change | Hydration and flour changed together | One variable per round |
| A bread that doesn't belong | The point of view forgotten | Carry the grain or technique into it |

**Safety:** a pastry element with a cream or custard follows the egg rows of assessment §9: egg-thickened components to
71 °C, cooled by the cooling rule.

**Record:** the test log; the cross-section photos; the panel scores; the draft recipe in baker's percentages.

### RDV-5.M2.L6 — A dessert

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l6 -->
*5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `PST-4.M5.L2` · moves CR-ORIG→4, RD-WRIT→4*

*Objectives:* [S] develop an original plated dessert
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m2-l6 -->

**Why it matters:** a plated dessert closes the menu and is the dish another person is most likely to get wrong from a
text. Developing it and having someone else cook it from your recipe tests both the dish and the writing.

**You need:** the plan for your dessert; the plated-dessert structure of `PST-4.M5.L2`; Dutch dairy, grains, honey and
seasonal vegetables; the probe; the photo corner; the panel; a second cook for the final recipe.

**Explanation:**

*The cycle.* As in `RDV-5.M2.L1`.

*A plated dessert,* built as in `PST-4.M5.L2`:
- a main element;
- a contrast in texture and temperature;
- a sauce;
- sweetness balanced by acid or bitterness.

Use Dutch dairy, grains, seasonal vegetables (rhubarb, beetroot, carrot, pumpkin) and honey rather than imported fruit.

| Part | Example: a rhubarb dessert |
|---|---|
| Main element | A baked buttermilk custard |
| Contrast | Rhubarb poached until just tender; a buckwheat crumble |
| Sauce | The reduced poaching syrup |
| Balance | Rhubarb's acid against the custard's sweetness |

*Written for others.* Give the final recipe to another person to cook from the text alone, as in `RDV-4.M5`.
Photograph both results in the corner. Watch them cook without helping, and note where they hesitate: each hesitation is
a missing cue or weight.

**Practice:**
1. **Version 1 (60 min).** From the plan; plated; photographed; scored.
2. **Version 2 (60 min).** One variable changed; blind comparison.
3. **Version 3 (60 min).** Refined; the final chosen; the recipe written and given to another person to cook.

**Look for:**
- the dessert is not too sweet for the panel;
- the other person's result matches yours in the photos.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The idea is lost | Too many components | Four parts; cut the rest |
| An unsafe custard element | Not probed | Probe every version |
| The other cook's result differs | A cue or weight missing from the text | Add it; ask where they hesitated |

**Safety:** egg-thickened components to 71 °C (assessment §9); raw or lightly cooked egg pasteurised, for every guest;
cooked creams cooled by the cooling rule. Rhubarb: stalks only.

**Record:** the test log; the photos; the panel scores; the final recipe; the other cook's photo and notes.

### RDV-5.M2.X1 — Six original dishes, developed and written

- **`RD-WRIT` level 4, further evidence:** six reproducible recipes; the dessert cooked by another person from the text,

AI review reads the logs, the photos, the recipes, the guide and the second cook's result: *competent* is one variable
per round with its reason, every reading logged at its §9 row, finals chosen by the scores, and a dessert the other cook

follow.

### RDV-5.M3.L1 — The portfolio write-up

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m3-l1 -->
*3 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `RDV-5.M2.L6` · moves RD-WRIT→4, JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [S] recipes, test logs and photos in one document; [J] a reflection on your style
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m3-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a portfolio is read by people who weren't in your kitchen. Writing it as one document, with an
honest reflection on your style, turns six dishes into evidence of who you are becoming as a cook.

**You need:** the six final recipes; the test logs; the evidence photos; your bands and the outside bands for each dish;
the agreement log of `REV-5`.

**Explanation:**

*One document.* In this order:
1. the point of view;
2. for each dish: the idea, the final recipe, the test log in short, the evidence photos, and the seasonal window.

*The reflection on style.* Answer four questions:
- what do the six dishes have in common (ingredients, techniques, balance, look)?
- what surprised you?
- where is the style still borrowed?
- what are your next steps (a specialisation, a technique to deepen, a cuisine to learn)?

*Honest scoring.* For each dish, your band and the outside band, and why they differ where they do (the `JD-REFL`
agreement log of `REV-5`).

| Dish | Your band | Outside band | Why they differ | What would settle it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leek main | Excellent | Competent | I like more char than the panel | A blind re-taste with less char (assessment §3.4) |

A reflection names a weakness with evidence (a score, a photo, a log entry), and a next step that addresses it. Write it
after the outside bands are in, not before: the disagreements are often the most useful part, because they show where
your own palate or eye pulls away from your guests'.

**Practice:** None: theory only.

**Look for:**
- a reader could cook every dish from the document;
- the reflection names a weakness with evidence, not only strengths.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |

### RDV-5.M3.L2 — A tasting day

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rdv-5-m3-l2 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `RDV-5.M3.L1` · moves CR-ORIG→4, FL-SENS→5*

*Objectives:* [S] serve a selection to a panel; [J] collect blind scores and act on them
<!-- END generated:lesson-rdv-5-m3-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** your household has tasted every version and knows your ideas. A panel scoring blind, before they
hear the idea, shows whether the dishes speak for themselves, and a triangle test shows whether a change is real.

**You need:** the portfolio; ingredients for at least four dishes for 4–6 people; coded cups or plates; a hidden key;
score cards; the probe; space in the fridge.

**Explanation:**

*The panel.* 4–6 people, asked in advance about allergens and diets (`NUT-3`). Adjust the menu, or keep a dish apart
with its own tools, and tell every guest what each dish contains.

*Blind scoring.* Each dish is scored on a card before its idea is told:
- flavour;
- texture;
- appearance;
- whether the idea comes through.

Then tell the idea, and ask whether they recognised it.

*The triangle test* (assessment §5, [S20]). For one dish with two close versions:
1. three coded portions, two the same and one different;
2. served in random order;
3. each taster picks the odd one out.

With three portions, a guess is right one time in three. A difference counts only if clearly more than a third of the
panel find it. With a panel this small, only a large majority is a clear signal: two of six is what guessing gives.

*Acting on the scores.* Change the dish whose scores or comments point to a fault, and note it in the recipe. Write in
the log any scores that don't agree with your own band.

**Practice:**
1. **The tasting day (3 h).** At least four dishes served to the panel; blind cards collected; the triangle test run;
   the results written and one change decided.

**Look for:**
- the panel names the idea of most dishes without being told;
- the triangle result is read correctly, including when it shows no difference.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Scores that only echo the idea | The idea told before scoring | Score blind first |
| A false "difference" | Two of six read as clear | Compare with the one-in-three guess rate |
| Food standing too long | Courses delayed | Hold hot or cold; watch the 2 hours |

**Safety:** dishes held at 57 °C or above, or 5 °C or below, and out of the fridge for at most 2 hours (assessment §9).
Allergens asked in advance; each guest told what each dish contains.

**Record:** the blind cards; the recognition answers; the triangle key and result; the change decided.
