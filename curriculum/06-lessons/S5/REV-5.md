**Before you start.** By `MS-S4` you have a record of four stages: competency levels, evidence with dates, milestone
bands and the journal. That record is uneven. Some competencies were shown once, under one condition, a long time ago.
Others passed at *Developing* on a milestone row, and a few rest on evidence that you and the reviews disagreed about.
This course turns the record into practice. You find your two or three weakest competencies, practise them in three
deliberate blocks, and decide on the evidence whether each gap is closed before the capstone.

The course is personal: the gaps come from your own record, because evidence ages and weak spots are revisited (Phase 1
§10.3). This file gives one template for the gap analysis and the practice blocks, and a short catalogue of worked gap
types. New evidence for each gap goes into the record against that competency's own success row
([`04-competencies/`](../../04-competencies/)).

`REV-5` comes first in S5, so its gap analysis steers `IND-5` and `RDV-5`. A gap in a technique the capstone must show
is always a candidate:
- a stock- or emulsion-based sauce;
- a protein to an exact doneness;
- a bread or pastry element;
- a preserve;
- a vegetable-led course.

**Revision material.** The knowledge checks of S1–S4 (about 1,360 items) are your item bank. Retake the checks of the
modules that taught each gap competency. If a safety item disagrees with assessment §9, follow §9.

**The gap catalogue.** The first match in this order decides priority: a safety gap, then a capstone requirement, then
the oldest evidence, then the rest.

| Gap type | Record shows | Lesson revisited | Practice (one variable) | Closed when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Safety (`SA-TEMP`) | A cooling log with a missing reading, or a gate passed only after a resit | `SAF-1`, `SAF-3.M1` | Three batches cooled and logged (a stock, a braise, cooked rice), varying the quantity | Every reading within §9, the row met, none missing; always block 1 |
| Capstone technique (`TS-SAU`) | The emulsion of `MS-S2` and `MS-S4` at *Developing*; a sauce split once | `SAU-2.M5.L1`, `CLS-4.M3` | The warm emulsion three times, varying the fat, then held for service | The row met at the target level, the photo *Competent* |
| Judgement (`JD-CUES`) | Roast predictions outside tolerance more than pan work | `PRO-4.M6.L1` | Ten roast predictions, varying the cut and the oven | At least 8 of 10 within tolerance |
| Old or missing evidence (`TS-KNF`, `TS-FIRE`) | Last shown more than six months ago, or a household cook not yet logged | `KNF-2`; `IND-5.M5.L2` | Timed cuts against the grid, varying the vegetable; or the missing cook, planned and dated | The tolerances met again, the new date in the record |
| Self-assessment (any) | Two or more entries two bands apart (assessment §3.4) | The competency's lesson; `ORI-1` on scoring | Each session scored before the outside band is seen | Disagreements down to one band or less |

Plan three blocks of three sessions, each block spread over about three weeks: about ten weeks of calendar time at
about 1.5 hours a week, alongside `IND-5` and `RDV-5`.

**Have ready:** your record and journal; the success rows of `04-competencies/`; the S1–S4 knowledge checks; the
probe; the photo corner; household tasters' cards.

## REV-5.M1 — Gap analysis and targeted practice

**Knowledge check:** none of its own. The module's two judgment objectives (choosing the gaps, and deciding whether each
is closed) are shown in the plan and decisions of `REV-5.M1.X1`. Retake the knowledge checks of the modules that taught
each gap competency from the S1–S4 item bank, at the same pass rule: 80 %, with every safety item correct.

### REV-5.M1.L1 — Gap analysis

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `MS-S4` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [J] review the competency record and evidence age; pick the two or three weakest competencies; plan practice
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** practice without a diagnosis goes to what you already enjoy. Reading your own record honestly, and
writing what "closed" means before you start, points the last weeks before the capstone at the gaps that matter.

**You need:** your record: every competency with its level, the date and conditions of each piece of evidence, the
milestone bands, and the journal entries with your band and the outside band (`ORI-1`, assessment §3.4); the gap
catalogue; the success rows.

**Explanation:**

*What counts as a gap:*
- a competency below its stage target;
- a milestone row passed at *Developing*;
- evidence older than about six months: it comes up for review, but is not taken away (Phase 1 §10.3);
- a level reached under fewer conditions than its row asks for;
- two or more entries with a two-band disagreement (assessment §3.4).

*Choosing two or three.* Use the catalogue's order: safety, capstone requirements, oldest evidence, the rest. Then
prefer the competencies where practice costs least and helps most across dishes. More than three spreads the practice
too thin.

*The plan, per gap:*

| Field | Example: `TS-SAU` |
|---|---|
| Competency and level | `TS-SAU` at the S5 target |
| Record line | `MS-S4` emulsion at *Developing*; split once |
| Lesson revisited | `SAU-2.M5.L1`, `CLS-4.M3` |
| Variable | The fat: clarified, whole, brown butter |
| Test condition | Held for service for 30 minutes |
| Closed when | The row met; the photo *Competent*; your band within one of the outside band |

Write the closing rule before practice starts.

*The agreement log.* From the first session of block 1, every S5 entry has your band, then the outside band. Count the
share within one band every two weeks; this is the `JD-REFL` level 5 measure.

**Practice:** None: theory only.

**Look for:**
- every chosen gap points to a line in the record, not to a feeling;
- the closing rule is written before practice starts.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The chosen gaps are favourite techniques | Chosen by enjoyment | Use the catalogue's order |
| A gap called "sauces" | Not a competency and a level | Name the competency, its row and level |
| Gaps that stay open, or close by hope | No closing rule | Write it first |

**Safety:** a safety gap always goes first, in block 1. Where an old safety item disagrees with assessment §9, §9 wins.

**Record:** the gap analysis and the plan, ready for `REV-5.M1.X1`.

### REV-5.M1.L2 — Targeted practice, block 1

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l2 -->
*4.5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `REV-5.M1.L1` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [S] deliberate practice on the first gap, with the matching lesson revisited
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** ordinary cooking repeats what you already do well. Deliberate practice repeats the weak element,
with feedback and one change at a time, so a block of three sessions can move a competency that months of meals did
not.

**You need:** the plan for the first gap; the lesson that taught it; its knowledge check from the item bank; ingredients
as the gap needs, on household meals where possible; the probe; the photo corner; tasters' cards.

**Explanation:**

*Deliberate practice.* A narrow target, repeated with feedback, one variable at a time. It is harder than ordinary
cooking, and shorter.

*The lesson revisited.* Before the first session, reread the lesson that taught the competency, with its cues and
faults, and retake its knowledge check.

*Feedback at every session.* A reading, a photo or a taster's card against the success row. Write your band before you
see the outside band.

*The block:*

| Session | Purpose | Scored by |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | The competency as the record left it, under the plan's starting condition | The success row; the knowledge check |
| Focused practice | The weak element repeated, one variable changed | Readings or photos at each attempt |
| Test | The competency under the row's condition | Your band, then the outside band; the closing rule |

A worked safety block (`SA-TEMP`): the baseline cools a stock and logs it; the practice cools a braise and a batch of
cooked rice, the quantity varied; the test cools the largest batch with every reading on time.

**Practice:**
1. **Baseline (90 min).** The competency as the record left it, scored against the success row; the knowledge check
   retaken.
2. **Focused practice (90 min).** The weak element repeated with one variable changed; readings or photos at each
   attempt.
3. **Test (90 min).** Under the row's condition; outside band logged; the gap judged against its closing rule.

**Look for:**
- the baseline and the test are scored the same way, so the change shows;
- your band and the outside band move closer over the block.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The whole dish practised | The weak element not isolated | Repeat only the weak element |
| The improvement can't be explained | Several things changed | One variable per session |
| Your band copies the outside band | Scored after seeing it | Write yours first |

**Safety:** the practice uses the assessment §9 figures the lesson used. For a cooling gap: from 57 °C to 21 °C within 2
hours, and to 5 °C or below within 6 hours in total; leftovers kept at most 2 days.

**Record:** the three sessions with both bands; the knowledge-check result; the decision against the closing rule.

### REV-5.M1.L3 — Targeted practice, block 2

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l3 -->
*4.5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `REV-5.M1.L2` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [S] deliberate practice on the second gap
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** a gap that closed in practice can open again when attention moves on. Bringing the first gap back
inside an ordinary meal, while you work on the second, shows whether the first improvement held.

**You need:** the plan for the second gap; its lesson and knowledge check; the block 1 results; ingredients for a meal
that uses both competencies; tasters' cards.

**Explanation:**

*The second gap.* The same pattern as block 1, on the second competency of the plan: a baseline, focused practice with
one variable, and a test under the row's condition.

*Interleaving.* The first gap comes back once in this block, inside an ordinary meal, to see whether it held. A sauce
gap closed in block 1, for example, returns as the sauce of a weeknight dinner in block 2, scored the same way.

*The plan adjusted in writing.* If block 1 showed the gap was larger than planned, change the plan in writing (another
block, or a lower target for now), not quietly:

| Date | Gap | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 4 | `TS-SAU` | A fourth session added | The emulsion held for 30 minutes only once of three |

*The closing rule stays.* A rule written before practice is not moved to fit a result. If the result falls short, the
gap stays open, and block 3 or the capstone plan deals with it.

**Practice:**
1. **Baseline (90 min).** The second competency as the record left it, scored; its knowledge check retaken.
2. **Focused practice (90 min).** The weak element with one variable changed; the first gap shown once in the same meal.
3. **Test (90 min).** Under the row's condition; outside band logged; judged against the closing rule.

**Look for:**
- both gaps scored on the same day show whether the first held;
- every plan change has a date and a reason.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The first gap reopens unnoticed | Dropped once block 2 starts | Bring it back once in a meal |
| A gap closed on a moved rule | The rule changed to fit the result | Keep the rule; log the shortfall |

**Safety:** the second gap's practice uses its own §9 figures; the interleaved meal follows the rows for every dish in
it, and the first gap's readings are logged again.

**Record:** the three sessions with both bands; the first gap's interleaved result; any plan change with its date and
reason.

### REV-5.M1.L4 — Targeted practice, block 3

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l4 -->
*4.5 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `REV-5.M1.L3` · moves JD-REFL→5*

*Objectives:* [S] a third block on the weakest remaining gap, under a condition not yet used (time, guests or no recipe); [J] decide whether the gap is closed
<!-- END generated:lesson-rev-5-m1-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** a competency that holds only in practice will fail at the capstone. A condition not yet used shows
whether it holds outside practice, and a written decision turns an open gap into a known risk with a plan.

**You need:** the results of blocks 1 and 2; the plan; ingredients for a meal for at least two people other than you;
their cards; the capstone plan of `CAP-5.M1.L5`.

**Explanation:**

*The weakest remaining gap.* Chosen from the results of blocks 1 and 2, or the third competency of the plan.

*A condition not yet used* (Phase 1 §10.3): time pressure, guests, or no recipe. The new condition shows whether the
competency holds outside practice, which levels 4 and 5 ask for.

*Closed or not:*
- **closed:** the success row met with new evidence, and the outside band agrees within one band;
- **open:** the row not met, so the gap goes into the capstone plan as a known risk, with a fallback dish and a test
  cook (`CAP-5.M1.L5`).

*The written decision.* For each gap:

| Gap | Before | After | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `JD-CUES` | 5 of 10 roast predictions within tolerance | 9 of 10, under guests | Closed | Row met; bands agree |
| `TS-SAU` | Split once; *Developing* | Held twice of three | Open | Fallback: a *beurre blanc* style pan sauce is replaced by a stock reduction |

**Practice:**
1. **Under the new condition (90 min).** For example a timed run, or the dish without a recipe.
2. **For others (90 min).** The competency inside a meal for at least two people other than you, scored by their cards.
3. **Test and decision (90 min).** The final demonstration; the decision for every gap written; the agreement log
   counted.

**Look for:**
- every gap has a decision with evidence on both sides;
- an open gap has a fallback in the capstone plan.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A gap closed too easily | One good result under easy conditions | Test under the new condition |
| An open gap hidden | Fear of a weak record | Plan for it: a fallback and a test cook |

**Safety:** a meal for others follows assessment §9 for every dish, with allergens asked in advance (`NUT-3`); a safety
gap is never left open into the capstone.

**Record:** the three sessions; the decision table; the agreement log count; the capstone plan entries for open gaps.

### REV-5.M1.X1 — The gap plan and three practice blocks

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-rev-5-m1-x1 -->
*brief, brief* — The gap-practice plan and the three blocks, with new evidence for each gap.
<!-- END generated:exercise-rev-5-m1-x1 -->

**Task:** analyse your record honestly, close the gaps that matter before the capstone, and show that your own scoring
agrees with the outside evidence. Write the gap-practice plan and run three blocks, each over about three weeks. The
variable changes within each block, and block 3 adds a condition not yet used. The agreement log runs over every S5
entry from block 1 on, for at least 8 weeks.

**You need:** your record and journal; the success rows of the chosen competencies; the item bank of the S1–S4
knowledge checks; ingredients as each gap needs, on household meals where possible.

*Procedure:*
1. Write the gap analysis: the candidates from the record, the two or three chosen, and why.
2. Write the plan per gap: the success row and level, the lesson revisited, the variable, the test condition, the
   closing rule.
3. Run the three blocks; at each session, write your band, then the outside band.
4. Write the decision for each gap, with evidence before and after; put any open gap into the capstone plan with a
   fallback.
5. Count the agreement log every two weeks, and at the end.

**Show:**
- **The gap evidence:** for each gap, the new evidence against its own success row, entered in the record with the date
  and condition.
- **`JD-REFL` level 5, in part:** the gap analysis and the closing decisions; the agreement log over at least 8 weeks,
  with at least 90 % of entries within one band of the outside evidence. `RDV-5.M3.X1` adds the style reflection, and
  the capstone defence (`CAP-5.M3.L3`, scored by `CAP-5.R12`) completes the row.

AI review reads the analysis, the plan, the session logs and the decisions: *competent* is every gap traced to a record
line, a closing rule written before practice, one variable per session, your band written before the outside band, and
a decision with evidence on both sides; *not yet* is a gap chosen by preference, a moved closing rule, or an open gap
without a fallback.

**Time:** 13.5 h active, over about nine weeks.
