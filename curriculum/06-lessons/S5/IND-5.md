**Before you start.** This course asks the stage question in practice: can you cook anything well without a recipe? The
teaching is done. What remains is consistent, independent cooking under conditions that change: an unfamiliar
ingredient, a basket someone else chose, what is left in the fridge, a dish known only from a description or a taste,
too little time, the wrong equipment, a guest's diet, and faults introduced while you cook. Almost every exercise is a
brief: a goal, constraints and criteria, and nothing else.

The course is a series of briefs:
- **first principles** (`IND-5.M1`): a decision framework from structure to method to flavour, used on three
  unfamiliar ingredients;
- **mystery baskets** (`IND-5.M2`): a method, then three rounds of increasing difficulty;
- **pantry and leftovers** (`IND-5.M3`): a weekly habit for four weeks;
- **recreating dishes** (`IND-5.M4`): from a written description, a tasted dish, and a classical text without
  quantities;
- **constraints** (`IND-5.M5`): time, equipment and diet;
- **a troubleshooting clinic** (`IND-5.M6`): rescues under pressure, and doneness by the senses alone.

**Rules for every brief.**
- **Safety first.** Assessment §9 applies to every dish. A brief, a basket or a historical text never overrides it:
  poultry to 75 °C, minced meat to its row, leftovers within 2 days and reheated once, raw egg and warm egg sauces
  pasteurised for every guest. Doneness judged by the senses is checked with the probe before a §9 food is served.
- **Ingredients.** Dutch seasonal vegetables (velt.nu harvest windows) and MSC or seasonal North Sea fish. A basket or
  an "unfamiliar ingredient" is bought, not foraged.
- **The pantry.** Salt, oils, vinegars, flour, sugar, dried spices and herbs, stocks from the freezer, dried pulses and
  grains are always available unless the brief removes them.
- **The record.** Every session logs the plan, timestamps, photos, your own band, then the outside band (the agreement
  log of `REV-5`).

Most competencies here reach level 5, which asks for at least five demonstrations under three conditions over at least
8 weeks. Each exercise says which part of a row it gives and where the rest is.

Plan about 43.5 hours over at least 8 weeks, at about 5 hours a week, alongside `RDV-5` and the blocks of `REV-5`. The
pantry meals run once a week for four weeks.

**Have ready:** the full kitchen of S1–S4; the probe; the lidded charcoal grill, chimney starter and heat-resistant
gloves if you have outdoor space; the fire blanket; separate boards and tools for allergens (`NUT-3`); the calibration
cups and the 1 g scale (`TST-1`); the photo corner (`PLT-4`); a notebook for plans and timestamps.

### IND-5.M1.L1 — A decision framework

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `HEA-2.M1.L2`, `RDV-4.M2.L1` · moves JD-CHSE→5, CK-METH→5*

*Objectives:* [J] structure (water, fat, protein, connective tissue, starch) → method → flavour → texture; [K] the transfer questions from Phase 1 §2.3
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** faced with something new, a cook without a recipe starts from what the ingredient is made of. A
framework from structure to method to flavour turns everything you have learned into a way to choose, and to explain the
choice.

**You need:** a familiar vegetable (a leek or a celeriac, about 500 g); the pantry; a notebook; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*From structure to method:*
- **water:** high-water vegetables (courgette, cucumber, spinach) collapse and release liquid, so they suit fast, hot
  methods or salting first; dense, starchy roots (celeriac, parsnip, potato) need time to soften and take browning well
  (`VEG-3`, `HEA-2.M1.L2`);
- **fat:** fatty fish and cuts tolerate high heat and longer cooking; lean ones dry out and want gentle or fast methods;
- **protein:** coagulates and tightens with heat, so the target doneness sets the method (`SCI-2`);
- **connective tissue:** much collagen needs long, moist cooking until it becomes gelatine; little allows a fast method
  (`PRO-4`, `SLW-3`);
- **starch:** needs water and heat to gelatinise, and retrogrades as it cools (`STA-1`).

*Then flavour, then texture.* What the ingredient brings (sweet, bitter, umami), what it needs (acid, fat, salt), and
the texture contrast it lacks (`FLV-3`).

*The transfer questions* (Phase 1 §2.3). What is it and what is it for? How is it done? Why does it work? What happens
physically and chemically? Which variables change it? Which mistakes are common, how do you recognise them, and how do
you correct them? Where else does it transfer?

*The written analysis:*

| Line | For a celeriac |
|---|---|
| Structure | Dense, starchy, low water |
| Chosen method | Roasted in wedges: time to soften, browning for sweetness |
| Rejected | Sautéed (stays raw inside); boiled (watery, no browning) |
| Flavour | Sweet and earthy; needs acid and salt (a sharp dressing) |
| Texture | Soft; needs crunch (toasted seeds) |
| Done when | A knife slides in with no resistance; edges deep golden |

**Practice:**
1. **Framework on a known ingredient (60 min).** Analyse a familiar vegetable in writing with the framework, cook it by
   the chosen method, and check the analysis against what happened.

**Look for:**
- the analysis predicts the result before cooking;
- every rejected method has a named risk, not only "I don't like it".

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The analysis just describes a dish you know | Started from the dish, not the structure | Fill in the structure line first |
| A protein with no target reading | The safety figure forgotten | Every protein's §9 row in the "done when" line |

**Safety:** every protein in an analysis carries its §9 figure in the "done when" line; the senses suggest, the probe
decides.

**Record:** the analysis; the result; what the analysis got right and wrong.

### IND-5.M1.L2 — Unfamiliar ingredients

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `IND-5.M1.L1` · moves JD-CHSE→5, IN-VEG→5, CK-METH→5*

*Objectives:* [S] cook three ingredients you've never cooked (e.g. kohlrabi, salsify, a new fish); [J] justify each choice of method
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** the test of the framework is an ingredient you have never cooked. Cooking each one twice, for
different reasons, shows which method suits which use, and teaches more than one good result would.

**You need:** three ingredients your record shows you have never cooked, bought in season: for example kohlrabi,
salsify, Jerusalem artichoke, sea kale, purslane, or a North Sea fish not yet used (dab, gurnard, witch); about 300–500
g of each vegetable, one whole fish or two fillets; the probe; a reliable reference [S16].

**Explanation:**

*Choosing the three.* Check your record, and choose from the season. At least two should be vegetables.

*First contact.*
1. Look at it, smell it, and taste a small piece raw (vegetables only).
2. Cut a sample: how hard, how wet, how fibrous?
3. Read a reliable source [S16] for its structure and any known risks.

*Twice, with different reasoning.* Cook each ingredient by two methods chosen for different reasons, and compare them on
the same plate. For example, salsify roasted for browning, and simmered then glazed for tenderness.

*Safety.*
- A new fish follows the fish row of §9 (63 °C for 15 seconds), unless it was frozen first for serving translucent
  (assessment §9).
- A vegetable new to you is checked for any known need to cook it, as with dried beans (§9).

**Practice:**
1. **Ingredients one and two (90 min).** Analyse each, cook it two ways, taste the versions side by side, and
   photograph them.
2. **Ingredient three (60 min).** Analyse it and cook it two ways; serve it in a meal and collect household cards.

**Look for:**
- both versions of each ingredient are good, and they differ for the reason you wrote;
- you can say which method suits which use.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Two versions that taste the same | Two methods that are really one (roasted and baked) | Choose methods with different heat and moisture |
| A fish judged by "looks done" | No reading | Probe it: 63 °C for 15 seconds |

**Safety:** the fish row of §9 for a new fish; a new vegetable's cooking need checked in the source before it is served.

**Record:** the analyses; the paired photos; which method you would choose for which use.

### IND-5.M1.X1 — Three unfamiliar ingredients, each cooked twice

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-ind-5-m1-x1 -->
*brief, brief* — Three unfamiliar ingredients, each cooked twice with different reasoning.
<!-- END generated:exercise-ind-5-m1-x1 -->

**Task:** reason from structure to method for three ingredients you have never cooked, at least two of them vegetables,
and explain the choice well enough for someone else to follow. Cook each twice with different reasoning. Then write a
short guide to the method choice for one ingredient group.

**You need:** three ingredients new to you, bought in season (about 300–500 g of each vegetable, one whole fish or two
fillets); the pantry; the probe; household cards.

*Procedure:*
1. Write the analysis for each ingredient: structure, the chosen methods and the rejected ones with their risks, the
   flavour and texture plan, the cue and the reading.
2. Cook each twice; photograph both versions together; probe the fish.
3. Collect household cards; write what the analysis got right and wrong.
4. Write the guide to the method choice for one ingredient group (for example the roots, or the leafy brassicas): the
   structure, the methods that suit it, the ones that fail and why.

**Show:**
- **`JD-CHSE` level 5, brief one of five:** the written analysis naming alternatives and risks, and the result to
  standard. The further briefs are `IND-5.M4.X1`, `IND-5.M4.X2`, the round 3 analysis of `IND-5.M2.X1`, and the
  capstone (`CAP-5.M1.L1`).
- **`CK-METH` level 5, in part:** method choices reasoned from principles with the rejected alternatives named, and the
  written guide. The capstone brief completes the row (`CAP-5.R1`).
- **`IN-VEG` level 5, the unfamiliar-vegetable condition:** two vegetable-led dishes reasoned from structure. The other
  conditions are in `IND-5.M2.X1`, `IND-5.M3.X1`, `RDV-5.M2.X1` and the capstone.

AI review reads the analyses, the paired photos, the probe reading and the guide: *competent* is two genuinely different
methods per ingredient, each reasoned with its risks, both results to standard, and a guide another cook could use;
*not yet* is two versions of one method, a fish without a reading, or a guide that lists without reasons.

**Time:** 2.5 h, plus the written guide.

### IND-5.M2.L1 — The mystery basket method

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `MNU-4.M3.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→4, KM-PLAN→5*

*Objectives:* [S] inventory, constraints, menu, timeline in 15 minutes; then execute
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a basket someone else chose is the purest test of cooking without a recipe. A fixed method for the
first 15 minutes turns the surprise into a plan, and the plan is what gets the meal out on time.

**You need:** a notebook and a timer; the framework of `IND-5.M1`; the timeline method of `MNU-4.M3.L1`; a household
member willing to choose baskets.

**Explanation:**

*The basket.* Someone else chooses it from a list of what is in season and in budget, with the pantry available. The
chooser notes any allergen or diet of the people who will eat.

*The first 15 minutes,* written down:
1. **Inventory:** every item, its weight and state, and what is perishable.
2. **Constraints:** the time, the guests, the equipment, the diet.
3. **Menu:** each item given a role (main, support, accent), with the method chosen from the framework of `IND-5.M1`.
4. **Timeline:** worked back from the serving time, the longest task first, and a control point for each protein
   (`MNU-4.M3.L1`).

*The fallback.* Decide in the plan a simpler version of each dish, to use if a step fails.

*Everything used, nothing forced.* An item that doesn't fit is kept for another meal rather than spoiling a dish.

A worked plan for a winter basket (leeks, celeriac, red cabbage, two eggs, a lemon), two courses in 90 minutes:

| Line | Entry |
|---|---|
| Inventory | Leeks 600 g, celeriac 800 g, red cabbage 1 kg, 2 eggs, 1 lemon |
| Menu | Starter: leeks charred, with a lemon dressing and a chopped hard-boiled egg; main: celeriac roasted in wedges, red cabbage salad |
| Timeline | 0:00 oven on; 0:10 celeriac in; 0:20 eggs boiled; 0:40 leeks on; 1:20 dress and plate |
| Fallback | Leeks soft? Braise them in stock instead |
| Kept | Half the red cabbage, for a later braise |

**Practice:** None: theory only.

**Look for:**
- the plan is written and timed before the first knife cut;
- every basket item has a role or a reason to keep it.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Chaos after 30 minutes | Cooking started before the plan was complete | Fifteen minutes of writing first |
| Every course tastes of the same thing | Every item used in every course | One role per item |
| One failure sinks the meal | No fallback | A simpler version in the plan |

**Safety:** each protein's §9 figure written into the timeline as a control point; the chooser's allergen or diet note
read before the menu is written.

**Record:** a practice plan for a basket you imagine from this week's season.

### IND-5.M2.L2 — Round 1: vegetables and pantry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `IND-5.M2.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→4, TS-DRY→5*

*Objectives:* [S] a household member picks the basket; two courses within 90 minutes
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a basket of vegetables and the pantry, with no protein, asks for depth from technique alone. Dry
heat gives it, and two courses that differ in method keep the meal from tasting twice of the same thing.

**You need:** two baskets of three to five seasonal vegetables, each chosen by a household member (about €10–15 each);
the pantry; sheet pans, a heavy pan, the grill pan if you have one; a timer; the notebook.

**Explanation:**

*The round.* A household member picks a basket of three to five seasonal vegetables. You make two courses within 90
minutes, from the vegetables and the pantry.

*Dry heat as the backbone* (`TS-DRY`). With no protein, browning, roasting, grilling and frying give depth. Vary the
method between the two courses: for example, a starter of charred leeks from the grill pan and a main of roasted roots.

*Browning well.* Dry the vegetables, give them space on the tray or in the pan, and use enough heat. A crowded tray
steams; an empty corner burns. Turn once when the underside is deep golden.

*Using the whole vegetable* (`KMG-3`). Leaves, stalks and peel become a garnish, crisps or a quick stock: celeriac peel
in a stock, beetroot leaves wilted, carrot tops in a herb sauce.

*Improving the plan.* After basket A, read its log: which step ran late, which dish was weaker, which item went unused.
Change one thing in the plan for basket B.

**Practice:**
1. **Round 1, basket A (90 min).** Write the 15-minute plan; cook two courses; log timestamps and photos; collect
   household cards.
2. **Round 1, basket B (90 min).** A second basket from another season, or with a vegetable not used in A. Improve the
   plan from A's log.

**Look for:**
- both courses on the table within 90 minutes;
- each course uses a different dry-heat method, to standard.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Two courses that taste the same | Same fat, spice and method | Change the method, the fat and the accent |
| Pale, steamed vegetables | A crowded tray | Two trays; space between the pieces |

**Safety:** a hot oven and pan handled with dry cloths; trimmings for stock cooled by the cooling rule and kept 2 days
or frozen (assessment §9).

**Record:** both plans with timestamps; the photos; the cards; what you changed for basket B.

### IND-5.M2.L3 — Round 2: protein and season

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `IND-5.M2.L2` · moves CR-IMPV→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a basket with one protein and seasonal produce; a main and a side
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** with a protein in the basket, the meal is planned around its safety figure, its cooking and its
rest. A side that contrasts with it and finishes at the same moment is what makes it a dish rather than two.

**You need:** two baskets, each with one protein (a chicken leg, a pork chop, a fish, eggs or a pulse) and seasonal
produce (about €15–25 each); the pantry; the probe; a timer; the notebook.

**Explanation:**

*The round.* A basket with one protein and seasonal produce: you make a main and a side.

*The protein first.*
1. Write its §9 figure: 75 °C for chicken, 63 °C for 15 seconds for fish and intact pork, and for dried beans the bean
   row (soaked at least 5 hours, the water poured away, then boiled at least 30 minutes; assessment §9).
2. Choose the method from its structure (`IND-5.M1.L1`): a chicken leg's connective tissue wants time; a pork chop wants
   a fast sear and a gentle finish.
3. Build the timeline around its cooking and its rest.

*The side as a partner.* Choose a contrast in texture and flavour with the main, and time it to finish with it: a crisp,
sharp salad with a braised leg; a soft purée with a seared chop.

*Resting.* Rested meat waits under loose foil while the side finishes; it is not left to go cold.

**Practice:**
1. **Round 2, basket A (90 min).** Plan, cook the main and side, log the probe reading, and collect household cards.
2. **Round 2, basket B (90 min).** A different protein; improve the plan from A.

**Look for:**
- the protein at its target doneness and above its §9 figure, rested;
- the side ready at the same moment.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A cold main | The protein cooked first and left | Time it to finish with the side, plus its rest |
| A dull plate | The side repeats the main's texture | Add crunch or acid to the side |

**Safety:** the protein probed against its §9 row and the reading logged; raw meat and fish on their own board, the
board washed before the side's vegetables are cut.

**Record:** both plans; the probe readings; the photos; the cards.

### IND-5.M2.L4 — Round 3: for guests, against the clock

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l4 -->
*4 h · 1 practical session · builds on `IND-5.M2.L3` · moves KM-PLAN→5, CR-IMPV→4*

*Objectives:* [S] three courses from a basket for four, with a fixed serving time
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m2-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** three courses for four guests from a basket, with a fixed serving time, is the round that shows you
can cook without a recipe when it counts. The plan must be written fast and then kept.

**You need:** a basket for three courses for four, chosen by a household member (about €40–60); the pantry; the guests'
allergen and diet information from the chooser; plates for four; the probe; the timeline as a checklist on the counter;
guest cards.

**Explanation:**

*The round.* Three courses for four from a basket, with a fixed serving time, within 3 hours of opening the basket. The
written plan is made in the first 15 minutes.

*Running service* (`MNU-4.M3`).
- Use the timeline as a checklist: tick each step as it is done.
- Aim for each course within 5 minutes of its planned time.
- Write slips down as they happen, and absorb them with the buffer or the fallback.

*Guests.* An allergen or diet named by the chooser is handled as in `NUT-3`, from the basket to the plate.

*The dessert.* Plan it early: something made in the first hour that sets or chills (a poached fruit, a custard, a
sorbet base), or something quick that needs no oven at the end.

*The written analysis.* For each course, write the alternatives you considered and their risks, so the round counts as a
brief for `JD-CHSE`.

**Practice:**
1. **Round 3 (3 h).** Open the basket, plan in 15 minutes, and serve three courses for four on time. Log timestamps,
   take photos, collect guest cards.

**Look for:**
- every course out within 5 minutes of the plan;
- the guests judge every course good.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No time for dessert | The dessert left to the end | Start it in the first hour |
| The main waits while you plate | Plating for four took longer than planned | Plates and tools out before the main is done |

**Safety:** the allergen plan from basket to plate (`NUT-3`); every protein probed; held food at 57 °C or above
(assessment §9).

**Record:** the plan; the timestamps against the plan; the analysis; the photos; the cards.

## IND-5.M3 — Pantry and leftover cooking

### IND-5.M3.L1 — Cooking from what's there

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m3-l1 -->
*4 h · 3 practical sessions · elapsed 3 wk · builds on `KMG-3.M4.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→4, RD-ADPT→5*

*Objectives:* [S] one meal a week for four weeks from pantry, fridge and leftovers only; [J] adapt known dishes
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m3-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** most home cooking starts from what is already in the house. A weekly meal from the pantry, the
fridge and the leftovers, made safely and well, is the habit that makes a cook independent of the shop as well as the
recipe.

**You need:** whatever the week leaves in the fridge and pantry, with their dates; the notebook; the probe; no shopping
for these meals.

**Explanation:**

*The weekly habit* (`KMG-3.M4.L1`). One meal a week, for four weeks, from what is in the pantry, the fridge and the
leftovers, with no shopping for it.

*The inventory first.* Write what must be used soonest, and what is safe to use:
- leftovers within 2 days in the fridge, covered, and reheated until steaming hot, only once (assessment §9);
- anything out of the fridge for more than 2 hours in total is thrown away, not cooked (§9).

*Adapting known dishes.* Choose a dish family you know (a frittata, a soup, a gratin, a fried rice, a pie) and adapt it
to what is there. Keep the structure; swap the ingredients by their role (`RDV-4`). Predict the adaptation in writing,
and check it on the plate.

| Dish family | Structure kept | Swapped by role |
|---|---|---|
| Frittata | Eggs, a filling, a hot pan then a low finish | Leftover roast roots and wilted greens as the filling |
| Soup | A base, a liquid, a body, a finish | Yesterday's braising liquid as the liquid; a potato for body |
| Gratin | A starch, a liquid, a crust | Cooked squash with a little cream and crumbs |

*Making it a meal.* Add a contrast from the pantry: a quick pickle, toasted crumbs, a herb oil, an acid at the end.

**Practice:**
1. **Week 1 (45 min).** Write the inventory; cook the first meal; predict its adaptation and confirm it.
2. **Week 2 (45 min).** The second meal, from a different dish family.
3. **Weeks 3 and 4 (2 × 45 min).** The third and fourth meals; compare the four in the log.

**Look for:**
- every meal is one the household would choose to eat again;
- nothing past its limit goes into a dish.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Unsafe leftovers | Reheated a second time | Reheat only the portion you will eat, once |
| A muddled pot | Everything thrown in without a plan | Choose one dish family; leave out what doesn't fit |

**Safety:** leftovers within 2 days and reheated until steaming hot, only once; food out of the fridge for more than 2
hours in total thrown away; a reheated dish with a §9 core figure probed (assessment §9).

**Record:** each week's inventory with dates; the dish family; the prediction and the result; the photos and cards.

### IND-5.M3.X1 — Four pantry meals, logged

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-ind-5-m3-x1 -->
*brief, brief* — The four pantry meals, logged.
<!-- END generated:exercise-ind-5-m3-x1 -->

**Task:** make cooking from what is there a habit, with adaptations predicted and confirmed. Cook one meal a week for
four weeks from the pantry, fridge and leftovers, with no shopping for it. Each meal adapts a known dish family,
predicted in writing before cooking.

**You need:** whatever the week leaves, within the §9 limits; the pantry; the notebook; household cards.

*Procedure:*
1. Each week: the inventory with dates, the dish family and the predicted adaptation.
2. Cook; photograph; collect household cards.

### IND-5.M4.L1 — From a written description

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m4-l1 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `RDV-4.M2.L2` · moves JD-CHSE→5*

*Objectives:* [J] infer components, techniques and proportions from a menu or cookbook description
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m4-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a menu line names a dish and leaves out almost everything needed to cook it. Inferring the missing
parts from the frameworks and ratios you know, and testing the least sure part, is how a cook works from a description.

**You need:** a menu line or cookbook headnote chosen by a household member; the ratio sheets of `RDV-4.M1`; the
classical frameworks (`CLS-4`, `SAU-2`, `BAK-2`); seasonal ingredients for two attempts; the notebook.

**Explanation:**

*Reading a description.* A menu line or a cookbook headnote names components and techniques. What it leaves out
(proportions, temperatures, order) you infer from the classical frameworks and ratios.

*The inference table.* Each component, its likely technique, its proportion from a known ratio, and how sure you are.
The least sure gets a test.

A worked example, "Roasted North Sea plaice, brown butter, capers, celeriac purée":

| Component | Technique | Proportion | Sure? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plaice | Roasted whole or on the bone; 63 °C for 15 seconds | One fish per guest | Likely |
| Brown butter | Butter cooked until nutty and brown (`SAU-2`) | About 20 g per portion | Sure |
| Capers | Rinsed, added to the butter at the end | A spoonful per portion | Sure |
| Celeriac purée | Simmered in milk, blended | Roughly equal weights of celeriac and liquid, reduced | Guess: test it small |

*The plan and the fallback.* As in the mystery-basket method (`IND-5.M2.L1`), with a simpler version of the least sure
component: here, a celeriac mash if the purée is too loose.

*The second attempt.* Change only the least sure component, for the reason the first attempt showed.

**Practice:**
1. **A description (75 min).** A household member chooses a menu line. Write the inference table, cook the dish, and
   collect a photo and household cards.
2. **The second attempt (75 min).** Change the least sure component from what the first attempt showed, and compare.

**Look for:**
- the household member recognises the dish they chose;
- the second attempt is closer, for a reason you wrote down.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The dish has something the menu didn't say | A component invented | Cook only what the description names |
| A proportion far off | Guessed where a known ratio exists | Use the ratio; test the guess |

**Safety:** every protein in the table carries its §9 figure; a dish the description serves raw or rare is cooked to
§9 for risk groups (the risk-group row).

**Record:** the inference table; both attempts' photos; what changed and why.

### IND-5.M4.L2 — From a tasted dish

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m4-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M6.L2` · moves FL-SENS→5*

*Objectives:* [S] taste a dish (restaurant or prepared by someone else) and recreate it; [J] compare side by side
<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m4-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** recreating a dish from its taste asks your senses to measure. Calibrated tasting notes, and a side-
by-side comparison with the original, show whether your palate can be trusted as an instrument.

**You need:** a dish tasted at a restaurant, or cooked by someone else (a portion kept if possible); the tasting form of
`FLV-3.M6.L2`; your salt and acid test results from the last 6 months; seasonal ingredients for the recreation; a
household member as blind taster.

**Explanation:**

*Tasting to take apart* (`FLV-3.M6.L2`). Taste the dish with the calibrated method, and note each with an estimate:
- salt, acid, sweetness, bitterness and umami;
- fat;
- aromatics;
- texture;
- temperature.

Estimates in measurable terms help: "more acid than a usual vinaigrette", "salt like a well-seasoned stock".

*Recreating it.* Infer the components and techniques from the notes, then cook. Start with the balance you noted, not
the look: a dish that tastes right but looks a little different is closer than the reverse.

*Side by side.* Where possible, taste the original and the recreation together: a kept portion, or the other cook
making it again. A household member tastes them blind, coded and in random order (`RDV-4.M4.L1`). Otherwise, compare
against your notes.

*Calibration still holds.* Your salt and acid tests passed in the last 6 months stand behind your sensory notes
(assessment §5.5). If they are older, retake them first (`TST-1`).

**Practice:**
1. **Tasting and first recreation (75 min).** Write the tasting notes and the inferred plan; cook the dish.
2. **Side by side (75 min).** Adjust the recreation and taste it next to the original; name the differences.

**Look for:**
- the tasting notes name the balance in measurable terms;
- the blind taster can't easily tell the two apart, or names one clear difference.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| It looks right but tastes off | The look recreated, not the balance | Recreate from the tasting notes, not the photo |
| Notes you can't rely on | No calibration test in the last 6 months | Retake the salt and acid tests |

**Safety:** a kept portion stored as leftovers: covered, in the fridge, within 2 days, reheated once until steaming hot
(assessment §9).

**Record:** the tasting notes; the plan; the side-by-side result and the differences named.

### IND-5.M6.L1 — Rescue under pressure

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-ind-5-m6-l1 -->
*3 h · 3 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M6.L1`, `SAU-2.M7.L1` · moves JD-TRBL→5, FL-CORR→5*

<!-- END generated:lesson-ind-5-m6-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** faults happen in every kitchen, usually at the worst moment. Diagnosing fast by the senses and
rescuing quietly, so guests never notice, is what separates a trained cook from a recipe follower.

**You need:** a household member willing to introduce faults without warning; household meals for three sessions,
the third for guests; spare stock, a pasteurised yolk, starch, a clean pan; a camera for before and after photos.

*The clinic.* A household member introduces a fault mid-cook without warning: too much salt, a split sauce,

*The rescues:*

| Fault | Rescue | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Over-salted | Dilute with unsalted liquid or more of the main ingredient; add fat or starch to carry it | Sugar or acid: they don't remove salt |
| Split emulsion | A fresh pasteurised yolk or a spoon of water; whisk the broken sauce in slowly | A raw unpasteurised yolk in an uncooked sauce |

| Burnt base | Moved at once to a clean pan without scraping; tasted | Stirred in |
| Too much acid | Rounded with fat or a little sweetness, or diluted with more of the main ingredient (`FLV-3.M6.L1`) | Served without tasting again |

1. **Fault one (45 min).** A meal with one fault introduced; diagnosed, rescued and logged with before and after photos.
2. **Fault two (45 min).** Another fault in another dish.
3. **Fault three (45 min).** A fault while cooking for guests, who aren't told.

- the diagnosis names the fault and its cause within a minute;
- the rescued dish passes the household's or the guests' card.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The salt is still there under sweetness | Masked with sugar or acid | Dilute or carry it with fat or starch |
| The rest of the meal is lost | The rescue took too long | Decide within a minute: rescue or a reasoned restart |

**Safety:** a split mayonnaise or warm egg sauce is rescued with a pasteurised yolk, for every guest (assessment §9). A
rescued protein still meets its §9 row.

**Record:** for each fault, the time noticed, the diagnosis, the cause, the fix, before and after photos, and the cards.

### IND-5.M6.L2 — Doneness by the senses

**Why it matters:** an experienced cook knows a dish is done before the probe confirms it. Writing a prediction and
