**Before you start.** Dried beans, lentils, whole grains, milk and cheese are the cheapest and most versatile food in
the kitchen. They are also where home cooking most often falls back on tins and packets. This course gives you control
over them:
- **legumes:** soaked or not, salted or not, cooked to creamy but whole, across several cuisines;
- **whole grains and polenta:** cooked in a batch and used through the week, safely;
- **dairy:** how milk, cream, butter and cheese behave with heat and acid; browned butter; a smooth cheese sauce; a
  fresh cheese made at home;
- **the pantry:** sensible substitutions, an allergen-free dish, and a meal from what is in the cupboard.

*The thread is prediction.* Before each comparison and substitution, write what you expect to happen to the time, the
texture and the flavour, and then check it. This is the start of adapting recipes (`RD-ADPT`), which grows through S3
and S4.

*Two safety rules run through the course*
([assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment)):
- **dried common beans** (*Phaseolus vulgaris*: kidney, white, borlotti, black and Dutch brown beans) are soaked for at
  least 5 hours with the water poured away, then boiled in fresh water for at least 30 minutes; never in a slow cooker;
- **cooked beans, grains and polenta** are cooled and in the fridge within 2 hours, kept there at most 2 days, or
  frozen; reheated once, to 74 °C.

Plan about three weeks. Beans need overnight soaking and an hour or two on the hob; the fresh cheese needs a day to
drain and set. Everything else fits a weeknight. The pot of beans and the grain batch cover several meals.

**Have ready:** the heavy saucepan with a lid and the casserole; four small pans for the comparison; a fine sieve and
muslin; the probe; sheet pans for cooling; freezer containers, labels and a pen; the scale; bowls for soaking; the
tasting form; the waste log; the allergen checklist (`SAF-1`); the journal.

### LEG-2.M1.L1 — Legume types and soaking

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 12 h · builds on `STA-1.M1.L1` · moves IN-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [K] beans, chickpeas, lentils, split peas; soaking, salting, baking soda; digestibility
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** half of what is said about cooking beans is myth: salt toughens them, soaking doesn't matter, tomato
is fine from the start. The other half is a real safety rule. This lesson sorts one from the other.

**You need:** 400 g of dried chickpeas for the comparison of `LEG-2.M1.X2`; four bowls; salt; the scale; labels.

**Explanation:**

*The types:*
- **beans:** white beans, borlotti, kidney beans, black beans, Dutch brown beans (*bruine bonen*) and *kapucijners*;
- **chickpeas;**
- **lentils:** green and brown (*Puy*-style), which hold their shape; red and yellow, which collapse;
- **split peas:** green or yellow, which collapse into a purée (*snert*).

*Soaking.* Whole beans and chickpeas soaked for 8–12 hours in plenty of water (three times their volume) cook faster and
more evenly. Lentils and split peas need no soaking. Soaked beans are drained and cooked in fresh water.

*Salt in the soak* (about 1 % of the water's weight, 10 g per litre). The myth says it toughens beans; in fact it
softens the skins and gives creamier, more intact beans. The comparison of `LEG-2.M1.X2` tests it.

*Baking soda.* A pinch in the soak or the cooking water softens skins in hard water. Too much makes the beans mushy and
soapy.

*Acid* (tomato, vinegar, wine) slows softening, so it goes in once the beans are tender.

*Digestibility.* Beans contain sugars (oligosaccharides) that the gut ferments. Soaking, cooking well and eating legumes
regularly help.

*Safety.* Dried kidney beans and the other common beans (*Phaseolus vulgaris*: white beans, borlotti, black beans,
*bruine bonen*) contain a lectin, phytohaemagglutinin, that proper cooking destroys. Four or five raw beans can make
someone ill. The rule (assessment §9):
- soaked for at least 5 hours, and the soak water poured away;
- boiled in fresh water for at least 30 minutes, before or as the start of the simmer;
- never cooked in a slow cooker, which may not get above 75 °C; undercooked beans are the danger, not raw ones alone;
- tinned beans are already cooked.

Chickpeas, lentils and split peas are other species and are not covered by this rule.

**Practice:**
1. **Soak (20 min, then overnight).** Set up the soaks for `LEG-2.M1.X2`: two bowls of 100 g of chickpeas in 500 ml of
   water, one with 5 g of salt; weigh, label and leave them overnight.

**Look for:** for each legume, you can say whether it needs soaking, how it will cook, and what salt, soda and acid do.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Mushy lentils | Soaking lentils | Lentils go in dry |
| Beans still hard after hours | Adding tomatoes at the start | Acid once they are tender |
| Soapy, mushy beans | A spoonful of baking soda | A pinch, only in hard water |
| A risk of poisoning | Kidney beans in a slow cooker, or common beans that never boiled for 30 minutes | Soak, pour away, boil 30 minutes |

**Safety:** the common-bean rule of assessment §9: soaked at least 5 hours, the water poured away, boiled in fresh water
for at least 30 minutes, never in a slow cooker.

**Record:** the soaks, weighed and labelled, and the predictions for `LEG-2.M1.X2`.

### LEG-2.M1.L2 — Cooking beans and chickpeas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `LEG-2.M1.L1` · moves TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] simmer or pressure-cook to creamy and whole; cook with aromatics; store in their liquid
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a pot of home-cooked beans in their own liquid is creamier and better than any tin, costs a
fraction, and is the start of a week of meals. The skill is to cook them through without bursting them.

**You need:** 500 g of dried white beans or chickpeas, soaked overnight; a halved onion, 2 cloves of garlic, a bay leaf,
a carrot, and a Parmesan rind or a strip of kombu (optional); salt; the heavy saucepan; a slotted spoon; a timer;
shallow containers; for session 2, greens (kale, chard or spinach) and bread or stock.

**Explanation:**

*Simmer gently:*
1. Drain the beans, and cover them by a few centimetres of fresh water.
2. Bring them to a boil and skim. Common beans (*Phaseolus*) then stay at a steady boil for 30 minutes (`LEG-2.M1.L1`,
   assessment §9); chickpeas need not.
3. Lower to a bare simmer, partly covered.

A hard boil for the whole cook bursts the skins. Most soaked beans take 45–90 minutes, chickpeas up to 2 hours,
depending on age and size. Old beans take longer.

*Aromatics:* a halved onion, garlic, bay, a carrot, a strip of kombu or a Parmesan rind, all lifted out at the end.

*Salt:* in the soak, and in the cooking water (about 8 g per litre) once the beans start to soften.

*The target:* creamy all the way through, with no chalky centre, and the skin intact. Test five beans, because they cook
unevenly: bite them, or press them against the roof of your mouth.

*Pressure-cooking* (optional) cuts the time to about a third. The pot is not required on this course.

*Storing:*
- the beans kept in their cooking liquid, which keeps them moist and makes a ready-made sauce base;
- cooled in shallow containers, and in the fridge within 2 hours;
- kept for at most 2 days in the fridge, or frozen in portions with their liquid (assessment §9).

**Practice:**
1. **A pot of beans (60 min active, about 2 hours in total).** Soaked white beans or chickpeas with aromatics; tested
   every 15 minutes from the 45-minute mark; cooled and stored, logged.
2. **Use them (40 min).** A dish from the pot: beans on toast with garlicky greens, or a white-bean and kale soup with
   some of the cooking liquid.

**Look for:** five beans in a row creamy to the centre; skins intact; a slightly thickened, flavoursome liquid.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Burst skins, or a safety risk | A hard boil for the whole cook, or skipping the 30-minute boil for common beans | 30 minutes' boil, then a bare simmer |
| Chalky beans in the dish | Judging on one bean | Test five |
| Dry beans, no sauce base | Draining away the liquid | Keep them in it |
| Flat-tasting beans | Salting only at the very end, so the beans taste flat inside | Salt in the soak and as they soften |

**Safety:** common beans boiled for 30 minutes (assessment §9). Cooked beans in the fridge within 2 hours, kept at most
2 days or frozen, reheated once to 74 °C.

**Record:** the test times, the cooling log and a photo of the dish.

### LEG-2.M1.L3 — Lentils and split peas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l3 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `LEG-2.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→1, TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] lentils that hold their shape versus ones that collapse; a dal and a pea soup; [K] legumes across cuisines
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m1-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** lentils need no soaking and cook in under half an hour, so they are the quickest legume meal there
is. Whether they hold or collapse decides the dish: a salad or a dal.

**You need:** for session 1, 200 g of green or brown lentils, 250 g of red lentils, an onion, garlic, ginger, cumin
seeds, mustard seeds, turmeric, a dried chilli, ghee or oil, a lemon, a vinaigrette (`SAU-2.M6`), herbs; for session 2,
the *snert* ingredients (500 g of green split peas, a leek, a piece of celeriac, 2 potatoes, a carrot, a smoked sausage,
celery leaves) or brown beans and their soup vegetables.

**Explanation:**

*Lentils that hold their shape* (green, brown, black *beluga*) are simmered in plenty of water for 20–30 minutes, for
salads and sides. They are drained when just tender, and dressed while warm, so they take up the dressing.

*Lentils that collapse* (red, yellow) cook in 15–20 minutes into a purée: the base of a **dal**. Rinse them, simmer them
in about three times their volume of water with turmeric, stirring now and then, and salt them once they are soft.

*The tadka.* A dal is finished with a *tadka*: whole spices, garlic and onion fried in hot fat and poured over at the
end (`IN-ARO`). Heat the fat, add the mustard seeds until they pop, then cumin, chilli, onion, and garlic last, for
seconds. Pour it, sizzling, over the hot dal.

*Split peas.* A Dutch *snert* simmers green split peas with leek, celeriac, potato and smoked sausage until thick, as in
`STK-2.M4.L3`. The spoon stands up in a good one.

*Legumes across cuisines* (`CK-CUIS` level 1):

| Tradition | Legume dishes |
|---|---|
| Indian subcontinent | *Dal*, *chana masala* |
| Middle East | Hummus, *ful medames*, falafel |
| Mediterranean | *Pasta e fagioli*, cassoulet, chickpea and spinach stews |
| Latin America | Black beans and rice |
| The Netherlands | *Snert*, *bruine bonensoep*, *kapucijners* with bacon |

Each pairs a legume with a grain or bread, an aromatic base and a fat.

**Practice:**
1. **Lentils two ways (45 min).** Green lentils as a warm salad, dressed while warm with a vinaigrette and herbs; red
   lentils as a dal with a *tadka*.
2. ***Snert* or a Dutch brown-bean soup (60 min, more on the hob)** for a winter dinner.

**Look for:** green lentils separate and tender; a dal smooth and spoonable, not runny; a *tadka* that sizzles and
smells toasted, not burnt.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A mushy salad | Green lentils cooked to mush | Drain at just tender |
| No sizzle, a raw spice taste | A *tadka* added to cold dal | Hot fat onto hot dal |
| A bitter dal | Garlic burnt in the *tadka* | Garlic last, for seconds |
| A flat dal | An under-salted dal | Salt once soft; taste |

**Safety:** hot fat in the *tadka* spits as the seeds pop: a lid at hand, and pour it away from you. Leftover dal and
*snert* are cooled and in the fridge within 2 hours (assessment §9).

**Record:** photos of the salad and the dal, and the soup.

**Task:** test two bean myths at once, by cooking the same legume four ways and comparing time and texture. One variety

6. **Test** five chickpeas from each pot every 15 minutes; log the time when all five are creamy to the centre. Top up

### LEG-2.M2.L1 — Polenta

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M4.L2` · moves TS-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] soft polenta and set polenta; grilled or fried slices
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** one pot of polenta gives two meals: soft and spoonable tonight, then set, sliced and crisp tomorrow.
It is also the easiest gluten-free starch in the pantry (`LEG-2.M4.L1`).

**You need:** 250 g of coarse polenta (maize meal); about 1.25 litres of water or light stock; salt; 40 g of butter and
50 g of grated hard cheese; a heavy saucepan; a whisk and a wooden spoon with a long handle; an oiled tin of about 20 ×
20 cm; a mushroom or bean ragù and greens to serve.

**Explanation:**

*Polenta:* coarse maize meal cooked in water, stock or milk. A common ratio is 1 part of meal to about 4–5 parts of
liquid by volume for a soft polenta, and less liquid for a firmer one.

*The method:*
1. Whisk the meal into simmering salted liquid (about 8 g of salt per litre) in a thin stream.
2. Lower the heat to a gentle bubble, and swap the whisk for the wooden spoon.
3. Stir often until the grains are tender: about 30–45 minutes for coarse polenta, a few minutes for instant.

It thickens fast and spits: keep the heat low, and stir with a long spoon.

*Soft polenta* is finished with butter and cheese and served at once, because it sets as it cools. Loosen it with hot
water if it stiffens before it reaches the table.

*Set polenta:*
1. Spread it 1.5–2 cm thick in an oiled tin.
2. Cool it completely, in the fridge within 2 hours.
3. Cut it into slices.
4. Grill or fry the slices until crisp outside and soft inside.

*The safety of starches* (`STA-1.M4`): cooked grains and polenta are cooled and chilled within 2 hours, and kept at most
2 days (assessment §9).

**Practice:**
1. **Soft polenta (45 min).** With butter and cheese, served with a mushroom or bean ragù. Spread half in the tin
   to set.
2. **Set polenta (40 min).** The set half sliced and grilled or fried; served with greens.

**Look for:** a soft polenta that slowly flows on the plate; smooth and without lumps; set slices that hold and crisp
without sticking.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lumps | Meal dumped in, so it forms lumps | A thin stream, whisking |
| A gritty texture | Undercooked, gritty polenta | Cook until tender: taste |
| Slices that fall apart | Slices fried before they set | Cool it completely |
| A solid lump at the table | A pan of soft polenta left to stiffen while waiting | Serve at once; loosen with hot water |

**Safety:** bubbling polenta spits and sticks to skin: a long spoon, low heat, and a sleeve over your wrist. The set
polenta goes into the fridge within 2 hours (assessment §9).

**Record:** photos of the soft polenta on the plate and the crisp slices.

### LEG-2.M2.L2 — Batch-cooking grains

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STA-1.M4.L3` · moves KM-WAST→3, IN-STA→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cook, cool and store grains for the week; [J] plan uses across meals
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m2-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a tray of cooked spelt or barley in the fridge is three weeknight meals half made. Batch-cooking
only saves time if the batch is stored safely and used in meals that don't taste the same.

**You need:** 500 g of a whole grain (spelt, pearl barley or wheat berries); a large pot; a colander; two sheet pans;
freezer containers or bags; labels; the probe; the plan for `LEG-2.M2.X1`.

**Explanation:**

*Whole grains grown in or near the Netherlands:* spelt (*spelt*), pearl barley (*gerst*), oat groats, buckwheat and
wheat berries. They are cooked like pasta, in plenty of salted water, until tender with a slight chew, then drained.
That takes 20–60 minutes, depending on the grain: pearl barley and buckwheat are quickest, wheat berries slowest.

*A batch for the week, safely* (assessment §9):
1. Spread the cooked grains on a tray to cool quickly, and chill them within 2 hours.
2. Keep two days' worth in the fridge, covered (at most 2 days).
3. Freeze the rest flat in portions.
4. Thaw in the fridge, and reheat to 74 °C, once.

This follows the stricter Dutch rule (§2 of the overview): two days in the fridge, not four.

*Planning the uses:*
- a warm grain salad with roast vegetables;
- a grain bowl with beans and a vinaigrette (`SAU-2.M6`);
- a soup thickened with grains;
- an *orzotto*, a risotto-style dish of barley;
- fried grains with egg.

Each meal uses a different flavour direction, so the batch doesn't feel repeated.

*The waste log.* What is left at the end of the week is weighed and compared with earlier weeks (`KM-WAST`).

**Practice:**
1. **Cook and store a batch (40 min active).** A grain batch for the week, cooled on trays, portioned, labelled; the
   fridge and freezer photographed; the three meals planned.

**Look for:** grains tender with a slight chew, not split or mushy; in the fridge within 2 hours; every container
labelled and dated.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Warm grains at bedtime | A deep pot of grains cooling on the stove | Spread on trays |
| Grains past their time | A week's batch kept in the fridge for a week | 2 days; freeze the rest |
| Bored household | Three meals of the same flavour | A different direction each meal |

**Safety:** this lesson is the safety rule: cooled on trays and in the fridge within 2 hours, at most 2 days there, the
rest frozen, reheated once to 74 °C (assessment §9).

**Record:** the cooling time, the photos of the fridge and freezer, and the plan.

### LEG-2.M3.L1 — Milk, cream and butter

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M4.L1` · moves IN-DAI→3*

*Objectives:* [K] fat content, curdling with acid and heat, reducing cream, browning butter; [S] *beurre noisette*
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a lemon sauce made with milk curdles; the same sauce with cream holds. Knowing why, and which Dutch
dairy product does what, saves sauces and lets you substitute with confidence.

**You need:** 200 ml of whole milk; 400 ml of whipping cream (*slagroom*); a lemon; 50 g of butter; three small pans; a
cold bowl; a spoon; a vegetable or a piece of fish for the *beurre noisette*.

**Explanation:**

*Milk* is water with fat droplets, lactose and proteins: casein, which curdles with acid and rennet, and whey proteins,
which set with heat.

*Fat content* decides how dairy behaves under heat and acid (`SCI-2.M4.L1`):

| Product (NL) | Fat | Boil and reduce? | With acid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Milk (*volle melk*, *halfvolle melk*) | About 3.5 % or 1.5 % | Scorches, skins, curdles easily | Curdles |
| Cooking cream (*kookroom*) | About 15–20 % | Usually, thanks to its stabilisers | Mostly holds |
| Whipping cream (*slagroom*) | About 35 % | Yes: reduces to a sauce | Holds |
| *Crème fraîche* | About 30–40 % | Yes | Holds |
| Sour cream (*zure room*) | About 20 % | Splits when boiled | Use off the heat |

*Curdling.* Heat and acid together make casein clump; the more fat, the more it is protected. So a lemon sauce is made
with cream, not milk, or the acid goes in off the heat.

*Reducing cream.* Whipping cream simmered until it coats a spoon becomes a sauce on its own. It must not boil hard with
salt and acid too early: add salty stock and lemon at the end.

*Butter* is about 80 % fat, the rest water and milk solids.
- ***Beurre noisette*:** butter heated until the water boils off (the foaming stops) and the milk solids turn golden
  brown and smell of hazelnut. Stop it by pouring it into a cold bowl, or with lemon. Seconds separate golden from
  black.
- **Clarified butter:** the milk solids removed, so it can be heated higher without burning (`SAU-2.M5`).

**Practice:**
1. **Three dairy tests and a *beurre noisette* (50 min):**
   - 200 ml of milk and 200 ml of cream, each brought to a simmer with a tablespoon of lemon juice, compared;
   - 200 ml of cream reduced to coat a spoon;
   - a *beurre noisette* stopped at golden, and spooned over a vegetable or fish.

**Look for:** the milk curdling and the cream holding; the reduced cream coating the spoon; the *beurre noisette* golden
with brown flecks, smelling of nuts, not burnt.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A curdled sauce | Lemon into a milk sauce on the boil | Cream, or acid off the heat |
| A split sauce | Sour cream boiled | Add off the heat |
| Black, bitter butter | *Beurre noisette* left in the hot pan until black | Pour into a cold bowl at golden |
| A split cream sauce | Cream reduced with salty stock until it splits | Reduce first; stock and salt at the end |

**Safety:** browning butter is very hot and foams up: a heavy pan, and the cold bowl ready before you start.

**Record:** photos of the three tests and a line on each.

### LEG-2.M3.L2 — Cooking with cheese

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAU-2.M3.L2` · moves IN-DAI→3*

*Objectives:* [K] cheese families; melting and stringiness; [S] a smooth cheese sauce
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** an old Gouda that is perfect on bread turns oily in a sauce. Knowing how each family melts lets you
pick the right cheese for a gratin, a sauce or the grill.

**You need:** five cheeses (for example young Gouda, *oud* Gouda, mozzarella, Parmesan and *paneer* or halloumi); five
slices of bread; a baking tray; the oven grill; for the sauce, a *béchamel* of 500 ml (`SAU-2.M3.L2`), 100 g of a young
cheese, a little white wine, and a vegetable or pasta.

**Explanation:**

*Cheese families:*
- **fresh:** ricotta, *paneer*, cottage cheese (*hüttenkäse*), *kwark*;
- **soft-ripened:** brie, camembert;
- **semi-hard:** young Gouda (*jong*, *jong belegen*), Edam;
- **hard and aged:** *belegen*, *oud*, Parmesan;
- **blue;**
- **stretched curd:** mozzarella.

*Melting:*
- **young, moist cheeses** melt smoothly, such as young Gouda, Emmental and Gruyère;
- **aged, dry cheeses** melt less and can turn oily and grainy, so they are grated finely, added off the heat, or used
  for flavour;
- **fresh acid-set cheeses** (*paneer*, halloumi) don't melt at all: they fry and grill.

*Stringiness.* Stretched-curd and some young cheeses form strings when hot; stirring hard and boiling make it worse.

*A smooth cheese sauce:*
- start from a *béchamel*, or toss the grated cheese with a little starch;
- add the cheese off the boil, in handfuls, stirring gently until each has melted;
- a little acid (wine) or a melting salt (sodium citrate) keeps it smooth.

*Dutch cheese ages:* *jong* (about 4 weeks), *jong belegen*, *belegen*, *extra belegen*, *oud* (about a year). The older
the cheese, the more flavour and the less melt. A mix works well: young for the melt, a little old for the taste.

**Practice:**
1. **A melt test and a sauce (50 min).** Five cheeses melted on toast under the grill, photographed side by side; then a
   smooth cheese sauce for a vegetable gratin, a macaroni cheese or a cauliflower cheese.

**Look for:** young cheeses melting into a smooth layer; old ones browning and turning oily; a sauce glossy and smooth,
with no grains or oil.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A grainy, oily sauce | Boiling a cheese sauce | Off the boil |
| A lump of cheese in the sauce | Adding all the cheese at once, in one lump | In handfuls, stirring |
| A disappointing grill | Expecting *paneer* to melt | Fry or grill it for colour |
| Oily threads in the sauce | Grating old cheese coarsely for a sauce | Grate finely; mix with young |

**Safety:** the grill is close and fierce: watch it, and use oven gloves for the tray.

**Record:** the melt-test photo and a spoon photo of the sauce.

### LEG-2.M3.L3 — A fresh cheese

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l3 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 1 d · builds on `LEG-2.M3.L1` · moves IN-DAI→3*

*Objectives:* [S] make ricotta-style cheese or *paneer* from milk and acid
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m3-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** a fresh cheese takes milk, lemon and half an hour. Making it shows you casein curdling in front of
you, which is the explanation behind every split sauce.

**You need:** 2 litres of whole pasteurised milk (not UHT); about 60 ml of lemon juice or 50 ml of white vinegar; a
little cream for ricotta; salt; a heavy pan; the probe; a fine sieve lined with muslin over a bowl; a slotted spoon; for
*paneer*, a plate and a weight (a pan filled with water).

**Explanation:**

*Acid-set cheese.* Whole milk (pasteurised, not UHT, which sets poorly) is heated to near the boil, around 85–90 °C on
the probe, then acidified with lemon juice or vinegar. The casein clumps into curds and leaves a yellow-green whey.

*Paneer:*
1. Stir in all the acid at once, gently, off the heat.
2. Strain the curds through muslin.
3. Rinse them briefly with cold water, to take away the taste of lemon.
4. Gather the muslin into a flat parcel, and press it under a weight for 1–2 hours into a firm block that slices and
   fries.

*Ricotta-style:*
1. Heat the milk, with a little cream, more gently.
2. Add the acid, and let the curds rest without stirring for 10 minutes.
3. Ladle them into the muslin and drain for 15–60 minutes, depending on how firm it should be.

*Yield:* about 150–200 g of cheese from a litre of whole milk. The whey makes a bread dough (`BRD-2`) or a soup.

*Storage.* A fresh cheese is perishable: covered in the fridge at 4 °C, eaten within 2 days (assessment §9).

**Practice:**
1. ***Paneer* or ricotta (45 min, plus pressing or draining and a night in the fridge).** From 2 litres of milk; the
   heating temperature logged; the curds, whey and yield weighed and photographed.

**Look for:** clear separation into curds and a yellow-green whey; a block that holds when sliced (*paneer*), or soft,
moist curds (ricotta).

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Few, weak curds | UHT milk | Fresh pasteurised milk |
| Milky whey, a low yield | Too little acid, so the whey stays milky | A little more acid, stirred gently |
| Tiny, grainy ricotta | Stirring ricotta hard, so the curds are tiny and grainy | Leave the curds to rest |
| Crumbling *paneer* | Unweighted *paneer* that crumbles | Press it under a weight |

**Safety:** hot milk boils over and scalds: a large pan, watched. The fresh cheese is in the fridge at 4 °C and eaten
within 2 days (assessment §9).

**Record:** the temperature log, the yield and the photos (in `LEG-2.M3.X1`).

### LEG-2.M3.X1 — *Paneer* or ricotta, then a dish

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-leg-2-m3-x1 -->
*guided, recipe* — *Paneer* or ricotta, then used in a dish.
<!-- END generated:exercise-leg-2-m3-x1 -->

**Task:** make a fresh cheese from milk and acid, use it in a dish with a reduced cream sauce, and explain in writing
how dairy behaves with heat and acid. The dish is, for example, *paneer* in a spinach sauce finished with reduced cream
(*palak paneer*-style), or ricotta gnudi or a pasta in a reduced cream and herb sauce.

**You need:** for 4: 2 litres of whole milk; lemon or vinegar; 200 ml of whipping cream; 400 g of spinach in season, or
flour and an egg for gnudi; spices or herbs; muslin and a weight; the probe; the scale.

**Recipe:** the *paneer* and the dish.
1. **Heat** the milk in a heavy pan, stirring now and then, until the probe reads 85–90 °C. Log it.
2. **Off the heat,** stir in 60 ml of lemon juice gently. Within a minute the curds separate from a yellow-green whey;
   if the whey is still milky, add another tablespoon.
3. **Strain** through the muslin-lined sieve; keep the whey. Rinse the curds briefly.
4. **Press** the muslin parcel flat under a weight for 1–2 hours. Weigh the block, and chill it overnight.
5. **Next day:** cut the *paneer* into cubes, and fry them in a little oil until golden.
6. **Cook** an onion, garlic and ginger in oil; add cumin, garam masala and the spinach; wilt it, then blend roughly.
7. **Reduce** the cream in a small pan until it coats a spoon; photograph it on the spoon. Stir it into the spinach,
   then add the *paneer* and salt to taste.
8. **Photograph** the cheese, the sauce on a spoon and the dish.
9. **Write** the explanation: one page on why milk curdles and cream doesn't, at what temperatures milk and egg proteins
   set (from this module, `EGG-1` and `SCI-2`), and how to keep a dairy sauce smooth.

For ricotta, follow `LEG-2.M3.L3` and reduce the cream with herbs for gnudi or pasta.

**Show:** `IN-DAI` level 3: the explanation of setting temperatures and curdling in writing; a cream sauce reduced
without splitting, photographed on a spoon; the cheese-making temperature logged; this check at 80 %. The stirred
custard, the other part of the row, is in `BAK-2.M5`.

**Time:** 1.5 h, plus pressing and a night in the fridge.

### LEG-2.M4.L1 — Substitutions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m4-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `LEG-2.M1.L2` · moves RD-ADPT→2, SA-ALLG→3*

*Objectives:* [J] swap legumes, grains and dairy and predict the effect on time, texture and flavour; replace milk, egg or gluten for an allergic eater and check every label
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m4-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a recipe rarely matches your cupboard, and some guests can't eat what it asks for. Substituting by
what an ingredient does, and predicting the effect, turns a recipe into something you can adapt.

**You need:** one dish from earlier in the course and the ingredient you will swap; the allergen checklist (`SAF-1`);
the labels of three pantry products (a stock cube, soy sauce, a spice blend); the journal.

**Explanation:**

*Substituting by function.* Before swapping an ingredient, ask what it does in the dish (body, starch, fat, acid,
binding), then choose a replacement that does the same.

*Legumes, grains and dairy:*

| Swap | Effect to predict |
|---|---|
| Chickpeas for white beans | Firmer, nuttier, longer cooking from dry |
| Green for red lentils | Holds its shape, so the dal doesn't thicken |
| Pearl barley for rice in a risotto | Chewier, longer cooking, cloudier |
| Spelt for wheat berries | Similar, a little faster |
| *Crème fraîche* for cream | Tangier; holds when boiled |
| *Kwark* or yoghurt for sour cream | Tangier; splits on the boil, so add off the heat |

*The prediction.* Each swap is written with its effect on time, texture and flavour, then checked.

*Cooking for an allergic eater* (`SA-ALLG` level 3):
- **replacing milk:** oil or a plant-based butter; oat or soy drink;
- **replacing egg:** a flax "egg" (a tablespoon of ground flax with three of water), or aquafaba (chickpea cooking
  liquid);
- **replacing gluten:** gluten-free grains such as buckwheat, rice and polenta, and certified gluten-free oats.

*Reading every label.* The 14 EU allergen groups are printed in bold in the ingredient list (`SAF-1.M4.L1`). Hidden
sources include milk in stock cubes, gluten (wheat) in soy sauce, and mustard and celery in spice blends. "May contain"
warnings count for a severe allergy.

*Cross-contact.* Clean tools, boards and pans; the allergen-free dish cooked first and covered, with its own spoon.

**Practice:**
1. **A swap with a prediction (45 min).** One dish from earlier in the course, cooked with one substitution; the
   prediction written first, the result noted against it. Then read the three pantry labels and mark every allergen.

**Look for:** a prediction written before every swap; labels checked and photographed for the allergen dish.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A swap that fails the dish | Swapping by name rather than function | Ask what it does |
| An allergic reaction | Missing a hidden allergen in a stock cube | Read every label in bold |
| Cross-contact | Cooking the allergen-free dish after the others, with the same spoon | Cook it first, with its own tools |

**Safety:** this lesson carries the allergen rules: every label read for the named allergen, "may contain" counted for a
severe allergy, and the allergen-free dish cooked first with clean tools (`SAF-1.M4`).

**Record:** the prediction and the result; the marked labels.

### LEG-2.M4.L2 — A meal from the pantry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-leg-2-m4-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `LEG-2.M4.L1` · moves CR-IMPV→1*

*Objectives:* [S] plan and cook a balanced meal from pantry staples only
<!-- END generated:lesson-leg-2-m4-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** the evening when there is "nothing in the house" is when a cook is most useful. With a stocked
pantry and the frameworks of this stage, there is almost always a balanced meal in the cupboard.

**You need:** your pantry, freezer and vegetable drawer, as they are; the phone; the frameworks of S1 and S2; an hour.

**Explanation:**

*A stocked pantry* (`ORI-1`):
- dried and tinned legumes;
- grains, pasta and polenta;
- tinned tomatoes;
- onions and garlic;
- spices, oil and vinegar;
- stock from the freezer;
- eggs and a hard cheese.

*Building a balanced meal from it:*
- a protein (legumes, egg, cheese);
- a starch;
- a vegetable (frozen, or a keeping vegetable such as cabbage, carrots, onions or squash);
- a sauce or dressing;
- something fresh or sharp to finish.

*The frameworks of the stage do the work:* a soup (`STK-2`), a pilaf (`STA-1`), a pasta, a dal (`LEG-2.M1.L3`), a
frittata (`EGG-1`). Choose the framework that fits what you have, not the dish you wish you had.

*Improvising* (`CR-IMPV` level 1): start from what is there, choose one framework, and adjust as you go. Taste at each
stage, and finish with acid or something fresh: lemon, vinegar, a handful of herbs, raw onion.

*An example.* A tin of chickpeas, an onion, half a savoy cabbage, tinned tomatoes, cumin and a lemon: a chickpea and
cabbage stew with rice, finished with lemon and yoghurt.

**Practice:**
1. **A pantry supper (60 min).** Photograph the pantry first. Choose a framework, cook, and note what was used.

**Look for:** a meal with a protein, a starch and a vegetable; cooked within an hour; nothing bought for it.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A heavy, beige plate | Three starches on one plate | One starch; add a vegetable |
| A flat meal | No acid or freshness | Lemon, vinegar, herbs |
| Not a pantry meal | Going out to buy a missing item | Substitute by function |

**Safety:** pantry leftovers follow the usual rules: in the fridge within 2 hours, eaten within 2 days (assessment §9).

**Record:** the pantry photo, the framework chosen and what was used.
