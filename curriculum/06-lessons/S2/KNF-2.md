**Before you start.** In S1 you learned to cut accurately and safely. In S2 the work grows: soups and stocks for ten, a
whole chicken, a loin to portion. The knife has to be **fast, still accurate, and sharp**. This course is short, but it
runs through the whole stage:
- **speed with uniformity:** the timed drills continue weekly through S2, with targets that rise; a fast set out of
  range still doesn't count;
- **prep at volume:** aromatics and herbs for 8–10 portions, organised so nothing is cut twice and nothing waits too
  long;
- **the first protein cuts:** trimming silver skin and portioning by weight, at a raw-protein station kept separate from
  everything else;
- **the whetstone:** a sharp knife kept sharp, judged by tests and logged.

*The targets* are the program's tolerances ([TS tolerances](../../04-competencies/TS.md)):

| Cut | Range |
|---|---|
| Julienne | 2–3 mm thick, 5–6 cm long |
| Brunoise | 2–3 mm |
| Small dice | 5–7 mm |
| *Paysanne* | 10–12 mm across, 2–3 mm thick |
| *Chiffonade* | Ribbons 1–2 mm wide (`KNF-1.M2.L3`) |

"8 of 10" means 8 of 10 randomly picked pieces fall within the range.

Plan about two weeks for the lessons. After them, the drills (`KNF-2.M1.X1`) and the edge log (`KNF-2.M3.L2`) run
weekly through S2, in about 40 minutes a week. The soup prep for ten is a weekend batch cook that stocks the freezer.

**Have ready:** the chef's knife and the paring knife, a honing steel (`EQP-1`); a combination whetstone (1000/6000
grit) with a non-slip base; a boning knife if you have one; the grid board or a ruler; the raw-protein board (`SAF-1`);
the scale; containers with lids, masking tape and a pen; a timer; a phone on a stand; the drill log and the calibration
log.

### KNF-2.M1.L1 — Speed with uniformity

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `KNF-1.M5.L1` · moves TS-KNF→4*

*Objectives:* [S] timed drills against S2 targets; [J] find where time is lost
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** in S1 you made each cut accurate; now the whole list has to be done in a working time. Speed doesn't
come from moving the knife faster, but from removing the movements that don't cut. A video of your own drill shows them
at once.

**You need:** the drill vegetables for the full list (about 1 kg: 2 onions, 3 carrots, a leek or piece of celeriac, a
bunch of parsley or a few cabbage leaves for *chiffonade*); the chef's knife, honed; the grid board; a waste bowl; a
timer; a phone on a stand; the drill log.

**Explanation:**

*The S2 target:* the `MS-S1` cut list (julienne, small dice and brunoise, chopped parsley, crushed garlic) plus
*paysanne* and *chiffonade*, all within range for 8 of 10 pieces, in 30 minutes. That is the cutting part of the
`TS-KNF` level 4 row.

*Where time is lost:*
- **handling a piece twice:** picking it up, putting it down, picking it up again;
- **an untidy board:** pieces in the way, so you stop to push them aside;
- **stopping to sweep;**
- **sticks that are not stacked:** cutting one stick at a time instead of a neat stack;
- **the guiding hand moving in jumps** instead of a steady retreat.

You won't notice them while cutting. Film the drill from the side and watch it back at half speed.

*Economy of motion:*
- **the knife stays low:** the tip barely leaves the board;
- **finished cuts are moved off the board with the back of the blade,** never the edge, which dulls it and can slip;
- **the waste bowl is within reach,** so trim goes straight in;
- **the order is planned:** all the peeling first, then all the cutting.

*Speed never at the cost of size.* A set out of range doesn't count, however fast. If the count falls below 8 of 10,
slow down until it is back, then build speed again.

**Practice:**
1. **Baseline and film (60 min).** Time the full list, from the first cut to the last. Measure 10 random pieces of each
   cut on the grid board. Film 2 minutes of the drill from the side. Log the time and the counts.
2. **Fix one loss (60 min).** Watch the video, and name the biggest time loss. Correct only that one, and repeat the
   drill. Log the new time and counts.

**Look for:** the time falling while the count within range stays at 8 of 10 or more.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A dull edge, pieces flicked off the board | Scraping the board with the edge | Turn the knife over; use the spine |
| Faster but ragged | Speeding up the knife instead of removing wasted motion | Same knife speed; fewer movements |
| A log better than the cuts | Measuring only the best pieces | 10 at random, every time |

**Safety:** the claw grip stays the same at any speed (`KNF-1.M1`). If the video shows your fingertips creeping forward,
slow down until it doesn't.

**Record:** the drill log, the video and the loss you corrected.

### KNF-2.M1.L2 — Prep at volume

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `WRK-1.M1.L3` · moves TS-MEP→4, TS-KNF→4*

*Objectives:* [S] aromatics and herbs for 8–10 portions; workflow and storage of cut produce
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** cutting for ten is not cutting for two, five times over. Done item by item, it takes all afternoon
and leaves a pile of cut onions waiting. Done by batching the motions, from a written list, it is quicker, tidier and
safer, and it is how professional kitchens prep.

**You need:** for session 1, aromatics for a large batch: 1 kg of onions, 750 g of carrots, half a celeriac (or a head
of celery), a head of garlic; for session 2, herbs and garnishes for ten (two bunches of parsley, a bunch of chives, a
leek or spring onions); containers with lids; masking tape and a pen; the scale; a timer; the prep list.

**Explanation:**

*Batch the motions.* Peel all the onions, then halve all, then slice all. One task at a time across the whole batch is
faster than finishing each item: the knife, the hands and the board stay set up for one movement.

*The prep list for volume:*

| Column | What it says |
|---|---|
| Item | Onion, carrot, celeriac, garlic, parsley |
| Quantity | Scaled for the batch, by weight |
| Cut | Small dice, *paysanne*, chopped |
| Container | Which tub, labelled |
| Order | What keeps longest is cut first; herbs last |
| Time | Planned and actual |

*Storing cut produce:*
- covered and cold, in the fridge;
- cut onions and garlic in closed containers, so the fridge doesn't smell of them;
- herbs cut last, or stored whole in a damp towel until needed;
- potatoes and celeriac under water if prepped ahead, so they don't brown;
- labelled with the item and the time (`SAF-1`).

*Clean as you go.* Between tasks, the board is cleared, wiped and the waste bowl emptied. A clear board is faster and
safer.

**Practice:**
1. **Aromatics for ten (75 min).** Write the prep list, then cut the onions and carrots in small dice, the celeriac in
   *paysanne* and the garlic crushed, timed, batching each task. Label each container. They go into the soup of
   `KNF-2.M1.X2` or the week's cooking.
2. **Herbs and finish (75 min).** Cut the herbs and garnishes for ten at the right moment: the parsley chopped and the
   chives sliced just before serving, the leek in fine rings. Label the containers.

**Look for:** one task at a time across the batch; containers labelled and in the fridge; the board clear between tasks.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A slow afternoon | Finishing one onion completely before starting the next | One task across the batch |
| Black, limp parsley | Herbs chopped an hour early | Cut herbs last |
| A fridge of mystery tubs | Unlabelled tubs | Label the item and the time |

**Safety:** a long session tires the hands: stop and stretch every 20 minutes, and keep the board steady on a damp cloth
(`KNF-1.M1`).

**Record:** the prep list with planned and actual times.

### KNF-2.M2.L1 — Trimming and portioning

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `SAF-1.M2.L2` · moves TS-FAB→2, SA-HYG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] remove silver skin and excess fat; portion by weight; [S] keep the raw-protein station separate
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m2-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** buying a whole loin and portioning it yourself is cheaper than buying steaks, and the steaks come
out the size you want. It is also the first time raw meat sits on your board for a long session, so the raw-protein
station becomes a skill of its own.

**You need:** the inputs of `KNF-2.M2.X1`: a piece of pork loin of about 1.2 kg (or a tenderloin of 500 g); the boning
or paring knife and the chef's knife; the raw-protein board; the scale; a container for trim; cling film or freezer
bags; labels; disinfectant spray; a phone on a stand.

**Explanation:**

*Silver skin* is a thin, shiny, tough membrane that doesn't soften with cooking: left on, it curls the meat in the pan
and chews like rubber. To remove it:
1. Slide the knife tip under one end, to make a tab.
2. Hold the tab.
3. Run the knife along under the membrane, the blade angled slightly up towards it, so you cut the skin and not the
   meat.

*Excess fat* is trimmed to the level the method needs. A layer of fat protects and flavours a roast; for a quick sauté
or a steak most of it is removed, leaving a thin edge.

*Portioning by weight:*
1. Decide the weight per portion: for example 150 g.
2. Cut the first steak, weigh it, and adjust the thickness of the next.
3. Aim for an even thickness, so the portions cook alike.

The end pieces that don't make a full steak go into a stir-fry or mince.

*The raw-protein station* (`SAF-1.M2`):
- a separate board and knife, used only for the raw meat;
- raw protein last in the work order: everything else for the meal is prepped first;
- hands washed at every change, and before touching anything else (the phone, the fridge, the tap);
- the trim straight into a container for stock, or into the bin;
- the station cleaned and disinfected at the end, the board and knife washed hot.

**Practice:**
1. **Trim (60 min).** Trim the silver skin and fat from the loin or tenderloin. Weigh the trim; keep it in a container
   for stock.
2. **Portion (60 min).** Portion the loin into equal steaks by weight, weighing each. Film the station for three
   minutes. This is `KNF-2.M2.X1`.

**Look for:** clean, membrane-free meat with little flesh lost; portions within a few grams of the target; no contact
between the raw board and anything else.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Much meat on the membrane | Sawing off thick slabs with the silver skin | A tab, then a thin, angled stroke |
| Steaks that cook unevenly | Portions by eye | Weigh each and adjust the next |
| A risk on the plate | The raw board used for the salad | A separate board; raw last |
| Raw juice on the screen | The phone touched with raw hands | Wash hands first, or a helper films |

**Safety:** raw meat stays at the bottom of the fridge until the moment you need it, and goes back within 2 hours at
most. Steaks not cooked within 2 days are frozen, labelled and dated (assessment §9).

**Record:** the trim weight, the steak weights and the video (in `KNF-2.M2.X1`).

### KNF-2.M2.X1 — Portion a pork loin into equal steaks

*drill, recipe* — Portion a piece of pork loin into equal steaks; weigh each.
<!-- END generated:exercise-knf-2-m2-x1 -->

**Task:** a loin trimmed and portioned into equal steaks at a hygienic raw-protein station, filmed for three minutes,
with the raw protein last in the work order.

**You need:** a piece of pork loin of about 1.2 kg (or a tenderloin of 500 g); the knives; the raw-protein board; the
scale; containers; freezer bags; labels; disinfectant; a phone on a stand.

**Recipe:**
1. **Prep everything else for the meal first** (vegetables, herbs, a sauce base), and put it away. Write the work order.
2. **Set up the raw station:** the raw board on a damp cloth, the knives, the scale with a piece of baking paper on it,

3. **Start filming** from the side, so the board, the hands and the sink are in view.

5. **Portion:** set a target, for example 150 g per steak (or 6 pieces from a 1.2 kg loin). Cut, weigh, adjust the next.

- **`TS-FAB` level 2, the portioning part:** the steaks within a few grams of the target (photo, video). The chicken

AI review checks the video for contact between raw and ready-to-eat items, hand washing and board changes: *competent*

### KNF-2.M3.L1 — Sharpening on a whetstone

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `EQP-1.M3.L2` · moves KM-EQPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] angle, pressure, burr, grits, stropping
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** honing keeps an edge straight, but it can't make a dull knife sharp again. A whetstone can, and it
lets you keep your own knives sharp for good. The skill is mostly about one thing: holding the same angle, stroke after
stroke.

**You need:** a combination whetstone (1000/6000 grit) with a non-slip base, or a damp towel under it; water; an
inexpensive old knife to practise on; the chef's knife; two coins; a sheet of printer paper; a marker pen; a phone on a
stand.

**Explanation:**

*Grits:*

| Stone | Grit | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Coarse | Around 400 | Repairs a dull or chipped edge |
| Medium | Around 1000 | Sharpens |
| Fine | 3000–6000 | Refines and polishes |

A combination stone of 1000/6000 covers most home needs.

*Soaking or splashing.* Water stones are soaked or kept wet, as the maker says. A dry stone clogs and scratches.

*The angle:* about 15–20° for most kitchen knives: the angle of the existing edge, held constant. Two coins stacked
under the spine give about that angle on a chef's knife, to learn the feel. Colour the edge bevel with a marker: after
a few strokes, the ink should be gone from the whole bevel. If it is left at the very edge, the angle is too low; if it
is left at the top of the bevel, too high.

*The burr:* grind one side, in strokes along the whole edge from heel to tip, until you can feel a fine burr (a rough
lip) along the whole edge on the other side when you draw your thumb across it, away from the edge. Then grind the other
side until the burr flips. Then make lighter strokes, alternating sides, to remove it.

*Stropping* on leather, newspaper or the fine stone, with the edge trailing, finishes the edge.

*Flattening.* A stone wears into a dish, which rounds the edge. Flatten it now and then on a flattening plate, or on
wet-and-dry sandpaper on a flat surface.

**Practice:**
1. **Coarse to fine (75 min).** Sharpen the old knife first, then the chef's knife, on the 1000 side until a burr forms
   along the whole edge on both sides, then on the 6000 side with lighter strokes. Strop. Test on the paper.
2. **Film the angle (60 min).** Sharpen again, filmed from the side, and compare the angle in the video with the coin
   reference.

**Look for:** a burr along the whole length; a constant angle on the video; the paper test clean afterwards.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A rounded, dull edge | A rocking angle | Coins under the spine; slower strokes |
| No improvement | Stopping before a burr has formed | Keep going until the burr runs the whole length |
| Scratches, a ragged edge | Pressing hard on the fine stone | Light strokes on the fine side |
| A clogged, glazed stone | A dry stone | Keep it wet |
| A rounded tip | Grinding the tip round | Lift the handle as you reach the tip |

**Safety:** the stone sits on a non-slip base, the edge always moves away from your fingers, and you feel for the burr
across the edge, never along it. A freshly sharpened knife cuts more easily: take extra care with the claw for the first
days (`KNF-1.M1`).

**Record:** the sharpening in the calibration log, with the paper test before and after.

### KNF-2.M3.L2 — Judging and logging the edge

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l2 -->
*1 h · 1 practical session · elapsed 4 wk · builds on `KNF-2.M3.L1` · moves KM-EQPT→3, JD-REFL→3*

*Objectives:* [S] paper and tomato tests; [J] decide when to hone or sharpen; keep a log
<!-- END generated:lesson-knf-2-m3-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a knife is only as good as the cook's judgment of it. Sharpening every week wears the blade away;
never sharpening leaves you pushing a dull edge. Four weeks of testing and logging teach you to tell the difference, and
an outside score shows whether your judgment can be trusted.

**You need:** the chef's knife; printer paper; a tomato; the honing steel; the whetstone; the edge log; a phone.

**Explanation:**

*Tests:*
- **paper:** hold a sheet by one corner and slice down through it; a sharp edge slices hanging paper cleanly, without
  catching or tearing;
- **tomato:** rest the edge on the skin; a sharp edge bites with no pressure, and a dull one slides;
- **thumbnail, with care:** rest the edge lightly on a thumbnail at an angle; a sharp edge catches, a dull one slides.

*Honing or sharpening?*
- **Hone** when the edge has rolled: it cuts well after a few strokes on the steel (`EQP-1.M3.L2`).
- **Sharpen** when honing no longer restores it: the paper still tears after honing.

*The edge log:*

| Date | Test result | Action (none, honed, sharpened) | Self-score (band 1–4) | AI band |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Bands: 1 tears the paper; 2 catches; 3 slices with a few catches; 4 slices cleanly.

Kept weekly for four weeks, with a photo of the paper test that AI review scores. If your band and the AI band differ by
more than one, look at the photo again: what did you miss?

**Practice:**
1. **Weekly check (four times, 15 min each, logged as one session).** Each week: the paper and tomato tests, the
   decision to do nothing, hone or sharpen, the action, and the log entry with the photo of the paper.

**Look for:** your score and the AI band agree; the edge stays sharp through the four weeks.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A knife getting narrower | Sharpening every week whether needed or not | Test first; hone if that is enough |
| A dull knife pushed through onions | Never sharpening because honing "still works" | Sharpen when the paper still tears after honing |
| Bands that don't match the photos | Scoring the edge without testing it | Test, then score |

**Safety:** the thumbnail test is optional: rest the edge, never draw it. Keep fingers clear of the paper's path.

**Record:** the four weekly entries (in `KNF-2.M3.X1`).

### KNF-2.M3.X1 — Sharpen the chef's knife, filmed

<!-- BEGIN generated:exercise-knf-2-m3-x1 -->
*drill, recipe* — Sharpen the chef's knife; video for AI review of the angle.
<!-- END generated:exercise-knf-2-m3-x1 -->

**Task:** a working edge from the stone, with a filmed angle, and four weeks of edge judgments checked against outside
review: five log entries in all.

**You need:** the whetstone; the chef's knife; printer paper; a tomato; a marker pen; two coins; the calibration log and
the edge log; a phone on a stand.

**Recipe:**
1. **Before:** paper-test the knife and photograph the result.
2. **Prepare the stone:** soak or wet it as the maker says, on its non-slip base.
3. **Mark** the edge bevel with the marker.
4. **Sharpen on the 1000 side,** filmed from the side: one side at a constant angle until a burr runs the whole length,
   then the other side. Check the ink is gone from the whole bevel.
5. **Refine on the 6000 side:** lighter strokes, alternating sides, until the burr is gone. Strop.
6. **After:** paper-test, photograph, and enter it in the calibration and edge log (entry 1).
7. **Each week for four weeks:** test, decide, act, self-score and photograph (entries 2–5).
8. **Compare** your self-scores with the AI bands.

**Show:**
- **`KM-EQPT` level 3, the knife part:** the sharpening in the calibration log, with the paper test before and after.
- **`JD-REFL` level 3:** five log entries where your band agrees with the AI band within one band in at least 4.

The clean paper test after sharpening is also the stone part of the `TS-KNF` level 4 row. AI review scores the angle in
the video and the paper-test photos: *competent* is a constant angle and a clean slice through the paper; *not yet* is a
rocking angle and torn paper.

**Time:** 1 h, plus 15 min a week for four weeks.
