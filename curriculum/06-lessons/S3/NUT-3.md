**Before you start.** A cook who feeds a household and guests needs to know three things: what a balanced meal is, how
to adapt a dish for someone who eats differently, and how to cook for someone with an allergy without putting them at
risk. This course is applied. It doesn't teach dietetics; it teaches cooking decisions:
- **applied nutrition:** what protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre do in a dish and for satiety; a balanced plate and a
  balanced week; what cooking methods do to nutrients;
- **adapting to diets:** vegetarian and vegan (replacing the functions of meat, egg and dairy), gluten-free,
  lactose-free and lower-salt cooking, without losing quality;
- **allergens:** the 14 groups of EU Regulation 1169/2011 [S23] in depth, their hidden sources, and a cross-contact plan
  from shopping to plate for an allergic guest.

The reference for a balanced diet in the Netherlands is the Voedingscentrum's *Schijf van Vijf* [S48]. Personal amounts
are in the Voedingscentrum's separate tool, not on that page.

*No individual advice.* The course gives no medical or dietary advice for individuals. A guest's doctor or dietitian
decides what they may eat, and the cook follows it. The guest's word on their allergy decides; the cook doesn't guess.

Plan about three weeks, after `VEG-3` and `KMG-3`. The dishes are household meals and one guest dinner. Every item of
the allergen check (`NUT-3.M3`) is a safety item, and `MS-S3` tests the 14 groups again in its safety quiz.

**Have ready:** the balance checklist and the plate model; last week's plan and journal (`KMG-3`); the tasting forms and
household cards; a separate, marked board and spoon for allergen-free dishes (Best: a colour-coded set with a pan);
labels and a marker; the phone for photographing labels; the list of the 14 groups (`SAF-1.M4`).

### NUT-3.M1.L1 — Macronutrients in cooking

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SCI-2.M2.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→2*

*Objectives:* [K] protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre: their roles in satiety and in cooking
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre each do a job in the pan and a job on the plate. A cook who
knows both can make a dish lighter or more filling without making it worse.

**You need:** pasta (white and wholemeal); cream and thick yoghurt; seasonal vegetables (leeks and mushrooms in autumn,
courgette in summer); Parmesan-style cheese; the scale; the tasting form, with a line for satiety ("hungry again after
how long?").

**Explanation:**

*Protein* (`SCI-2.M2`):
- **on the plate:** it satisfies for longest;
- **in cooking:** it sets, binds, browns and holds structure: eggs, meat, legumes, dairy.

*Carbohydrate:*
- **starches** give energy, body and texture: thickening, crisping, the base of a meal;
- **sugars** sweeten, brown and preserve.

*Fat:*
- carries flavour and aroma, because many aroma compounds are fat-soluble;
- gives richness and a satisfying mouthfeel;
- browns and crisps;
- is energy-dense, so the amount matters.

*Fibre:*
- from vegetables, legumes and whole grains;
- adds bulk and satiety, and slows digestion;
- changes texture: a wholemeal dough behaves differently (`BRD-3.M4`).

*Satiety in a dish.* Protein, fibre and some fat together make a meal satisfying. A plate of only starch, or only
vegetables, leaves people hungry soon.

*A substitution from a guide.* Change one thing and note its effect on taste and texture:
- wholemeal pasta for white: nuttier and firmer; note whether it keeps you full for longer;
- yoghurt for some of the cream: lighter and tangier. Yoghurt splits if it boils, so stir it in off the heat.

*Fat is not the enemy of a dish.* Removing all of it loses flavour and mouthfeel. Reduce it and keep some, where it is
tasted: a spoon of good olive oil on top, rather than a lot in the pan.

**Practice:**
1. **One dish, three ways (60 min).** A pasta dish made three ways: white pasta with a cream sauce; wholemeal pasta with
   the same sauce; and white pasta with half the cream replaced by yoghurt and more vegetables. Note the satiety and the
   taste of each on the form.

**Look for:** you can say what each macronutrient contributes to a dish, in cooking and on the plate.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A flat, dull "light" version | All the fat removed | Keep some fat where it is tasted |
| Hungry an hour later | A plate of only starch or only vegetables | Protein, fibre and some fat together |
| A grainy, split sauce | Yoghurt boiled | Off the heat; stir in at the end |

**Safety:** milk (cream, yoghurt, cheese) and gluten (pasta) named for anyone eating; the cream and yoghurt back in the
fridge.

**Record:** the three versions, the taste and satiety notes, and the substitution's effect.

### NUT-3.M1.L2 — The balanced plate

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l2 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `KMG-3.M1.L1` · moves CR-COMP→2*

*Objectives:* [K] balanced meals; how cooking methods affect nutrients; [S] check a week's menu
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a balanced week is mostly small changes to the meals you already cook. Knowing the plate model and
what cooking does to nutrients is enough to make them.

**You need:** last week's plan and journal (`KMG-3`); the plate model; the *Schijf van Vijf* page [S48]; a dinner plate;
the ingredients for one rebuilt meal; a steamer.

**Explanation:**

*A balanced meal:*
- about **half the plate vegetables** (and fruit across the day);
- **a quarter whole grains or potatoes;**
- **a quarter protein:** legumes, fish, eggs, dairy, poultry, meat;
- a modest amount of healthy fats;
- water as the drink.

*The* Schijf van Vijf [S48] frames a balanced week in five sections: vegetables and fruit; wholegrain products and
potatoes; water, tea and coffee without sugar; spreadable and liquid fats; and a section that includes unsalted nuts
and dairy or fortified alternatives. It asks for mainly plant-based eating, and sustainable fish once a week.

*A balanced week* (`KMG-3.M1`): legumes and fish more often; red and processed meat less often; vegetables at every
meal; variety across the week.

*How cooking methods affect nutrients:*
- **water-soluble vitamins** (C, several B vitamins) leach into cooking water: steaming, or using the cooking water in a
  soup, keeps more;
- **long cooking and keeping warm** destroy more vitamin C;
- **fat-soluble carotenoids** (carrots, pumpkin, tomatoes) are absorbed better with a little fat;
- **deep-frying** adds fat; grilling and roasting need little.

*Checking a menu.* Hold the week's plan against the plate model and the week guidelines. Make one or two changes, not a
rebuild: a vegetable side added to Tuesday; Thursday's sausages swapped for a lentil dish; Friday's chips roasted
instead of fried. A tomato sauce counts towards vegetables; a spoon of ketchup does not.

**Practice:**
1. **Check a week (60 min).** Last week's meals checked against the plate model; one meal rebuilt and plated, with half
   the plate vegetables, steamed or roasted.

**Look for:** a menu check that names specific, small changes.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Soft, grey vegetables, the water poured away | Boiled long | Steam, or use the water in a soup |
| The plan "has vegetables" every day | Ketchup or a garnish counted | Count real portions; half the plate |
| Nobody follows the new plan | The whole week rebuilt | One or two changes a week |

**Safety:** no advice for individuals: a guest's or household member's diet set by a doctor or dietitian is followed as
given.

**Record:** the week's check, the changes, and the photo of the rebuilt plate.

### NUT-3.M2.L1 — Vegetarian and vegan

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `VEG-3.M4.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] replace meat, egg and dairy functions (protein, umami, binding, richness)
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** a meat dish with the meat taken out is a dish with a hole in it. Asking what the meat, egg or dairy
did, and replacing that, gives a dish that is good on its own terms.

**You need:** a meat dish the household likes (a *ragù*, a stew, a shepherd's pie); legumes, tofu or tempeh; mushrooms,
miso, soy, tomato paste, *kombu*; olive oil, tahini, white beans or cauliflower; a vegetable stock (`STK-2`); the
tasting form; the household cards.

**Explanation:**

*Replacing functions, not ingredients.* Ask what the meat, egg or dairy does in the dish, then replace that function:
- **protein and substance:** legumes, tofu, tempeh; eggs and cheese (vegetarian); seitan (wheat gluten, so not for
  gluten-free guests);
- **umami and depth:** mushrooms, miso, soy, tomato paste, seaweed, browned onions (`FLV-3.M3`);
- **binding** (egg): a flax or chia "egg", aquafaba (chickpea water), starch, mashed legumes;
- **richness** (butter, cream): olive oil, plant creams, tahini, coconut milk, a purée of white beans or cauliflower;
- **stock:** a vegetable stock with mushrooms and *kombu* (`STK-2`).

*An example: a* ragù:
- the minced beef gave substance and browning: brown lentils, and mushrooms chopped fine and browned hard;
- it gave umami: tomato paste fried until dark, a spoon of miso;
- the butter or milk gave richness: olive oil (vegan), or butter kept (vegetarian).

*Hidden animal products.* Read the labels for:
- gelatine (`PST-3.M4`);
- fish sauce and anchovy (in Worcestershire sauce too);
- meat stock;
- rennet in some cheeses;
- lard in pastry;
- honey, for vegans.

*Vegan baking.* Egg and butter do several jobs at once in a bake (structure, rising, moisture, browning). A straight
swap often fails; use a tested vegan formula.

*New allergens.* Some replacements bring allergens of their own: soy (tofu, tempeh, miso), sesame (tahini), gluten
(seitan), lupin (some plant products). Name them.

**Practice:**
1. **A vegetarian version (60 min).** A meat dish adapted to vegetarian, with its functions replaced; compared with the
   original.
2. **A vegan version (75 min).** The same dish adapted further to vegan; compared by the household.

**Look for:** the adapted dish has the depth, richness and texture of the original.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Thin and bland | The meat removed and nothing added | Replace substance, umami and richness |
| Not vegetarian after all | Meat stock in the soup | Vegetable stock; check stock cubes |
| Not vegan after all | Gelatine in the dessert | A plant-based setter, by a tested recipe |
| A dense, flat vegan cake | A straight swap for egg and butter | A tested vegan formula |

**Safety:** the new allergens in the replacements (soy, sesame, gluten, lupin) named for guests.

**Record:** the function table for the dish, the two versions, and the household's comparison.

### NUT-3.M2.L2 — Gluten-free, lactose-free and lower salt

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l2 -->
*2.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `NUT-3.M2.L1` · moves RD-ADPT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] adapt thickening, baking and seasoning; [J] what to tell guests
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m2-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** each of these adaptations has a technique, and each has a hidden trap. What you tell the guest
afterwards must match what you actually did.

**You need:** cornflour, potato or rice starch; a gluten-free flour blend and a tested formula; lactose-free milk and
cream; aged Gouda or Parmesan; a soup recipe; lemons, herbs, spices, mushrooms or miso; the labels of your stock cubes,
soy sauce and spice blends; the tasting forms.

**Explanation:**

*Gluten-free:*
- **thickening** with cornflour, potato or rice starch instead of a flour *roux*: mixed with cold liquid first, stirred
  in, brought to the simmer;
- **baking** with gluten-free flour blends by a tested formula: the structure comes from starches, eggs and gums;
- **hidden gluten:** soy sauce (tamari is usually gluten-free; check the label), stock cubes, breadcrumbs, beer, malt,
  spice blends;
- **oats** only if labelled gluten-free;
- for **coeliac guests,** gluten is also a cross-contact risk, handled like an allergen (`NUT-3.M3`).

*Lactose-free:*
- lactose-free milk and cream behave like the normal ones in cooking;
- aged hard cheeses (old Gouda, Parmesan) are naturally very low in lactose;
- butter contains little.

A milk *allergy* is different: every milk protein is excluded, including butter and aged cheese (`NUT-3.M3`).

*Lower salt:*
- reduce gradually: taste adapts within weeks;
- build flavour with acid, herbs, spices, umami and browning;
- salt at the end, on the surface, where it is tasted most;
- count the salt in stock cubes, soy, cheese and cured meats.

*What to tell guests.* Say what the dish contains and what was replaced, clearly, and don't promise more than you did.
"Made without gluten-containing ingredients, in a kitchen that also uses flour" is honest. "It's gluten-free" is not,
for a coeliac guest, if it was cooked next to flour.

**Practice:**
1. **Gluten-free and lactose-free (75 min).** A sauce thickened without flour, and a gratin made lactose-free; each
   compared with the original.
2. **Lower salt (45 min).** A soup made with a third less salt, built up with acid, herbs and umami; tasted blind
   against the original.

**Look for:** a gluten-free sauce with the same body; a lower-salt soup the tasters don't call flat.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Gluten in a "gluten-free" dish | Ordinary soy sauce or a stock cube | Read every label; tamari checked |
| A coeliac guest misled | "Gluten-free" said of a dish cooked next to flour | Say exactly what was done |
| A flat, lower-salt soup | The salt simply left out | Add acid, herbs, umami; salt on the surface |
| Lumps in the starch-thickened sauce | Starch added to hot liquid dry | Mix with cold liquid first |

**Safety:** gluten and milk are allergen groups: for a coeliac guest or a milk allergy, the cross-contact plan of
`NUT-3.M3` applies, not just the substitution.

**Record:** the comparisons, the blind tasting of the soup, and the words you would use for a guest.

### NUT-3.M3.L1 — The 14 allergens in depth

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l1 -->
*2 h · 1 practical session · builds on `SAF-1.M4.L1` · moves SA-ALLG→4*

*Objectives:* [K] hidden sources (sauces, stocks, spice mixes, processed foods); labelling for guests
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** most allergen mistakes come from an ingredient nobody thought of: the celery in a stock cube, the
fish in Worcestershire sauce. Reading labels well is the first line of defence.

**You need:** ten products from your pantry (stock cubes, soy sauce, a spice blend, mayonnaise, pesto, bread, sausages,
dried fruit, a sauce, a biscuit); the list of the 14 groups; the phone for photos; a notebook.

**Explanation:**

*The 14 groups* (EU Regulation 1169/2011, Annex II [S23], `SAF-1.M4`):
1. cereals containing gluten;
2. crustaceans;
3. eggs;
4. fish;
5. peanuts;
6. soybeans;
7. milk;
8. tree nuts;
9. celery;
10. mustard;
11. sesame;
12. sulphites (above a threshold);
13. lupin;
14. molluscs.

*Hidden sources:*
- **sauces and condiments:** soy sauce (soy, wheat), Worcestershire sauce (fish), mayonnaise (egg, mustard), pesto
  (nuts, milk), *romesco* (nuts);
- **stocks and bouillon:** celery, milk, gluten;
- **spice mixes:** mustard, celery, gluten as a carrier;
- **processed foods:** milk in sausages and bread, egg in pasta, sesame in breads, lupin flour in some baked goods,
  sulphites in dried fruit and wine.

*Labels:*
- the 14 groups are emphasised in the ingredients list, for example in bold;
- "may contain" warnings are voluntary, but for a severe allergy they count;
- a maker can change a recipe, so a product needs a new check each time it is bought for an allergic guest. Last year's
  label is not this year's.

*Labelling for guests.* A menu card, or a word at the table, that names the allergens in each dish. Name the group
("tree nuts: walnut") rather than a vague "nuts".

**Practice:**
1. **A label hunt (60 min).** Ten pantry products read for the 14 groups and their hidden sources. Then a dish's
   ingredient labels photographed and its allergens listed.

**Look for:** you find every allergen on ten labels.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| An allergen missed | Only the front of the pack read | Read the ingredients list; look for the emphasis |
| A reaction from traces | "May contain" ignored for a severe allergy | It counts; choose another product |
| A changed recipe missed | Last year's label trusted | Check the label at every purchase |
| Celery missed in a soup | The stock cube not read | Read every component, stock included |

**Safety:** every item of this module's check is a safety item; for a guest, the guest's word on the allergy decides.

**Record:** the ten labels with their allergens, the photos of the dish's labels, and the dish's allergen list.

### NUT-3.M3.L2 — Cooking for an allergic guest

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l2 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `NUT-3.M3.L1` · moves SA-ALLG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] plan the menu, shopping, prep order and serving to prevent cross-contact; communicate clearly
<!-- END generated:lesson-nut-3-m3-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** for an allergic guest, a trace on a shared spoon can be enough. A written plan from shopping to
plate removes the chances for that to happen, and makes the guest's dish as good as the others.

**You need:** the cross-contact plan template and checklist; a guest with a stated allergy (real or role-played, for
example milk or celery); the menu's ingredients; a separate, marked board and spoon (Best: a colour-coded set and a
pan); labels and cling film; the phone for label photos.

**Explanation:**

*Ask first:*
- the allergen;
- how severe: traces, or only ingredients;
- what the guest usually does.

The guest's word decides; the cook doesn't guess.

*The cross-contact plan, from shopping to plate:*
- **menu:** the whole meal free of the allergen where possible, so nothing can be mixed up. Otherwise, the guest's dish
  designed to be as good as the others;
- **shopping:** every ingredient's label read and photographed; no product with a "may contain" warning for a severe
  allergy;
- **storage:** the guest's ingredients kept apart and labelled;
- **preparation:** the guest's dish first, on cleaned surfaces with washed tools, pans and boards; hands washed; no
  shared oil, spoons or tasting spoons;
- **cooking and serving:** covered, labelled, plated first and served first; no garnish from a shared bowl;
- **communication:** the guest told what is in each dish.

*An example, for a milk allergy.* A whole meal without milk: a celeriac soup finished with olive oil; a chicken roasted
with herbs and potatoes in oil; a pear poached in wine. Nothing to mix up, and nobody's dish is the "special" one.

*If something goes wrong.* A dish that may be contaminated is not served: it is made again, or replaced. Picking the
allergen off a plate does not remove it. For a reaction: the guest's own plan (their medication), and call 112.

**Practice:**
1. **Plan (60 min).** A cross-contact plan written for a guest with a stated allergy, checked against the checklist.
2. **Cook (2 h).** The meal cooked by the plan; the guest's dish and the others tasted by the household.

**Look for:** every step of the plan done in order; the guest's dish as good as the others.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The allergen picked off a plate | A mistake "rescued" | Not served; make it again |
| Cross-contact between pots | The same spoon for both | A marked spoon for the guest's pot |
| Celery in the "safe" soup | A stock cube nobody checked | Every label read and photographed |
| A dull "special" plate | The guest's dish an afterthought | The whole meal allergen-free where possible |

**Safety:** the guest's dish prepared first, with washed tools; no shared oil or tasting spoons; any doubt means the
dish is not served; 112 for a reaction.

**Record:** the plan, the label photos, the log of each step, and the tasters' comparison.
