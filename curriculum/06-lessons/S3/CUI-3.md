**Before you start.** This is the largest course of the program: eight culinary traditions, each studied through the
same lens and cooked in four lessons (history and pantry, two signature techniques, and a meal). It is taught in two
sections, each a unit of progress:
- **`CUI-3.SEC1` Europe and the Mediterranean** (`CUI-3.M1`–`M5`): the lens, then French regional, Italian, Iberian,
  and Levantine and North African cooking;
- **`CUI-3.SEC2` Asia and Mexico** (`CUI-3.M6`–`M9`): the Indian subcontinent, Chinese, Japanese and Mexican cooking.

A section is complete when the assessments of its modules are passed; `MS-S3` and `CUI-4` need the whole course.

*The lens.* Every tradition is studied with the same five questions (`CUI-3.M1.L1`): the pantry; the core techniques;
the flavour profile; the meal structure and drinks; history and geography.

*Respect and adaptation.* Each tradition is regional and alive. The course teaches representative dishes and the logic
behind them, not a single "authentic" version. Adaptations to a Dutch kitchen are allowed and named:
- **produce:** Dutch seasonal vegetables stand in where the original is out of season or flown in (pointed cabbage or
  chard for Chinese greens; Dutch greenhouse peppers and tomatoes in summer; pumpkin and roots in winter). Every
  substitution is noted in the exercise, with its effect;
- **pantry goods** (spices, rice, noodles, soy sauce, miso, *masa harina*, chillies, olive oil) come from specialist
  shops, which `CUI-3.M1` maps;
- **meat and fish** are Dutch or North Sea, by the rules of the earlier courses.

*Safety* follows [assessment §9](../../04-assessment.md#9-safety-figures-used-in-assessment) in every tradition:
- minced fillings and poultry to 75 °C; fish to 63 °C for 15 seconds; no raw-fish dishes here (`GRD-3.M3` has the
  rules);
- cooked rice cooled fast and kept cold;
- raw-yolk sauces with pasteurised egg; soft-set egg dishes and cured meats not served to risk groups;
- allergens (sesame, peanuts, tree nuts, soy, fish, crustaceans, gluten) named for guests.

Plan twelve to fifteen weeks, roughly one tradition every one and a half weeks, with the section boundary as a natural
pause. Most dishes are household meals; each tradition ends with a meal, several with guests.

**Have ready:** the pantry map (`CUI-3.M1.X1`); the lens template; the flavour plan and component table (`FLV-3`); the
scales and the probe; a wide frying pan or *paella* pan; a pasta machine or a long rolling pin; a wok; a heavy flat pan
or *tawa*; a rice pan with a lid; a mortar and pestle; the household and guest cards; the journal.

### CUI-3.M1.L1 — The lens

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l1 -->
*1.5 h · 0 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M2.L2` · moves CK-CUIS→2, JD-CHSE→3, CK-BEVR→1*

*Objectives:* [K] pantry, core techniques, flavour profile, meal structure and drinks, history and geography
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** eight traditions are too many to memorise as recipes. Five questions, asked the same way each time,
turn them into patterns you can recognise, compare and cook from.

**You need:** a cookbook or a set of recipes from a tradition you don't know; the lens template; the spice notes of
`FLV-3.M2`; the journal.

**Explanation:**

*The five questions:*
1. **the pantry:** the fats, starches, aromatics, acids and condiments that recur;
2. **the core techniques:** the methods that define the cooking;
3. **the flavour profile:** the balance of tastes and aromas that makes a dish "taste of" the tradition;
4. **the meal structure and drinks:** how a meal is put together, served and eaten, and what is drunk with it;
5. **history and geography:** why the tradition is as it is: climate, trade, religion, migration.

*Start at home.* Apply the lens first to the cooking you know best, Dutch home cooking: butter; potatoes and *stamppot*;
the *hachee* braise; mustard and vinegar; the evening meal of meat, potatoes and vegetables; coffee and a *koekje*. The
lens is clear before it meets the unfamiliar.

*The pantry as a fingerprint.* A tradition can often be recognised from its fat, its starch, its aromatic base and its
acid:
- butter, wheat, shallot and wine;
- olive oil, wheat or rice, garlic and tomato;
- ghee, rice and wheat, onion, ginger and spices, tamarind or yoghurt;
- neutral oil, rice, ginger, garlic and spring onion, soy and rice vinegar;
- lard or oil, corn, chilli and lime.

*Meal structure and drinks.* Courses in sequence (France, Italy); many dishes at once (China, India, the Levant); a set
tray (Japan). Water, wine, beer, tea or fresh drinks with the meal: at this level, knowing what is usually drunk.

*History and geography as explanation.* Trade routes (spices; chillies from the Americas), religion (pork, beef,
fasting), climate (preserving, fats). A date matters only if it explains a dish.

*Reading for the lens.* Read a cookbook or recipe from the tradition for what it assumes the cook already knows.

**Practice:** theory only (1.5 h): fill in the lens for Dutch home cooking, then for one tradition of the course from a
cookbook; list three things the cookbook assumes you know.

**Look for:** for any tradition in the course, you can name its fat, starch, aromatic base and acid.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Chinese food" as one cuisine | A country taken for a tradition | Ask the lens per region |
| A tradition judged by its takeaway | Restaurant versions abroad as the model | Read home cookbooks from the tradition |
| History as a list of dates | Facts without reasons | Keep only the history that explains a dish |

**Safety:** none in this lesson; each tradition's safety points are in its own lessons.

**Record:** the lens for Dutch home cooking and for one tradition.

### CUI-3.M1.L2 — Reading and sourcing

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l2 -->
*1.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `WRK-1.M2.L2` · moves CK-RCPT→4, IN-SEL→4*

*Objectives:* [J] interpret implicit technique in recipes from other traditions; [S] find ingredients and sensible substitutes for two recipes
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m1-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a recipe from another tradition leaves out what every home cook there knows, and uses measures and
names you don't. Reading it well, and knowing where to shop, decides whether the dish works.

**You need:** two recipes from different traditions (for example an American and an Indian one); the scale; a pen; a
phone for photos; a specialist shop near you (Asian, Turkish, Moroccan, Surinamese or Mexican).

**Explanation:**

*Implicit technique.* Recipes leave out what every home cook in that tradition knows: "fry the *masala* until the oil
separates", "cook the rice", "make a *sofrito*", "add *dashi*". Recognise the gap and fill it from the lens and the
course.

*Converting conventions:*
- **cups and spoons to grams,** by ingredient: a US cup holds about 240 ml, so a cup of water weighs about 240 g, a cup
  of flour roughly half that, a cup of sugar about 200 g;
- **Fahrenheit to Celsius:** subtract 32, multiply by 5, divide by 9; gas marks and fan ovens by your oven's table;
- **names:** US cilantro is coriander leaf; "scallions" are spring onions;
- **pans and equipment:** a wok, a *paella* pan, a *tawa*, and what you have instead.

*Finding errors.* Check a recipe whose ratio, time or quantity doesn't make sense against what you know: three
tablespoons of salt for 500 g of rice; 10 minutes for a beef shin.

*Sourcing.* Which ingredients are in a Dutch supermarket, which at an Asian, Turkish, Moroccan, Surinamese or Mexican
shop, which online. Note sensible substitutes and their effect, and what to buy whole and in small amounts
(`FLV-3.M2`): a large bag of a spice used once goes stale on the shelf.

**Practice:**
1. **Two recipes (45 min).** Two recipes from different traditions read, converted to the program's conventions, and
   annotated with the implicit steps.
2. **A shop visit (60 min).** One specialist shop near you visited; the shelves photographed; the key pantry items for
   two traditions noted with prices.

**Look for:**
- every cup turned into grams;
- every implicit step written in;
- a sourcing list with shops and substitutes.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A dry, heavy bake | Cups of flour converted as 250 g | Convert by ingredient |
| A raw-tasting curry | "Fry until the oil separates" missed | Write implicit steps into the recipe |
| Stale spices | A large bag bought for one dish | Buy small, buy whole |

**Safety:** imported pantry goods carry allergen labels in other languages: check them, or buy a brand with a Dutch or
English label, for every allergic guest (`SAF-1.M4`).

**Record:** the two annotated recipes and the shop notes with prices.

### CUI-3.M2.L1 — The regions

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] Normandy, Burgundy, Provence, the South-West, Alsace: fat, produce and signature dishes
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** the classical French cooking of `SAU-2` grew from regional home and inn cooking. Seeing France as
regions, each with its own fat and produce, explains its dishes far better than a list of famous recipes.

**You need:** mussels (in season, by `FSH-2`); cider from Dutch or French apples; dry white wine; butter; olive oil;
shallots; garlic; tomatoes; parsley; two pans with lids; the lens template.

**Explanation:**

*France as regions.* Each region is shaped by its fat, its produce and its neighbours:
- **Normandy:** butter, cream, apples and cider, Calvados; *poulet vallée d'Auge*, mussels in cider;
- **Burgundy:** red wine, beef, mustard, snails; *bœuf bourguignon*, *œufs en meurette*;
- **Provence:** olive oil, garlic, tomatoes, herbs, fish; *ratatouille*, *aïoli*, *bouillabaisse*;
- **the South-West:** duck and goose fat, confit, beans; *cassoulet*, *pommes sarladaises*;
- **Alsace:** lard, cabbage and sauerkraut, pork, Riesling; *choucroute garnie*, *tarte flambée*.

*Terroir.* The idea that place shapes taste: cheese, wine, butter and poultry sold with a named origin.

*The lens applied:*
- **pantry:** butter, oil or duck fat; shallots; wine; herbs;
- **techniques:** braising, *sauté*, emulsions, pastry;
- **flavour profile:** rich, rounded, herb-scented;
- **meal structure:** *entrée*, *plat*, cheese, dessert;
- **drinks:** a regional wine or cider, and water.

*Dutch kitchen notes.* Normandy and Alsace match Dutch produce closely: apples, cabbage, pork and dairy. Provence is
best cooked in summer, with Dutch greenhouse tomatoes, courgettes and peppers.

**Practice:**
1. **A regional tasting (60 min).** Mussels two ways side by side: Normandy-style in cider and butter with shallots, and
   Provence-style in white wine with olive oil, garlic and tomato; tasted and described with the lens.

**Look for:** you can place a French dish in its region from its fat and produce.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "French food" as fine dining | Restaurant food taken as the cuisine | Start from regional home dishes |
| A Normandy dish that tastes southern | Olive oil without a reason | Match the fat to the region |
| Two dishes that taste the same | Same aromatics in both | Change the fat, the liquid and the herbs |

**Safety:** mussels by the rules of `FSH-2`: closed or closing when tapped before cooking, cooked until open; shellfish
is an allergen to name for guests (`SAF-1.M4`).

**Record:** the lens notes for both mussel dishes.

### CUI-3.M2.L2 — A regional dish project

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M2.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cook a regional dish and its accompaniments; [J] adapt to local ingredients
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** cooking one regional dish properly, with its accompaniments and its place in the meal, teaches more
about a region than reading about ten. Adapting it honestly to what you can buy is the judgment.

**You need:** a French regional cookbook or reliable sources; the ingredients of the chosen dish, Dutch and in season;
the lens template; the household cards.

**Explanation:**

*Choosing a dish.* One regional dish with its accompaniments, researched with the lens: its region, its fat, its
produce, its place in the meal. Choose for the season, not for fame: a *choucroute garnie* in winter, a *ratatouille* in
late summer, chicken in cider and cream in autumn when the apples come in.

*Adapting to local ingredients.* Three kinds:
- **what can be bought in the Netherlands in season:** often most of it;
- **what is substituted:** cider from Dutch apples; a Dutch farmhouse cheese for a regional one; pointed cabbage for a
  regional cabbage. Name each substitution and its effect;
- **what can't be:** if the core of the dish isn't available, it isn't that dish any more, so its name changes ("in the
  style of").

*The write-up against the lens.* One page that places the dish: its region, fat and produce, its techniques, its
flavour profile, its place in the meal, the drink, and the history that explains it; then the adaptations and their
effects.

*Cooking it.* Use the techniques of the earlier courses: the braise of `SLW-3`, the sauce work of `SAU-2`, the pastry of
`PST-3`. Note which you used and where.

**Practice:**
1. **Research and plan (60 min).** The dish chosen and researched; the adaptations written.
2. **Cook (2 h).** The dish and its accompaniments cooked; household cards.

**Look for:** a dish a French home cook would recognise, with every adaptation named.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A dish out of season | Chosen for its fame | Choose from what the season offers |
| A dish that isn't what it claims | Unnamed substitutions | Name each; rename the dish if its core changed |
| A page of history, no cooking logic | Research without the lens | Fill every question of the lens |

**Safety:** the dish follows the §9 figures of its ingredients (poultry 75 °C; a braise cooled by the standard if made
ahead); an *aïoli* is made with pasteurised egg.

**Record:** the lens page, the adaptations and their effects, and the cards.

### CUI-3.M2.L3 — Butter, cream, olive oil and duck fat

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M2.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] cook one dish from a butter-and-cream region and one from an oil or duck-fat region; [K] how the cooking fat shapes a regional cuisine
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** the cooking fat is the quickest key to a French region. Cooking one dish from each side of the
butter-oil line shows how the fat shapes the heat, the sauce and the balance of a dish.

**You need:** for the butter dish: chicken thighs, leeks or potatoes, butter, cream, cider or white wine, shallots; for
the oil or duck-fat dish: summer vegetables and olive oil, or waxy potatoes, duck fat and garlic; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*How the cooking fat shapes a cuisine:*
- **butter and cream** (the north and west): rich, rounded sauces; browned butter; gentle heat, because butter burns;
- **olive oil** (the south): fruity and peppery, good for high heat, and raw as a finish;
- **duck and goose fat** (the South-West): savoury, for crisp potatoes and confit;
- **lard** (Alsace and the centre): pork dishes and pastry.

*One dish from each side:*
- **a butter-and-cream dish:** chicken in cider and cream, leeks in cream, or a *gratin dauphinois*;
- **an oil or duck-fat dish:** a *ratatouille* in summer, or *pommes sarladaises*: sliced potatoes fried slowly in duck
  fat, finished with garlic and parsley.

*The fat in the flavour profile.* The fat carries the aromatics: shallot and thyme in butter, garlic and herbs in oil.
It also needs balance: acid, wine or mustard to cut the richness. Taste each dish for it.

*Browned, not burnt.* Butter foams, then turns golden and smells of hazelnuts (*beurre noisette*); a moment later it
burns. Take it off the heat at golden and pour it into a cool bowl.

**Practice:**
1. **The butter dish (75 min).** A dish from Normandy or Burgundy.
2. **The oil or duck-fat dish (75 min).** A dish from Provence or the South-West.

**Look for:** each dish tastes of its fat and its region.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Bitter, black butter | Burnt where browned was meant | Off the heat at golden; into a cool bowl |
| Acrid, smoking oil | Olive oil heated past its smoke point | Moderate the heat; add the oil to a warm pan |
| A heavy, cloying dish | No acid to balance the fat | Cider, wine, mustard or lemon |

**Safety:** chicken to 75 °C (§9); hot fat handled with dry tools; potatoes fried in fat added to the pan carefully,
away from you.

**Record:** both dishes, the fat and its balance, and a photo of each.

### CUI-3.M2.L4 — A regional French meal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M2.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, JD-CHSE→3*

*Objectives:* [S] *entrée*, *plat* and cheese or dessert from one region; [J] the order, portions and drink of a French home meal
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m2-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** a French home meal is a sequence, and its pleasure is in the proportions: a light start, a
satisfying main, cheese or dessert, and a cook who sits at the table. Planning makes that possible.

**You need:** the menu from one region; the components prepared ahead where possible (a braise, a terrine from `GRD-3`,
a tart from `PST-3`); bread; a regional wine or cider, and water; the guest cards.

**Explanation:**

*The French home meal:*
- **an *entrée*:** a salad, a soup or a terrine;
- **a *plat*** with its garnish;
- **cheese or dessert,** or both;
- bread on the table throughout; water and a regional wine, or cider.

*Order, portions and drink:*
- **a light *entrée* before a rich *plat***: a leek vinaigrette before a *bœuf bourguignon*, not a cream soup;
- **modest portions** per course, so each is welcome;
- **the cheese before the dessert,** with the bread;
- **the drink from the same region:** cider with Normandy, a Burgundy red with a Burgundy braise, or a non-alcoholic
  choice on the same lines.

*Planning.* Most of the meal is prepared ahead: a braise improves overnight, a terrine is made days before, a tart's
components the day before. On the day, only the garnish and the assembly remain, so you sit at the table.

*An example, Normandy in autumn:*
- **entrée:** a salad of chicory and apple with a cider vinaigrette;
- **plat:** chicken in cider and cream with buttered leeks;
- **cheese:** a Dutch soft cheese in place of Camembert, named;
- **dessert:** an apple *tarte fine* (`PST-3.M2.L3`).

**Practice:**
1. **The meal (3 h).** A three-course meal from one region for the household or guests.

**Look for:** portions that leave room for the next course; the courses flowing.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody wants the dessert | Three rich courses | A light *entrée*; one rich course |
| The *plat* left unfinished | A huge *entrée* | Modest portions per course |
| An awkward end | Cheese after the dessert | Cheese first, then dessert |

**Safety:** components made ahead are cooled by the standard and reheated until steaming hot throughout; cheese and
cold dishes are out of the fridge for at most 2 hours (§9).

**Record:** the menu, the timeline, the portions and the cards.

### CUI-3.M3.L1 — Regional Italy

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] north versus south; *cucina povera*; pasta and sauce logic; meal structure
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** Italian home cooking is built on few, good ingredients, matched to their region and season. Its
pasta logic, the right shape with the right sauce, finished together, changes how you cook pasta every week.

**You need:** dried pasta (spaghetti or a short shape); good olive oil; garlic; tomatoes (summer) or a tin of good
tomatoes; butter; Parmigiano Reggiano or Grana Padano; salt; a large pot; a wide pan; tongs.

**Explanation:**

*North against south:*
- **the north:** butter, rice (*risotto*), polenta, fresh egg pasta, cured meats;
- **the south:** olive oil, dried durum-wheat pasta, tomatoes, vegetables and fish.

*Cucina povera.* Cooking from few, humble ingredients made good: bread soups, beans, greens, pasta with breadcrumbs,
using everything. It is close to the whole-vegetable cooking of `VEG-3`.

*Pasta and sauce logic:*
- **the shape matches the sauce:** long and flat with butter and cream or a *ragù*; tubes and ridges with chunky
  sauces; small shapes in soups;
- **finished in the sauce:** drained a minute early, tossed in the pan with the sauce and a little of the starchy
  cooking water until glossy and clinging;
- **sauced, not drowned.**

*Meal structure.* *Antipasto*; *primo* (pasta, rice or soup); *secondo* (meat or fish) with a *contorno* (vegetables);
*dolce*. At home, often only *primo* and *secondo*. Wine and water at the table, an espresso after.

*Few ingredients, high quality.* The best olive oil, cheese and tomatoes you can find. Dutch summer tomatoes and winter
greens fit: *cavolo nero* grows well in the Netherlands.

**Practice:**
1. **Two pastas (60 min).** A southern dried-pasta dish (tomato, garlic and olive oil) and a northern butter-and-cheese
   pasta; compared with the lens.

**Look for:** the pasta finished in the sauce, glossy and clinging.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Watery sauce sliding off | Sauce poured over plain pasta | Finish the pasta in the sauce with cooking water |
| Sauce that won't cling | Pasta rinsed | Never rinse; the starch binds the sauce |
| A muddled taste | Ten ingredients in one sauce | Three to five, each tasted |

**Safety:** a large pot of boiling water is drained at the sink, the colander in place first and the pot tipped away
from you. Parmigiano and pasta contain milk and gluten (`SAF-1.M4`).

**Record:** both pastas, the lens notes, and a photo of the sauced pasta.

### CUI-3.M3.L2 — Fresh egg pasta

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M3.L2` · moves TS-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] mix, knead, rest, roll and cut by hand or machine; filled pasta introduced
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** fresh egg pasta is flour and eggs, and the difference between a silky ribbon and a thick, gummy one
is in the kneading and the rolling. Rolled evenly, it cooks evenly.

**You need:** Italian "00" flour (or a mix with semolina); eggs; semolina for dusting; a pasta machine or a long rolling
pin; a ruler; a sharp knife; a clean tea towel; a large pot; butter and Parmigiano for the sauce; the scale.

**Explanation:**

*The dough.* Flour and eggs by weight: the classic is roughly one egg per 100 g of flour, adjusted by feel. Make a well
in the flour, break the eggs into it, beat them with a fork, and draw in the flour little by little. Knead 8–10 minutes
until smooth and elastic, like a soft earlobe. Wrap it and rest it for 30 minutes, so the gluten relaxes.

*Rolling.*
- **by machine:** pass the dough through the widest setting, fold it in three, and pass it again a few times until
  smooth; then through each setting in turn;
- **by hand:** with a long pin, rolling from the centre and turning the sheet;
- **to an even thickness:** thin enough to see the shadow of a hand through it for tagliatelle, thinner for filled
  pasta; check it against a ruler.

*Cutting.* Tagliatelle (about 6–8 mm), *pappardelle* (wider), *lasagne* sheets. Dust them with semolina and toss them
loosely into nests, so they don't stick.

*Cooking.* In plenty of well-salted boiling water for one to three minutes; drained a moment early and finished in the
sauce.

*Storage.* Dried in nests for a day, or frozen. Cooked fresh pasta is treated as leftovers: in the fridge within
2 hours, eaten within 2 days (§9).

**Practice:**
1. **Make and roll (75 min).** A batch of dough made and rested, rolled to two thicknesses, photographed against a
   ruler.
2. **Cut and cook (60 min).** Tagliatelle cut and cooked, finished in a simple butter sauce; the thickness and bite
   judged.

**Look for:**
- a smooth, elastic dough;
- even sheets that show the hand's shadow;
- cooked in minutes, with bite.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A sticky dough | Too much egg | Knead in flour a spoon at a time |
| Some strands soft, some hard | Uneven sheets | Fold and pass on the widest setting; roll every sheet to the same setting |
| Clumped strands | Cut without dusting | Dust with semolina; nest loosely |

**Safety:** raw dough contains raw egg: wash hands and the board after handling. Egg and gluten are allergens
(`SAF-1.M4`).

**Record:** photos of the sheets against the ruler and the cooked pasta; notes on bite.

### CUI-3.M3.L3 — Filled pasta and *gnocchi*

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M3.L2` · moves TS-STA→4, FL-TEXT→3*

*Objectives:* [S] ravioli and tortellini shapes, sealed without air; potato or ricotta *gnocchi*; [K] why floury potatoes and little flour make light *gnocchi*
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** filled pasta and *gnocchi* are where Italian home cooking turns leftovers and potatoes into
something special. Both depend on technique more than ingredients: a seal without air, a light hand with flour.

**You need:** fresh pasta dough (`CUI-3.M3.L2`); a filling of ricotta and greens, or roasted pumpkin and Parmigiano;
floury potatoes (*kruimig*); flour; butter; sage; a potato ricer; a pastry wheel or knife; a slotted spoon; a small
brush and water.

**Explanation:**

*Filled pasta.* A thin sheet; a filling that is dry and well seasoned (ricotta and greens, pumpkin, mushrooms, a
braise); sealed without air pockets, because air expands and bursts them. Brush the edges with a little water or egg
wash and press out the air around each mound. Shapes:
- **ravioli:** squares or rounds, two sheets or one folded;
- ***tortellini*:** small squares folded into triangles, wrapped around a finger and joined;
- ***agnolotti*:** a strip folded over a line of filling and pinched.

*Fillings.* Raw meat fillings are avoided at this level. Cooked fillings (a braise from `SLW-3`) are cooled before use
and kept as leftovers. Drain ricotta and squeeze greens well: a wet filling soaks the dough.

*Light gnocchi.* Floury potatoes, baked or steamed (not boiled wet), riced while hot, with as little flour as will hold
them, mixed briefly. Waxy potatoes and extra flour make them dense and gluey (`STA-1`). Cook one test dumpling first:
if it falls apart, add a little flour; if it holds, cook the batch. Ricotta *gnocchi* use drained ricotta, egg and a
little flour.

*Cooking.* In gently boiling water; lift them out a moment after they float. Finish them in butter and sage, or a light
sauce.

**Practice:**
1. **Ravioli and *tortellini* (75 min).** A ricotta-and-greens or pumpkin filling; two shapes; cooked and served.
2. ***Gnocchi* (60 min).** Potato *gnocchi* from floury potatoes; a test dumpling cooked before the batch; finished in
   brown butter.

**Look for:**
- filled shapes that hold, with no bursts;
- light *gnocchi* that keep their shape.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Burst ravioli | Air pockets | Press the air out around each mound |
| A soft, torn dough | A wet filling | Drain ricotta; squeeze greens; cool cooked fillings |
| Heavy, gluey *gnocchi* | Kneaded like bread, or too much flour | Mix briefly; test one; add flour only if needed |

**Safety:** cooked fillings cooled by the standard before use; ricotta *gnocchi* contain egg and milk (`SAF-1.M4`).

**Record:** photos of the shapes and the *gnocchi*, the test dumpling, and the amount of flour used.

### CUI-3.M3.L4 — An Italian meal in courses

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M3.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, JD-CHSE→3*

*Objectives:* [S] *primo*, *secondo* and *contorno*; [J] proportions between the courses, and when one simple sauce is enough
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m3-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** an Italian meal is a balance between its courses: a *primo* that opens the appetite, a *secondo*
that is still welcome. Getting the proportions right is the skill.

**You need:** the menu: a *primo*, a *secondo* with a *contorno*, a *dolce*; seasonal Dutch produce; the scale for the
pasta portions; the timeline; the guest cards.

**Explanation:**

*The Sunday meal:*
- **a *primo*:** pasta or *risotto*;
- **a *secondo*** of meat or fish: a roast, a braise, grilled fish;
- **a *contorno*:** seasonal vegetables;
- **fruit or a simple *dolce*.**

*Proportions between the courses:*
- **a modest *primo*:** about 80 g of dried pasta or 100 g of fresh per person, so the *secondo* is still welcome;
- **the *contorno*** served on its own plate or beside, not as a garnish on the meat.

*When one simple sauce is enough.* A *primo* with one sauce of few ingredients, done well (butter and sage; tomato and
basil; *cacio e pepe*-style cheese and pepper), rather than a complex one competing with the *secondo*. Keep the
complexity in one course.

*Timing.* The *secondo* goes in the oven, or rests, while the *primo* is eaten; the pasta is cooked at the last moment.
A braise can be made the day before. Write the timeline backwards from when the *secondo* is served.

*An example, in late autumn:*
- **primo:** fresh tagliatelle with butter and sage;
- **secondo:** pork shoulder braised in milk, or a chicken roasted with rosemary;
- **contorno:** *cavolo nero* with garlic and olive oil;
- **dolce:** pears poached in red wine.

**Practice:**
1. **The meal (3 h).** A *primo*, a *secondo* with *contorno*, and a *dolce* for the household or guests.

**Look for:** a light *primo*, a welcome *secondo*, the courses timed.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody finishes the roast | A huge plate of pasta first | 80 g dried or 100 g fresh per person |
| A meat-only *secondo* | The *contorno* forgotten | Plan the vegetable as a course |
| Two heavy courses | A rich sauce on both | One rich course; the other simple |

**Safety:** the *secondo* cooked to its §9 figure (poultry 75 °C; pork as intact meat 63 °C for 15 seconds, or a
braise until tender); leftovers in the fridge within 2 hours (§9).

**Record:** the menu, the portions as weighed, the timeline, and the cards.

### CUI-3.M4.L1 — The Iberian pantry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] olive oil, *pimentón*, *sofrito*, cured pork, salt cod, tapas culture
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** Spanish and Portuguese cooking is built from a short pantry used with confidence: olive oil,
paprika, a slow *sofrito*, cured pork and salt cod. Knowing what each does lets you cook the dishes and recognise
them.

**You need:** sweet (*dulce*) and smoked *pimentón*, two olive oils, a cured sausage (*chorizo* or *chouriço*), a
Spanish or Portuguese cheese, bread; the lens template; tasting forms.

**Explanation:**

*The pantry:*
- **olive oil** for everything, from frying to finishing;
- ***pimentón***, Spanish paprika, smoked or sweet, *dulce* or *picante*; with saffron, garlic and parsley;
- ***sofrito***: onion, garlic, peppers and tomato cooked slowly in olive oil until jammy, the base of rice dishes,
  stews and sauces;
- **cured pork:** *jamón*, *chorizo*, *lomo*; in Portugal *chouriço* and *presunto*;
- **salt cod** (*bacalao*, *bacalhau*): salted and dried, soaked before cooking, central to Portuguese cooking;
- **staples:** short, round, absorbent rice such as *bomba*; chickpeas and beans; almonds; sherry vinegar.

*Tapas culture.* Small dishes, hot and cold, shared standing or at the table, with a drink. It is a way of eating
rather than a set of recipes.

*The lens applied:*
- **techniques:** a slow *sofrito*, rice in a wide pan, frying in olive oil, the *plancha*;
- **flavour profile:** smoky paprika, garlic, olive oil, saffron;
- **meal structure:** a late lunch as the main meal, tapas in the evening;
- **drinks:** wine, sherry, *vinho verde*, water.

*Paprika burns.* Ground paprika turns bitter in seconds in hot oil. Add it off the heat, or with the liquid, and taste
smoked against sweet before choosing: smoked paprika everywhere flattens every dish into one.

*Cured meats.* Dry-cured hams and sausages are raw animal foods: they are in the risk-group row of §9. Name them for
pregnant guests, young children, older people and anyone with lowered immunity, and offer a cooked alternative.

**Practice:**
1. **A pantry tasting (45 min).** Sweet against smoked *pimentón* (a pinch of each warmed briefly in oil off the heat,
   tasted on bread), two olive oils, a cured sausage and a cheese. Describe each with the lens.

**Look for:** you can name the *sofrito*, tell the two paprikas apart, and say what cured pork brings to a dish.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Every dish tastes of smoke | Smoked paprika used everywhere | Sweet paprika by default; smoked where the dish asks for it |
| Bitter, dark paprika | Fried in hot oil | Off the heat, or with the liquid |
| The tasting notes are just "nice" | No lens | Use the five questions for each item |

**Safety:** cured meats named for risk groups (§9); the cheese and sausage back in the fridge within 2 hours.

**Record:** the tasting form, the lens notes, and the shop where each item came from.

### CUI-3.M4.L2 — Rice in a wide pan

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `STA-1.M2.L3` · moves TS-STA→4, IN-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *paella*-style rice: *sofrito*, stock, no stirring, *socarrat*
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a *paella*-style rice is cooked without stirring, in a thin layer, and finished with a crust on the
bottom. Each of those is the opposite of a *risotto*, so the rice and the method have to be chosen together.

**You need:** a wide, shallow pan (a *paella* pan or the widest frying pan you have); *bomba* or *calasparra* rice and a
risotto rice for the comparison; a stock; saffron, *pimentón*; seasonal vegetables; the probe; the scale.

**Explanation:**

*The method:*
1. Make a *sofrito* in the pan.
2. Brown the main ingredients.
3. Add a well-flavoured stock (fish, chicken or vegetable) with saffron, and bring it to the boil.
4. Scatter the rice in an even, thin layer.
5. **Do not stir** from here on. Cook until the liquid is absorbed and the grains are tender but separate.

*The* socarrat. In the last minutes, raise the heat and listen. When the bottom crackles, a thin, caramelised crust
forms: crisp and toasty, not burnt. Lift a corner with a spoon to check, and take the pan off the heat when it smells
toasty, before it smells burnt.

*The rice.* Short, round, absorbent rice (*bomba*, *calasparra*) takes up a lot of liquid and stays firm. Risotto rice
releases more starch and goes creamy, which is wrong here. Choose the variety and write down why (`IN-STA`).

*A home stove.* A pan wider than the burner heats unevenly: the middle cooks and the edge stays wet. Move and turn the
pan, set it over two burners, or finish the rice in the oven. Note which adaptation you used, and how the grains at the
edge compared with the middle.

**Practice:**
1. **A vegetable rice (75 min).** A wide-pan rice with seasonal vegetables (green beans in summer; pumpkin and
   chickpeas in autumn), the *socarrat* made.
2. **A comparison (60 min).** The same dish made with *bomba* and with risotto rice. Compare the texture, and
   photograph a spoonful of each.

**Look for:** an even, thin layer of rice; separate grains; a crackling, golden *socarrat*.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Creamy, sticky rice | Stirred, or risotto rice | Leave it alone; use *bomba* |
| Raw grains on top, mush below | Too thick a layer | A thin layer; a wider pan or two pans |
| A black, bitter base | The heat raised too long | Listen for the crackle; check a corner; stop at toasty |
| Wet edges, dry middle | Pan wider than the burner | Turn the pan, use two burners, or finish in the oven |

**Safety:** chicken browned and cooked through to 75 °C; fish and other seafood to 63 °C (§9). Leftover rice cooled
fast and kept cold (`SCI-2.M6`): into the fridge within 2 hours, eaten within 2 days.

**Record:** the rice varieties, the liquid and its ratio, the stove adaptation, and the two photos.

### CUI-3.M4.L3 — *Sofrito*, *tortilla* and tapas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 1 d · builds on `CUI-3.M4.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-EGG→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a slow *sofrito*, a *tortilla española*, two hot tapas; [K] salt cod: soaking and cooking
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** the slow *sofrito* is the flavour base under much of Iberian cooking; the *tortilla* tests egg
cookery; and salt cod is the Portuguese staple that needs a day of planning.

**You need:** onions, peppers, garlic, tomatoes and olive oil for the *sofrito*; potatoes, onion and eggs for the
*tortilla*; a non-stick pan and a plate wider than it; prawns, mushrooms or potatoes for the tapas; salt cod, soaked a
day ahead; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*A slow* sofrito. Cook onions, peppers and garlic gently in plenty of olive oil for 30–45 minutes, until soft and
sweet. Add grated tomato and cook it down until jammy and the oil separates. Make a batch and freeze it in portions.

*The* tortilla española:
1. Confit sliced potatoes and onion slowly in olive oil, until soft but not coloured.
2. Drain them, mix with beaten eggs, and rest for a few minutes.
3. Cook in the pan over medium heat, shaking it, until set at the edges.
4. Slide it onto a plate, invert it back into the pan, and cook the second side.

It can be set at the edges and soft in the centre, or set through. **A soft-centred *tortilla* is not served to risk
groups (§9);** for them, cook it through.

*Two hot tapas,* for example:
- ***gambas al ajillo*,** prawns in garlic oil, cooked to 63 °C;
- ***patatas bravas*** or ***champiñones al ajillo***.

*Salt cod:*
- soak it in cold water **in the fridge** for one to two days, changing the water several times, until it tastes
  pleasantly seasoned;
- poach it gently: it flakes when done. Use it in *brandade*-style dishes, fritters or a *bacalhau* bake;
- handle it as raw fish until it is cooked.

**Practice:**
1. ***Sofrito* and *tortilla* (90 min).** A batch of *sofrito*; a *tortilla* turned with a plate.
2. **Tapas and salt cod (75 min, after a day's soaking).** Two hot tapas; salt cod fritters or a salt cod bake.

**Look for:** a jammy, sweet *sofrito*; a *tortilla* that holds its shape when cut; salt cod pleasantly seasoned, not
salty.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A raw, sharp *sofrito* | Cooked quickly | 30–45 minutes, gently, before the tomato |
| Crisp, fried potatoes in the *tortilla* | Oil too hot | Confit slowly; soft, not coloured |
| The *tortilla* breaks on turning | Not set at the edges, or a small plate | Wait for the edges; a plate wider than the pan |
| Salt cod still salty | Too short a soak, the water not changed | Longer, with more changes; taste a flake |

**Safety:** salt cod soaked in the fridge, never on the counter; prawns to 63 °C; the soft *tortilla* not served to
risk groups (§9); crustaceans and egg named for guests.

**Record:** the *sofrito* time, the *tortilla* (soft or set, and for whom), the soak time and water changes.

### CUI-3.M4.L4 — An Iberian meal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M4.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, KM-PLAN→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a tapas evening or a Portuguese family meal; [J] timing hot and cold dishes so that they arrive together
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m4-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** a tapas evening is several dishes with different holding times. Hot and cold dishes have to arrive
at their best, and the cold ones have a limit on the table.

**You need:** the menu; the timeline; the fridge space for the cold dishes; the probe; the guest cards.

**Explanation:**

*Two formats:*
- **A tapas evening:** four to six small dishes, hot and cold, plus bread, olives and a drink. The cold dishes are ready
  ahead; the hot ones are finished in a sequence.
- **A Portuguese family meal:**
  - a soup: *caldo verde*, kale and potato, a good match for Dutch kale in winter;
  - a centre dish: a salt cod bake, a pork and clam dish, or grilled sardines in season;
  - rice or potatoes, and a salad.

*Timing hot and cold dishes so that they arrive together:*
- cold dishes plated and kept in the fridge until they go out, then on the table for at most 2 hours (§9);
- hot dishes ordered by how long they hold: a stew holds, prawns don't. The stew is made first and kept hot; the prawns
  go in the pan at the last moment;
- the last dishes are cooked while the first are eaten. A tapas evening is meant to arrive in waves.

*The timeline.* Write it backwards from when the guests arrive. Mark which dishes are done the day before (the
*sofrito*, the soaked salt cod, a cold dish that improves overnight), which are done that afternoon, and which are
cooked to order. Note the time each cold dish leaves the fridge.

**Practice:**
1. **The meal (3 h).** A tapas evening or a Portuguese family meal for the household or guests, on a written timeline.

**Look for:** hot dishes arriving hot, cold dishes cold, and no long gaps.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Everything arrives at once and goes cold | No sequence | Order by holding time; send dishes in waves |
| Cold tapas out all evening | No time noted | Note the time out; back in the fridge, or away, at 2 hours |
| Long gaps between dishes | Everything left to the end | More done ahead; one hot dish per wave |

**Safety:** cold dishes out for at most 2 hours (§9); prawns and chicken probed; cured meats and soft egg named for risk
groups; the allergens on the table named.

**Record:** the timeline against the actual times, the time out for each cold dish, and the cards.

### CUI-3.M4.X1 — A wide-pan rice with seasonal vegetables

### CUI-3.M5.L1 — The pantry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, IN-ARO→4*

*Objectives:* [K] tahini, sumac, preserved lemon, *ras el hanout*, *harissa*, pomegranate molasses
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** the cooking of the Levant and the Maghreb balances sour, herbal, rich and warm-spiced flavours on
one table. A handful of pantry items carries each of those, and most keep for months.

**You need:** tahini, sumac, *za'atar*, preserved lemon, pomegranate molasses; flatbread and thick yoghurt; Aleppo or
*pul biber* chilli flakes if you can get them; the lens template; tasting forms.

**Explanation:**

*The pantry:*
- **tahini,** sesame paste, for sauces and dips. Sesame is one of the allergens you name for guests;
- **sumac** (sour, red, lemony), ***za'atar*** (`FLV-3.M2`), **Aleppo or *pul biber*** chilli flakes;
- **preserved lemon:** salt-cured; the peel is used, chopped, in tagines and salads;
- ***ras el hanout*** (a North African spice blend) and ***harissa*** (a chilli paste);
- **pomegranate molasses** (sweet-sour); **orange blossom and rose water** for sweets;
- **staples:** olive oil, chickpeas, lentils, bulgur, couscous, flatbreads, yoghurt and *labneh*.

*The flavour profile.* Four elements, balanced across the table rather than in one dish:
- **sour:** lemon, sumac, pomegranate;
- **herbal:** parsley, mint, coriander and dill, used in quantity, as an ingredient;
- **rich:** tahini, olive oil, lamb;
- **warm-spiced:** cumin, cinnamon, allspice.

*Two regions, one lens:*
- **the Levant** (Lebanon, Syria, Palestine, Israel, Jordan): mezze and grills;

*Meal structure and drinks.* Many dishes on the table, with bread as the utensil. Mint tea, water and *ayran*, a salted

bulgur, not a bulgur salad with herbs.

**Practice:**
1. **A pantry tasting (60 min).** Sumac, *za'atar*, preserved lemon peel, pomegranate molasses and tahini, tasted on
   bread and yoghurt. Describe each with the lens, and sort it into sour, herbal, rich or warm.

**Look for:** you can name the sour, herbal, rich and warm elements, and say which item carries which.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A salty, bitter dish | Preserved lemon flesh in quantity | Mostly the peel, finely chopped |
| A pale herb salad | Herbs treated as garnish | Herbs as the main ingredient |
| Tahini seizes when mixed | Too little liquid at first | Whisk in lemon juice and water a little at a time; it loosens |

**Safety:** sesame named on the tasting form, and for every guest later in the module; the yoghurt back in the fridge

### CUI-3.M5.L2 — A mezze project

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M5.L1` · moves CR-COMP→3*

*Objectives:* [S] dips, salads, a grilled or braised centre; [J] balance a spread
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a mezze spread is a composition: several small dishes that together give colour, texture,
temperature and taste. Five beige dips are not a spread.

**You need:** dried chickpeas, tahini, lemons, garlic, ice water; an aubergine or beetroot; parsley, mint, bulgur; the
centre's ingredients; bread; a blender; the component table (`FLV-3`).

**Explanation:**

*The parts of a spread:*
- **dips:**
  - hummus: chickpeas cooked very soft, blended with tahini, lemon, garlic and ice water until silky;
  - *baba ghanoush* or *mutabal*: charred aubergine;
  - *muhammara*: roasted peppers with walnuts, or a nut-free version with bread and seeds, and pomegranate molasses;
- **salads:** *tabbouleh* (mostly parsley), *fattoush* (with toasted bread and sumac), a shredded cabbage or carrot
  salad in winter;
- **a centre:** grilled meat or vegetable skewers, *falafel*, or a braise;
- **bread** and pickles.

*Silky hummus.* The chickpeas must be soft enough to crush between two fingers with no resistance. Undercooked
chickpeas give grainy hummus whatever the blender. Blend the tahini, lemon and ice water first, then the warm chickpeas,
and blend for longer than seems needed.

*Balancing a spread.* Fill in the component table for the whole table:
- **colour:** green herbs, red peppers, purple beetroot, not all beige;
- **texture:** smooth dips, crisp bread, a crunchy salad;
- **temperature:** something hot beside the cold dips;
- **taste:** sour, herbal and rich all present.

*Seasonal adaptations.* Dutch greenhouse aubergines and peppers in summer; in winter, cabbage, carrots, beetroot and
pumpkin (a beetroot dip, roasted pumpkin with tahini).

**Practice:**
1. **Dips (90 min).** Hummus from dried chickpeas, and a charred aubergine or beetroot dip; silky and balanced.
2. **Salads and centre (90 min).** A herb salad and a grilled or braised centre; the spread composed and photographed.

**Look for:** silky hummus; a green *tabbouleh*; a balanced table.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Grainy hummus | Chickpeas undercooked | Cook until they crush with no resistance |
| A *tabbouleh* of mostly bulgur | Herbs measured as garnish | Mostly parsley; a little bulgur |
| All dips, no crunch | Texture not planned | Add a crunchy salad, toasted bread, pickles |
| A flat, heavy table | No acid | Lemon, sumac or pomegranate in at least two dishes |

**Safety:** dips out of the fridge for at most 2 hours (§9). Sesame (tahini), tree nuts (walnuts in *muhammara*) and
gluten (bulgur, bread) named for guests.

**Record:** the component table, the photo of the spread, and what you would change.

### CUI-3.M5.L3 — Tagines and couscous

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 2 h · builds on `MEA-2.M6.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-CMB→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a tagine with layered spices and fruit or preserved lemon; couscous steamed in two stages; [K] how the pot shapes the braise
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m5-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** a tagine is a braise with little liquid, where the pot's shape gives a concentrated sauce. Couscous
steamed in two stages is a different food from the quick pour-over kind, and the difference is worth tasting once.

**You need:** lamb shoulder or chicken legs (or vegetables); onions, ginger, saffron, cinnamon, cumin, *ras el hanout*;
dried apricots or prunes, or Dutch apples or pears; preserved lemon and olives; a tagine with a heat diffuser, or a
heavy casserole with a tight lid; couscous; a steamer or a sieve over the pot; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*The tagine* is both the pot and the dish. The conical lid returns condensed steam to the food, so little liquid is
needed. Meat or vegetables cook slowly with onion, spices, a little liquid, fruit, preserved lemon and olives.

*How the pot shapes the braise.* Little liquid plus the cone's condensation gives a concentrated sauce that coats. On a
home stove, a heavy casserole (`HEA-2`) with a tight lid works the same way. A clay tagine needs a heat diffuser and
gentle heat.

*Layering spices* (`FLV-3.M2`):
- **at the start:** ginger, cumin, cinnamon and saffron, bloomed with the onions;
- **at the end:** *ras el hanout* and fresh herbs, for the top notes.

*Couscous steamed in two stages:*
1. Moisten the grains and rub them with oil.
2. Steam over the stew, or in a steamer, for about 15 minutes.
3. Turn them out, sprinkle with salted water, and break up the lumps with your fingers.
4. Steam again until fluffy.

The quick method (boiling water poured over, covered, forked) is the shortcut. Taste the two side by side.

*Doneness.* Lamb is done when it is tender to a fork. Chicken is cooked to 75 °C.

**Practice:**
1. **A tagine (75 min active, 2 h in total).** Lamb shoulder or chicken legs with fruit or preserved lemon, in a
   casserole or a tagine.
2. **Couscous two ways (45 min).** Steamed in two stages, and by the quick method; compared.

**Look for:** a concentrated sauce that coats; tender meat; fluffy, separate couscous grains.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A soup, not a tagine | Too much liquid | Little liquid; reduce uncovered at the end |
| Gluey, lumpy couscous | Too much water at once; not broken up | Moisten in stages; break up between steamings |
| No top notes | All the spices at the start | *Ras el hanout* and herbs at the end |
| A cracked clay tagine | Direct high heat | A heat diffuser; gentle heat |

**Safety:** chicken to 75 °C (§9); the hot lid lifted away from you; leftovers cooled and in the fridge within 2 hours.

**Record:** the cut, the spice layers, the time to tender, and the couscous comparison.

### CUI-3.M6.L1 — Regional India

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m6-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] north, south, east, west; *tadka*, *masala*, dals, breads, rice
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m6-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** "Indian food" is a subcontinent of cuisines, and "curry powder" flattens all of them. A few core
terms (*tadka*, *masala*, *dal*) and a map of the regions are enough to start cooking with intent.

**You need:** split red lentils, turmeric, salt; ghee or oil; cumin seeds, garlic, mustard seeds, curry leaves, dried
chilli; a small pan for the *tadka*; the lens template; tasting forms.

**Explanation:**

*A subcontinent of cuisines:*
- **north:** wheat breads (*roti*, *naan*, *paratha*), dairy (ghee, *paneer*, yoghurt, cream), rich gravies, tandoor
  cooking;
- **south:** rice, lentils, coconut, curry leaves, mustard seed, tamarind; *dosa*, *idli*, *sambar*, *rasam*;
- **east:** mustard oil, fish, *panch phoron* (a five-seed blend), sweets from fresh cheese;
- **west:** Gujarati sweet-sour vegetarian cooking, Goan vinegar-and-chilli dishes, Maharashtrian spice blends.

Pakistan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka share and extend these traditions.

*Core terms:*
- ***tadka*** (*tarka*): whole spices, with garlic, curry leaves or chilli, fried in hot ghee or oil and poured over a
  dish at the end;
- ***masala***: a spice mixture, or the cooked base of onion, ginger, garlic, tomato and spices;
- ***dal***: split pulses, and the dish made from them;
- **breads and rice** as the base of the meal.

*Meal structure and drinks.* Several dishes with rice or bread, eaten together. Water and *lassi* with the meal; tea
(*chai*) outside meals. Many households are vegetarian, for religious or family reasons; ask your guests.

*The Dutch connection.* Surinamese-Hindustani cooking (*roti* with *masala* chicken, potatoes and long beans) is a
living Indian-descended tradition in the Netherlands, and its shops are a good source for the pantry: whole spices,
*atta* flour, lentils and basmati.

*The* tadka *goes on at the end.* Its aroma is strongest in the minutes after it hits the dish. Stirred in long before
serving, it fades into the background (`CUI-3.M6.L3` explains why).

**Practice:**
1. **A spice and *dal* tasting (60 min).** A simple *dal* (red lentils simmered with turmeric until soft, salted),
   divided in three: one with no *tadka*, one with a cumin-and-garlic *tadka*, one with a mustard-seed-and-curry-leaf
   *tadka*. Taste them side by side, and place each regionally.

**Look for:** you can say what a *tadka* adds, and which region uses which.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Every dish tastes the same | "Curry powder" for everything | Whole spices, chosen for the region |
| The *tadka* adds little | Stirred in long before serving | Pour it over at the table |
| Burnt garlic in the *tadka* | Added with the seeds to very hot fat | Seeds first; garlic when they crackle; seconds only |
| Mustard seeds stay hard and bitter | Fat not hot enough | Wait until they pop |

**Safety:** hot fat poured away from you, in a small pan with a handle; curry leaves and mustard seeds spit, so stand
back.

**Record:** the three *dals* tasted, the lens notes, and the regional placing.

### CUI-3.M6.L2 — A curry from whole spices

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m6-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `FLV-3.M2.L1` · moves FL-ARCH→3, TS-SAU→4*

*Objectives:* [S] build a *masala* and a sauce; finish with a *tadka*; [J] balance heat and acid
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m6-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a curry is built in stages, and each stage has a sign that tells you it is done. Rushing one stage
gives a raw-tasting, flat sauce that no amount of spice at the end will fix.

**You need:** whole spices (cumin, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, bay); ground turmeric, coriander and chilli; onions,
ginger, garlic, tomatoes; the main ingredient (chicken thighs, *paneer*, chickpeas or seasonal vegetables); yoghurt or
coconut milk; *garam masala*, fresh coriander; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*Building a* masala:
1. **Whole spices** (cumin, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, bay) bloomed in oil or ghee.
2. **Onions** cooked slowly to deep golden. This is the longest stage, and pale onions give a raw-tasting sauce.
3. **Ginger-garlic paste** fried until the raw smell goes.
4. **Ground spices** (turmeric, coriander, chilli) fried briefly, with a splash of water so they don't burn.
5. **Tomatoes** cooked down until the oil separates at the edges: the sign that the *masala* is done.

*The sauce.* The main ingredient simmers in the *masala* with water, stock, yoghurt or coconut milk. Finish with
*garam masala* and fresh coriander, or a *tadka*.

*Balancing heat and acid:*
- chilli added in steps, tasted between (`FLV-3.M2.L4`);
- acid from tomato, yoghurt, tamarind, lemon or vinegar;
- a pinch of sugar or jaggery to round;
- salt last, once the sauce has reduced.

*Yoghurt.* Yoghurt boiled hard splits into curds and whey. Take the pan off the boil, stir it in a spoonful at a time,
and keep the sauce below a boil from then on.

**Practice:**
1. **A *masala* (60 min).** The base built step by step; the "oil separates" stage photographed.
2. **The curry (90 min).** A chicken, *paneer* or vegetable curry from the *masala*, balanced, and finished with a
   *tadka* or *garam masala*.

**Look for:** deep golden onions; the oil separating at the edges; balanced heat and acid.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A raw-tasting, pale sauce | Onions not cooked to deep golden | Give the onions the time; low heat, stirring |
| Bitter, dark spices | Ground spices burnt in hot oil | A splash of water with them; seconds, not minutes |
| A split, grainy sauce | Yoghurt boiled | Off the boil, spoon by spoon; no boiling after |
| Too hot | Chilli added all at once | In steps; balance with yoghurt, acid or sugar |

**Safety:** chicken to 75 °C (§9); dairy (ghee, yoghurt, *paneer*) named for guests; leftovers in the fridge within
2 hours.

### CUI-3.M6.L3 — Dal, rice and flatbreads

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m6-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M6.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a *tadka dal*, basmati by absorption, *chapati* or *paratha*; [K] why ghee and the *tadka* come last
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m6-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** *dal*, rice and bread are the base of the meal. Each has a clear standard: creamy *dal*, long and
separate grains, soft breads that puff.

**You need:** split red lentils, yellow split peas (*toor* or *chana*) or mung; turmeric; ghee and *tadka* spices;
basmati; a pan with a tight lid; *atta* flour; a rolling pin; a heavy dry pan; a timer.

**Explanation:**

*Tadka* dal. Simmer split pulses with turmeric until soft and creamy. Season, and finish with a *tadka* poured over at
the table. Dried kidney beans (*rajma*) follow the bean rule of §9: soaked for at least 5 hours with the water poured
away, then boiled in fresh water for at least 30 minutes, and never cooked in a slow cooker.

*Why ghee and the* tadka *come last.* The spices' aromas are volatile and fat-soluble. Frying them in fat at the end and
pouring them over gives the strongest, freshest aroma. Long cooking would drive it off.

*Basmati by absorption:*
1. Wash until the water runs almost clear.
2. Soak for 20–30 minutes; drain.
3. Cook with a measured amount of water (by the packet, often about 1.5 times the rice's volume) and a tight lid.
4. Rest off the heat, lid on, before forking.

Don't lift the lid while it cooks: the steam does the work. Leftover rice is cooled fast and kept cold (`SCI-2.M6`).

*Flatbreads:*
- ***chapati*:** wholemeal *atta* flour and water, kneaded and rested, rolled thin, and cooked on a dry hot pan until it
  puffs (over a flame with tongs, or pressed in the pan with a cloth);
- ***paratha*:** the same dough layered with ghee and folded, rolled again, and cooked with ghee.

**Practice:**
1. ***Dal* and rice (75 min).** A *tadka dal* and basmati by absorption.
2. **Flatbreads (75 min).** *Chapati* and *paratha*; the *chapati* puffed over a flame or pressed in the pan.

**Look for:** creamy *dal*; long, separate grains; puffed, soft breads.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sticky rice | Unwashed | Wash until the water runs almost clear |
| Wet or crusted rice | The lid lifted; too much heat | Lid on; low heat; rest off the heat |
| A thick, dry *chapati* | Rolled thick; cooked slowly | Roll thin; a hot pan; turn quickly |
| Grainy *dal* | Pulses not soft | Simmer longer; whisk at the end |

**Safety:** the bean rule of §9 for *rajma*; leftover rice cooled fast and in the fridge within 2 hours (`SCI-2.M6`);
puffing a *chapati* over a flame with tongs, sleeves clear.

**Record:** the pulses, the rice ratio and soak time, and the photos of the grains and breads.

### CUI-3.M7.L1 — Regional China

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] Cantonese, Sichuan, Hunan, Shanghai, Beijing; balance and texture; *wok hei*
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** "Chinese" is not one cuisine. Five regions are enough to start placing a dish by its seasoning and
its method, and a balanced meal is judged on texture as much as on taste.

**You need:** light and dark soy sauce, black (Chinkiang) vinegar, Shaoxing wine, Sichuan pepper, chilli bean paste
(*doubanjiang*), sesame oil; cucumbers or a seasonal green; garlic; the lens template; tasting forms.

**Explanation:**

*Regional cuisines.* Traditionally there are eight great cuisines; five are a start:
- **Cantonese** (Guangdong): fresh and light; steaming and quick stir-frying, *dim sum*, roast meats;
- **Sichuan:** numbing Sichuan pepper with chilli (*mala*), fermented bean paste, bold layered flavour;
- **Hunan:** fresh and dried chilli, smoked and cured meats, sour pickles;
- **Shanghai** (Jiangnan): sweet-savoury red-braising with soy and sugar, Shaoxing wine, delicate river fish;
- **Beijing** and the north: wheat (noodles, dumplings, buns), lamb, roast duck.

*Balance and texture.* A meal balances tastes (salty, sweet, sour, bitter, umami, and in Sichuan the numbing *ma*),
methods, and textures: crisp, tender, slippery, chewy. Texture is often valued as much as flavour.

*Wok hei.* The "breath of the wok": a smoky, seared flavour from very high heat, small batches and constant movement.
On a home stove it is only partly possible: heat the wok until it smokes, and cook small amounts.

*The pantry:*
- **sauces:** light soy (for seasoning) and dark soy (mostly for colour); oyster sauce; chilli bean paste;
- **vinegar and wine:** rice vinegar, black Chinkiang vinegar, Shaoxing wine;
- **aromatics:** ginger, garlic, spring onion;
- **dried goods:** dried mushrooms, fermented black beans, Sichuan pepper, star anise;
- **sesame oil,** a finishing oil, not a cooking oil; **cornflour** for velveting and sauces.

*Meal structure and drinks.* Rice (or noodles, or buns) with several shared dishes, and a soup; tea. Chinese-Indonesian
restaurant food in the Netherlands is its own tradition, related but distinct.

**Practice:**
1. **A flavour tasting (60 min).** Light and dark soy, black vinegar, Shaoxing wine, Sichuan pepper and chilli bean
   paste tasted. Then one simple dish (a smashed cucumber salad in summer, or quick-braised greens) seasoned two
   regional ways: for example Sichuan (chilli oil, Sichuan pepper, black vinegar) and Cantonese (light soy, ginger, a
   little sesame oil).

**Look for:** you can place a dish by its seasoning and method.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A dark, bitter-sweet dish | Dark soy used as a seasoning | Light soy to season; dark soy for colour |
| A burnt, acrid taste | Sesame oil used for cooking | Sesame oil at the end, a few drops |
| All dishes taste alike | "Chinese" as one cuisine | Choose a region; season by its profile |

**Safety:** soy, sesame, wheat (check the soy sauce's label), fish and crustaceans (oyster sauce, dried shrimp) named
for guests.

**Record:** the tasting form, the two seasonings of the same dish, and the regional placing.

### CUI-3.M7.L2 — Stir-frying

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `HEA-1.M3.L1` · moves TS-DRY→4*

*Objectives:* [S] prep everything first, velvet the protein, wok heat on a home stove, the sauce at the end
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** a stir-fry takes minutes, so it is won or lost in the preparation. On a home stove, the batches and
the heat decide whether the food sears or steams.

**You need:** a flat-bottomed carbon-steel wok or a large heavy pan; the biggest burner (on induction, a flat wok); the
protein (chicken, pork or beef); soy, Shaoxing wine, cornflour, egg white or oil; seasonal vegetables (pointed cabbage,
leeks, green beans, mushrooms); a lid; small bowls; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*Prep everything first* (`WRK-1`):
- every ingredient cut to a similar size;
- the sauce mixed in a bowl;
- everything lined up, in the order it goes in.

*Velveting.* Thin slices of chicken, pork or beef marinated briefly in soy, Shaoxing wine, cornflour and a little egg
white or oil, then flashed in oil or water. The meat comes out tender and silky.

*Wok heat on a home stove:*
1. Heat the wok until it smokes before the oil goes in.
2. Cook in small batches, so the wok stays hot: a crowded wok steams.
3. Sear the protein and lift it out; cook the vegetables; put everything back together.

*The sauce at the end.* Pour the premixed sauce (soy, wine, stock, sugar, cornflour) in at the end, and toss until it is
glossy and coats. Stir the bowl just before pouring: cornflour settles, and a settled sauce goes in as lumps.

*Doneness.* Chicken is cooked to 75 °C; pork and beef slices are cooked through for a family meal. Probe a thick piece
from each batch.

**Practice:**
1. **A stir-fry (75 min).** A velveted chicken or pork stir-fry with seasonal vegetables, the batches timed.
2. **A vegetable stir-fry (45 min).** Greens with garlic, and a second vegetable dish, on the same hot wok.

**Look for:** seared, not steamed; vegetables bright and crisp-tender; a glossy sauce that coats.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Grey, steamed meat in liquid | A crowded wok | Smaller batches; reheat the wok between them |
| Burnt garlic, raw vegetables | Cutting during cooking | Everything cut and lined up first |
| Lumps in the sauce | Cornflour settled in the bowl | Stir the sauce just before it goes in |
| Tough meat | Not velveted; overcooked | Velvet; sear briefly and finish in the sauce |

**Safety:** a smoking wok and hot oil: no water near the oil, a lid at hand, the extraction on (`HEA-2.M4`); chicken to
75 °C (§9); pork and beef cooked through.

**Record:** the batch sizes and times, the probe readings, and photos of the sear and the gloss.

### CUI-3.M7.L3 — Dumplings and steaming

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M7.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-MOI→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a dumpling dough, a filling and two folds; steamed fish with ginger and spring onion; [K] hot-water versus cold-water dough
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** dumplings and steamed fish are the moist methods of the Chinese kitchen. The dough's water
temperature decides how it rolls and cooks; the probe decides when the filling and the fish are done.

**You need:** plain flour, boiling water; minced pork or chicken, or mushrooms, cabbage and tofu; ginger, spring onion,
soy, Shaoxing wine, sesame oil; a whole fish (a North Sea fish or a Dutch-farmed trout); a steamer; a pan with a lid;
the probe.

**Explanation:**

*Hot-water against cold-water dough:*
- **hot water** partly cooks the starch and weakens the gluten: a soft, tender dough, easy to roll thin, for steamed and
  pan-fried dumplings;
- **cold water** keeps the gluten strong: a chewier dough that holds up to boiling.

*The filling.* Minced pork (or chicken, or a vegetarian mix of mushrooms, cabbage and tofu) with ginger, spring onion,
soy, Shaoxing wine and sesame oil, beaten in one direction until sticky. Salt the cabbage and squeeze out its water
first; a watery filling bursts the wrapper.

*Two folds:*
- **a half-moon with pleats** along one side;
- **a simple pinched fold.**

Seal well, without trapped air.

*Cooking:*
- **boiled:** into boiling water, brought back to the boil with a splash of cold water, until they float and the
  filling is cooked;
- **pan-fried** (*guotie*): browned, then steamed with water under a lid, then crisped again.

A minced-pork filling reaches 75 °C (§9). Cut one dumpling from each batch and probe it.

*Steamed fish with ginger and spring onion.* Steam a whole fish over boiling water until it reaches 63 °C at the bone
(§9). Top with shredded ginger and spring onion, pour over smoking oil, and add a light soy dressing.

**Practice:**
1. **Dumplings (90 min).** A hot-water dough, a filling, two folds; boiled and pan-fried.
2. **Steamed fish (60 min).** A whole fish steamed, probed and dressed.

**Look for:** thin, sealed wrappers; a juicy filling at 75 °C; fish just opaque at the bone.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wrappers burst | A watery filling | Salt and squeeze the cabbage; beat the filling sticky |
| Thick, doughy pleats | Wrapper rolled evenly to the edge | Roll the edges thinner than the centre |
| Dry, flaky steamed fish | Steamed past 63 °C | Probe early at the thickest point by the bone |
| Dumplings stuck to the pan | Too little oil, lid lifted early | Oil the pan; steam until the water is gone |

**Safety:** the filling probed to 75 °C and the fish to 63 °C at the bone (§9); steam lid lifted away from you; hot oil
poured slowly over the fish.

**Record:** the dough, the probe readings for each batch, and photos of the folds and the fish.

### CUI-3.M7.L4 — A Chinese family-style meal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M7.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, JD-CHSE→3*

*Objectives:* [S] rice with three dishes of different methods and textures; [J] balance of the table, not the plate
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m7-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** in a Chinese family meal the balance is in the table, not on the plate. Three dishes made by
different methods, around rice, also need to reach the table hot together.

**You need:** rice; the ingredients for three dishes of different methods; the steamer, the wok and a braising pot; the
timeline; the probe; the household cards.

**Explanation:**

*The table.* Rice for everyone, and three or more dishes shared, for example:
- a stir-fry;
- a steamed or braised dish;
- a vegetable dish;
- perhaps a soup.

*Balance of the table, not the plate:*
- **methods:** stir-fried, steamed, braised;
- **main ingredients:** meat, fish, vegetable, tofu;
- **textures and tastes:** one spicy, one mild, one sour.

The rice is the centre: each dish is seasoned to be eaten with plain rice, so it can be saltier than a dish eaten on its
own.

*Timing.* The braised dish is made ahead: it holds, and many braises improve. The steamed dish goes in the steamer while
the wok heats. The stir-fry is last and fast, and goes straight to the table.

*An example, in autumn:*
- a red-braised pork belly (Shanghai), made the day before;
- a steamed trout with ginger and spring onion;
- stir-fried pointed cabbage with garlic and black vinegar;
- rice.

Three methods, three main ingredients, and three tastes: rich and sweet-savoury, delicate, and sharp. The pork is
reheated while the fish steams; the cabbage takes a few minutes at the end.

*Serving.* Everything goes on the table at once, with a bowl of rice for each person; the dishes are shared, each eater
taking a little at a time.


**Practice:**
1. **The meal (3 h).** Rice with three dishes of different methods for the household.

**Look for:** three distinct dishes, all hot, the stir-fry last.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Three dishes alike | Three stir-fries | One stir-fry; one steamed or braised; one vegetable |
| A limp, cold stir-fry | Made first | Braise ahead; stir-fry last |
| Bland with rice | Seasoned as a dish on its own | Taste each dish with a mouthful of rice |

**Safety:** the braise reheated until steaming hot throughout (§9); the fish and any poultry probed; leftover rice
cooled fast (`SCI-2.M6`).

**Record:** the menu with the methods, the timeline against actual, and the cards.

### CUI-3.M8.L1 — Dashi and Japanese principles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `STK-2.M2.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-STK→4*

*Objectives:* [K] seasonality and umami; [S] *dashi* from *kombu* and *katsuobushi*; [K] how it differs from Western stock
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** *dashi* is the stock under most Japanese cooking, and it is made in minutes, not hours. It carries
umami and aroma rather than body, which is why the Western stock habits of `STK-2` spoil it.

**You need:** *kombu*, *katsuobushi* (dried bonito flakes), dried shiitake; cold water; a pan; a fine sieve lined with
cloth or kitchen paper; a thermometer; salt and soy for tasting; tasting forms.

**Explanation:**

*Seasonality (*shun*) and restraint.* Ingredients at their peak, cooked to show their own flavour. Small portions, many
textures, and attention to presentation and the season.

*Umami as the backbone.* *Kombu* brings glutamate and *katsuobushi* brings inosinate; together they give the synergy of
`TST-1.M3.L4` and `FLV-3.M3`: more umami than either alone.

*The first* dashi:
1. Soak the *kombu* in cold water.
2. Heat it gently, and take it out just before the boil. Boiled *kombu* turns slimy and bitter.
3. Add the *katsuobushi* off the heat, steep for a few minutes, and strain without pressing. Squeezed flakes make it
   cloudy and fishy.

It should be clear, pale gold and delicate.

*The other* dashi:
- **a second *dashi*** (*niban dashi*): the used *kombu* and flakes simmered again in fresh water for a stronger,
  coarser stock, used for simmering;
- **a vegetarian *dashi*:** *kombu* with dried shiitake, soaked in cold water.

*How it differs from Western stock* (`STK-2`): minutes rather than hours; no bones and no browning; no gelatin. It is an
infusion that carries umami and aroma, not body. Simmered for an hour, it goes bitter and murky.

*The pantry:* soy sauce, *mirin*, sake, rice vinegar, white and red miso, *kombu*, *katsuobushi*, dried shiitake,
short-grain rice, sesame, *nori*, pickles.

**Practice:**
1. **Three *dashi* (60 min).** A *kombu*-*katsuobushi dashi*, a second *dashi* from its leftovers, and a
   *kombu*-shiitake *dashi*. Taste each plain, then with a pinch of salt and a few drops of soy.

**Look for:** a clear, delicate *dashi*; the umami obvious once salted.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Slimy, bitter *dashi* | The *kombu* boiled | Out just before the boil |
| Cloudy, fishy *dashi* | The flakes squeezed | Let them drain on their own |
| Murky and bitter | Simmered for an hour | Minutes, not hours |
| Tastes of nothing | Tasted unsalted | Taste with a pinch of salt; the umami appears |

**Safety:** fish (bonito), soy and sesame named for guests; the *dashi* cooled and in the fridge within 2 hours if kept
(§9).

**Record:** the three *dashi*, the tasting notes plain and salted, and the photos of their colour.

### CUI-3.M8.L2 — A set meal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M8.L1`, `STA-1.M2.L2` · moves TS-STK→4, IN-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *ichijū sansai*: rice, miso soup, three small dishes; [K] rice washing and cooking
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** *ichijū sansai*, "one soup, three dishes", is the shape of a Japanese home meal. It teaches the
rice, the miso soup, and the habit of small, distinct dishes.

**You need:** Japanese short-grain rice; a pan with a tight lid; *dashi*; white or red miso; tofu, wakame and spring
onion, or seasonal Dutch vegetables (leek, spinach, turnip, mushrooms); greens for *ohitashi*; sesame seeds; a cucumber
or cabbage for a quick pickle; small bowls and a tray.

**Explanation:**

*The set meal.* Rice, miso soup and three small dishes, each in its own bowl or plate on a tray:
- **a main:** often grilled fish or a simmered dish;
- **two sides:** for example a dressed vegetable and a pickle.

*The rice:*
1. Wash the short-grain rice until the water runs almost clear, to remove surface starch.
2. Soak for about 30 minutes.
3. Cook with a measured amount of water (by the packet) and a tight lid.
4. Rest for 10 minutes off the heat.

It should be sticky but with distinct grains. Leftover rice is cooled fast and kept cold (`SCI-2.M6`).

*Miso soup.* Heat the *dashi* with the tofu, wakame or vegetables until they are cooked. Then take it off the boil and
dissolve the miso in a ladle of the broth before stirring it in. Boiling dulls the miso's aroma. Finish with spring
onion.

*Small dishes:*
- ***ohitashi*:** blanched greens squeezed and dressed with *dashi* and soy;
- ***goma-ae*:** blanched greens with a sesame dressing;
- **a quick pickle:** cucumber or cabbage salted and pressed.

*Portions.* Each dish is small: a few mouthfuls. The tray is varied rather than large.

**Practice:**
1. **Rice and miso soup (60 min).** Rice by absorption; miso soup with seasonal vegetables.
2. **The side dishes (75 min).** Two small vegetable dishes and a quick pickle; the tray set.

**Look for:** glossy, sticky rice with distinct grains; miso soup not boiled; each dish small and distinct.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Gluey, mushy rice | Unwashed | Wash until the water runs almost clear |
| Flat, dull soup | The miso boiled | Off the boil; dissolve it in a ladle |
| A tray of Western portions | Each dish served as a main | A few mouthfuls each |
| Watery *ohitashi* | Greens not squeezed | Squeeze firmly after blanching |

**Safety:** leftover rice cooled fast and in the fridge within 2 hours (`SCI-2.M6`); soy, sesame and fish named for
guests.

**Record:** the rice ratio and soak, the photo of the tray, and the tasting notes.

### CUI-3.M8.L3 — Simmering and grilling the Japanese way

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M8.L2` · moves CK-CUIS→3, FL-SEAS→4*

*Objectives:* [S] *nimono* (simmered vegetables in seasoned dashi) and *yakimono* (salt-grilled fish or teriyaki); [K] the *sa-shi-su-se-so* seasoning order
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** *nimono* and *yakimono* are the simmered and grilled dishes of the set meal. Both depend on order:
the order the seasonings go in, and when the glaze goes on.

**You need:** seasonal Dutch roots (turnip, carrot, pumpkin, potato) and mushrooms; *dashi*; sugar, salt, soy, *mirin*;
a drop lid (*otoshibuta*) or a circle of baking paper; a whole mackerel or fillets, or chicken thighs; the grill or a
heavy pan; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*Nimono.* Vegetables simmered gently in seasoned *dashi* under a drop lid: a lid that rests on the food, keeping it
submerged and the pieces from tumbling. They absorb the seasoning and stay intact. Dutch roots fit well: turnip for
daikon, carrot, pumpkin, potato, mushrooms.

*The* sa-shi-su-se-so *order:*
1. sugar (*sa*);
2. salt (*shi*);
3. vinegar (*su*);
4. soy sauce (*se*, an old spelling);
5. miso (*so*).

Sugar goes first because its larger molecules penetrate slowly, and salt firms the food. Soy and miso go last because
their aroma fades with heat.

*Yakimono:*
- ***shioyaki*,** salt-grilled fish: a whole mackerel or fillets salted 20–30 minutes ahead, then grilled until the skin
  is crisp and the fish is cooked to 63 °C at the bone;
- ***teriyaki*:** chicken thighs or fish glazed with soy, *mirin* and sugar. The glaze goes on near the end, so it
  doesn't burn. Chicken is cooked to 75 °C.

*Seasoning by taste* (`FL-SEAS`). Taste each dish before serving, adjust the balance, and note the adjustment: a
*nimono* that needs a little more soy, a *teriyaki* glaze that is too sweet.

**Practice:**
1. ***Nimono* (60 min).** Seasonal roots simmered under a drop lid, seasoned in order.
2. ***Yakimono* (60 min).** Salt-grilled mackerel or a chicken *teriyaki*, probed.

**Look for:** vegetables intact and seasoned through; crisp-skinned fish at 63 °C; a glossy glaze, not burnt.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dark, flat *nimono* | Soy at the start of a long simmer | Sugar first; soy near the end |
| Broken vegetables | No drop lid; boiled hard | A drop lid; a gentle simmer |
| A burnt, bitter glaze | Glaze added early | Cook first; glaze at the end |
| Soggy skin on the fish | Not dried after salting | Pat dry; a hot grill |

**Safety:** fish to 63 °C at the bone and chicken to 75 °C (§9); the grill tray's fat watched, and a lid at hand.

**Record:** the seasoning order with amounts, the adjustments, and the probe readings.

### CUI-3.M8.L4 — Rice dishes and quick pickles

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M8.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, IN-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] a *donburi*, *onigiri* and *tsukemono*; [J] use leftovers in the Japanese way
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m8-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** the Japanese home kitchen wastes little: leftover rice and fish become lunch. That only works inside
the time limits of §9, and with an egg cooked for the eater.

**You need:** freshly cooked or properly stored short-grain rice; chicken, beef and onion, or tofu; *dashi*, soy,
*mirin*; eggs; fillings for *onigiri* (salted salmon, a pickled plum, a cooked vegetable); *nori*; salt; cucumber,
cabbage, radish or turnip for pickles; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*Donburi.* A bowl of rice with a simmered or grilled topping:
- ***oyakodon*:** chicken and egg simmered in seasoned *dashi*;
- ***gyūdon*:** beef and onion;
- **a vegetable or tofu version.**

The topping moistens the rice without drowning it. Chicken is cooked to 75 °C. **A soft-set egg is not served to risk
groups (§9);** for them it is cooked through.

*Onigiri.* Rice balls shaped with wet, salted hands around a filling, and wrapped in *nori*. The rice must be warm and
sticky enough to shape. *Onigiri* are eaten the same day and kept cool; cooked rice follows the rules of §9.

*Tsukemono.* Quick pickles of cucumber, cabbage, radish or turnip with salt (and *kombu*, rice vinegar or miso), ready
in hours, kept in the fridge.

*Using leftovers the Japanese way:*
- leftover rice as *onigiri*, fried rice or rice porridge;
- leftover grilled fish flaked into *onigiri*;
- a little leftover simmered dish on a *donburi*;
- always within the 2-day limit (§9). Write the day each leftover was cooked on its box.

**Practice:**
1. **A *donburi* and *onigiri* (75 min).** A *donburi* and three *onigiri* with different fillings; a quick pickle.

**Look for:** *onigiri* that hold together; a *donburi* topping that moistens the rice without drowning it.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| *Onigiri* fall apart | Rice too dry or cold | Shape warm, freshly cooked rice; wet, salted hands |
| A soggy bowl | Too much liquid in the topping | Reduce the topping; spoon it on |
| A leftover past its time | Age not logged | Date every box; at most 2 days |
| A soft egg for a pregnant guest | Risk groups not asked | Ask; cook the egg through for them |

**Safety:** leftovers within 2 days (§9); rice reheated until steaming hot throughout, once; a soft egg not for risk
groups; *onigiri* kept cool and eaten the same day.

**Record:** the leftovers used with their ages, the egg (soft or set, and for whom), and the photos.

### CUI-3.M9.L1 — The Mexican pantry

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l1 -->
*2.5 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M1.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3*

*Objectives:* [K] fresh and dried chillies, corn and *nixtamal*, beans; regional styles
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l1 -->

**Why it matters:** Mexican cooking rests on chillies, corn and beans. A dried chilli is a different ingredient from its
fresh form, and corn treated with lime is a different food from plain ground corn. Both have to be understood before
the salsas and tortillas make sense.

**You need:** three dried chillies (*ancho*, *guajillo*, *chipotle*); a fresh chilli (jalapeño or serrano); a dry heavy
pan; hot water; a blender; onion, garlic, salt, lime; tongs; gloves for handling chillies; the lens template; tasting
forms.

**Explanation:**

*Chillies, fresh and dried:*
- **fresh** (jalapeño, serrano, *poblano*): brightness and heat;
- **dried** (*ancho*, *guajillo*, *pasilla*, *chipotle*): toasted, soaked and blended for depth, colour and sweetness.

A dried chilli is a different ingredient from its fresh form: an *ancho* is a dried *poblano*, and tastes of raisins and
cocoa rather than green pepper.

*Toasting dried chillies.* Seeds and stems out; flat on a dry, hot pan for seconds per side, until fragrant and just
pliable; then soaked in hot water. A few seconds too long and they turn bitter.

*Corn and* nixtamal. Dried corn is cooked with lime (calcium hydroxide, *cal*). This loosens the hulls, makes the dough
hold together and improves its nutrition. The treated corn is ground into *masa* for tortillas and *tamales*.

*Beans.* Black or pinto beans cooked slowly with onion and herbs (*epazote*), often refried. Dried beans follow the bean
rule of §9: soaked for at least 5 hours with the water poured away, then boiled in fresh water for at least 30 minutes.

*Regional styles:*
- **the north:** wheat tortillas, grilled beef;
- **Oaxaca:** moles, cheese;
- **the Yucatán:** achiote, sour orange, pit-cooking;
- **central Mexico:** street food, *tacos*.

*Meal structure and drinks.* The main meal at midday; tortillas at every meal; salsas on the table. *Agua fresca*
(fruit or flower water), water, beer.

**Practice:**
1. **A chilli tasting (60 min).** Three dried chillies toasted, soaked and blended into small salsas, with salt and a
   little garlic; a fresh salsa alongside. Taste each for heat, sweetness and smoke.

**Look for:** you can tell *ancho*, *guajillo* and *chipotle* apart by taste and use.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Bitter, acrid salsa | Chillies burnt while toasting | Seconds per side; off when fragrant |
| Everything tastes of "chilli" | Chilli powder blends for everything | Whole dried chillies, chosen by taste |
| Gritty salsa | Seeds and skins left in | Seeds out before toasting; blend longer or sieve |

**Safety:** gloves for cutting chillies, and hands kept from the eyes; the extraction on while toasting (the fumes
sting).

**Record:** the tasting form for each chilli, and the lens notes.

### CUI-3.M9.L2 — Salsas and a braise

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l2 -->
*3.5 h · 2 practical sessions · elapsed 3 h · builds on `FLV-3.M2.L4` · moves FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] toasted-chilli and fresh salsas; a braise in a chilli sauce
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l2 -->

**Why it matters:** salsas give a Mexican meal its brightness; a braise in a dried-chilli sauce gives it depth. The same
chillies do both jobs, prepared two different ways.

**You need:** Dutch tomatoes (in summer), onion, a fresh chilli, coriander, lime; tomatillos, or green tomatoes from a
late-season glut; dried chillies; garlic and spices (cumin, oregano); pork shoulder or chicken legs; a dry heavy pan; a
blender; a heavy casserole; the probe.

**Explanation:**

*Fresh salsas:*
- ***pico de gallo*:** tomato, onion, chilli, coriander and lime, in summer with Dutch tomatoes;
- **a raw green salsa;**
- seasoned and balanced, kept in the fridge, and out for at most 2 hours (§9).

*Cooked salsas.* Tomatoes or tomatillos (green tomatoes stand in), chillies, onion and garlic charred on a dry pan
(`VEG-3.M2`), then blended. A toasted-dried-chilli salsa is made the same way, from soaked chillies.

*A braise in a chilli sauce:*
1. Toast, soak and drain the dried chillies.
2. Blend them with aromatics, spices and some of the soaking liquid into a smooth sauce.
3. Fry the sauce briefly in hot fat, stirring, until it darkens and thickens. This deepens it; skipping it leaves a raw
   taste.
4. Braise pork shoulder or chicken legs in it until tender (`SLW-3`). Chicken is cooked to 75 °C.

*Balance.* Heat against acid, salt and a little sweetness. Choose the salsa to contrast the braise: a sharp, fresh salsa
against a deep, rich sauce.

**Practice:**
1. **Two salsas (60 min).** A fresh salsa and a charred, blended salsa.
2. **The braise (90 min active, 3 h in total).** A pork or chicken braise in a dried-chilli sauce.

**Look for:** salsas bright and balanced; a deep red, glossy braising sauce; tender meat.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A bitter sauce | Burnt chillies | Seconds per side; discard any that blacken |
| A raw-tasting sauce | Not fried after blending | Fry it in hot fat until it darkens |
| A flat salsa | No acid or salt | Lime and salt, tasted in steps |
| A sour, tired salsa | Left out all afternoon | In the fridge; out for at most 2 hours |

**Safety:** the blended sauce spits when it hits the hot fat: a deep pan, a lid at hand, the arm clear; chicken to
75 °C (§9); salsas out for at most 2 hours.

**Record:** the chillies, the sauce steps, the braise time, and the balance notes.

### CUI-3.M9.L3 — *Masa* and tortillas

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l3 -->
*3 h · 2 practical sessions · builds on `CUI-3.M9.L1` · moves CK-CUIS→3, TS-STA→4*

*Objectives:* [S] corn tortillas from *masa harina*, pressed and cooked on a dry pan; tacos and a simple *tamal*; [K] *nixtamalización*
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l3 -->

**Why it matters:** a corn tortilla is made from corn alone, because the corn has been treated with lime. A good one
puffs on the pan and stays pliable; a dry dough or a cool pan gives a stiff disc.

**You need:** *masa harina*; warm water; salt; a tortilla press, or a heavy pan and two sheets of plastic (a freezer bag
cut open); a dry cast-iron pan or *comal*; a cloth; fillings, onion, coriander and a salsa for tacos; for the *tamales*,
lard or another fat, stock, corn husks soaked in hot water, a steamer.

**Explanation:**

*Nixtamalización.* The lime treatment of corn (`CUI-3.M9.L1`) is why tortillas can be made from corn alone. *Masa
harina* is dried, ground *nixtamal* corn, mixed with water to a dough.

*Corn tortillas:*
1. Mix *masa harina* and warm water to a soft, smooth dough, like play-dough, and rest it.
2. Press a small ball: if the edges crack, the dough is too dry. Add water a little at a time until they don't.
3. Press the balls between two sheets of plastic in a tortilla press, or with a heavy pan.
4. Cook on a dry, hot cast-iron pan: turn once, then again. A good tortilla puffs.
5. Keep them warm, stacked and wrapped in a cloth, where they steam and stay pliable.

*Tacos.* A warm tortilla, a filling, a salsa, onion and coriander. Keep them simple.

*A simple* tamal:
1. Beat *masa* with fat and stock until light.
2. Spread it on a soaked corn husk, add a filling, and fold.
3. Steam until the *masa* comes away from the husk.

**Practice:**
1. **Tortillas (75 min).** A batch pressed and cooked; the puff photographed; tacos.
2. ***Tamales* (75 min active, 2 h in total).** A small batch with a vegetable or the braise as the filling, steamed.

**Look for:** tortillas that puff and stay pliable; *tamales* that come away from the husk.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cracked edges | Dough too dry | Water a little at a time; rest again |
| Stiff, pale tortillas | Pan not hot enough | Heat the pan well; turn once, then again |
| Tortillas dry out | Stacked uncovered | Wrap in a cloth as they come off |
| *Tamales* stick to the husk | Not steamed long enough | Steam on; test one |

**Safety:** a braise used as a filling reheated until steaming hot throughout and not reheated again; the steamer lid
lifted away from you; the hot pan's handle covered.

**Record:** the water added to the dough, the photo of the puff, and the *tamal* steaming time.

### CUI-3.M9.L4 — A Mexican meal

<!-- BEGIN generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l4 -->
*3 h · 1 practical session · builds on `CUI-3.M9.L3` · moves CK-CUIS→3, FL-ARCH→3*

*Objectives:* [S] tortillas, two salsas, a braise or grilled centre, beans and a fresh drink (*agua fresca*); [J] heat levels for the table
<!-- END generated:lesson-cui-3-m9-l4 -->

**Why it matters:** at a Mexican table, the heat is set by each eater, not by the cook. Planning heat levels lets
children and guests who don't like heat eat the same meal.

**You need:** tortillas (or the dough); two salsas; a braise or a grilled centre (`FIR-3`); beans; a fresh side; an
*agua fresca*; labels for the salsas; the timeline; the household or guest cards.

**Explanation:**

*The meal:*
- warm tortillas;
- two salsas, one fresh and one cooked;
- a braise or a grilled centre;
- beans;
- a fresh side: a cabbage and radish slaw, sliced radishes, or pickled onions;
- an *agua fresca*, for example with Dutch rhubarb (the stalks only), cucumber and lime, or dried hibiscus flowers.

*Heat levels for the table:*
- the main dishes moderately hot;
- the heat added at the table, through salsas of different strengths, each labelled;
- a mild option for children and for guests who don't like heat.

*Timing:*
- the braise ahead: it holds and improves, and is reheated until steaming hot throughout;
- the salsas and slaw ahead, kept cold;
- the tortillas cooked last and kept warm in a cloth, or cooked in batches through the meal.

*A fresh, sharp side.* A rich braise and beans need something sharp and crunchy beside them: pickled onions, radishes,
or a lime-dressed slaw.

*Labelling the salsas.* Write each salsa's heat on a card beside it (mild, medium, hot), and taste them in that order
yourself before the meal, so the labels match.


**Practice:**
1. **The meal (3 h).** A Mexican meal for the household or guests, the heat levels planned.

**Look for:** everyone can eat at their own heat; warm tortillas throughout.

**If it goes wrong:**

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Some guests can't eat | Everything very hot | Moderate dishes; heat in the salsas |
| Cold, stiff tortillas | Made all at once, early | Cook last; wrap in a cloth; batches |
| A heavy, flat table | No fresh, sharp side | Pickled onions, radishes, a slaw |

**Safety:** salsas and slaw out for at most 2 hours (§9); the braise reheated until steaming hot throughout; the heat
levels labelled for guests.

**Record:** the heat plan, the labels, the timeline, and the cards.

**Task:** cook a Mexican meal with layered flavour, and heat set by the table.
- **The brief:** tortillas, two salsas, a braise or grilled centre, beans, a fresh side and a drink.
- **The constraints:** a flavour plan for the braise and the salsas; heat levels planned and labelled; Dutch seasonal
  produce, with every substitution named.
